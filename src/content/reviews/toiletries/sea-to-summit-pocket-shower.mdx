## Overview

The Sea to Summit Pocket Shower is a 10-liter roll-top bag of black 70D nylon with a twist-valve showerhead sewn into the base. It weighs 120 g and folds to roughly the size of a fist. It is aimed at backpackers who want a warm rinse at a basecamp or desert camp without carrying a real shower system. The trade-off is plain: very little weight buys a gravity-fed shower whose pressure fades as the bag empties and whose cord is the weakest part of the kit.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** This is the reason to buy it. At 120 g, the shower weighs about as much as a small canister of sunscreen. Testers at Hiking Insights scored it 4.9 out of 5 on weight and packability, and noted that most camp showers are too heavy to justify. Few other pieces of gear that weigh this little can also be called a luxury.

**Water flow.** Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found that it can't match the pressure of the pump-driven showers in their lineup. They judged it good enough to rinse hair and wash campsite dishes. The showerhead's wide spray pattern, with some holes angled toward the edges, gives decent coverage for a gravity unit. Because the bag has no hose, pressure depends on the head of water above the nozzle. One owner on a bushcraft forum got about five minutes of true shower spray before it turned into a stream. The whole session ran roughly ten minutes with the water shut off during soaping. Two Hiking Insights conclusions apply here: shower quickly while the bag is full, and treat it as a functional rinse, not a home-style shower.

**Water use.** The 10-liter capacity is generous for how most people use it. One van-life reviewer got a full wash from about 1.5 liters. Another reviewer found that less than half the bag is enough for an adequate shower. Owners often report getting two showers from one fill if they shut the valve while lathering.

**Solar heating.** The black fabric absorbs heat well. Outdoor Gear Lab says it won't heat in minutes like larger showers, but a few hours of midday sun gives pleasantly hot water. Results depend heavily on the weather, and Hiking Insights cautions that meaningful warming needs the right conditions. A reviewer at Adventure Kit suggests adding a little pre-heated water to speed things up, as long as the total stays under 60°C (140°F) to protect the seams. The open top lets users reach in and test the temperature by hand, which one reviewer counted as a plus.

**Hanging it.** This is where the Pocket Shower draws its loudest complaints. A full bag weighs over 20 lb, and owners on Trailspace say hoisting it over a branch chews up the supplied cord. Some of those owners report that the cord broke, and the usual advice is to swap in paracord or spare guyline. Reviewers also report the bag sagging under its own weight, so hang it higher than seems necessary. Without a good anchor point it's awkward. One owner says it's easy to hold up with one hand for short rinses.

**Durability and leaks.** The 70D nylon is the same fabric Sea to Summit uses in its lightweight dry sacks, and long-term owners describe the bag itself as tough. A Trailspace owner reported four years of use with some leaks, and a bushcraft forum reviewer saw no wear after repeated use. Reviewers report three recurring problems:

- The roll-top can seep if the bag sits full in the sun.
- Over-tightening the showerhead can warp its rubber washer and cause a leak.
- Small debris can clog the showerhead, so filter or strain the water you pour in.

**Dry sack duty.** The roll-top works as a bag for protecting gear from rain. Owners also use it as a camp wash basin, a laundry bag (add water and detergent, then shake), or a food hang for keeping supplies away from animals.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 120 g and fist-sized, so it is easy to justify carrying
- 10-liter capacity gives one or two quick showers
- Black fabric heats water in a few hours of midday sun
- Open top is easy to fill, clean and dry, and lets you check the temperature
- Doubles as a dry sack, wash basin or laundry bag
- Tough 70D nylon with taped seams
- Often priced under $40

**Cons**

- Pressure falls as the bag empties and no hose concentrates the flow
- The supplied 20 ft cord is the first thing to fail
- A full bag is heavy and hard to hang high enough
- Showerhead clogs and the washer can leak if over-tightened
- Roll-top may weep if left full in the sun
- Solar heating is unreliable in cool or overcast conditions

## Who Should Buy This

This is the shower for weekend backpackers, basecamp campers, desert hikers, bikepackers and car campers who want a hot rinse for under 5 oz. It suits warm, sunny trips where hanging a bag for a few hours is easy. Pack a length of paracord and a carabiner and it will do its job.

Thru-hikers should think twice. Trail reviewers point out that most long-distance hikers wash in lakes and streams. Anyone counting grams on a shoulder-season trip with cold, cloudy weather will gain little from a solar bag. Those who want strong, steady pressure should look at a pump or pressurized shower, and accept the extra weight.

## Verdict

The Pocket Shower delivers on weight, packed size and simplicity. It's a quick, warm rinse that also works as a dry sack. The weak cord, fading pressure and finicky showerhead keep it from being flawless, but they are cheap and easy to work around. Buy it for sunny multi-day trips, replace the cord, and expect a rinse rather than a spa.
