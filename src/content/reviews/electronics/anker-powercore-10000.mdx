## Overview

The Anker PowerCore 10000 spent years as the default trail-forum recommendation for a compact, affordable 10,000 mAh battery. The Standard (model A1263) is a single-port, USB-A-only brick that charges one device at a time and carries a CPSC recall on a large share of US units. The Redux adds USB-C and a much faster recharge for about 16 g more, which makes it the easier version to recommend to anyone shopping today.

## Performance

**Capacity.** The Standard's biggest weakness is how much of its rated capacity actually reaches a phone. In discharge testing at a 2.4A draw, only 5,273 mAh came out, about 53 percent of the rating. Dropping to a gentler 1A draw lifted that to roughly 64 percent. Anker's marketing promises 3.5 iPhone 8 charges or 2.5 Galaxy S8 charges. Testing landed closer to 2.2 S8 charges. Some conversion loss is normal for any power bank, but the A1263 sits toward the inefficient end. Tech Gear Lab scored its charging efficiency 5.0 out of 10, against 8.0 for portability. In practice, plan around 6,000–6,500 mAh of usable energy.

**Size and weight.** At 179 g in a 2.4 x 3.6 x 0.9 in body, the Standard slips into a hip-belt pocket without much fuss, and reviewers consistently rate its size well for the capacity. The Redux trades that blocky shape for a pill-like profile with a carrying strap. TechRadar called it light and easy to pack, but too thick to carry comfortably in a pants pocket. On a pack, that matters little. In a running-shorts pocket, it matters more.

**Charging speed.** The Standard's 2.4A output handles phones and larger devices. Anker's PowerIQ reads the connected device and adjusts output, which works across iPhones, Android phones and USB accessories. The limit is the single port. Phone, headlamp, watch and GPS have to take turns, and there's no pass-through charging, so the bank can't be topped up while it feeds a phone.

The Redux is faster on both ends. TechRadar's testers took a Pixel 7a from half to full in just over an hour through USB-C. USB-A took about an hour and a half. Four LEDs showed 50–75 percent remaining after the USB-C test, which the reviewer read as roughly three to four charges. The trickle-charge mode keeps low-draw devices such as earbuds and fitness bands from shutting off early. TechRadar's list of drawbacks was short: no display and no wireless charging, neither of which a hiker needs.

**Recharging the bank.** This is where the versions diverge most. The Standard's Micro-USB input needs 6–7 hours on a 5V/2A charger. That means an overnight plug-in at a hostel or trailhead, not a quick top-up over lunch. Micro-USB cables are also disappearing from most hikers' kits. The Redux recharged in about 2 hours 40 minutes through USB-C, which fits a town stop or a long resupply meal.

**The recall.** Anker identified a potential overheating issue in certain A1263 units manufactured between January 1, 2016 and October 30, 2019 and sold in the US between June 1, 2016 and December 31, 2022. The lithium-ion cell in affected units may overheat, with a risk of melting, smoke or fire. Owners should check their serial number at anker.com/a1263-recall before packing the bank. Confirmed units should be retired, and Anker offers a replacement power bank. A recalled battery doesn't belong in a pack or a tent.

## Choosing a Version

**Standard (179 g).** This is the lighter, simpler and cheaper option, built around a single USB-A port and a Micro-USB input. It suits a hiker with a post-recall unit that has cleared the serial check, or someone who finds one at a steep discount for phone-only weekend trips. New buyers should treat the recall and the dated ports as real strikes against it.

**Redux (195 g).** The extra 16 g, about 0.6 oz, buys USB-C in and out, a USB-A port as well, faster phone charging and a recharge time less than half the Standard's. That's a sensible trade for anyone running a USB-C phone, headphones or a modern headlamp. The Redux is the better pick for most hikers. The A1263 recall notice applies to the Standard's model number.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Compact for 10,000 mAh, at 179 g (Standard) or 195 g (Redux)
- PowerIQ matches output to the connected device
- Redux adds USB-C in and out, a USB-A port and a trickle-charge mode
- Redux recharges in about 2 hours 40 minutes, against 6–7 hours for the Standard
- Under airline limits for carry-on lithium batteries
- 18-month warranty on the Standard, with responsive support

**Cons**

- A CPSC recall covers a large share of US Standard units
- Standard usable capacity measured at 53–64 percent of the rating
- Standard has one USB-A output, a Micro-USB input and no pass-through
- Standard recharge time is slow at resupply stops
- Redux is thick for pocket carry
- No flashlight, display or built-in cable

## Who Should Buy This

The Redux fits hikers who want a simple, light 10,000 mAh bank with USB-C and a recharge time that fits a town stop. It covers a phone, a watch and the occasional headlamp across several days. The Standard only fits owners whose A1263 cleared the recall check, and bargain hunters who need a phone-only backup. Hikers running a camera, a GPS and a phone from one bank should look at a bank with more ports, or carry the Redux and sequence their charges.

## Verdict

The Redux is the version to buy: modern ports, fast recharging and a modest 16 g penalty over the Standard. The Standard A1263 earned its following, but the recall, the measured capacity shortfall and a 6–7 hour Micro-USB recharge make it hard to choose new. Owners of an A1263 should check the serial number before the next trip.
