## Overview

The Exosphere 0° is Deuter's synthetic-fill mummy bag built around a stretchy "ThermoStretch" shingle construction instead of the usual sewn-through or box-wall baffles you see on most synthetic bags. It's aimed at three-season backpackers and bikepackers who want down-like packability and fit without down's moisture sensitivity or price tag. If you camp somewhere damp — the UK, the Pacific Northwest, shoulder-season alpine trips where condensation is a given — this is the kind of bag that's designed to shrug that off.

## Performance

The headline feature is the stretch. Deuter builds the bag from shingled, elasticated chambers that <cite index="10-5">expand by up to 25% in width and improve comfort, fit and efficiency in the process</cite>. In practice, this is the part that surprises most reviewers. One tester who was skeptical going in said <cite index="10-1,10-2">he was a bit dubious about the stretchiness of the bag, but it actually works really well, with the stretch being quite gentle but enough to make the bag hug your body nicely</cite>. That close fit isn't just about comfort — it's doing thermal work too, since <cite index="12-3,12-4">the elastic seams stretch up to 25% in width for added comfort, and this also makes them more thermally efficient by eliminating dead space</cite>. The trade-off is that it feels a little odd on the first night. Testers of the related -10° model noted <cite index="12-10">when you first climb into this bag, its elasticated chambers result in a slightly unusual body-hugging sensation that feels strange at first</cite> — expect an adjustment period, not a dealbreaker.

Warmth distribution is the other thing Deuter gets right here. The 360° fill means <cite index="10-11,10-12">the synthetic Thermo ProLoft fill is distributed totally evenly top and bottom, so the bag conforms to its temperature rating regardless of sleep position — no rolling over and getting a cold back</cite>. For side sleepers (most of us, if we're honest), that's a genuinely useful design choice that a lot of synthetic bags skip in favor of concentrating loft on top only.

Where the Exosphere 0° struggles is the same place every synthetic bag struggles: weight-to-warmth against down. One long-term UK reviewer was blunt about it, noting <cite index="3-2,3-3">at over 1kg this synthetic-filled bag is a lot heavier for its insulating performance than the best lightweight down-filled alternatives, with one down model of equivalent temperature rating weighing less than half as much</cite>. That's the calculus you're signing up for with any synthetic bag in this temperature range — you're paying a weight tax for moisture resistance and a lower price. The same reviewer, though, was quick to add some perspective: <cite index="3-4">the weight isn't excessive for a synthetic bag, and it hasn't been enough to worry him in a rucksack</cite>.

Packability is better than you'd expect for synthetic fill. The bag <cite index="3-5">comes with its own compression sack and packs down really neatly for backpacking and bikepacking, feeling less bulky than other synthetic bags</cite> the reviewer had used. At a 16 x 32 cm packed size, it's not going to disappear in your pack the way a premium down bag would, but it's respectable for the category.

On the durability and weather-resistance side, synthetic fill continues to earn its keep. <cite index="3-1,3-8">Unlike many down bags, testers report no fill leakage with the Exosphere, and overall it feels like a bag you don't have to treat with real care</cite>. That's echoed in the moisture performance too — <cite index="3-9">for UK use, the obvious advantage of synthetic fills is that they tend to maintain their performance better than down when things get a bit damp</cite>. The shell fabric backs this up: <cite index="3-12,3-13">the 30D nylon outer feels durable enough for a sleeping bag and dries quickly, with a patch of water-repellent Dryzone fabric at both ends where your head or feet might nudge the tent inner</cite>.

Value is a genuine strong point. <cite index="3-10">At £145, this sleeping bag is a lot more affordable than most down bags of similar warmth</cite> — and that price gap between synthetic and down-of-equivalent-rating hasn't really closed in recent years.

One veteran reviewer summed up her take succinctly: <cite index="13-11">"This is my new favourite synthetic sleeping bag - snug, well-designed, and not overly heavy for backpacking and general outdoor use"</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Stretchy ThermoStretch seams give a snug, body-hugging fit without feeling restrictive, and reduce dead air space that would otherwise need warming
- 360° fill distribution means consistent warmth no matter what position you sleep in — good news for side sleepers
- Synthetic fill shrugs off dampness and packs down smaller than most synthetic competitors
- Durable 30D nylon shell with reinforced water-repellent panels at head and foot
- Noticeably cheaper than a down bag with a comparable temperature rating

**Cons**

- Over 1kg puts it well behind ultralight down bags in warmth-per-gram — not a bag for gram-counting thru-hikers
- The stretch-hug fit takes a night or two to get used to; some sleepers find it odd at first
- Packed size (16 x 32 cm) is good for synthetic but still bulkier than comparable down bags
- Temperature ratings, as with all EN/ISO tested bags, are based on a mannequin lying on its back — expect the real-world lower limit to run warmer for side sleepers

## Who Should Buy This

This bag makes the most sense for backpackers, bikepackers, and car campers in wet or unpredictable climates — think the UK, Pacific Northwest, or shoulder-season trips where a soggy tent wall or damp night in a bivy is a real possibility. If you're chasing base weight and mostly camp in dry, predictable conditions, a down bag rated to 0°C will beat it on weight and pack size every time. But if you want a bag you don't have to babysit — one that keeps insulating even if it gets damp and doesn't demand a dry sack obsession — the Exosphere 0° delivers that at a fair price.

## Verdict

The Exosphere 0° won't win any weight weenie awards, but it does what a synthetic bag should: keep working when things get wet, hold its temperature rating regardless of sleep position, and cost noticeably less than a comparable down bag. For UK-style three-season use where damp is a given, I'd call it a smart, if not featherlight, choice. Rating: 7.5/10.
