## Overview

The CamelBak Crux 2L is a mainstream hydration reservoir built for day hikes, overnights, and everyday trail use rather than gram-counted thru-hiking. It's the reservoir you'll find bundled into most CamelBak packs, and it's sold standalone for anyone upgrading an older Antidote-style bladder or replacing a bargain-bin bladder that leaked or tasted like a garden hose. If you want the easiest-drinking, easiest-to-fill bladder on the market and don't mind a little extra weight and bulk, this is the one to look at.

## Performance

**Fill and cleaning:** The wide-mouth opening with the fold-out handle is the standout feature here. You can dunk it in a stream, tip it under a spigot, or dump ice cubes in without fighting a narrow neck, and the handle holds the mouth open on its own so you're not wrestling with a floppy bladder while you pour. <cite index="16-7,16-8">The elongated ergonomic handle is easy to hold with one hand and ensures the reservoir stays wide open for filling and provides for a spill-free closing, and the cap now flops away from the handle and stays completely out of the way to simplify both filling and cleaning.</cite>

**Drinking experience:** This is where the Crux earns its reputation. CleverHiker's testing put it bluntly: <cite index="1-13,1-14,1-15">the Camelbak Crux is user-friendly, affordable, and doesn't taste like rubber, and among all the bladders they tested, the Crux delivered the best drinking experience, with water flowing easily through the bite valve and tasting superior to nearly all the other bladders with hose systems.</cite> The wider-bore hose backs that up — CamelBak's own materials note the tube <cite index="10-1">delivers 25% more water per sip and an on/off lever that makes it easy to prevent leaks.</cite>

**Leak resistance:** Most long-term users report the current cap and on/off lever system holds up well. One rider testing it over five weeks found <cite index="16-6">it hasn't leaked or caused any difficulty issues getting it open and closed, which is a vast improvement over its predecessor</cite>. That said, it's not universal — a cluster of REI reviews flagged fit and sealing complaints when the redesigned Crux replaced the older 2L reservoir, with one reviewer saying <cite index="4-6,4-7">the new design is garbage, because unless you hold the retainer perfectly level, it distorts and the cap will not screw on tight, so it leaks</cite>. In my own experience and in most other write-ups I found, this seems to be the exception rather than the rule, but it's worth double-checking the seal on yours before you trust it in a full pack.

**Practical quirks worth knowing:** A couple of smaller field notes turned up across reviews. One tester noted you need to make sure <cite index="2-12,2-14">the hose is clipped fully into the bottom part of the reservoir to connect to the rest of the bladder, since there might be leaks if the hose doesn't fully clip into that connection point</cite> — a five-second check before you pack it that solves 90% of "why is my back wet" moments. Cold-weather users should also plan ahead, since it can be <cite index="2-16">difficult to use in the winter as the drinking tube tends to freeze up</cite> — CamelBak sells an insulated tube kit specifically to address this if you're a winter regular.

**Weight and packability:** This is the honest trade-off. CleverHiker's review sums it up well: <cite index="1-3,1-16,1-17">the Crux is not the lightest or most packable bladder available, and its bulky plastic handle makes it easy to fill but difficult to fold and store when not in use, and it also lacks structural features like a rigid back plate.</cite> At 211g it's noticeably heavier than minimalist options built for trail running or ultralight backpacking.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Best-in-class drinking experience — wide-bore hose, no plastic aftertaste
- Wide-mouth opening and handle make filling, adding ice, and cleaning genuinely easy
- Quicklink system lets you disconnect the hose and pull the reservoir without draining it
- On/off lever at the bite valve reliably prevents drips during transport
- Hydroguard antimicrobial lining helps keep the reservoir cleaner between washes

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than dedicated ultralight/running bladders
- Bulky handle and cap don't fold flat, so it takes up more pack space empty
- No rigid backplate, so it can slump or shift in a loosely packed bag
- Hose is prone to freezing in cold weather without an insulated cover
- A minority of users have reported cap-sealing issues after CamelBak's design update

## Who Should Buy This

The Crux 2L makes the most sense for day hikers, overnight backpackers, and mountain bikers who prioritize easy filling and pleasant drinking over shaving ounces. If you're already running a CamelBak pack, it's the natural, well-integrated choice. Ultralight backpackers and trail runners chasing every gram will be better served by a slimmer, baffle-less bladder — but for most people who just want a bladder that works and doesn't taste weird, the Crux is hard to beat.

## Verdict

The CamelBak Crux 2L isn't trying to be the lightest reservoir on the trail, and it isn't — but it nails the fundamentals that actually matter day-to-day: easy filling, clean taste, and a leak-resistant on/off valve. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: excellent for general hiking and biking use, but ultralight hikers should look elsewhere for a lighter, more packable option.
