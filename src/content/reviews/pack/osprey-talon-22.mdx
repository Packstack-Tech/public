## Overview

The Osprey Talon 22 is one of the most recognizable daypacks on the trail — and for good reason. It's a 22-liter, feature-packed pack aimed at day hikers, peak-baggers, and multi-sport users who want structured support, solid organization, and the confidence that the pack will outlast them. It's not a lightweight minimalist option, but for hikers who value a padded hipbelt, a semi-rigid frame, and enough pockets for a well-organized kit, it's hard to beat at this price point.

## Performance

**Fit & Comfort**

The Talon 22's biggest selling point isn't a spec — it's the carry. The AirScape back panel uses a mesh-covered, ridge-molded foam design that keeps the pack close to your back while offering decent airflow. The back panel is vertically adjustable, sliding up or down to dial in torso fit, and testers across different body types — male and female — consistently land a comfortable fit. That said, the ventilation is average compared to suspended-mesh designs like those on the Gregory Zulu or Osprey's own Stratos line; on warm days with a moderate load, expect a damp shirt.

The padded hipbelt is a genuine differentiator at this pack volume. Most competitors in the 22L class use thin webbing or skip a hipbelt altogether. The Talon's hipbelt actually transfers load to your hips, which matters on longer days with 15–20 lbs on your back. Load lifters round out the suspension system. It's a lot of frame hardware for a daypack — critics fairly point out that day hiking loads rarely _need_ that infrastructure, and it's a major contributor to the pack's weight. That's a legitimate design-philosophy debate, but for hikers who do push toward the upper end of the 30-lb load range, the frame earns its keep.

**Organization & Features**

The layout is well thought-out for active days. Two side stretch pockets handle 1-liter bottles with room to spare. The large rear shove-it pocket is ideal for a rain shell or wet layers you need accessible fast. A small zippered top pocket handles snacks and sunglasses. The zippered front pocket includes a mesh organizer and key clip for smaller items. Hipbelt pockets fit a phone and a few bars — though a consistent minor complaint across testers is that the pockets sit slightly too far back to be easy one-handed grabs mid-stride.

The 2024–2025 redesign moved the hydration sleeve to the pack's interior, which is generally an improvement for fit and balance. One caveat: the bladder suspension clip doesn't play perfectly with all third-party bladders, so if you're using a non-Osprey reservoir, have a small carabiner handy as a backup.

For technical use, the Talon brings ice axe loops, trekking pole attachments, and a LidLock helmet carry system. The helmet carry also accepts bike helmets, and there are slots at the base for attaching a rear bike light — a small touch that signals how genuinely multi-sport this pack is.

**Durability & Sustainability**

This is where the Talon earns its long-term reputation. The three-fabric system — 100D ripstop main body, 420D reinforced bottom, and 70D lining — is all bluesign-approved, 100% recycled nylon with a PFAS-free DWR finish. Osprey's commitment to sustainability here is genuine, not just marketing. In real-world terms, the materials hold up: users report minimal wear after years of regular use across desert hikes, bushwhacks, mountain summits, and commuting. Smooth zippers, reinforced strap attachment points, and dense mesh that resists snags round out a construction that punches above the price.

One gap: there's no included rain cover, an omission that stings a bit at this price given that competitors like the Osprey Stratos include one. The PFAS-free DWR sheds light rain, but in a sustained downpour you'll want a separate cover or a pack liner.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Padded, load-transferring hipbelt with zippered pockets — rare for this volume class
- Adjustable torso fit accommodates a wide range of body types
- Excellent organization with dedicated pockets for nearly every use case
- Technically capable: ice axe loops, trekking pole attachments, helmet carry, bike light slots
- Genuinely durable; holds up well over years of hard use
- Solid sustainability story: bluesign-certified, recycled fabrics, PFAS-free DWR
- Osprey's All Mighty Guarantee provides long-term repair/replacement coverage

**Cons**

- 1,080 g is heavy for 22L — ultralight alternatives exist at half the weight
- AirScape ventilation is average; sweaty back on warm, active days
- Hipbelt pockets positioned slightly too far back for easy on-the-go access
- Hydration bladder clip not compatible with all third-party reservoirs
- No rain cover included
- The rigid framesheet means the pack won't compress small when not in use

## Who Should Buy This

The Talon 22 is the right call for day hikers who regularly push toward heavier loads — think a technical day with extra water, layers, and a camera kit — and value the structured support and organization to match. It's also a strong pick for the multi-sport user who hikes weekdays and bikes commutes on weekends. If your typical day load is under 15 lbs and weight savings are a priority, a frameless option like the Hyperlite Elevate 22 or even Osprey's own Daylite Plus is worth a look. But if you'd rather carry a bit more pack and a lot more comfort on longer, more demanding days, the Talon 22 is as dialed as it gets in this category.

## Verdict

The Osprey Talon 22 is an honest, well-built daypack that does exactly what it promises: comfortable load carry, thoughtful organization, and trail-ready versatility, backed by durable, sustainably sourced materials. The weight is the real trade-off — at 1,080 g, you're paying a pound of bodyweight for features and frame that not every day hiker will need. For those who will, it's one of the best options at the price. **Rating: 7.5/10**
