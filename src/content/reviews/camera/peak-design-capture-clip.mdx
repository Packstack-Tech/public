## Overview

The Peak Design Capture is a CNC-machined aluminum clip that bolts to a pack strap or belt and holds a camera by a small Arca-compatible plate screwed into its tripod socket. A press of the side button releases the camera; a twist of the same button locks it in place. It suits backpackers and photographers who want a mirrorless body on the shoulder strap instead of swinging from a neck or buried in a lid pocket. The trade-off: it adds about 84 g with its plate, and it fits some pack straps far better than others.

## Performance

### Build and finish

Owners and reviewers keep coming back to the same point: this is metal all the way through, with no plastic in the load path, smooth action and no sharp edges. The current generation is notably more compact than the one before it, at 2.1 cm narrower, 1.2 cm shorter and 0.8 cm thinner. The anodized finish also feels smoother than the powder-coated look of the earlier clip. The result is a clip small enough to leave on a shoulder strap all season without it snagging on layers or looking like an afterthought.

### Security and deployment

The system is rated well past 200 lb, and the plate seats in the clip so it can't be twisted or levered against the housing. Pressing the side button retracts a steel pin, and rotating that button locks the pin so the camera can't release by accident. With practice, the camera comes out one-handed. Putting it back is the easy half.

The catch is that release means pressing the button and pulling at once, which in practice often takes two hands. The clip can't be set to stay unlocked. A few owners have reported cameras releasing unexpectedly, but reviewers who have used the clip for years with full-frame bodies and zoom lenses describe no such trouble. Most backpackers add a safety strap to the camera body anyway, and on rough terrain, using the lock is the sensible habit.

### Strap compatibility

This is where the Capture earns or loses its place. The packaging lists straps up to 3 in wide, but the clamping holes sit 2.5 in (6.4 cm) apart, and real-world results fall between the two numbers. One reviewer found that a strap just under 3 in wide was too stiff to clamp, while a similar-width padded strap worked without trouble. Flexible, padded shoulder straps, the kind found on Osprey-style packs, are the easy case. Wide, rigid straps on stripped-down or alpine-oriented packs are a gamble. Measure width and thickness before buying; the hex-drive bolts stretch the thickness limit from 1.5 cm to 2.2 cm.

Weight matters too. A bare clip on one shoulder is noticeable, and a loaded one more so. Clipping the sternum strap keeps the strap from drifting off-center.

### Tripod and ecosystem

The included plate is Arca-Swiss compatible, so it drops straight into any Arca-type tripod head, which is a real convenience for anyone already shooting that way. Otherwise the plate stays on the camera and needs a hex key to remove, so the key belongs in the repair kit. The current clip accepts earlier Peak Design plates, with the exception of the DUALplate v1, and it works with the company's Lens Kit and Bino Kit accessories.

### Camera size

Mirrorless bodies with primes or moderate zooms are the sweet spot, and owners regularly report success with Sony a6000-class bodies, Fuji X-series cameras and full-frame mirrorless with standard lenses. Heavy DSLRs with long telephotos work mechanically, but the camera swings and pulls downward, and the body can jab the ribs while walking. Peak Design's Pro Pad accessory exists for that reason. Two fit quirks are worth knowing: on a few cameras, such as the Fujifilm X-T1, the plate blocks the battery door, and L-brackets generally don't suit the clip's geometry.

The plate slides in from any of four sides, so orientation can be set to taste.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Slim enough to leave on a strap permanently
- All-metal, weatherproof construction
- Camera is reachable without dropping a pack or fumbling a lid pocket
- Arca-Swiss plate doubles as a tripod mount
- Plate enters from four sides for flexible orientation
- Thumb-drive and hex-drive bolts included for quick or semi-permanent installs

**Cons**

- Won't clamp onto some rigid, wide pack straps
- Release usually takes two hands, with no always-unlocked mode
- Plate removal requires a hex key
- Plate blocks the battery door on a handful of cameras
- L-brackets are largely incompatible
- Premium price for two pieces of machined metal

## Who Should Buy This

The Capture suits day hikers and multi-day backpackers carrying a mirrorless body or compact DSLR who are tired of neck-strap swing and chest-harness heat under a loaded pack. It is especially good for those who already use an Arca-type tripod head and have a pack with flexible, padded straps. Anyone with a stiff, wide-strapped pack should check measurements first, or consider a different carry method. Photographers hauling a big DSLR with a long telephoto should look at a chest-mounted harness or budget for the Pro Pad.

## Verdict

The Capture does what it promises: it holds a camera firmly, releases it quickly and adds only 84 g with its plate. Strap fit and the two-handed release keep it from being a universal answer, but for mirrorless shooters with compatible straps it is the best way to keep a camera on the trail. Rating: 8/10.
