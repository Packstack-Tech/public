## Overview

Sea to Summit's Trek & Travel Pocket Body Wash is soap reduced to its barest logistics: thin, dry leaves in a snap-shut plastic case about the size of a pack of breath strips. Each leaf dissolves in water into a light green tea-scented lather, which makes it a natural fit for ultralight backpackers who want something better than nothing at a creek or camp. The trade-off is plain. It saves weight and eliminates leaks, but it asks for more leaves, and more attention, than the label suggests.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** This is the whole argument for the product. A 4-leaf supply weighs 0.6 g, which is less than most hikers' bag ties, and even the full 50-leaf case sits around half an ounce. There is no bottle to burst at altitude and no bar to turn to slime in a pocket. Reviewers who have carried it on long trips consistently single out the absence of leakage, and one early reviewer found the case held up to being jostled in a pack with the leaves staying dry and intact.

**Cleaning and lather.** Results depend on expectations. Reviewers describe the leaves dissolving quickly, even in cold water, and producing a workable lather with a subtle scent. For hands, face and a camp bird bath, owners are generally satisfied; one REI reviewer reports a single leaf is enough for hands and face on the trail. Full-body washing is where complaints surface. Sea to Summit recommends one or two leaves per use, but a tester at Active Gear Review used up to three for a body wash and wanted more suds than the label suggested. REI buyers echo that: using it for a proper shower means going through more leaves than the packaging implies. Tester feedback on the sibling laundry leaves points the same direction, with weak suds and a need for extra leaves.

**Dry hands required.** The leaves are thin enough to stick together at the first hint of moisture. Owners repeatedly warn to pull leaves out with fully dry hands, because damp fingers can glue the stack into a single useless clump. This is easy to forget in the middle of a wash, when a thumb is already wet and the lather is thin. Experienced users lay out a few extra leaves before getting wet and return unused ones to the case afterward. Wet leaves are also fragile and tear easily.

**Skin feel and scent.** Users describe it as cleansing without being especially drying. The green tea scent is mild, though scent-averse hikers will note that any fragrance draws attention in bear country. The formula is biodegradable and phosphate-free, but Sea to Summit's own guidance ties the environmental claims to Leave No Trace practices. That means washing well away from water sources and not treating the formula as license to rinse in a lake.

**Value and rationing.** Pricing runs from roughly $4 to $7 for a 50-leaf case, depending on retailer. That sounds like plenty of washes, but at two or three leaves per body wash, a full case becomes a couple of weeks of daily use at most. Rationing works best for hand and face cleanup, with full washes saved for town stops. REI customers rate the product 4.1 out of 5 across 53 reviews, a respectable score with a split between hikers who treat it as a minimalist bird-bath tool and shower-seekers who find it underpowered.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Almost no weight: 0.6 g for four leaves, about half an ounce for the full case
- No leaks, no spills, no confiscation at airport security
- Dissolves quickly, even in cold water
- Easy to ration by the leaf; a single leaf handles hands and face
- Biodegradable, phosphate-free and paraben-free formula
- Mild green tea scent
- Inexpensive

**Cons**

- Full-body washes often take two to three leaves, so 50 goes faster than expected
- Leaves clump if touched with damp fingers, and the whole case can be ruined by a wet hand
- Lather is modest compared with liquid or bar soap
- Wet leaves tear easily
- Scented, which matters in some backcountry settings

## Who Should Buy This

Weight-focused section hikers, thru-hikers and bikepackers who want a hygiene option for hands, face and occasional wash-ups will get the most from it, as will carry-on travelers sick of liquid limits. It also suits anyone building a kit for a multi-day trip that includes a solar shower or a river bath. Hikers who expect a lathery, shower-quality wash, who tend to handle gear with wet hands, or who want unscented toiletries should look at a small bottle of concentrated biodegradable soap instead.

## Verdict

Sea to Summit's Pocket Body Wash is a smart, nearly weightless way to carry soap, as long as it is used as a bird-bath and hand-wash tool rather than a shower replacement. The dry-hands rule and the leaf appetite for full-body use keep it from being a no-brainer. For ultralight kits, it earns a spot; for anyone wanting a real lather, it falls short.
