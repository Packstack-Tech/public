## Overview

The Talon 0C is Mountain Equipment Company's only backpacking quilt, a hybrid that pairs a quilt's open back with a sewn-shut footbox and straps that cinch it to a sleeping pad. At 635 g and filled with 800-fill-power goose down, it targets backpackers making the jump from a mummy bag who want to shed weight without learning a new system from scratch. The trade-off is familiar for the category: a quilt only keeps you warm if the pad underneath is doing its job.

## Performance

**Warmth and loft.** Owners describe the Talon as lofty for its price and note that it compresses small in a pack. The box baffle construction keeps the 800-fill down in place instead of letting it shift to the edges, which matters on a quilt, where there is no zipper and hood to hold everything in. Buyers who have taken it into colder weather are fewer than those who bought it for summer, and the reports skew toward mild conditions.

**The pad is part of the warmth system.** A quilt has no insulation under the sleeper, so the 0°C rating depends on a pad with a decent R-value. One owner who logged roughly a dozen nights over a year put it bluntly: a cheap air mattress will not keep a sleeper warm near the bottom of the quilt's range. Backpackers who run cold should treat 0°C as a limit rather than a comfort rating, and a common view in the quilt world holds that the comfortable range of a quilt sits five to ten degrees warmer than a similarly lofted sleeping bag. One reviewer who sleeps cold called the Talon a summer-only piece for themselves.

**Pad attachment.** This is the feature owners mention most. The integrated pockets and straps keep the quilt on the sleeper and the sleeper on the pad, which cuts down on the drafts that plague a loose quilt on restless nights. One buyer found that the lower of the two straps alone kept the quilt in place. A side sleeper who likes to splay out reported that the quilt avoids the restriction of a mummy bag while the straps still hold it to the mattress.

**Fit.** The enclosed footbox gives the quilt a more bag-like feel than a flat-footed, open-foot design, and it keeps feet warm without fiddling with a drawcord. A 6'4" owner found the length just sufficient, so taller sleepers are working with no margin. Backpacking Light's market survey notes that MEC offers limited sizing on this model.

**Fabric and weather.** The shell is 10D fabric, a weight that saves grams and demands care around sticks, rocks and the occasional Velcro tab. The PFC-free DWR finish helps the quilt shed morning dew and stay cleaner, though it is a finish rather than a waterproof layer. Down still wants a dry stuff sack and a tent or tarp that manages condensation.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 635 g for a 0°C-rated goose down quilt
- Pad pockets and straps keep the quilt in place and cut drafts
- Enclosed footbox for bag-like warmth at the feet
- Box baffles help hold the 800-fill down where it belongs
- RDS-certified down and a PFC-free DWR finish
- Owners describe good value for an entry quilt, and repeat buyers have bought it for family members

**Cons**

- Limited sizing; a 6'4" owner reports only just enough length
- The rating depends on a well-insulated pad
- Cold sleepers may find the 0°C rating optimistic for sustained use near the limit
- 10D shell calls for careful handling
- Heavier 800-fill goose down gives up some warmth-to-weight against the 850–950-fill down quilts from cottage makers

## Who Should Buy This

The Talon suits three-season backpackers, especially Canadians shopping at MEC, who are leaving a mummy bag behind and want an easy first quilt with a footbox and pad straps that remove most of the learning curve. It also makes sense as a spring and fall layer for warm sleepers and as a summer bag for people who find enclosed bags confining. Winter campers, cold sleepers who want a real margin below freezing, and very tall hikers should look at a longer, higher-fill or custom-sized quilt from a cottage maker. Anyone sleeping on a thin, low-R-value pad will lose the warmth the rating promises.

## Verdict

The Talon 0C is a sensible, well-featured entry quilt: light at 635 g, simple to use and secured to the pad in a way that shuts out most of the usual quilt drafts. It lacks the fill power and size options of dedicated cottage-brand quilts, but for a first quilt used on 0°C-and-up nights with a proper pad, it does the job.
