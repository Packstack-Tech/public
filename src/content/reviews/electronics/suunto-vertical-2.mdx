## Overview

The Suunto Vertical 2 is the Finnish company's flagship adventure watch, and the first Vertical to trade a solar-assisted memory-in-pixel screen for a bright AMOLED panel. It is built for people who spend long days, and several of them in a row, navigating off-trail terrain without a phone. The headline trade-off is plain: the best battery life of any AMOLED watch its reviewers have tested, in a case that is large and heavy on the wrist.

## Performance

**Battery.** This is the reason the watch exists. Suunto rates it at 65 hours of dual-band GPS tracking, and Outdoor Gear Lab's testers measured 66 hours of continuous GPS use, outlasting every other bright-AMOLED watch in their lineup. DC Rainmaker's test was harsher: a 70 km mountain hike and trail run with navigation and the always-on display enabled. The watch burned about 2 percent per hour, which projects to roughly 50 hours, and it still outlasted the Garmin Fenix 8 Pro and other watches he carried. Suunto's Tour mode, which drops heart rate tracking and reduces GPS sampling, stretches the figure to as much as 250 hours. As a daily watch, the claim is 20 days between charges. One mountain-bike reviewer saw lower real-world numbers and blamed frequent wrist-raise screen wake-ups, so owners who want the full figure should tune that setting.

**Display.** The AMOLED panel is a large step up from the original Vertical's MIP screen in dim light and in direct sun. Reviewers describe it as readable in harsh daylight and at pre-dawn alpine starts. Strong glare can still wash out any AMOLED, particularly when a hat brim or glasses cast shade over the face.

**Navigation.** Offline color maps are free, with no subscription, and the dual-band GNSS earned strong marks for accuracy: Outdoor Gear Lab scored accuracy 9.0 out of 10. Route planning is easiest in the Suunto app, which testers found more intuitive than the watch-side controls on a comparable Garmin. The limits are real, though. Maps are not preloaded and must be downloaded by region, a process reviewers call dated. Route storage is capped at roughly 10 to 12 slots despite the 32 GB of memory, and the watch does not re-route on its own if a hiker strays from the line. Garmin's Fenix line does offer that feature.

**Flashlight.** A dedicated LED, with a red mode, is the Vertical 2's party trick. Testers praised it for dark trailheads and camp chores, though Wareable noted that the way it is switched on and off needs refinement.

**Fit and controls.** At 87 g, with a 48.6 mm case about 13.5 mm thick, the Vertical 2 is not subtle. Outdoor Gear Lab weighed the stainless steel version at 3.0 ounces and called it one of the heaviest watches in its test group, noting that the weight shows up after a long day. Wareable found the case less bulky on the wrist than the numbers suggest. The three-button-plus-touch layout works with gloves, but without a crown or scroll wheel, long menus are slower to move through.

**Smart features.** Basic by Garmin standards. Notifications are read-only, and there is no music storage, contactless payment, microphone or phone-call handling. Heart rate tracking uses the same optical sensor as the Suunto Race 2 and tracked closely with a Fenix 8 and a Coros Pace 3 in Wareable's tests, including during pool swims. Training analytics are thinner than Garmin's.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Longest battery life among bright AMOLED adventure watches
- Free offline maps with no subscription
- Built-in LED flashlight with red mode
- Sapphire glass and a stainless steel or titanium case
- Accurate dual-band GPS and heart rate
- Costs less than a Garmin Fenix 8: $599 for steel and $699 for titanium, against $999 and up

**Cons**

- 87 g is hefty for a watch carried on a multi-month hike
- No on-watch re-routing; limited route slots
- Maps must be downloaded by region; none are preloaded
- No music, payments or voice features
- Menu navigation is slower than on watches with a crown or wheel
- Titanium version carries a significant price premium

## Who Should Buy This

The Vertical 2 suits section hikers, ultrarunners, mountaineers and packrafters who want a single device for navigation, a night light and multi-day tracking, and who can go several days between charges. It also fits Garmin skeptics who want free maps and a longer-lasting AMOLED screen at a lower price. Thru-hikers who count every gram, or who want a watch that handles texts, music and payments, should look at a lighter, simpler watch. Anyone who depends on automatic off-route recalculation should stay with Garmin.

## Verdict

The Vertical 2 is the strongest backcountry watch for the money right now, with battery life, maps and a flashlight that justify its weight for most trips beyond a weekend. The extra heft and the dated map workflow keep it from a higher score. Hikers who accept both will get a watch that rarely needs plugging in.
