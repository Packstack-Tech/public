## Overview

The Evernew Ti U.L. Solo Set 750 (ECA540) is a titanium cookware combo built for solo ultralight backpackers who boil water more than they cook. It pairs a 750ml pot with the Ti 400FD cup, nested together into one 144g package that swallows your stove and fuel canister for storage. If your cooking system is "boil water, add food," this is about as refined as that use case gets.

## Performance

The headline number here is volume-to-weight ratio. <cite index="9-11">Evernew's pot has probably the best volume-to-weight ratio compared to other 750ml pots, beating out the Toaks 750 (7.38 ml per gram) with a figure closer to 8 ml per gram</cite>. In practical terms, that's the difference between carrying a pot that feels like an afterthought versus one that still nags at your base weight spreadsheet.

The 0.3mm titanium is the thinnest Evernew makes, and it shows both in the scale reading and in how the metal behaves. Owners consistently note that <cite index="10-24">these pots are a little less durable due to how thin they are</cite> compared to thicker titanium or steel options — this isn't a pot you want to stuff carelessly into a pack or use to stir with a metal spoon. That said, nobody in the user reports I found described actual failures; one long-term owner called it <cite index="9-2">a great set overall and was sure it would last a lifetime</cite>.

Packing efficiency is where this set earns its keep. The pot swallows a stove and canister with room to spare — one reviewer reported fitting <cite index="9-1">a 4oz fuel can and a Soto Windmaster inside with the lid closed</cite>, and another managed to nest <cite index="10-9">a small isobutane canister, a mini Bic lighter, a small piece of sponge, a BRS stove, and an MSR folding spoon</cite> all inside the cup alone. If you're the type who wants your entire cook kit to disappear into one Russian-doll unit, this delivers.

The insulated handles get called out repeatedly as a highlight. Multiple users specifically praised them — one simply said <cite index="10-5">they appreciate the insulated handles</cite>, and another described them as <cite index="10-16">outstanding</cite>. That's a meaningful detail on a titanium pot, since bare titanium handles get hot fast.

Heat behavior is the flip side of that thin-wall efficiency. Titanium is a poor heat conductor and this pot heats up quickly and unevenly — one user learned this the hard way, reporting they <cite index="10-12">scorched the bottom while cooking a premade meal, noting that titanium heats fast and extremely hot</cite>. This tracks with what most ultralight cooks already know about titanium: it's excellent for boiling water, mediocre for anything that needs simmering or actual cooking without a heat diffuser. If your meal plan is dehydrated dinners and instant coffee, you'll never notice. If you want to actually cook rice or pasta in it, expect some babysitting and probably a scorched spot or two.

The included stuff sack is a mixed bag — literally. One owner noted the <cite index="10-22">storage bag is great in concept but already ripped</cite> after limited use, and was already shopping for a third-party replacement. It's a low-cost item to swap out, but worth knowing going in that it's not built to the same standard as the metal.

On the broader durability question, CleverHiker's testing of Evernew's related Pasta Pot line found that <cite index="5-1,5-2">Evernew cooksets are among the more durable titanium options tested, even though titanium this thin tends to warp over time</cite>. That's a reasonable proxy for what to expect from the Solo Set 750, since it uses the same 0.3mm stock and construction philosophy.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent volume-to-weight ratio — among the best in the 750ml titanium pot category
- Insulated silicone handles are genuinely well-executed and frequently praised by owners
- Nests a full stove-and-canister setup inside, making it a true all-in-one storage solution
- Two vessels (pot + cup) give you flexibility for simultaneous water boiling and eating/drinking
- Hand-crafted build quality that owners trust for long-term use

**Cons**

- 0.3mm titanium is noticeably thinner and less forgiving than 0.4mm alternatives — more prone to denting or warping with rough handling
- Poor for actual cooking beyond boiling; hot spots and scorching are a real risk without a diffuser
- Included stuff sack has been reported to tear with light use
- Premium Japanese titanium pricing — you're paying for the weight savings and craftsmanship
- Tight nesting fit means shoving gear in requires some patience and a protective fabric layer to avoid scratching the pot walls

## Who Should Buy This

This set is built for the solo thru-hiker or ultralight backpacker whose cooking routine is boil-and-pour: rehydrated meals, coffee, tea, and not much else. If you want a single compact unit that houses your entire cook system — pot, cup, stove, and canister — and you're willing to pay a premium for shaving off every possible gram, this is one of the more well-regarded options in the category. It's less suited to people who actually want to simmer, sauté, or cook real food on trail, where the thin titanium walls and fast, uneven heating will work against you.

## Verdict

The Evernew Ti U.L. Solo Set 750 does exactly what it promises: it's an extremely light, well-built boil-water system that nests your whole cook kit into one 144g package. It's not the pot for anyone who wants to cook rather than rehydrate, and the thin titanium demands a bit more care than heavier alternatives, but for dedicated ultralight solo hikers, it's hard to beat on the gram-per-milliliter math. I'd rate it an 8.5/10 — outstanding at its core job, with minor gripes around the stuff sack and cooking versatility keeping it from a perfect score.
