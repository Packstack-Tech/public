## Overview

Decathlon's almond paste bar is a 25 g slab of marzipan-style energy food: 37.8% almonds, 14.5% honey, a little sugar and pectin, and added vitamins C, B3 and B6. It is sold in a 12-bar, 300 g box for €7.99 in France. The trade-off is simple: it is fast, soft and easy to eat on the move, but at 113 kcal per bar it is a quick-carb top-up, not a dense trail-food staple.

## Performance

**Energy.** The numbers are modest. At 453 kcal per 100 g, the bar carries less energy per gram than the nuts, nut butters and fatty bars that dominate most thru-hiker food bags. The calories here come mostly from sugar, with 13 of the 14 g of carbohydrate being sugars. That makes it a different tool: something to eat mid-climb when a stomach is not interested in a tortilla and peanut butter, rather than something to build a day's calorie budget on.

**Carb math.** Decathlon's own copy cites sports-nutrition guidance of 60 to 90 g of carbohydrate per hour for high-intensity efforts. At 14 g a bar, that would mean four to six bars an hour, which is neither practical nor cheap. In realistic use, a bar or two an hour tops up a longer day, and the bar sits best alongside drink mix or gels in a race or fast-packing setting.

**Texture and eating.** The paste is soft and chewy, which is the whole point. It does not shatter in cold weather the way a dense granola bar can, and it needs little chewing when breathing hard. The individual wrappers open and reseal with one hand, so half a bar can ride in a hip-belt pocket until the next grade. The 25 g format is small enough to stash in a pocket without fuss.

**Ingredients.** The recipe is short by energy-bar standards: sugar, almonds, honey, pectin, sea salt and added vitamins. Decathlon's product copy describes the almond paste as organic. The vitamin additions (C, B3, B6) carry the brand's fatigue-reduction claims, but they are a label feature rather than something a hiker will feel on trail.

**Owner feedback.** The product holds a 4.7 rating from about 600 reviews on Decathlon's French site, and buyers generally treat it as a reliable, inexpensive endurance snack. Its sweetness is the common caveat with almond-paste products: several hours of marzipan-flavored sugar is a lot for some palates, which is why Decathlon itself suggests alternating almond, date and fruit-paste bars on long outings.

**Value.** At about €0.67 a bar, it undercuts most branded energy bars and gels, and it is stocked in any Decathlon store. For hikers in France and nearby countries, that convenience counts.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Short ingredient list built around almonds (37.8%) and honey (14.5%)
- Soft paste that eats easily at a hard effort and in the cold
- 25 g bars with one-hand open and reseal wrappers
- About €0.67 per bar, and widely available through Decathlon
- Adds 15% of daily vitamin C, B1, B3 and B6 per bar

**Cons**

- 453 kcal per 100 g is below the density of nuts and fatty trail bars
- 13 g of sugar in a 14 g carb hit; sweet over a long day
- Only 2.1 g protein per bar, so it won't carry a meal
- Reaching Decathlon's cited carb targets would take four to six bars an hour
- Individual wrappers add packaging waste to the pack

## Who Should Buy This

Day hikers, trail runners, cyclists and fast-and-light backpackers who want a cheap, soft, pocketable carb source for moving hours. It also suits thru-hikers who want a sweet in-motion snack to rotate with salty food. Anyone who counts calories per ounce for a multi-day resupply should look at nut butter, nuts or oil-rich bars as the base of the food bag, and use these bars as a supplement.

## Verdict

A well-priced, simply made almond paste that does one job, fast carbs that are easy to eat while moving, and does it for less than most bar brands. It is no calorie-density champion, so treat it as a trail-side top-up rather than a staple.
