## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 zip-neck is Decathlon's midweight merino long-sleeve for trekkers who want real wool without a boutique-brand price tag. A 190 g/m² fabric and a quarter-length zip make it a three-season workhorse for cool-weather walking and multi-day trips. The trade-off is weight: at 243 g in size L, it is a sturdy shirt rather than a gossamer one.

## Performance

**Comfort.** The 18.5-micron wool sits at the fine end of what budget merino usually offers, and reviewers consistently describe the MT500 line as soft and non-itchy. French outlet Test 4 Outside noted that the sleeves run long enough to reach the hands and that the seams caused no irritation, a detail that matters under a pack's shoulder straps. Buyers who have owned Icebreaker tops say the Forclaz is a fair match for softness, though it lacks the refined hand of the premium brands.

**Warmth and breathability.** At 190 g/m², this is a true midweight. Test 4 Outside found it kept testers warm in the cold and stayed reasonably breathable when temperatures rose. Decathlon's own chamber testing found that wool holds body temperature after heavy sweating, while synthetic fabrics cool faster. On a wet climb followed by a windy ridge, that means less of the clammy chill that polyester produces. Decathlon rates the shirt for cool weather, and owners treat it as a shoulder-season and winter layer.

**The zip.** The collar zip is the main reason to pick this version over the round-neck MT500. Open it on a grind uphill and the shirt dumps heat; close it at the pass and the neck is covered. Test 4 Outside found the round neckline snug, a plus in the cold that turns into a minor annoyance in warm weather. The zip answers that complaint. It also adds about 20 g over the round-neck version of the shirt.

**Odor.** Decathlon backs the shirt with an ISO 17299-3 laboratory odor test, and owners report little smell after days of wear. Test 4 Outside's testers noted the limit: the shirt resists odor for a long time but eventually saturates, which they attribute to the polyamide content. Pure merino holds out longer. For a four- or five-day section between washes, the MT500 is fine. For two weeks in the same shirt, it is not the answer.

**Durability.** The merino fibers are wrapped in polyamide yarn, which protects the wool from abrasion at the shoulders and hips. One long-term owner of the MT500 line reported two shirts nearly a year old, worn and washed often, with no visible wear. Expect some pilling over time, and keep it away from thick brush and abrasive pack straps if longevity matters. Decathlon's 30°C wash limit is worth respecting.

**Fit.** The cut is snug and follows movement, and the shirt covers the lower back and stays put under a pack. Decathlon's trekker-tested pedigree shows here: eight testers wore the design for four days in the Southern French Alps before it launched.

**Owner ratings.** Decathlon's UK listing carries a 4.7 average, and the Greek listing shows 4.6 from nearly 500 reviews. A small share of buyers left one- and two-star ratings, a minority view against a large majority of four- and five-star scores.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 18.5-micron merino that owners and testers say does not itch
- Zip collar adds real ventilation control on climbs
- Polyamide-wrapped yarn improves abrasion resistance over plain merino
- Long sleeves and flat seams work well under a pack
- Prices well below the major merino brands, according to owners who have compared them
- Holds warmth when damp and dries quickly for a wool blend

**Cons**

- 243 g in size L is heavy next to ultralight merino and synthetic tops
- The 14% polyamide blend trades some long-term odor resistance for durability
- Less refined feel than premium merino
- Pilling is likely with heavy pack-strap contact
- 30°C maximum wash temperature

## Who Should Buy This

This shirt suits backpackers and trekkers who want a reliable cool-weather base layer and care more about durability and price than gram counting. It works for shoulder-season thru-hikes, winter day hiking, and anyone who runs hot on climbs and wants a zip to manage it. Hikers shaving every ounce should look at lighter merino tees in the 150 g/m² range or synthetic options. Anyone planning very long stretches without washing should consider pure merino.

## Verdict

The MT500 zip-neck delivers soft, durable merino with useful venting at a price that undercuts most of the field. It is heavier than the ultralight ideal, but for cool-weather trekking, the trade is a fair one.
