## Overview

The Olicamp Space Saver Mug is a squat titanium pot-mug hybrid built around one idea: the cookware should take up no pack space of its own. It wraps around a small fuel canister, and a locking lid keeps the stove kit from rattling loose. It suits solo boil-in-bag and freeze-dried hikers who want one vessel for dinner and coffee. The trade-off is a wide, short body that is better at storing things than at drinking from.

## Performance

**Nesting is the point.** Olicamp designed the Space Saver line so the mug wraps around a Nalgene-style bottle or a canister instead of sitting in the pack as dead volume. The 550 mL mug is sized to fit snugly around a small 3.9 oz isobutane canister, the format most weekend and thru-hiking stove kits use. Reviewers who tested the earlier anodized aluminum Space Saver reported that a micro stove, canister and folding spork all fit inside it. That makes a 4.5–5 oz cook kit when it is paired with a micro stove, according to one tester. A bikepacker using the 750 mL titanium sibling got a stove, a 100 g canister and a lighter into it. The titanium mug follows the same layout.

**Boiling and heat.** At 550 mL, the mug holds roughly two cups of water to the fill line, which covers one freeze-dried meal or a pair of hot drinks. Owners of the aluminum version warned that the handles and rim get hot when water is boiling hard. Titanium conducts heat less quickly, but the handles still sit close to the pot wall and a rolling boil warms them. A hiker comparing titanium and aluminum mugs on a bushwalking forum noted that titanium tends to bite the lip on the first sip and then settle down. The broad 10.1 cm footprint is stable on a micro stove's pot supports, and Olicamp pairs the mug with its Ion Micro stove for that reason. Tall, narrow pots boil faster for their size. This one trades speed for stability and packability.

**Measurement and lid.** The graduated marks suit hikers who dose water for freeze-dried meals. Buyers of the stainless version of the mug singled them out as the feature that separated it from rival nesting cups. The marks are stamped into the wall, so they read from the outside and are not easy to check mid-pour. The locking triangular tab and vent holes make the lid usable as a simmer cover, and it stays put in the pack. The mesh drawstring sack keeps soot off everything else.

**Drinking.** The mug is wide for sipping. A forum owner of the aluminum version called it uncomfortably large as a drinking cup, though exceptionally well made. The folding handles are comfortable for a pot this light.

**Weight.** At 103 g with its lid, it isn't a record setter. Narrow 375 mL titanium cups weigh much less. What the Space Saver offers is a lid and a nesting shape in the same weight class, and for many kits that is worth the extra grams.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ by 3 g. The Standard mug weighs 103 g and holds 550 mL, enough for one hiker's dinner and a hot drink. The 600 mL version weighs 106 g and adds a little headroom. It suits hikers who regularly fill the 550 mL to the top and don't want boil-overs. The size gain is small, so the choice comes down to whether the pot will hold your usual meal. Hikers who plan to nest a 32 oz Nalgene need Olicamp's larger 750 mL mug, which is a separate listing from these two.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Nests around a 3.9 oz canister, so it takes almost no pack volume
- Locking lid with steam vents ships in the box, along with a mesh sack
- Graduated marks make measuring freeze-dried meal water easy
- Folding integrated handles, no extra hardware to lose
- Titanium body at 103–106 g, with no coating

**Cons**

- Wide, short shape boils slower than a tall pot and is awkward to drink from
- Handles and rim get hot at a hard boil
- Heavier than bare titanium cups with no lid
- Priced around $40–45, versus roughly $10–15 for the older aluminum version
- Stamped graduations are hard to read from above

## Who Should Buy This

Solo hikers and bikepackers who cook with a canister stove, and who want a pot that stores their fuel and stove and has a lid. It suits people who boil water and add it to a bag, or heat a single pouch. Those who want to drink from one cup all day, or cook for two, should choose a taller pot. Gram-counters who skip the lid can save weight with a bare titanium cup.

## Verdict

The Olicamp Space Saver Mug is a well-thought-out solo cook pot whose strength is how little room it takes in the pack. It costs more than the aluminum original, and the wide shape does not suit sipping, but at 103 g with a lid it is a sound choice for canister-stove hikers.
