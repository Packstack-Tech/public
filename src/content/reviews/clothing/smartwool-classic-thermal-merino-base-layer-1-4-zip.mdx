## Overview

The Classic Thermal Merino 1/4 Zip is Smartwool's heavyweight cold-weather top: 250 g/m² of 100% merino in an interlock knit, with a slim cut and a deep quarter-zip collar. It is built for winter hill days, camp evenings and sleeping bags that are a little too optimistic about their ratings. The trade-off is simple. It is very warm and very comfortable, and it is not a fast-moving, three-season piece for anyone who runs hot.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The fabric weight is the headline. Better Trail points out that most base layers use 150 to 200 g/m² merino, which puts this top well into winter territory. Its testers wore it under an insulated ski jacket in the low 20s °F and stayed comfortable. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers ranked it among the warmest tops they had put through their base layer comparison. The raised collar adds warmth at the base of the neck when zipped, and Better Trail notes it is a favorite for sleeping on backpacking trips.

**Comfort.** This is where the Classic Thermal earns its reputation. Outdoor Gear Lab found the merino soft and lofty, with none of the scratchiness that cheaper wool can have. The Great Outdoors' tester described the fabric as very comfortable with good stretch. Flatlock seams keep the inside smooth. The same tester did flag one detail for pack-carriers: the shoulder seams are rolled forward and sit on the collarbone, right where a pack strap lands. Worn as part of a layering system, it was not a problem. Outdoor Gear Lab, by contrast, praised the shoulder panels under a heavy pack, so experience varies with pack and layering.

**Ventilation and moisture.** The 25 cm zip (size M) gives real venting range, and owners report it opens wide enough to dump heat on a climb. The Great Outdoors' tester found the zip snag-free, with an internal baffle, a pull chunky enough to grab with thick gloves, and no beard guard. Merino's moisture handling is good, but a 250 g/m² knit is slow to dry. That same tester reported overheating on steep sections, wetting out the fabric, and then staying warm only by keeping moving afterward. Better Trail reached a similar conclusion and recommends it for low-output activity in the cold rather than hard aerobic efforts.

**Odor.** Merino's best party trick holds up. Owners consistently say it stays clean-smelling over multi-day use, which matters when a top is worn day and night.

**Fit.** The cut is slim, with a long body and long sleeves. The Great Outdoors' tester found the hem and cuffs rolled back so the cuffs stretch enough to push over the elbows. Outdoor Gear Lab called the fit semi-fitted and true to size. Retail reviewers are less unanimous: some say it runs small and suggest sizing up, while a tall REI buyer found the sleeves long and the fit slightly generous. Smartwool has also updated the men's fit, and a few repeat buyers on the women's version say the newer model feels a bit tighter and less plush. A couple of Canadian reviewers made the same complaint about thinner fabric on a replacement top.

**Durability.** Outdoor Gear Lab marked it as more delicate than some competitors and noted that merino can lose shape and sag without proper care. Repeat owners are more upbeat. One REI reviewer owns six or seven and says they wash up well. Wash gently and skip the dryer, as with any merino.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Among the warmest base layers tested by Outdoor Gear Lab and Better Trail
- Soft merino with no reported itch, and flatlock seams
- 25 cm quarter-zip vents well and works with gloves
- Strong odor resistance for multi-day use
- Doubles as a sleep shirt and camp layer

**Cons**

- Too warm for sustained high-output hiking outside winter
- Slow to dry compared with lighter knits
- Heavier than the thin merino tops most ultralight hikers carry (298 g)
- More delicate than some synthetic and blended alternatives
- Premium price
- Some owners report sizing and quality variation between production runs

## Who Should Buy This

Cold-season backpackers, winter day hikers, snowshoers and cross-country skiers who run cool or move at a steady pace will get the most from it. It also suits anyone who wants a single top for sleeping and lounging at camp. Fast-and-light three-season hikers, ski tourers and anyone who sweats heavily on climbs should look at a lighter or more open knit instead. The Classic Thermal will leave them overheated and damp.

## Verdict

The Classic Thermal Merino 1/4 Zip is a very good cold-weather top that knows its job: keeping a moderately active or stationary person warm and comfortable for days without smelling. It is a poor fit for strenuous summer or shoulder-season hiking and carries a weight penalty for ultralight setups. For winter use and for sleeping, it is easy to recommend.
