## Overview

The Snow Peak Titanium Single Wall Mug is a Japanese-made titanium cup with folding handles. It can go straight onto a stove or fire, so it works as a drinking vessel and a one-person pot. The 450 ml version weighs 70 g. The trade-off is built into the name: one thin wall means a mug that boils water well and holds heat poorly, and a rim that is warm to the lips when the contents are hot.

## Performance

**Durability.** Titanium at 0.4 mm looks fragile, and the mug does dent. Owners treat that as part of its character. One Pacific Crest Trail hiker bought the 450 before a 2014 thru-hike and, after two more PCT hikes, described it as scorched, battered and bent, but still in service. Outdoors Magic's tester reported no dings after a year of regular use and noted that the handles fold down tight against the body, so they don't rattle against the metal in a pack.

**Cooking.** This is the reason to choose single wall over Snow Peak's double-wall mugs. Without insulation, the metal passes heat directly to the water, so the mug works as a pot for boiling water, warming soup or rehydrating a freeze-dried meal. Reviewers who use it that way tend to carry nothing else for a solo trip. Titanium's low thermal mass means it heats and cools quickly, which suits a stove-side routine more than a slow sip at camp.

**Heat retention and handling.** Single wall also means drinks cool faster than they do in a double-wall mug, and the cup body and rim carry real heat when the contents are near boiling. The folding handles keep fingers off the hot metal, but anyone who wants a coffee that stays warm for twenty minutes will find the double-wall version, with its lid, a better fit.

**Taste and cleaning.** Owners report no metallic or chalky taste, which is a common complaint with cheaper titanium cookware. The surface wipes clean, and coffee, tea and soup leave no lingering flavor.

**Packing.** The mug is a nesting piece. A canister stove fits inside the 450. Several owners report the 450 then slides into a Snow Peak Trek 700 pot, and that the whole stack fits inside a Trek 1400. The 220 ml mug nests inside the 450, and a 100 g gas canister fits inside the 600 ml version without rattling.

**Fit and finish.** Testers consistently call out the craftsmanship, and the retro-modern shape has a following well beyond the backpacking world. It reads as a design object, and it still does the job on the trail.

## Choosing a Version

Snow Peak makes the single-wall mug in four capacities, and the weight gap between them is small enough that volume should decide.

- **220 ml (45 g):** A coffee-only mug or a nesting add-on. It fits inside the 450 and suits a small black coffee, but it is too small to cook a meal in.
- **300 ml (50 g):** A modest step up for tea and instant drinks. It is still a pure drinking cup for most users.
- **450 ml (70 g):** The one most owners pick. It holds roughly a standard home mug's worth of coffee, boils enough water for a freeze-dried pouch, and fits a small stove inside. Reviewers who eat from the mug also find this size sufficient.
- **600 ml (88 g):** The size for anyone using the mug as the only pot. It swallows a 100 g gas canister, and one owner who used it as a primary solo pot found it not too large to drink from. It ships without a lid, so owners who cook with it improvise one from foil.

Anyone building a one-pot system should buy the 600. Most others should buy the 450.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 70 g in the 450 ml size, with a 45 g option for minimalists
- Goes directly over a stove or flame, so it replaces a pot on solo trips
- Folding handles lock down for packing
- Thin titanium stands up to years of trail use
- No metallic taste and easy to clean
- Nests with a stove, canister and other Snow Peak cookware

**Cons**

- Single wall means drinks cool quickly and the rim runs hot
- The 0.4 mm wall dents and scorches with use
- No lid in the box
- Costs more than generic titanium mugs, and it is made in Japan

## Who Should Buy This

This mug suits solo backpackers and thru-hikers who want one vessel for boiling water and drinking from it, and who care about weight and build quality. It also suits the person who wants a mug that looks good at camp. Anyone who wants hot coffee to stay hot, or wants a lid on the cup, should look at the double-wall version. Groups cooking for more than one person need a proper pot.

## Verdict

The Snow Peak Titanium Single Wall Mug has been the default titanium cup on long trails for years because it does the basics well: light, tough, clean-tasting, and usable over a flame. Take the 450 ml for general use and the 600 ml if the mug is also your pot. The loss of heat retention is the only real compromise.
