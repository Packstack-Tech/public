## Overview

The Forclaz Zitpad MT500 is a folding closed-cell foam seat pad from Decathlon's in-house trekking brand, priced at €5.99 in the Netherlands. It weighs 60 g and exists for one job: keeping a wet log, cold rock or damp bench from reaching you during breaks and camp chores. The trade-off is a folded block that's chunkier than the thin foam would suggest, and insulation that's strictly a sit-down affair.

## Performance

**Insulation.** Decathlon rates the pad at R-1.3, and it notes that R-values aren't standardized across manufacturers. For context, that's well below the R-2.2 of the brand's full-length MT500 folding sleeping pad. For a sit pad, it's more than enough. A person sitting compresses little foam for a short time, and the job is to block conduction from cold stone, frosty logs and wet grass. Owners on Decathlon's Dutch site rate thermal insulation at or near five stars, and the pad's reputation rests on a simple outcome: no more wet or cold backside on a damp bench.

**Moisture.** The polyethylene foam is closed-cell, so it doesn't soak up water the way open-cell foam or a fabric cushion would. Wiping it down is the whole maintenance routine. The aluminized film is there for abrasion resistance, according to Decathlon, which suits a pad that spends its life being dropped on gravel and dragged across bark. Like all polyethylene foam, it will compress and crease with use, and owners shouldn't expect it to look new after a season.

**Size and comfort.** At 39 x 29 x 2 cm unfolded, the pad covers the seat area and not much more. It's a perch, not a groundsheet. Two centimeters of foam takes the edge off rocks and roots without pretending to be a cushion. Several owners also use it as a kneeling pad, which makes sense for stove tending, water filtering or fiddling with tent stakes.

**Packability.** This is the pad's one real quibble. Folded in four, it measures 29 x 9.5 x 4.5 cm, roughly 1.2 liters. That's a fat brick for 60 g of foam, and it won't vanish into a side pocket the way a trimmed-down 1/8-inch foam square would. Most buyers strap it outside the pack or tuck it into a back pocket. Ratings on Decathlon's site are overwhelmingly positive on portability, though a few owners marked it down on that score, a sign that the folded size bothers some people more than the weight does.

**Durability and value.** Decathlon backs the pad with a 10-year guarantee and a 365-day return window. At under six euros, that's more coverage than the product is likely to need. Buyer feedback is strong: the Dutch product page shows a 4.9 out of 5 average across thousands of ratings. A small minority of reviewers scored insulation and value at three out of five.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 60 g adds almost nothing to a base weight
- R-1.3 insulation is plenty for sitting on cold or damp surfaces
- Closed-cell foam doesn't absorb water and wipes clean
- Aluminized facing resists abrasion
- €5.99 price with a 10-year guarantee
- Works as a knee pad for camp chores

**Cons**

- Folded block (29 x 9.5 x 4.5 cm) is bulky relative to its weight
- Small unfolded footprint; it's a seat, not a ground pad
- Not a substitute for a sleeping pad, or even a torso-length pad for a pack frame
- Foam will crease and compress over time

## Who Should Buy This

This pad suits three-season hikers who take frequent breaks and don't want to sit on wet ground, as well as anyone who spends time at camp on cold rock or logs. It's also a sensible add-on for day hikers, dog walkers, bench-sitters and kids on school trips. Thru-hikers counting every cubic centimeter should look at a cut-down piece of 3 mm foam or a trimmed section of an old pad, which will pack flatter. Anyone hoping it doubles as a sleeping pad component should buy the full-length MT500 foam pad instead.

## Verdict

The Zitpad MT500 does what a seat pad should: it keeps cold and wet off you at a weight and price that make it a no-brainer carry. Its bulk when folded is the only meaningful complaint. For six euros, most hikers will find that a fair price for a dry seat.
