## Performance

The headline story with the Core Tights is the PrimaLoft® ACTIVE construction, and it's genuinely different from a standard fleece tight. <cite index="13-1,13-2,13-3">The fabric weaves PrimaLoft fibres onto a mesh, giving a super-high-volume and open structure that is exceptional at creating air pockets to insulate the wearer.</cite> The trade-off is baked right into the design: <cite index="4-21,4-22">near-total air permeability means the lightest breeze can take away the trapped heat — but this also allows you to keep the Core layer on even when you've warmed up, thermoregulating by removing or unzipping your shell instead.</cite>

In practice, that means these tights are a two-piece system, full stop. <cite index="3-3">The fabric does not provide any wind protection, so you will need a soft- or wind-shell over them if there is any cold wind.</cite> Once you accept that, the system genuinely works. <cite index="3-5,3-6">Unlike conventional insulating trousers that can make you very sweaty, the Core Tights solve that problem — and when combined with overtrousers, they're formidable in bad weather.</cite>

Wet performance is a real strength. <cite index="2-8">Core works wet or dry with strong wicking properties to ensure you won't stay wet for long</cite>, and the synthetic fill keeps insulating even when damp — a meaningful advantage over any down-based layer on a rain-soaked UK ridge.

The construction details are well thought-out for active use. <cite index="9-14">Flatlock seams are positioned away from high-chafe areas to ensure comfort, while reflective logos provide visibility during low-light conditions.</cite> <cite index="2-9">Panels of stretch nylon Flexile over the knees provide added protection with good wind and water resistance in that high-wear zone, and the wide elasticated waistband stays comfortable when layering.</cite>

One durability flag worth raising: PrimaLoft® ACTIVE's open-mesh construction is inherently vulnerable to snagging. <cite index="24-25">All weights of PrimaLoft Active are extremely vulnerable to snagging</cite>, so scrambling terrain, pack hipbelts, and bramble-covered trails all represent real risks to the fabric's longevity. The Flexile knee panels address the highest-contact zone, but the rest of the tight is delicate by design.

— a genuine bonus for fastpackers looking to squeeze double-duty out of every

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely ultralight at 130g for a full-length insulated tight
- PrimaLoft® ACTIVE delivers fleece-level warmth at a fraction of the packed volume
- Continues to insulate when wet — reliable in UK-style mountain conditions
- Air permeability prevents overheating during high-output activity
- Flexile knee panels add targeted wind/water resistance where it matters most
- Flatlock seams reduce chafe risk during long efforts
- Doubles as a sleep layer, adding system versatility
- Strong sustainability credentials: <cite index="1-8">58% post-consumer recycled material, bluesign and Oeko-Tex approved</cite>
- Reflective detail useful for pre-dawn or post-dusk outings

**Cons**

- Useless without a wind layer — not a standalone option in any breeze
- The open-mesh PrimaLoft® ACTIVE fabric snags easily; longevity on rocky or technical terrain is a concern
- No pockets — not even a small key loop
- Narrow seasonal window: too warm for shoulder-season use, too vulnerable for aggressive winter scrambling without careful care
- OMM's fitted cut may not suit everyone; sizing runs slim

## Who Should Buy This

<cite index="8-8">
  The Core Tights are built for endurance athletes, fastpackers, and trail
  runners who demand performance in all conditions.
</cite>
More specifically, they shine for anyone doing high-output winter running or
mountain marathons who needs real insulation without the bulk of a fleece tight
— and who already carries a wind or waterproof shell layer as standard. They'd
also appeal to the ultralight backpacker looking to shave grams from their sleep
system. They're not the right pick for a casual winter hiker who wants an
all-in-one solution, or anyone doing technical scrambling where snag risk is
high.

## Verdict

The OMM Core Tights do something genuinely hard: they deliver meaningful insulation at 130g with enough breathability to keep a moving body from overheating. The mandatory shell pairing isn't a flaw — it's a system design that actually works — but you need to go in eyes open about that dependency and the fabric's fragility. For winter trail runners and fastpackers already using a lightweight overtrouser, these are one of the most weight-efficient insulation options on the market for legs. Rating: **7.5/10**.
