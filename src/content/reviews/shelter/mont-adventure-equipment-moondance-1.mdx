## Overview

The Moondance 1 is a freestanding, three-season solo tent from Canberra's Mont Adventure Equipment, a company that has been building gear for Australian bushwalking since 1981. It is aimed at hikers and bikepackers who want a livable shelter more than a minimal one. The trade-off is plain: 2.31 m² of floor and a bathtub that doesn't leak, paid for in grams and dollars.

## Performance

**Space.** Mont's headline claim is a floor area 25% larger than the average one-person tent, and owners cite the interior as the reason to buy. The unusual feature is a triangular expansion along one side of the inner, 26 cm wide and 220 cm long. It is a waterproof strip inside the tent where a pack, boots and clothing can sit without being pushed into the vestibule. Steep walls at each end and a wide ridge pole across the roof keep the interior from feeling like a coffin. That matters on the day a storm pins a hiker in place for twelve hours.

**Weather protection.** The floor is the standout. The 40D nylon bathtub carries a 25,000 mm+ waterhead, far above what most tents in this class use. One long-term owner who has used the tent for a couple of years described both fly and floor as bombproof, with no leaks and no tears. That owner used the full-nylon 1FN variant, which shares the same fly and floor. The fly is a different story on paper: 20D ripstop nylon rated to 2,000 mm is ordinary, and it is the lighter-duty component of the system. Seams are factory tape-sealed on both fly and floor, so the tent is rain-ready out of the bag with no sealing chore.

**Weight and packing.** At 1,485 g the Moondance is under 1.5 kg, which Mont and its retailers lean on, but that is not ultralight by current standards. The same owner who praised its toughness noted that he owns another tent of around 650 g, and that the Moondance trades weight for durability. A retailer's own scale reading came in at 1.54 kg. The 1,325 g minimum weight, achievable by leaving behind the repair kit, extra pegs and stuff sack, is more competitive. The tent packs to 13 x 28 cm, and the DAC poles fold to 42 cm, so it fits inside a pack without lashing.

**Setup.** The design is symmetrical, and the non-directional pole hubs can face either way, which cuts down on fumbling in the dark. The inner pitches first, and the fly goes over the top. An older-generation owner described setup as medium difficulty at around seven minutes and sometimes fiddly, but the current hubs and symmetry were designed to address exactly that. It stands without pegs, and it can be pitched with just two pegs to save weight. Owners still tend to use the full set for tension. Mont also supports a fly-plus-footprint pitch for a much lighter shelter, though the footprint is sold separately.

**Ventilation and livability.** A roof vent can be opened from inside, there is an internal pocket, and a carabiner clip hangs a headlamp from the ceiling. The mesh inner is airy, which suits summer and shoulder-season nights. Anyone camping in colder, windier country should look at the 1FN, a 1,525 g variant with a full nylon inner that trades airflow for warmth and resistance to blowing snow.

**Durability and support.** Mont has a reputation for service. One reviewer on Trustpilot praised the company for tracking down a replacement fly for an old tent that had faded and torn after heavy use. Another reported a stripped thread where the ridge pole screws into the hubs, which is the kind of wear that comes with years of use rather than a design failure.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 2.31 m² floor plus a waterproof side expansion gives real room for gear
- 25,000 mm+ floor and taped seams hold up in wet ground and heavy rain
- Freestanding, with a symmetrical, non-directional pole layout
- Internal and external vestibules, roof vent, and ceiling clip
- Packs small (13 x 28 cm) with a 42 cm pole length
- Footprint-and-fly option for lighter pitching

**Cons**

- 1,485 g is heavy next to modern ultralight solo shelters
- A$789.95 is a lot for a tent in this weight class
- Only one door, with the internal vestibule not accessible from inside
- 2,000 mm fly is unremarkable
- Footprint is an extra purchase

## Who Should Buy This

The Moondance suits bushwalkers, bikepackers and travelers who sleep alone and want comfort and storm-proofing more than the lowest base weight. It makes sense for hikers who spend long evenings in the tent, or who camp on soggy ground where a rugged floor is worth carrying. Gram counters and thru-hikers chasing a sub-kilogram shelter should look elsewhere, as should anyone who won't pay near A$800 for a tent that isn't ultralight.

## Verdict

The Moondance 1 is a tough, spacious, freestanding solo tent that does what it says, and it keeps hikers dry and comfortable when a trip goes wrong. It is overweight and overpriced for the ultralight crowd, but for those who want a roomy home on the trail, it is a durable choice.
