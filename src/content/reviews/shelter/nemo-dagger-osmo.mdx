## Overview

The Dagger OSMO is NEMO's answer to a question most ultralight tents dodge: what if two people actually want to sit up, spread out and keep their boots dry? It's a freestanding, double-wall, two-door tent built from NEMO's OSMO poly-nylon ripstop, with a rectangular floor and a pole structure reworked to push the walls closer to vertical. The trade-off is plain. At 1,780 g it carries a pound or more over the true ultralight two-person shelters, and costs about $600.

## Performance

**Livability.** This is where the Dagger earns its reputation. The 88 x 50 in floor is a true rectangle, without the tapered foot end common to backpacking tents, and Section Hiker's reviewer found room for two 25-inch sleeping pads side by side. At 30.6 square feet, it out-measures the Big Agnes Copper Spur UL2 (29 sq ft). Better Trail measured the 43 in peak height as half an inch short of the Sea to Summit Telos and 3 in taller than NEMO's own Hornet. A 6'3" tester sat upright without trouble. Tall sleepers and anyone sharing with a dog or a partner who tosses will notice the difference most. Owners on Reddit repeatedly mention that a 6-footer has plenty of space.

**Weather protection.** OSMO is a blend of polyester and nylon, and its signature trait is that it doesn't sag when wet. NEMO claims 3x less stretch than standard tent fabrics, and testers say the claim holds. A reviewer at The Manual watched the fly shed rain through an afternoon and a full night of wind and downpour without re-tensioning. In the UK, Live for the Outdoors sat out heavy rain in North Wales without a drop reaching the inner. One REI buyer reported waking up dry after 13 inches of heavy, wet snow, with no sagging. This is a three-season tent, though. A few owners describe it as out of its depth in harsh conditions, and the 1,500 mm fly rating is typical for the category rather than exceptional.

**Setup.** The symmetrical layout and color-matched, intuitive pole structure make for a quick, taut pitch, and owners call it easy and fast. Two quirks come up. The inner pitches first, so a downpour setup is wetter than with a fly-first tent. Live for the Outdoors also found the fly a bit fiddly to attach at the center spreader bar.

**Vestibules and details.** The two trapezoidal vestibules are among the tent's best features, large enough for two packs and shoes each. A removable Landing Zone keeps wet gear off the dirt. One-handed toggles hold the doors open, and small interior pockets diffuse a headlamp into a soft glow. Section Hiker's reviewer called the vestibules his favorite feature. The interior pockets are the weak spot. Testers describe them as undersized and not much use for larger items.

**Ventilation.** Vestibule doors can be vented from inside by unzipping a section at the top, so there's no need to step out and prop the fly. Switchback Travel still ranked ventilation as short of class-leading, and one Section Hiker commenter found the vents small and delicate.

**Weight.** Better Trail pegged the all-in weight at 3 lb 15 oz and called it unremarkable against the Copper Spur UL2 (about 3 lb) and NEMO's Dragonfly OSMO (3 lb 2 oz). The Hornet OSMO 2P comes in at 2 lb 8 oz, a pound and a half lighter, with a lower roof and less room. The Dagger OSMO does come in under 2 kg, and it splits easily between two hikers into a pair of manageable loads. Reviewers also note that it is lighter and cheaper than the previous Dagger. Switchback Travel measured the savings at 3 oz and $30.

**Sustainability.** OSMO is PFC-free, made from recycled yarn and solution-dyed. T3 called the Dagger the first fully bluesign-approved backpacking tent. T3 also noted that the footprint is sold separately and that only eight stakes come in the box.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 30.6 sq ft rectangular floor fits two wide pads, with a 43 in peak height
- OSMO fabric resists sag in sustained rain
- Large vestibules and a waterproof Landing Zone
- Intuitive, symmetrical freestanding pitch
- Under 2 kg for a tent this roomy
- Recycled, PFC-free, bluesign-certified materials

**Cons**

- Heavier than the Hornet OSMO (a pound and a half), Copper Spur UL2 and Dragonfly OSMO
- Costs about $600, with the footprint sold separately
- Inner-first pitch is slower in the rain
- Interior pockets are small
- Only eight stakes are included
- Ventilation is good but not best in class

## Who Should Buy This

The Dagger OSMO suits couples, tall hikers and solo backpackers who value a roomy tent and don't mind an extra pound or two. It's also a good fit for trips where bad weather keeps you in the tent for long periods. Gram-counting thru-hikers, and anyone who mostly sleeps in the tent and doesn't live in it, should look at the Hornet OSMO or a trekking-pole shelter. Shoppers on a tight budget will find cheaper two-person tents that cover the basics.

## Verdict

The Dagger OSMO is one of the most livable two-person backpacking tents available, and its fabric holds tension through long storms. It's too heavy and expensive to win over weight-first hikers. For two people who want space and dependable weather protection on three-season trips, it's an easy recommendation.
