## Overview

The Dragonfly 18 is Blue Ice's smallest technical pack: a top-loading, 18-liter sack built for multi-pitch routes and alpine days where a full-size mountaineering pack would be dead weight. It carries a rope, two ice tools and a helmet without much fuss, and it strips down to 325 g when the belt and frame sheet come out. The trade-off is fabric. Owners praise the carry features and fault the shell for being thin.

## Performance

**Carry and comfort.** The Dragonfly 18 uses an ultra-static mesh back panel and thin, air-permeable shoulder straps, with engineered foam in the shoulders meant to carry load without collapsing. Owners describe it as comfortable on route and well suited to short approaches. One long-term user reports taking it up more than 100 peaks. A minority of buyers on European retail sites rate the carry as not comfortable, and this is a pack that rewards modest loads. A UK reviewer who tested an earlier version said it handled up to about 11 kg without trouble, but that is the upper end for a 415 g pack with a thin frame sheet.

**Rope and axe carry.** This is where the pack earns its reputation. A Mountain Project user who kept the 18 after selling his other small packs said both the rope carry and the axe carry work, and that the buckles are usable with gloves. Another buyer found the two axe holders work well with hammerless, adze-free tools, but called the rope-strap hardware a pain to unhook. Anyone who has fought a stiff clip with cold fingers will recognize the complaint.

**Capacity.** A winter user reports fitting everything he needs in the 18 when paired with the separate helmet carrier, which he considers better than using a larger pack. The pack is top-loading with a drawcord closure and no volume extension. The stretch pocket sits on one side only, and several owners say they wish it had a pair. One reviewer also found the zippered pocket was not reachable from inside the pack, contrary to the product description.

**Durability.** This is the pack's weak point. A Mountain Project poster said it wore holes through on the first multi-pitch he used it on. A buyer on Blue Ice's own site reported holes in the ripstop in three places after a week of climbing at Red Rocks. Another climber called it very light but thin, and said he would not throw it around. A fellow Blue Ice owner summed up the brand's approach: the heavy gear is burly, and the lightest gear is not. Not every owner has had problems. The pack has also been revised several times since the original yellow version. One owner on the Blue Ice site says he is on his third generation and finds it improving each time. Even so, expect to treat the Dragonfly 18 like a piece of ultralight gear. Haul bags and chimneys will find the weak spots.

**Weather.** The fabric sheds light weather, and the zippers are water-resistant YKK. The pack is not waterproof, and a dry bag or pack liner is still needed for a wet day.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 415 g, dropping to 325 g with the frame sheet and belt removed
- Rope carry and dual axe carry work as intended, with glove-friendly buckles
- Breathable mesh back panel and low-profile straps
- PFC-free, Bluesign-approved fabric
- Folds into its own front pocket, so it doubles as a summit or follower pack
- Priced around $96, and often discounted

**Cons**

- 210d shell has drawn repeated reports of holes and abrasion damage
- Stretch pocket on one side only
- Rope strap hardware is fiddly to unhook
- One size only
- Mesh back panel and minimal frame limit comfort with heavier loads

## Who Should Buy This

Multi-pitch climbers, alpine scramblers and ski mountaineers who want a small, clean pack for a day on route will find the most to like here. It also works as a collapsible summit pack for a longer trip. Thru-hikers shopping for a daypack for town days or side trips can use it, but it has none of the load support or pocket layout of a hiking pack, and a typical trail pack in this weight class will suit them better. Climbers who regularly squeeze through chimneys, drag a pack up granite or haul gear should look at a burlier pack with a heavier fabric.

## Verdict

The Dragonfly 18 does the things an alpine day pack needs to do, namely carry a rope and two axes at 415 g without getting in the way, and it does them for less than $100. Its thin shell keeps it from being a pack to rely on year after year, and rough use will shorten its life. It is a good buy for climbers who prioritize low weight over longevity and who will take care of it.
