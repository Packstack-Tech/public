## Overview

The Native Eyewear Highline is a 28 g polarized sunglass with a retro-leaning frame, made from bio-based castor resin and fitted with the brand's N3 lens. It suits hikers who want real glare protection without feeling anything on their face by mid-afternoon. The trade-off is a compact frame that doesn't fit every head, plus a semi-rigid case that weighs more than twice what the glasses do.

## Performance

**Weight and comfort.** At 28 g, the Highline sits at the light end of full-coverage sunglasses. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers described the glasses as well balanced, without the pressure points they found on competing frames, and rated comfort 8 out of 10. Padded Cushinol nose pads and temple tips do the work of holding the frame in place. A REI reviewer said the nose pads kept the glasses from sliding even when sweaty. The Trek's thru-hiker reviewer saw a little slippage while trail running downhill, and said he'd add a retainer strap for a long-distance hike. For walking-pace hiking, owners report no such issue.

**Lens quality.** Native's N3 lens is a polarized, glare-cutting design, and the company claims it blocks up to four times more infrared light than standard polarized lenses. Outdoor Gear Lab scored lens quality 8 out of 10 and said the Highline had the most protective lenses of any pair in its test group. Owners report crisp contrast in hazy light, and one REI buyer logged hours at about 9,000 feet without eye fatigue. The Trek's reviewer had no complaints about the lens and found that light entering from the side still left a clear view. That matters on bright talus, water crossings, and snowfields, where glare comes from every direction.

**Durability and care.** Testers called the frame durable, though Outdoor Gear Lab scored frame quality only 6 out of 10 and noted that the frames picked up scratches. Native's lifetime repair program is a consistent point of praise. Outdoor Gear Lab called its $30 repair offer one of the best manufacturer deals in the test. Repeat buyers on REI and Zappos also single out the warranty. For backpackers who lose or crush a pair of sunglasses every season, that policy changes the math.

**The case problem.** The Highline ships with a semi-rigid zippered case and a microfiber pouch that doubles as a cleaning cloth. The hard case weighs 71 g, which is the weight of the glasses plus nearly 50% more. The Trek's thru-hiker reviewer called it bulky and said he would carry only the cloth pouch on a long hike. He did note the case has interior pockets that are handy for car trips and town days. In practice, most ultralight users will leave the hard case at home, or in the resupply box, and carry the glasses in the pouch.

**Fit and style.** The Highline is a small-to-medium frame. Outdoor Gear Lab called it fairly small and said it may hit the eyebrows, and scored style and versatility 4 out of 10. Other owners find it works well on a smaller face, and one Zappos buyer found it a little large, so reports on fit are mixed. Anyone who prefers a larger, wraparound shape will not warm to the look. The frame doesn't offer the side coverage of a glacier-style wrap, so it is a poor fit for sustained snow travel at altitude.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 28 g frame that most wearers forget they have on
- N3 polarized lens with 100% UV protection and 99% polarization efficiency
- Padded nose and temple contact points that hold on sweaty climbs
- Bio-based castor resin frame
- Lifetime repair program that owners rate highly
- Includes both a hard case and a cleaning-cloth pouch

**Cons**

- Semi-rigid case weighs 71 g, over twice the glasses
- Small frame that may touch eyebrows and doesn't suit every face
- Limited side coverage for glacier or snow use
- Frames scratch more readily than the lenses
- May slip during fast, sweaty running without a retainer

## Who Should Buy This

The Highline suits three-season backpackers and thru-hikers with small-to-medium faces who want polarized lenses at a modest price and a company that will repair them. It also works as a day-to-day pair that goes from trail to town without looking like sport gear. Mountaineers and snow travelers needing wraparound coverage should look at a glacier-style frame. So should anyone with a large head, or anyone who dislikes the fit of smaller frames. Try them on, or use the 30-day return window Outdoor Gear Lab cited.

## Verdict

The Highline delivers what a hiker needs from sunglasses: very low weight, solid polarized optics, and a warranty that makes breakage less costly. Leave the 71 g case at home and carry the cloth pouch. Provided the fit works, it is an easy pick for the pack.
