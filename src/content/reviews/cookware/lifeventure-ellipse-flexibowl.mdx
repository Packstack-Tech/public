## Overview

The Lifeventure Ellipse Flexibowl is an oval eating bowl with flexible silicone walls and a rigid nylon base. It folds to a flat disc about 20 mm thick and pops open to roughly 65 mm deep. It is built for campers, scouts and Duke of Edinburgh groups who want a real bowl that doesn't eat pack volume. The trade-off is weight: at 90 g it costs more than a lot of purpose-built ultralight kit.

## Performance

**Packing.** This is the reason to buy it. The walls fold down into the nylon base, so the bowl packs flat against a pack's back panel or slides into a pocket with a pot and stove. Owners keep coming back to this point: it opens to a useful size and collapses into a neat, compact piece. A retailer lists the folded depth at 15 mm, and the manufacturer's spec sheet says 20 mm.

**In use.** The walls hold their shape once raised, so the bowl doesn't need a table. A reviewer at TentLife noted that it can be held in one hand with the other underneath, or balanced on a lap, and the kids' cereal didn't cause problems. Another owner said it is very easy to hold even with boiling hot food inside, and the finger loop helps there. The silicone is rated to 230 °C, so hot food and boiling water are well within its range. It is an eating vessel, though, not a pot. It belongs next to the stove, not on it.

**Capacity.** The bowl holds about 500 ml. Several buyers call that right for breakfast cereal or a portion of pasta. A few say it is slightly smaller than they expected. A hungry hiker eating a full dehydrated dinner will fill it quickly.

**Rigidity.** The walls are sturdy when empty, but they flex under pressure. One UK owner noted that the bowl loses its shape a little when squeezed. Stirring with a spoon is fine. Carrying it full with one hand at the rim is less secure than holding a rigid bowl.

**Cleaning and odors.** Most owners find it easy to clean and report no lingering taste or smell. The manufacturer says the silicone won't retain tastes or odors, and it can go in the dishwasher. Not everyone agrees. One retail reviewer who used it while trekking said food smells hang on and that cleaning it without washing-up liquid and hot water is a struggle. Silicone is known to hold oils, so greasy meals on a trail with no hot water will be the hard test.

**Extras.** The rigid nylon base doubles as a small chopping board, and the loop lets the bowl dangle from a pack to dry. Some owners have repurposed it, one as a dog water dish and another as a shaving rinse bowl. It also works as a small basin for a day hike with a dog.

**Weight.** At 90 g, the Flexibowl is not light. Lifeventure's own non-collapsible Ellipse Bowl weighs 74 g, and the rigid version stacks with the rest of the range. Hikers who eat out of the cooking pot or a freezer bag will carry less. The Flexibowl pays off only when volume matters more than grams.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Folds to about 20 mm, which is easy to pack
- Stays upright without support once opened
- Rated from -40 to 230 °C, so hot food is no problem
- Finger loop makes it easy to hold and hang
- The base doubles as a small cutting board
- Dishwasher safe and BPA free
- Low price, with owners calling it good value

**Cons**

- 90 g is heavy for ultralight use
- 500 ml is modest for hungry hikers
- Walls flex under pressure
- At least one owner reports lingering food smells on trail
- Not a cooking vessel

## Who Should Buy This

Duke of Edinburgh groups, scouts, family campers and weekend backpackers who want a proper bowl that doesn't take up room in a pack. It also suits travelers who want a flat bowl for hostel and picnic meals. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. A lighter bowl, a mug that does double duty, or eating from the pot will save weight and work better for a long trail with limited washing water.

## Verdict

The Flexibowl does what it promises: a usable bowl that folds flat and doesn't cost much. It is a convenience product, not an ultralight one. It earns a recommendation for casual campers and youth groups, and a pass for anyone serious about base weight.
