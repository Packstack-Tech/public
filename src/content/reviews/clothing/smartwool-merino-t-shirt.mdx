## Overview

The Smartwool Merino T-Shirt is a slim-fitting, short-sleeve crew in Merino 150 jersey, with merino wool spun around a nylon core. At 128 g, it suits hikers who want a shirt that can be worn for days without smelling like it. The trade-off is the usual one for thin merino: it is more comfortable than any synthetic tee, and it wears out sooner than a thicker or more heavily synthetic one.

## Performance

**Odor resistance.** This is why people buy merino, and owners consistently rank it first. Reviewers describe wearing the tee through a six-day trip without it turning rank. Appalachian Trail hikers who switched from synthetic shirts to the Merino 150 line describe the change in similar terms. After four days in the woods, the wool still outlasts a polyester tee by a wide margin. Buyers also report wearing it several times between washes at home.

**Temperature range.** Merino 150 is Smartwool's lightest weight, and owners use it across a wide span. Reviewers wear it for all-day hikes in the heat, and the same shirt serves as a base layer under a long-sleeve top for skiing and cold-weather backpacking. GearJunkie testers called it an all-arounder that kept them cool on warm-weather hikes while still covering the shoulders well, and gave it an overall score of 7.9 out of 10.

**Comfort and chafe.** The fabric is soft against the skin, and the offset shoulder and side seams are designed to keep pack straps from digging at stitching. Owners single out the seam work as a comfort advantage. A few wearers note some itch in warm climates or in the smaller sizes, which is normal for merino.

**Fit.** The slim cut follows the body. Owners say it runs small, and several buy a size up. The hem is also short. A recurring complaint is that it won't stay tucked in. That matters little on trail but does for anyone wearing it as a town shirt.

**Durability.** The nylon core is meant to fix the old weakness of ultralight merino, and GearJunkie scored the tee 8 out of 10 for durability. Owners are less uniform. Some call the fabric very durable. Others, mostly on multi-day trips, report pilling on the back, and one backpacker saw the fabric separate near the hipbelt contact area after a single trip. A longtime owner puts it plainly: thin merino wears out faster than heavier wool, and comfort comes at that price. Hikers who pair the tee with a pack that has hipbelt pockets and mesh back panels should expect the most wear in those zones.

**Drying.** The tee dries more slowly than synthetic fabric, though owners who air-dry say it takes a few hours rather than a day. It performs well when worn through light rain and sweat, then dried overnight in a tent or on a pack.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very good odor resistance over multi-day trips
- 128 g, with the soft hand of merino
- Core-spun nylon adds durability over a pure merino 150 tee
- Offset seams reduce chafe under pack straps and hipbelts
- Works in heat and in cold as a base layer
- Reads as an ordinary tee, so it works in town and on the trail

**Cons**

- Thin fabric can pill and abrade where a pack rubs, with some owners reporting early wear
- Slim fit runs small, and the short hem rides up
- Dries slower than a synthetic tee
- Priced well above a synthetic hiking shirt

## Who Should Buy This

This tee is for three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want one shirt to wear for days at a time and who value smell resistance and comfort over the longest possible service life. It also suits travelers who want a shirt that passes as everyday clothing. Hikers who carry heavy loads with a lot of hipbelt and shoulder-strap contact, or who want a shirt to survive an entire long-distance hike, should look at a heavier merino-blend or a synthetic tee. Anyone between sizes should go up one.

## Verdict

The Smartwool Merino T-Shirt delivers what merino promises: a comfortable shirt that stays tolerable through a long stretch of sweat and miles. Durability under a loaded pack is its weak point, and it is the reason this stops short of a top rating. For most weekend and section-hiking use, it is a solid buy.
