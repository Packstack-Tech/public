## Overview

<cite index="14-9,14-10">
  The Atom+ is a beefed-up version of Atom Packs' original frameless Atom, built
  on the same body but with an increased torso length to accommodate a removable
  hip belt and a foam back panel housing a removable carbon hooped frame.
</cite>
<cite index="10-4,10-5">
  Atom Packs is a cottage gear company based in the Lake District, founded by
  Triple Crowner Tom Gale — a professional mountain leader with over 12,000
  miles under his feet.
</cite>
The result is a pack that threads a real needle: ultralight enough to keep your
base weight honest, structured enough to actually carry a week's worth of food
without turning into a sack of suffering. Worth noting:
<cite index="10-25,10-26">
  the Atom+ was later renamed the Pulse, with reviews updated to reflect the new
  name as of August 2024.
</cite>
If you're shopping the current lineup, you'll find it listed as the Pulse — same
DNA, new badge.

## Performance

### Carry & Suspension

The carbon fibre hoop is the defining feature of the Atom+ and, frankly, what separates it from the crowd of sub-700g frameless packs. <cite index="24-1,24-2">By utilizing carbon fiber's exceptional stiffness-to-weight ratio, the frame provides the structural tension necessary to prevent the pack fabric from collapsing under the weight of a heavy resupply, and shifts the load from the shoulder straps down into the padded hip belt.</cite> In practice, <cite index="24-3,24-4">the hooped frame keeps the back panel flat and flush against the spine, maintaining a narrow and tall profile that helps you feel more agile on technical terrain — and even during high-volume desert hauls with extra water, it remains rigid enough to prevent the pack from sagging.</cite>

That said, there are real limits. <cite index="23-3,23-4,23-5,23-6">The pack is most comfortable when kept under its maximum load capacity; it gets a little uncomfortable around 25 pounds, as the minimalist carbon hoop frame and semi-narrow hip belt won't transfer weight to the hips as well as some other packs. It also lacks load lifters that could shift weight closer to the body.</cite> If your base weight is north of 20 lbs before food and water, this probably isn't the right tool. But used as intended — a refined UL kit under 25 lbs — <cite index="18-35,18-36,18-37">the Atom+ is exceptionally comfortable; just don't consistently carry loads over 30 pounds, and it'll be more comfortable than most ultralight packs.</cite>

The removable hip belt is a genuine asset for versatility. <cite index="22-6">With the hip belt removed, the pack drops to about 1.2 lbs / 550g — useful for day hikes or weekenders — yet with it attached it's good to carry 30+ pounds comfortably on a thru-hike.</cite> One practical note: <cite index="22-12,22-13">getting the hip belt on and off can be a challenge; sliding a piece of cardboard between the hip belt and the pack makes it much easier to remove and also helps you position it correctly when putting it back on.</cite> A few users on Trailspace and The Trek's comments echo this — it's a minor but real friction point on the trail.

### Shoulder Straps & Comfort

<cite index="18-12,18-13,18-14,18-15,18-16">
  The S-shaped shoulder straps are made of 3D mesh and 10mm foam, with the mesh
  allowing air to flow between the straps and your body. The S-shaped curve
  makes the pack very comfortable.
</cite>
<cite index="18-17,18-18">
  Even during long days of 8+ hours of hiking, one reviewer reported no chafing
  — even when using the pack without the hip belt.
</cite>
<cite index="24-11,24-12">
  Fit sensitivity is worth flagging: comfort and usability depend heavily on
  dialing in the correct torso size and strap positioning, which can lead to
  issues like chafing or hard-to-reach pockets if you get it wrong.
</cite>
Order the right torso length — Atom Packs' sizing guidance is worth spending
time on before you click buy.

### Organization & Features

<cite index="21-5">
  The simple roll-top closure gives access to a big main compartment, with a top
  compression/gear strap, plus stretch pockets on the side, front, base, and the
  shoulder straps.
</cite>
<cite index="21-17,21-18,21-19">
  The Atom+ is actually a lot more practical and versatile than most
  stripped-back ultralight packs — the harness incorporates two shoulder pockets
  either side of the sternum strap that are super stretchy and big enough to
  hold most smartphones, soft flasks, or a fistful of trail bars.
</cite>

The bottom pocket with its built-in trash port is a detail you won't find on most packs. <cite index="2-22,2-23">There's a small corner of the bottom pocket that stays open so you can stuff your trash into it, plus a snap closure at the top instead of a loud Velcro strip.</cite> Small things, but they add up.

<cite index="21-21,21-22">
  The lidless roll-top design takes a little getting used to — it's better
  suited to organised hikers, since you'll find yourself rooting around for a
  specific bit of kit otherwise.
</cite>
Color-coded dry bags are your friend here.

### Durability & Weather Resistance

<cite index="12-1,12-2">
  The pack uses 210-denier Robic Extreema material on the side pockets, shoulder
  straps, and hip belt — an even more durable material in all the high-wear
  areas.
</cite>
<cite index="12-6,12-7">
  One thru-hiker used the same Dyneema-reinforced stretch mesh on the CDT and
  after nearly 3,000 miles it showed no signs of wear.
</cite>
<cite index="9-13,9-14">
  One reviewer took the Atom+ on the Welsh 3,000s, 200 miles on the PCT, and
  countless overnighters in Snowdonia, the Lake District, and Scotland through
  all seasons — with no delamination of the VX21, though the X pattern in the
  fabric became more prominent over time.
</cite>

Weather protection is the one area where you need to manage expectations. <cite index="9-2">While VX21 is highly waterproof, water can still get through the seams, so a DCF pack liner is strongly recommended to keep insulated layers and down quilts dry in heavy rain.</cite> <cite index="23-16">The fabric's waterproofing is reinforced with an eco-DWR finish, but seams are not taped, so water may still get in during prolonged exposure to rain.</cite> Budget for a pack liner — either Atom Packs' own DCF option or a simple rubble sack.

### Customization

<cite index="2-14,2-15,2-16">
  Customization extends from selecting torso and hip belt dimensions to choosing
  colors for nearly all panels and materials for pockets. You can also specify
  shoulder strap and bottom pockets, top strap configuration, front elastic,
  side compression straps, and even the colors of the tabs that hold the front
  elastic — making designing a pack nearly as enjoyable as using it.
</cite>
It's genuinely rare at this price point.
<cite index="22-35">
  The pack can get expensive with a lot of modifications, and lead times are
  currently long
</cite>
— factor that into your planning if you're targeting a specific trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Carbon fibre frame punches well above its weight penalty — meaningfully better load transfer than frameless packs at a similar total weight
- Modular design: remove the hip belt, frame, and foam panel to strip roughly 5+ oz for lighter missions
- S-shaped shoulder straps are genuinely comfortable and work well for a wide range of body types
- Shoulder strap and bottom pockets are thoughtful, well-executed details most competitors skip
- Extraordinary customization options — fabric, color, pocket config, strap type, torso length
- Handmade by hikers, for hikers, from a small UK cottage operation with a strong quality track record

**Cons**

- Seams not sealed — a pack liner is a practical necessity in wet conditions, not an optional extra
- Removable hip belt can be tricky to re-seat correctly; requires some patience or a cardboard shim trick
- No load lifters; comfort starts to drop off noticeably as you approach 25 lbs
- Lidless roll-top requires organized packing discipline — poor fit for casual gear-dumpers
- Custom builds come with long lead times and cost can escalate quickly with add-ons
- Back panel provides minimal airflow — expect a sweaty back in warm weather

## Who Should Buy This

The Atom+ is the right pack for hikers who've already dialed in a genuine ultralight kit and want a framed pack that doesn't penalize them for it. <cite index="21-20">You need to already have an ultralight setup — in terms of shelter, sleep system, and cook kit — otherwise you'll struggle to get all your gear in and even if you do, it'll be too heavy for a comfortable carry.</cite> It's especially compelling for UK and European hikers who'd otherwise have to import from US cottage brands and pay the corresponding shipping and customs premium. <cite index="22-8">It would perform well on something like the PCT, where a hip belt and extra pockets would be helpful in the Sierra but could be ditched in NorCal/Oregon where the terrain and carry demands ease off.</cite> If you frequently carry loads above 25 lbs or want a pack with serious load lifters and a structured suspension, look at the Atom Packs Prospector or something like the ULA Circuit instead.

## Verdict

The Atom+ is one of the most compelling framed ultralight packs available, particularly for hikers based in Europe. The carbon fibre hoop genuinely earns its place — it keeps the pack honest under load in a way no frameless alternative can match at this weight — and the customization depth is almost unmatched in the cottage gear world. The unsealed seams and hip belt fussiness are real trade-offs, not dealbreakers. For a weight-conscious hiker with a refined kit, it's a confident buy at 8.5/10.
