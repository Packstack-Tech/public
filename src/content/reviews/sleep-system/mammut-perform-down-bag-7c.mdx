## Overview

The Perform Down Bag -7C is Mammut's midrange three-season-into-shoulder-season bag: a 700-fill duck down mummy with a centre two-way zip, a quiet hood fabric and a cut designed around how people actually sleep rather than how they pack. It weighs 1,020 g, which is respectable for a bag with this much room. It is not a bag for gram counters, and the trade-off is the point: Mammut is selling a better night's sleep, not the lowest number on the scale.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The -7°C figure is the EN lower limit, the temperature at which a cold sleeper in a curled position should still manage the night. The comfort rating for a standard sleeper is -1°C. That distinction matters: reviewers who tested the bag at the edge of its range supplemented it with clothing, and UKHillwalking's Dan Bailey used a jacket as an extra layer when temperatures fell below the comfort figure. In the -1°C to -5°C window, a TikTok reviewer who slept in it reported staying warm all night. Treat it as a bag for frosty shoulder-season camping and not a true winter bag. Mammut's own Protect line exists for that.

**Weight and packed size.** Bailey's scales matched Mammut's 1,020 g claim exactly, and he called it more midweight than ultralight, while noting that the volume of bag on offer justifies the figure. He also noted that lighter bags of similar warmth exist. The stuff sack compresses the bag well with a single strap, but it is not waterproof, so a separate dry bag adds a little more weight. Owners describe the packed size as acceptable rather than small. It will fit a mountaineering pack or a bike-touring bag without trouble, but it won't impress anyone who has packed a 750-fill quilt.

**Fit and sleep comfort.** This is where the bag earns its keep. The shape is a relaxed mummy with a gentle taper that leaves room to move the limbs without creating much dead air to heat, according to The Great Outdoors' tester. The hood is well-insulated and cinches with internal adjusters, and the footbox is shaped and insulated rather than just a sewn-off end. The hood panel is made from a fabric designed not to rustle, a small feature that light sleepers in a shared hut or tent will appreciate. Mammut ships the bag with a sleep mask and earplugs, which is more gesture than gear, but it signals the intent.

**The centre zip.** The zip runs down the middle of the bag, favouring neither left- nor right-handed users, and it runs smoothly with few snags according to reviewers. A magnetic closure at the top keeps it from creeping open overnight, a detail a TikTok reviewer singled out. Venting is intuitive: open the zip from the top for the chest or from the bottom for the feet. The drawback is one that a Backcountry owner put plainly: side sleepers end up with a zip beneath them. That owner got used to it, but not everyone will. A shoulder baffle and an internal pocket round out the feature list.

**Weather.** The PFC-free DWR finish extends the life of the down in damp conditions. A Backcountry owner who spent a week in the Grand Canyon, including three rainy days, reported the bag holding up well. Down is still down: in a persistently wet tent, the synthetic Perform Fiber Bag -7C is the safer choice, though it weighs roughly 1,550 g by one Norwegian listing.

**Durability and ethics.** The down is RDS-certified and the lining is recycled polyester. The box-wall construction keeps the fill distributed rather than shifting to the edges. Owner feedback across retailers is overwhelmingly positive, with ratings in the high fours.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roomy, natural-position cut that doesn't feel like a straitjacket
- Hood fabric designed to reduce rustling
- Two-way centre zip vents easily, with a magnetic top closure
- 1,020 g is reasonable for the volume and warmth
- RDS-certified down and PFC-free DWR
- Sleep mask, earplugs and storage bag included

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than narrower 700–800 fill bags of similar rating
- The -7°C figure is a limit rating; comfort is -1°C
- Centre zip is awkward for side sleepers
- Stuff sack isn't waterproof
- List price of $400 is steep when comparable bags often sell for less

## Who Should Buy This

The Perform suits trekkers, hut-to-hut mountaineers and cold-weather campers who sleep restlessly in snug mummy bags and would rather carry an extra 200–300 g than lie awake. It also works for cyclists and travellers who value a quiet, roomy bag over packed volume. Thru-hikers and base-weight minimalists should look elsewhere: a quilt or narrower bag will save weight and space. Side sleepers who prefer a side zip should also consider alternatives.

## Verdict

The Perform Down Bag -7C is a comfort-first bag with honest weight and a well-executed centre zip. It is best bought on discount, and best used by cold-shoulder-season campers who value sleep quality over ounces. Ultralight purists will find lighter options; everyone else will probably sleep better here.
