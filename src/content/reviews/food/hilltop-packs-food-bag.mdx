## Overview

The Hilltop Packs Food Bag is a roll-top sack sewn in Waynesburg, Pennsylvania, from Challenge D50T, a laminated, partly recycled sailcloth that undercuts Dyneema on both price and, in some cases, weight. It is built for hikers who hang their food and want the lightest sack that still survives a season of being stuffed, rolled and dragged up a tree. The trade-off is a lighter, thinner fabric than the company's own ECOPAK line, plus the usual caveat: this is a hanging bag, not a bear canister.

## Performance

**Weight and fabric.** At 36 g for the small and 45 g for the large, this is about as light as a roll-top food bag gets without paying Dyneema prices. Hilltop's own ECOPAK version of the same bag weighs 60 g and 88 g. That means D50T saves roughly 24 g on the small and 43 g on the large. Hilltop describes D50T as the lighter, cheaper choice and ECOPAK as the one to pick when maximum durability matters most. The company says D50T is lighter than both 2.92 oz/yd² Dyneema and ECOPAK's EPLX200.

**Loading and organization.** The flat bottom is the feature owners mention most. A thru-hiker reviewing Hilltop's Dyneema version on Trailspace called it the reason for choosing the brand, because the bag stands open on its own and sits squarer in a pack than the tapered sacks it replaced. Garage Grown Gear's reviewer made the same point about digging to the bottom of a full bag, and noted that the squared-off shape stacks dehydrated meals efficiently and leaves room for bars and nut butters in the gaps. The D50T bags share that construction. A customer who bought the large said it swallowed four Mountain House meals and snacks with room to spare, and guessed it would hold at least ten dehydrated meal pouches with room left to roll.

**Weather resistance.** Taped seams and a laminated interior make the bag highly water-resistant, though it is a roll-top, not a submersible dry bag. In practice, that covers the usual job of keeping food dry in a wet pack or on the ground at camp. Hilltop's own copy calls the seams "virtually waterproof," which is a fair description for rain and condensation.

**Durability.** Reports are mostly positive but not unblemished. One customer who took the bag on an eight-day San Francisco-to-Tahoe route said it performed well. Another reviewer said it held up to repeated trips to and from the hang over a weekend. Another buyer reported delamination on an older Hilltop bear bag. The company responded quickly with a credit for a replacement. Laminated sailcloth of this weight will eventually show wear at the roll and the hang loop, especially on a long thru-hike. The listing advertises a five-year warranty on some versions.

**Hanging, not bear-proofing.** Hilltop is explicit that its bags are not bear-proof or odor-proof. They are meant for hanging or stashing food away from animals. Anyone hiking where canisters are required still needs one.

**Extras.** A spoon stash can be added for $6, and custom printing is available. Bright prints make it easy to find your own bag in a shelter full of identical ones.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes use the same fabric and construction. Only the volume and the weight change.

- **Small (7–8 L, 36 g):** Suits one person on a weekend or a short resupply, roughly one to three days of food. It also suits hikers who prefer to split food across two bags.
- **Large (12–13 L, 45 g):** Sized for three to seven days of food for one person. A thru-hiker heading into a long carry, or anyone who hangs toiletries and trash with the food, should start here. The 9 g penalty is trivial next to the headroom.

Anyone who needs more than 13 L should look at Hilltop's 20 L ECOPAK bag, which weighs 105 g.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 36–45 g, among the lightest roll-top food bags available
- Flat bottom stands up on its own and packs squarely
- Taped seams and laminated interior shed rain and damp
- Made in the USA by a small shop, with a custom print option
- Priced well below comparable Dyneema bags

**Cons**

- Thinner and less abrasion-tolerant than Hilltop's heavier ECOPAK fabric
- Not bear-proof or odor-proof
- Laminated sailcloth can delaminate with heavy use, as at least one owner has reported
- Printed and custom versions cost noticeably more than the plain white bag

## Who Should Buy This

Ultralight hikers and thru-hikers who hang food in black-bear country and want a light, cheap, replaceable sack will get what they need here. It also suits anyone moving off a conical Dyneema bag who wants something that stands up in camp. Those who haul food through rough brush, jam in sharp-edged items or want one bag to last many seasons should spend the extra 24–43 g on the ECOPAK version. Hikers in regions that require canisters, such as the Sierra Nevada, need a canister regardless.

## Verdict

The D50T food bag does the one job a hanging bag has to do at a weight and price that are hard to beat. The flat bottom makes it easier to live with than most cheaper alternatives. Buy the large for anything longer than a weekend, and buy ECOPAK instead if durability matters more to you than a few grams.
