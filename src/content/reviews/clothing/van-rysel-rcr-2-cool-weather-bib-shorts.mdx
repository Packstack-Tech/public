## Overview

The RCR 2 Cool Weather Bib Shorts are Van Rysel's answer to a familiar shoulder-season problem: summer bibs are too thin for October, and full winter tights are too hot by midday. They use a brushed Super Roubaix fabric with a water-repellent finish and an Elastic Interface pad, and they're meant to be worn with leg warmers. The trade-off is a very good pad and useful warmth at £64.99, against a narrow temperature window and the QC questions that follow Van Rysel's budget apparel.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The design logic is "targeted warmth." Brushed Super Roubaix keeps the thighs and glutes insulated, while the stretch bib leaves the torso free to move. Super Roubaix is a fabric Van Rysel has used across its winter bibs for several years. Owners of the brand's earlier fleece-lined winter bib shorts describe a warm, draft-free feel, and some pair them with tights or leg warmers into sub-zero temperatures. The RCR 2's own brief is narrower. Van Rysel pitches it at cool weather, and the sensible range is the cold-but-dry days when leg warmers do the work below the knee. Owners of the earlier model generally put the handover to full tights at roughly 10–12°C.

**Weather protection.** The water-repellent finish is rated for light rain. That means a passing shower or road spray beads off for a while, but it isn't a waterproof layer. Anyone riding through a proper soaking will want a jacket and mudguards. For the occasional damp commute or a morning of wet roads, the finish takes the edge off the chill.

**Pad.** This is where the shorts earn their keep. The Racer 2.5 HD pad sits in Elastic Interface's Performance range and is an upgrade of the Endurance 2 pad. Van Rysel says it has a more anatomical shape and protects the whole pelvic area. High-density foam sits under the top layer, and the brand rates it for up to five hours in the saddle. Elastic Interface pads appear in bib shorts costing far more, and reviewers of Van Rysel's other bibs, including road.cc, BikeBar-type value roundups and the German magazines Tour and Bike, single out the chamois as a high point of the range. Owners of the earlier fleece-lined version also call the pad very good for the money.

**Fit and construction.** The bib section and legs use stretch fabric intended to move with the rider rather than clamp down. Van Rysel's performance-cut shorts have a reputation for running small, and the brand itself advises sizing up on its Racer 2 summer bib if a looser fit is wanted. Anyone between sizes should check the size chart carefully. Reviewers of the brand's winter bibs have also noted scratchy shoulder straps when worn without a base layer, and some owners report seams loosening under intensive use. A base layer is normal winter practice anyway.

**Practicality.** The two rear pockets are a rare feature on bib shorts. They put gels and bars within reach without digging under a jacket, which is the point on a cold, layered ride. For bikepackers and long-distance riders, they're a small but genuine convenience.

**Value.** Customer ratings on Decathlon's UK site sit around 4.6 out of 5 from more than 100 votes. At £64.99, the RCR 2 undercuts most fleece-backed bibs from established cycling brands by a clear margin.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Elastic Interface Racer 2.5 HD pad rated for five-hour rides
- Brushed Super Roubaix lining gives real warmth for leg-warmer weather
- Water-repellent finish handles light rain and road spray
- Two rear pockets for food
- Priced well below comparable fleece-backed bibs from other brands

**Cons**

- Narrow temperature window; too warm for mild days and not enough on its own for deep cold
- Van Rysel cuts tend to run small, so sizing needs care
- Related Van Rysel winter bibs have drawn comments about scratchy straps and seam durability
- Water repellency is for light rain only

## Who Should Buy This

Road riders and bikepackers who already own leg warmers and want a single pair of bibs to cover autumn through early spring will get the most from the RCR 2. It suits long, steady days where pad quality matters more than brand name, and the pockets suit anyone who eats in the saddle. Riders who need serious cold-weather insulation should buy full bib tights instead. Heavy rain riders need a proper waterproof layer. Those who ride mostly above 15°C can stick with summer bibs.

## Verdict

The RCR 2 Cool Weather Bib Shorts do one job well: they keep the thighs warm and the saddle comfortable on long, cool rides, for far less than most fleece-backed bibs cost. Buy them for the pad and the shoulder-season versatility, and size up if the usual size is borderline. A 7.5 reflects strong value tempered by the narrow use window and the brand's mixed record on fit and finish.
