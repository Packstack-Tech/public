## Overview

The X Ultra 5 Mid GORE-TEX is Salomon's answer to a question many three-season hikers ask: how much boot is actually necessary? It pairs a Matryx-and-leather upper, a GORE-TEX PFC-free membrane and an advancedChassis with a weight that lands closer to a trail runner than a leather backpacking boot. The trade-off is a narrow cut and a synthetic construction that testers say won't match the longevity of heavier boots.

## Performance

**Weight and agility.** Weight is the reason this boot gets recommended. Outdoor Gear Lab called it one of the lightest waterproof hiking boots it has tested, and noted that the extra height over a trail shoe costs very little weight. CleverHiker's size 13 pair came in at 18.5 oz per boot, still at the light end of the mid-height category, and its testers said the boot never dragged even under a moderate pack. A four-day Havasupai Falls trip reviewed by Better Trail produced the same verdict: the weight never registered as a burden.

**Support.** The advancedChassis gives the boot more lateral stability than its sneaker-like looks suggest. Reviewers place it in the middle of the field. Better Trail found it more supportive than a mid-height ultralight hiker like the Altra Lone Peak Hiker 3, but short of the rigidity of a full backpacking boot such as the Lowa Camino Evo. The consensus is that it suits moderate pack weights and rough ground. It is not built for sustained heavy hauls.

**Comfort and cushioning.** Owners consistently describe a short break-in period. A long-term UK reviewer reported no rubbing out of the box. Opinions on cushioning split. Some testers praise the soft ride, while RunRepeat's lab characterized the platform as stable and grounded rather than bouncy, and another reviewer pointed to a thin feel underfoot. The 11 mm drop and 33 mm heel stack sit between those camps: protective, but not plush.

**Traction.** Contagrip lugs earn good marks on mixed trail, with CleverHiker rating traction excellent and Outdoor Gear Lab praising the multi-directional bite. The dense tread has limits. T3's tester found it reluctant to shed thick mud, and one long-term owner wanted more grip on slick surfaces.

**Waterproofing and breathability.** The GORE-TEX liner handles rain, wet grass and shallow creek crossings, and CleverHiker's testers stayed dry through slush. It is not infallible: those same testers noticed dampness creeping in at flex points after a few weeks of steep, wet climbing. Heat is the other cost. Owners on REI describe the boot as warm, and CleverHiker found the new version less breathable than the X Ultra 4 Mid. Better Trail, by contrast, rated its breathability above most waterproof mids it has worn.

**Fit.** The last runs long and slim. Outdoor Gear Lab says it favors narrow feet with limited toe-box room. REI buyers mention sizing down a half size, while others report the fit tightening as feet swell over a long day. CleverHiker notes the wide version helps, though sizing still runs small overall.

**Durability.** This is the open question. Outdoor Gear Lab docked the women's model heavily after the metal hook on the ankle cuff broke within the first mile of testing. Before that, the boot had been a repeat award winner in its lineup. CleverHiker said it holds up reasonably well for a lightweight synthetic, but not to leather standards. A UK walker reported the boots still in very good shape after a full winter of use.

## Choosing a Version

The listing covers three options: the standard boot, a US 12, and a 44 / Wide.

- **Standard (454 g):** Salomon's regular-width boot, listed at 16 oz per shoe. This is the one for hikers with average to narrow feet, and the fit that reviewers describe as long and slim.
- **US 12 (879 g):** The pair weight REI lists for the men's boot. It suits larger-footed hikers on the regular last, which is the fit Outdoor Gear Lab pegs as narrow.
- **44 / Wide (925 g):** The wide last, and the heaviest of the three listings. It is the pick for anyone whose forefoot feels pinched in the regular boot, or who expects swelling on long days. Sizing still runs small, so wide-footed hikers should try before committing to a size.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roughly 1 lb per boot, light for a waterproof mid
- Stable advancedChassis platform for the weight
- Quick break-in
- Reliable GORE-TEX protection in normal wet conditions
- Contagrip tread bites well on rock and mixed trail
- Wide version available

**Cons**

- Narrow, long-cut regular fit; sizing runs small
- Warm in summer heat
- Moisture reports at flex points after weeks of wet use
- Durability concerns, including the broken cuff hook in Outdoor Gear Lab's women's test
- Not stiff enough for heavy loads
- Dense lugs hold mud
- $190 list price

## Who Should Buy This

Day hikers, fast-and-light weekenders and thru-hikers who want ankle coverage and waterproofing without a leather boot's mass will find the most to like. It suits wet climates, rocky trails and loads of moderate weight. Hikers with wide feet should go straight to the wide version. Those carrying heavy packs, hiking in hot weather, or needing a boot that survives years of abuse should look at a stiffer leather option. Anyone who runs warm and prefers a breathable shoe is better off with a non-waterproof trail runner.

## Verdict

The X Ultra 5 Mid GORE-TEX delivers a real balance of weight, stability and wet-weather protection, and its performance on trail justifies the strong reviews it collects. Buy it for light loads in wet, rocky country and size carefully. Durability and heat are the compromises that keep it from the top of the category.
