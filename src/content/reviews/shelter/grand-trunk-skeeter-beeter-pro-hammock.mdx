## Overview

The Skeeter Beeter Pro is a single-person parachute-nylon hammock with a no-see-um net sewn on and a full-length double-sided zipper. It has been a default first hammock for buggy-season campers for more than a decade. The trade-off is plain: a big, durable, forgiving hang at an entry-level price, in a package that weighs 992 g and asks the owner to supply their own insulation and rain cover.

## Performance

**Comfort and fit.** The Pro's selling point is its size. At 10'6" long, it gives taller sleepers room to lie on a diagonal, and owners well over six feet report no complaints about length. One 6'4" Trailspace reviewer found it comfortable, and another owner who stands 6' had worried it would be too short and found it wasn't. A Trailspace reviewer suggests the standard Skeeter Beeter for anyone under 5'10", since the Pro's extra length is wasted on shorter hikers. Width is the one gripe: a Trailspace reviewer wanted a few more inches, and a Camp Saver buyer warns that this is not a double-wide hammock, despite what the bug-net marketing images might suggest.

**Bug protection.** This is what the hammock is for. Buyers in Florida, the Rockies and the alpine Sierra-side of Northern California describe the netting as doing its job against mosquitoes and biting flies. The zipper runs the full length, so entry is easy, and the net can be flipped underneath on cooler nights to run the hammock as a plain open hang. One owner who has taken it on more than 20 backpacking trips notes that the net, tucked below, can even help hold a sleeping pad in place.

The weak point is the net itself. It is attached with elastic cordage rather than held off the body by spreader bars, and some owners say it sags onto the face. Others have documented tears: one reviewer got about five years before the netting gave out, and a hammock-forum user modified a second Pro into a double-layer build after the first one's mesh ripped. A hammock sags toward bugs from below, too: mosquitoes bite through a single layer of nylon where a back or backside presses against it. Owners spray the underside with permethrin or repellent, or slide a pad in underneath.

**Insulation.** Nothing here is built for cold. The Pro has no underquilt channel or integrated pad sleeve, and anyone who has spent a cool summer night in a hammock knows that air circulating beneath the sleeper steals heat that a sleeping bag can't replace. Owners use a pad, although pads slip around in any hammock, and this one is no exception. Shoulder-season hangers will need a separate underquilt. Rain is the same story: there is no fly in the box.

**Suspension.** The included cord and carabiners get the hammock up in minutes. One owner clocked the bare hammock at about 15 seconds to hang. But the cord is the first thing experienced hangers replace. An owner with over 20 trips swapped it for 5 m of polyester webbing, noting that the 8 mm rope bites into bark and the 3 m lengths fall short on thicker trees. Webbing straps are the standard upgrade, and they spare the trunks.

**Weight and packing.** At 992 g, this is a car-camper's and weekender's weight, not a thru-hiker's. A Hammock Forums owner who otherwise likes the Pro says weight is the only criticism he has, and that the Pro runs roughly 8 oz heavier than Grand Trunk's UL version, in exchange for an extra foot of length and thicker fabric with a higher weight rating. Against a tent and footprint, though, the numbers look better: one owner reckons the Skeeter Beeter plus a fly cut nearly 2 lb from a pack. The stuff sack is mostly mesh; one owner who used a Pro on numerous 10-day Ontario trips replaced it after eight years because the mesh had started to fray, and wished for a sturdier sack.

**Durability.** The nylon body holds up well. Long-term owners describe years of use, with the net and stuff sack wearing out before the hammock body does.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Long, 5-ft-wide body suits taller sleepers
- 400 lb capacity gives plenty of margin
- Full-length zipper and attached no-see-um net work in heavy bug season
- Reversible: flip the net under for bug-free nights
- Complete kit, hangs in minutes
- Low entry price compared with dedicated bug-hammock systems

**Cons**

- 992 g is heavy for an ultralight kit
- Net is held up by elastic cord and sags; it is the part most likely to tear
- No integrated underquilt or pad pocket, and no rain fly included
- Included 8 mm cord is hard on trees and short for large trunks
- Mostly mesh stuff sack wears out
- Single-person only, not wide

## Who Should Buy This

The Skeeter Beeter Pro suits first-time hammock campers, tall hikers, and anyone camping in humid, buggy places in warm weather who wants a working system without a large outlay. It also suits weekend backpackers and kayak campers for whom 992 g doesn't matter. Gram-conscious thru-hikers and three-season campers in cold climates should look elsewhere: a lighter, bug-netted hammock, or a ground setup, makes more sense when every ounce and every degree counts. Shorter hikers can drop to the standard Skeeter Beeter.

## Verdict

The Skeeter Beeter Pro is not a light hammock, and it isn't trying to be one. It is a roomy, durable, bug-proof starter hang at a price that makes hammock camping an easy experiment. Buy it for warm-weather trips, budget for webbing straps and a pad or underquilt, and upgrade when the ounces start to matter.
