## Overview

The Darn Tough Merino Crew is a crew-height merino wool sock from the Vermont mill that thru-hikers have adopted as a default. It weighs 56.7 g (2 oz) per pair, and it's built around a dense fine-gauge knit and a fit that stays put. The trade-off is familiar: it costs more than a multipack of synthetics and dries more slowly, in return for durability that is rarely questioned and a guarantee that makes the price easier to accept.

## Performance

**Fit and blister control.** Fit is the reason most hikers buy Darn Tough, and the reason they keep buying it. The knit has enough stretch to follow the foot without sagging. Treeline Review's thru-hike testers, who logged tens of thousands of miles with dozens of hikers on the Appalachian Trail and Pacific Crest Trail, describe blisters as rare to non-existent. They also note that the socks hold their shape at the end of the day. Owners say the same in retail reviews: the socks don't creep down inside shoes or boots, and they don't bunch at the toe. The toe seam is fused flat, a small detail that matters on mile 20.

**Durability.** This is where the brand has earned its reputation. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers call Darn Tough durability unparalleled among hiking socks. Some of their pairs have lasted more than five years and 600 miles and stayed wearable. The one consistent complaint is cosmetic: pilling around the heel and midfoot after long use. The lifetime guarantee covers the rest. Treeline's testers say they have used the warranty and found it easy and effective. Thru-hikers can swap a holed pair for a new one, which over a long trail offsets the sticker price.

**Temperature and moisture.** Merino regulates temperature across a wide range. Treeline's testers wore Darn Tough socks from the teens to above 100°F. The wool blend resists odor, which counts for a lot on a trip with one pair on your feet and another drying on your pack. Wicking is good, but merino does not dry as fast as an all-synthetic sock. Hikers who push through creek crossings and all-day rain often carry a second pair to swap. Darn Tough's own Coolmax models exist for people who want the fastest drying.

**Height.** In Darn Tough's sizing, men's crew socks average about 10.5 in. from heel to cuff. They rise roughly 8 in. above a casual sneaker. That's more coverage than the brand's micro-crew hiking socks, which Outdoor Gear Lab notes sit lower. A crew works well with mid-height boots and in brush or tall grass. It looks long with low trail runners, and it can bunch under high-top boots. Owners also say a crew is more sock than they want on hot days, and that the cuff shows below shorter pants.

**Care.** Heat is the enemy. Outdoor Gear Lab reports that an accidental trip through a hot dryer shrinks, tightens and thickens a Darn Tough sock, which makes it less comfortable. Washing cold and inside out, then line drying or tumbling low, protects the fit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fit that resists slipping and bunching, with a flat-fused toe seam
- Durability that long-distance hikers and testers consistently rank at the top of the category
- Unconditional lifetime guarantee, with no receipt or time limit
- Merino's odor resistance and wide comfort range
- Made in Vermont
- Light at 56.7 g (2 oz) per pair

**Cons**

- Higher price than synthetic or cotton-blend multipacks
- Dries more slowly than all-synthetic socks
- Pilling at the heel and midfoot with heavy use
- Crew height is too tall for some low shoes and too short for some tall boots
- Shrinks and thickens if put through a hot dryer

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers and thru-hikers who want one sock they can wear for hundreds of miles, and who accept the per-pair cost in exchange for fewer replacements and a guarantee that works. It suits mid-height boots, trail runners worn with a longer sock, and cooler or variable weather. It also fits the hiker who wears the same socks on the trail and in camp.

Hikers who need the fastest possible drying, such as people on persistently wet routes who won't carry a spare pair, should look at a synthetic option like Darn Tough's own Coolmax models. Those who want a sock that disappears under low-cut running shoes are better served by a micro-crew or no-show.

## Verdict

The Darn Tough Merino Crew does the basics better than most socks: it fits well, avoids blisters, and lasts. The lifetime guarantee turns the high purchase price into a long-term bargain. It is the easy choice for most backpackers, and the exceptions are wet-weather purists and hikers who dislike crew height.
