## Overview

The Petzl Boreo is a hybrid climbing helmet: a thick ABS shell wrapped around a full EPP and EPS foam liner. It replaced the long-running Elios in Petzl's line and is aimed at climbers, mountaineers, cavers and canyoneers who want one helmet that tolerates being stuffed in a pack, dropped at a belay and hit by falling ice. The trade-off is plain: it weighs more and breathes less than foam-shell helmets such as the Sirocco and Meteor.

## Performance

**Protection and durability.** This is where the Boreo earns its reputation. UKClimbing's reviewer described a helmet that feels notably safe and well covered compared with either a plain shell or a polystyrene-only design. The same reviewer said it can be shoved into a pack and generally abused without worry. Blister's tester credited the coverage around the sides and back of the head, which is wider than on the old Elios, and noted that the Boreo passes Petzl's own side-impact testing on top of the CE and UIAA standards. Northeast Alpine Start's reviewer called it the most durable and protective climbing helmet tested to that point. A buyer on Bergfreunde reported that it shrugged off two solid hits from falling ice and snow.

**Comfort and fit.** Adjustment is by a plastic ratchet at the back of the head, and testers found it easy to dial in, including on the wall. Blister's reviewer found it more comfortable than expected and much better than the Elios. The padding is minimal, with thin foam only at the forehead and crown, yet the system hugged the head without pressure points. The soft headband folds into the shell for storage. One owner's caution: bulky layers under the helmet create pressure points, so thin beanies work best.

**Ventilation.** Large vents on the sides and back give decent airflow for a shell-style helmet. Blister rated it noticeably better than the Black Diamond Half Dome, and Northeast Alpine Start found it much better than the Elios. Even so, UKClimbing noted that cooler headwear exists, and Petzl's own Sirocco and Meteor have considerably more air space. One owner found it a touch warmer than an older Petzl. There is no adjustable venting. That makes the Boreo a better fit for cold-weather alpine days and cragging in moderate temperatures than for summer sport climbing in a heat wave.

**Weight.** At 300 g in S/M and 329 g in M/L, this is a mid-weight helmet. Foam helmets run well under 250 g, and thru-hikers carrying a helmet for a high route or a via ferrata section will feel the difference. Reviewers who chose the Boreo did so knowingly. The consensus is that the extra grams buy a helmet that survives years of pack abuse. One reviewer said a Petzl helmet was still in good shape after 18 years of use.

**Headlamps and accessories.** Two front clips and a rear elastic band hold a headlamp securely. One reviewer found a Petzl Actik attached quickly and stayed put. The Boreo is also compatible with Petzl's Vizion face shield for ice climbing.

## Choosing a Version

The Boreo comes in two sizes, S/M at 300 g and M/L at 329 g, together covering 53–61 cm. The 29 g gap is small, so choose by fit rather than weight. Reviewers' experiences show how much sizing varies: Blister's tester, usually a small in helmets, fit the S/M well. Another reviewer who is a medium in almost everything went with the M/L. A Bergfreunde buyer reported that the helmet suits smaller or oddly shaped heads. Anyone planning to wear a thin beanie or balaclava should try both sizes with that layer, since the ratchet has limited room to compensate.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Thick ABS shell tolerates pack abuse, drops and scuffs
- Full foam lining and wide head coverage at the sides and back
- Easy ratchet adjustment that works on the move
- Soft, low-profile headband with few pressure points
- Priced low: UKClimbing called it great value at £55 at launch
- Compatible with a face shield and secure headlamp clips

**Cons**

- Heavier than foam-shell helmets at 300–329 g
- Ventilation is good for a shell helmet but behind airier options
- No adjustable vents
- Thin padding leaves bulky hats prone to pressure points

## Who Should Buy This

The Boreo suits climbers who want a single, durable helmet for everything: cragging, alpine routes, winter mountaineering, caving, via ferrata and canyoning. It is a good first helmet and a good one to keep in a gear bin for years. Weight-obsessed hikers who carry a helmet only for a handful of scrambles, and anyone climbing mainly in hot conditions, should look at a foam-shell model like the Sirocco or Meteor.

## Verdict

The Boreo is a rugged, comfortable, well-covered helmet that earns its extra grams through durability and side protection. It isn't the lightest or coolest option, but few helmets at this price are as tough. Buy it for hard use. Skip it if every ounce counts.
