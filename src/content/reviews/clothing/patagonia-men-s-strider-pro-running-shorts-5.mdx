## Overview

The Strider Pro is Patagonia's flagship trail running short, built around a lightweight recycled polyester/spandex shell, a built-in brief liner, and a pocket layout that's become something of an industry reference point. It's marketed for running, but its 5-pocket "integrated running belt" design and durability have made it a favorite well beyond the road or trail-running crowd — thru-hikers in particular have adopted it as a do-everything short. If you're someone who wants to ditch a separate running belt or hydration waist pack for short outings, this is the short built around that idea.

## Performance

**Pockets and carry.** This is where the Strider Pro earns most of its reputation. One long-term tester who used the shorts across the PCT and a Japan Alps traverse called them <cite index="1-10,1-11,1-12,1-13">"truly incredible," probably what has made them a favorite for so long — not only five pockets, but huge ones, large enough for an oversized cell phone, a pair of wind pants, a wind jacket, or several granola bars each.</cite> The design essentially replaces a running vest or hip belt for shorter outings: <cite index="18-18,18-19,18-20,18-21">wearing them is like running with a running belt, except the belt is integrated into the short, so you can carry gels, purees, and energy bars</cite> without any extra gear. One reviewer did flag a minor gripe worth knowing about — <cite index="17-17,17-18">he wished the waist pockets had small tabs for easy opening, since he often found it difficult to locate the opening while running</cite>.

**Comfort and fit.** The brief liner is a standard flat-knit design rather than a compression liner, and that seems to be a genuine point of difference from competitors. A FeedTheHabit reviewer who normally prefers compression liners found that <cite index="12-7,12-8,12-9">instead of compression-style liners, the Strider Pro features a standard brief, and while he expected not to like it, the briefs offer excellent support and are the ultimate in chafe-free performance</cite>. Sizing runs true but snug at the top of the range — the same reviewer, a 33" waist with what he calls a "cyclist's backside," found <cite index="12-4,12-5">that his usual medium sits right on the upper edge of that size range, fitting well but without much width to spare</cite>. On the women's version, a Patagonia site reviewer echoed a similar fit note, saying she loved the shorts but <cite index="15-1,15-2,15-3">wished the leg opening was just a little wider for thicker thighs</cite>.

**Breathability and drying.** Multiple sources agree this is a fast-drying, breathable fabric. One tester found <cite index="12-19,12-20">that on steep climbs the shorts move with the body and disappear on all other terrain, with excellent breathability and quick drying</cite>. Another long-distance user confirmed the same after extended wet-weather use, noting simply <cite index="1-17,1-18">that the shorts dry quickly, having waded through plenty</cite> of water crossings.

**Durability.** This is a mixed bag. On the positive end, Treeline Review's long-term testing across a 3,000-mile CDT thru-hike and multiple ultra-distance runs found the shorts held up well — <cite index="11-1,11-2">soft and comfortable, with plenty of accessible pocket space, and also very durable, backed by Patagonia's lifetime warranty</cite>, adding that they <cite index="11-7">last thousands of miles, and if you do wear them out Patagonia will repair or replace them</cite>. But not every buyer has had that experience. One 7" version reviewer reported <cite index="17-4,17-8,17-9,17-10">stitching in the waistband starting to fall out after just a week, with three rows of stitching holding the elastic in — and when one row starts to go, it rips all the way out</cite>, adding that this <cite index="17-12,17-13">made him second-guess his opinion of the brand, even though everything else he'd bought from Patagonia had been rock solid</cite>. Worth noting too: Patagonia's warranty policy on these shorts appears to have gotten stingier — one buyer of four pairs was <cite index="17-16">disappointed that the Patagonia store no longer offered a lifetime durability replacement, only a discount</cite> on this specific model. If you're buying partly for the "buy it for life" reputation, don't assume a worn-out pair gets replaced for free anymore — call your local store to confirm current policy before you count on it.

**Odor control.** The HeiQ Mint liner treatment is a newer addition and I didn't find much long-term, independent data on how well it holds up wash after wash. One runner who deliberately delayed washing to reduce environmental impact reported <cite index="18-1,18-2,18-3">running in them several times before washing, around 12 washes total, and that they still looked good</cite> — encouraging for fabric longevity, though it doesn't tell us much specifically about odor performance over that same stretch. Treat any odor-control claims here as unverified until more wearers weigh in.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Five pockets (four stretch side pockets plus a zippered rear pocket) that genuinely replace a running belt for short-to-medium outings
- Non-compression brief liner praised for comfort and chafe resistance by testers who normally prefer compression styles
- Fast-drying, breathable shell that performs well in wet conditions and on technical terrain
- Long track record of thru-hikers logging thousands of miles in a single pair
- PFAS-free DWR and OceanCycle-certified recycled shell fabric

**Cons**

- At least one reported case of waistband stitching failure within a week — quality control doesn't sound perfectly consistent
- Sizing runs snug at the top of the range; consider sizing up if you're between sizes or have a larger frame
- Patagonia's lifetime durability replacement for this model reportedly has been scaled back to a discount rather than free replacement
- Pocket openings can be hard to locate by feel mid-run, according to at least one long-term user
- No adjustable/tabbed pocket zippers or pulls to make on-the-move access easier

## Who Should Buy This

This short is aimed at trail runners and ultra-distance runners who want to carry gels, a phone, and a light layer without a hip pack, but it's just as well suited to thru-hikers and fastpackers looking for a single short that can handle both hiking and running duty for weeks on end. If you run in humid conditions or do multi-day trips where laundry access is limited, the combination of quick drying and decent odor-control claims makes this a sensible pick. It's less of a fit for anyone who strongly prefers compression liners or who has had bad luck with Patagonia's stitching in the past and wants total peace of mind on durability.

## Verdict

The Strider Pro's pocket system and liner comfort are genuinely well thought out, and the fact that thru-hikers keep choosing it for multi-thousand-mile trips says a lot about how it holds up in the field. It's not flawless — occasional stitching issues and a less generous warranty than Patagonia's reputation suggests take a little shine off — but for most runners and hikers, this remains one of the best all-around trail shorts on the market. I'd rate it an 8.7/10: buy it for the pockets and liner comfort, but keep an eye on the seams.
