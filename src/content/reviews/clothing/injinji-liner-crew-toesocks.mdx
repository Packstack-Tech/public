## Overview

The Liner Crew is Injinji's thin merino-blend toe sock, built to sit against the skin under a regular hiking sock or to be worn alone on warm, easy trails. Each toe gets its own sleeve, which removes the skin-on-skin rubbing behind many between-the-toes blisters. The trade-off is durability: it is a 200-needle liner with no cushioning, and long-distance hikers report burning through pairs.

## Performance

**Blister prevention.** This is the sock's whole argument, and hikers who get blisters between their toes tend to find it a convincing one. One Backpacking Light reviewer started wearing the wool Liner Crew around 400 miles into an Appalachian Trail thru-hike, after toe blisters persisted even once shoe size was corrected. The combination that finally worked was the liner under a Darn Tough Micro Crew. The same hiker had tried Injinji's polyester Coolmax version of the Liner Crew earlier on the same trip and still blistered, and credited the wool for the difference in humid, wet Appalachian conditions.

**Moisture and comfort.** Outdoor Gear Lab's testers rated the Liner Crew line well for wicking, noting that because the fabric wraps each toe, it pulls moisture off the whole foot, including the spaces a conventional sock leaves damp. Their test sample was the polyester version, which carries the same fit and construction as the merino model. The merino blend adds wool's odor resistance, a real advantage on a multi-day trip when socks don't get washed. Injinji's own pitch is that the liner works either as a first layer or on its own as an ultralight option.

**Cushioning.** There is none. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers flagged this as the main drawback and suggested heavily loaded hikers look elsewhere or add a thicker outer sock. In the intended layering setup, the outer sock supplies the padding and the liner supplies the friction control.

**Durability.** Here the thinness shows. The Backpacking Light reviewer bought three more pairs during a thru-hike as heels thinned and holes appeared past the point of repair. Outdoor Gear Lab likewise said the low price offsets the lack of durability. Budget for replacing pairs on a long trek rather than expecting one set to last a season of heavy mileage.

**Fit.** Toe socks are fussier than standard socks, since toe length varies from foot to foot. The same Backpacking Light reviewer found a small fit well but still noticed an occasional hot spot on the pinky toe when the fabric twisted. Pulling each toe sleeve fully on, and trying the sock with the shoes it will be worn in, solves most of this. Getting them on takes an extra thirty seconds each morning, which is the price of admission.

**Layering.** Because the liner is so thin, it adds very little bulk inside a trail runner or low-volume shoe. Under a Darn Tough-style outer sock, the toe sleeves act as a friction-free barrier that the outer sock can slide over rather than rub against.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Individual toe sleeves remove toe-on-toe friction, the root of many blisters
- Merino-heavy blend (74%) resists odor better than polyester liners
- Thin enough to fit under an outer sock without crowding the shoe
- Wicks moisture from between the toes as well as across the foot
- Elastic arch band keeps the sock from bunching
- Crew height covers the ankle and lower calf

**Cons**

- No cushioning; not a standalone sock for heavy loads or rough ground
- Thin 200-needle knit wears through at the heel and toes on long hikes
- Toe sleeves take practice to put on and can twist in the shoe
- Five-toe fit may not suit every foot shape
- Hand-wash care is recommended on some retail listings, which is inconvenient on the trail

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers with a history of blisters between or on the toes, especially those in wet climates, will get the most from the Liner Crew worn under a standard hiking sock. It also suits warm-weather hikers who want a featherweight sock on its own for easy day hikes or dry trails. Anyone who wants cushioning in a single sock, or who wants one pair to last an entire thru-hike, should look at a midweight merino hiker such as Injinji's own Outdoor Midweight line instead. Hikers who have never had a blister problem can skip it.

## Verdict

The Liner Crew is a cheap, targeted fix for toe blisters, and the merino blend makes it a better choice than the polyester version for multi-day trips. It is not a durable standalone hiking sock, so plan to replace pairs and layer it under something with padding. For hikers who blister, it's worth carrying a spare pair.
