## Overview

The X Ultra 5 GORE-TEX is Salomon's fifth swing at its best-selling do-it-all hiking shoe, and it's built for hikers who want the low weight and agility of a trail runner without giving up the stability and foot protection of a boot. It's aimed at day hikers and light backpackers who move fast over mixed terrain — think scree, wet rock, and rooty singletrack — and want a shoe that stays connected to the ground rather than a marshmallow-soft cruiser.

## Performance

**Traction and stability.** This is where the X Ultra 5 earns its reputation. Testers running it through Alaska's Chugach Range and the Grand Canyon reported that the shoe <cite index="11-11">proved trustworthy over all kinds of terrain, from sticky, clay-like mud to wet boulders, slippery logs, and loose scree</cite>. Another reviewer described how the shoe <cite index="10-2,10-3">truly earned my trust from the first crunch of loose gravel underfoot, giving a planted, stable feel that turned sketchy ledges and loose descents into confident strides</cite>. Salomon's <cite index="11-12">in-house All-Terrain Contragrip outsole</cite> is a big part of that, and the updated <cite index="10-15">ADV-C Chassis improves lateral stability and midfoot support without adding weight or stiffness</cite>.

**Cushioning and ride feel.** Don't expect a plush ride. Multiple reviewers noted the midsole is on the firm side compared to trail-runner-style hikers. One tester put it plainly: the shoe is <cite index="14-2,14-3">thinner and firmer than trail runner-inspired designs, such as Merrell's Moab Speed or Hoka's Anacapa series, which offer softer landings and superior shock absorption</cite>. That trade-off isn't necessarily bad — the same reviewer found the <cite index="14-4">stiffer, lower-slung design provides a closer connection to the ground and excellent trail isolation</cite> — but if you're coming from a maximalist cushioned shoe, expect an adjustment period. Switchback Travel's tester was more critical here, finding <cite index="11-6">the underfoot cushion lacking for long days on tough trails</cite>.

**Fit and break-in.** Fit seems to be the most divisive aspect. Treeline Review found them <cite index="13-12">comfortable right out of the box with almost no break-in</cite>, and Better Trail's reviewer had <cite index="14-1">no hot spots or foot fatigue even after full days on the trail</cite> once broken in — though they admitted <cite index="14-19">the reinforced upper material also felt fairly rigid out of the box</cite>. On the flip side, Switchback Travel flagged fit as a weak point, noting <cite index="11-6">the fit tricky to nail down</cite>, and Better Trail specifically called out a <cite index="14-16">relatively narrow</cite> toe box. If you have wide feet, try before you buy — or grab the Wide width Salomon offers for men's sizing.

**Waterproofing and breathability.** The GORE-TEX PFC-free membrane does its job. One reviewer measured <cite index="1-5,1-6">about 5 inches of flood height, and during a 10-minute submersion test the shoe remained completely dry with no signs of water leakage</cite>. The upgraded Matryx upper is also credited with keeping things breathable — <cite index="1-7,1-8">enhancing breathability and keeping airflow unrestricted despite the addition of a GTX membrane</cite>. On the flip side, waterproof membranes always mean slower drying if water gets in over the collar; one tester found it <cite index="1-9">took about 10% longer to dry after submerging them in water vs the Merrell Moab 3</cite>.

**Durability.** This is a strong point across the board. Reviewers praised the <cite index="11-13,11-14">very durable feel, from the beefy toe cap to the hardwearing outsole, with Kevlar reinforcements to boost abrasion resistance in wear-prone areas</cite>. After a week-plus of hard use, one tester's pair was <cite index="11-15">holding up flawlessly with no scuffs, scrapes, or damage to any part of either shoe</cite>. The one recurring nitpick is the Quicklace system — functional, but reviewers worry about long-term reliability. Switchback Travel noted <cite index="11-16,11-17">the Quicklace's flimsy feel, with thin laces and a plastic ratcheting system that seems susceptible to breakage or failure over time</cite>, though they added <cite index="11-18">they've never had to repair or replace Quicklaces</cite> despite years of testing Salomon shoes. GearJunkie's tester similarly said the system <cite index="10-17,10-18">divides opinions, but found it fast, even, and reliable, tucking away neatly for a sleek profile</cite> — while flagging that <cite index="10-19">there's no backup if it fails</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent traction and stability on loose, wet, and technical terrain
- Genuinely durable build — reinforced toe cap, Kevlar-backed upper, tough Contagrip outsole
- GORE-TEX membrane is fully waterproof in real submersion testing
- Breathable for a waterproof shoe thanks to the Matryx upper
- Versatile enough for day hikes and light backpacking loads

**Cons**

- Firmer, thinner midsole than trail-runner-style competitors (Moab Speed, Anacapa) — less forgiving on long days
- Fit runs narrow through the toe box; sizing/fit feedback is mixed across reviewers
- Quicklace system has no backup if it fails, even though failures seem rare in practice
- Slower to dry than non-waterproof or less protected alternatives once water gets inside

## Who Should Buy This

The X Ultra 5 GTX makes the most sense for hikers who want one shoe that can handle everything from quick after-work loops to multi-day trips with a loaded pack, and who prioritize ground feel, stability, and durability over maximum cushioning. If you're logging big mileage on relatively smooth trails and want a plush, trail-runner-like ride, look at something like the Hoka Anacapa or Merrell Moab Speed instead. If your trails involve scree, wet rock, mud, and unpredictable footing, the X Ultra 5's firmer, more connected ride is an asset rather than a drawback.

## Verdict

The X Ultra 5 GTX delivers on Salomon's core promise: a tough, trustworthy, all-terrain hiking shoe that punches above its weight class on technical ground. It's not the plushest option at $170, and the narrow toe box and no-backup laces are worth trying before you buy, but for stability and durability it's near the top of its class. I'd rate it an 8/10 — a smart buy for hikers who value control and confidence over cushioned comfort.
