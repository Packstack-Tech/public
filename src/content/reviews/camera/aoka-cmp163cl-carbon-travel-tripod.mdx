## Overview

The AOKA CMP163CL is a three-section carbon fiber tripod that, with its KB20 ball head attached, weighs about 500 g. It sits in the gap between a tabletop tripod and a full-size travel model, and it is aimed at hikers who want a real support for a mirrorless body without carrying a kilogram of metal. The trade-off is plain: very little weight, and very little height.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** The numbers are the case for this tripod. Ridgeline Images, a Japan-based landscape photography site, went looking for a hiking tripod that exceeded one metre in height and weighed under a kilogram, and settled on the AOKA after finding few alternatives. The reviewer noted it was 286 g lighter than the Velbon it was compared against, a gap that registers on multi-day trips. Folded, the legs are slim enough to slip into a camera bag or a side pocket, which a DPReview forum poster called out as a contrast to most mini tripods, which tend to be bulky.

**Stability.** Owners describe the legs as stiffer than their 10 mm lowest sections would suggest. A DPReview contributor found the legs of the FLM CP10-A1 flexed noticeably more than the AOKA's at comparable length and angle. A Leica shooter reviewing travel tripods reported less vibration than from a Vanguard model, even with the extension rod fitted. A Z6 with a 24-70mm lens is the kind of load forum users describe it handling comfortably. Past that, the 2.5 kg rating is a hard ceiling in practice. A hunter on Rokslide who uses one for glassing while seated also said that a heavier optic setup in wind would likely send him looking for something sturdier.

**Height.** This is where the compromise lives. At 71 cm with the head, the tripod puts a camera at roughly knee height to a standing adult. That suits ground-level foregrounds, seated shooting, camp-table work, and low-angle landscapes. It does not suit eye-level composition. The included screw-in extension rod stretches the maximum to 129 cm and adds about 62 g, but reviewers who use it say it costs rigidity, and the Leica reviewer avoids it unless the shot demands it. Another photographer pointed out that at this size the tripod's advantage over propping the camera on a pack and a sweater is only a few inches of height, a fair criticism for anyone who rarely shoots low.

**Head and handling.** The KB20 is a compact ball head that supports both horizontal and vertical orientation, and its Arca-Swiss compatible plate means existing L-brackets and accessory plates carry over. The same DPReview thread called the head surprisingly strong for its size. Deployment is quick: three-way leg-angle adjustment and twist-locks on a three-section design mean few collars to loosen. Ridgeline Images noted how speedily it sets up.

**Value.** Retail listings range from roughly $89 to $125 in the US, about €114 in Europe and £112 in the UK, depending on bundle and seller. That is low for carbon fiber with a head included.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About 500 g with head, with a slim folded profile
- Carbon legs with CNC aluminium joints that owners describe as rigid for the size
- Arca-Swiss compatible quick-release plate
- Quick setup with three sections and twist-locks
- Reasonable price with ball head included

**Cons**

- 71 cm working height means crouching or sitting for most shots
- Extension rod reaches 129 cm but costs stability
- 2.5 kg capacity rules out heavy telephoto and spotting-scope setups
- Narrow 10 mm bottom sections limit performance in wind
- Ball head is small and best matched to light mirrorless or compact kits

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the ultralight hiker or thru-hiker who carries a mirrorless camera with a compact lens and wants a support for dawn, dusk, night skies, and long exposures, and who is willing to shoot low. It also suits travel photographers who want a tripod that disappears in a bag. Photographers with a full-frame body and a big telephoto, anyone who wants to shoot at eye level without an extension, and hunters running a large spotting scope in exposed wind should look at a taller, heavier tripod with fatter legs.

## Verdict

The CMP163CL delivers on its main claim: a carbon tripod and ball head at about 500 g that folds small and holds a light mirrorless kit steady. The limited height is the cost, and buyers need to accept it going in. For weight-focused hikers with light cameras, it is the one to beat at this price.
