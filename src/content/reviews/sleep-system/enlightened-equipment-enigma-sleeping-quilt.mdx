## Overview

The Enigma is Enlightened Equipment's stripped-down quilt: no zipper, no hood, and a footbox sewn permanently shut. At 601 g in the 20°F version with 950-fill goose down, it is built for thru-hikers and weight-focused three-season backpackers who want warm feet and a small pack volume. The trade-off is that it only works well for people willing to learn how to seal a quilt around a sleeping pad.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** The Enigma exists to be light. CleverHiker's testers put the regular-length 20°F model with 850-fill duck at 1 lb 5.6 oz, which they ranked among the lightest true three-season options available. They also noted that the 950-fill goose upgrade squeezes smaller still. Even the duck version packed to roughly 13 x 6 inches. Dropping the zipper and hood removes bulk as well as grams, and a long-term owner who logged more than 2,000 km describes a quilt that compresses to about the size of a netball.

**Warmth.** A sewn-closed footbox eliminates the leak point where a zippered or cinched footbox would sit, and the design keeps feet warm. That is the reason this quilt exists. The Enigma's wide cut and pad-strap system seal in warmth better than most quilts CleverHiker has tried. The same testers add that it takes practice to use well. Backpackers who sleep restlessly or choose a narrow pad will find the gaps. The optional snap-and-drawcord draft collar closes the neck, which is where most quilts shed heat on a cold night.

**Construction.** Continuous U-shaped baffles let a sleeper shift down toward the cold zones and keep it from migrating to the sides. The long-term owner reports no cold spots from this layout. The down is treated to shed moisture, and the DWR shell dries fast after a damp night. Enlightened Equipment builds each quilt by hand in Minnesota, and reviewers at The Trek called the design carefully thought out and trail-tested.

**Fit and options.** Few quilts are as configurable. Buyers pick fill power, temperature rating, length, width, shell denier and colors. Wide cuts add weight. In one Backwoods Pursuit comparison, a regular/wide 30°F quilt with a draft collar weighed 16.77 oz, while a regular/regular without the collar came in at 15.43 oz. A 7D shell saves more weight but is more delicate than the 10D fabric that most owners choose.

**Ventilation.** This is the Enigma's main weakness. Without a zipper, it cannot open flat to become a blanket on warm nights. Hikers can still vent by lifting the top edge or sleeping with the quilt loosely draped. Those who often sleep in summer heat and cold alpine camps in the same trip may prefer Enlightened Equipment's own Revelation, which has a zippered, cinchable footbox.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 601 g at 20°F, among the lightest in its class
- Sewn-closed footbox for warm feet and no zipper to snag or fail
- 950-fill goose down packs smaller than duck alternatives
- Continuous baffles with no reported cold spots over thousands of kilometers
- DWR shell and treated down shrug off condensation
- Deep configurability across length, width and shell fabric
- Hand-built in the U.S.

**Cons**

- Cannot be opened flat for hot-night ventilation
- Pad straps and drafts demand practice, especially for restless sleepers
- Wider cuts and the draft collar add weight
- 7D shells trade durability for grams
- Premium pricing for 950-fill goose; the 850-fill duck version tested by CleverHiker listed at $320

## Who Should Buy This

The Enigma suits thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who sleep on a pad in three-season conditions and care about pack volume and base weight. It is best for anyone whose feet run cold, and for hikers willing to dial in strap tension before the first big trip. Those who want a bag-like feel, like to vent through a zipper, or share a bed with a quilt as a blanket should look at the Revelation or a conventional mummy bag.

## Verdict

The Enigma is a simple, well-built quilt that rewards hikers who understand how quilts work. At 601 g with 950-fill goose down, it carries a 20°F rating with room to spare in a small pack. Buy it if the lack of a zipper is a feature, not a flaw.
