## Overview

The Sender Boxer Briefs are Patagonia's answer to the question of what a hiker should wear under shorts for 20 miles a day: a thin nylon-spandex micromesh brief with a 6-inch inseam, flatlock seams and no extras. They're built for high-output days and travel. The trade-off is a plain, formfitting design with a pouch instead of a fly, and no thermal benefit when temperatures drop.

## Performance

**Chafe control.** The main complaint about hiking underwear is friction, and this is where the Sender earns its reputation. The combination of flatlock seams, a slick micromesh and a leg opening that stays put keeps the fabric from bunching at the thigh. The iRunFar team of trail runners and ultrarunners called the Sender their favorite of all the underwear they've tested. They reported no chafing, even on long, hot summer adventure runs.

**Moisture and drying.** The stretch micromesh is a wicking nylon blend that feels airy against the skin and dries fast. That matters on a thru-hike, where underwear gets rinsed in a creek or sink and has to be dry by the next morning. Backcountry.com owners who wear them on the trail describe them as very breathable and wicking. iRunFar's testers found them comfortable in moderate and hot temperatures, which matches the cool-wearing claim.

**Odor.** Patagonia treats the fabric with HeiQ odor control, a silver- and salt-based additive. That's the usual fix for synthetic underwear that gets ripe after day three. iRunFar's testers said they could vouch for the odor protection after long weeks of use. Synthetics still aren't wool, and anyone planning a week-long push without a rinse should expect some funk. The treatment just delays it.

**Fit and support.** The cut is formfitting, with ergonomic patterning and a contoured, no-fly pouch. Owners say the pouch holds everything in place. One long-term owner who bought a second pair after a year of use said they run true to size. Another, between a medium and a large on the sizing chart, found the large fit well. The anti-roll waistband stays flat under a hip belt and doesn't pinch. The 6-inch inseam is long enough to prevent thigh rub under running shorts or a pair of Baggies. It also disappears under a pair of 5-inch hiking shorts.

**Weight and durability.** At 77 g, a pair is light enough that carrying two (one to wear, one to swap) adds very little. They are made in Sri Lanka and sewn under Fair Trade Certification. Owners report wearing them daily for a year or more and buying replacements because they like them, not because they've fallen apart. A construction worker who bought the Sender in every available color reported wearing them daily. Patagonia's lifetime guarantee covers defects.

**What's missing.** There's no fly, which iRunFar acknowledges some men prefer, though its testers didn't find it a problem. The color palette is limited to a few muted options with thin stripes on the waistband. They look like nothing in particular, which suits camp life.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Flatlock seams and slick mesh keep chafe at bay on long, sweaty days
- Quick-drying fabric suits camp-sink washing
- Odor treatment holds up through multi-day wear
- 77 g keeps the weight penalty of a spare pair negligible
- Anti-roll waistband and stay-put legs don't ride up
- Recycled nylon, bluesign-approved fabric and Fair Trade Certified sewing

**Cons**

- No fly, so a trip to the bushes takes some maneuvering
- Synthetic fabric won't match merino for multi-week odor resistance
- Formfitting cut won't suit anyone who wants a looser boxer feel
- Muted color range
- Priced above ordinary department-store athletic underwear

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers, trail runners and travelers who want one dependable pair of underwear that handles heat, sweat and repeated sink washing. Thru-hikers who rotate two pairs will find the weight and drying time hard to beat. It's a good fit for anyone who has been bitten by seam chafe and wants a simple fix. Hikers who prefer a fly, a looser cut or the temperature-regulating properties of merino for cold-weather trips should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Sender Boxer Briefs do the one job that matters, which is keeping the hiker comfortable and unchafed through a long day, and they do it at 77 g. They are not exciting, but few trail purchases are as quietly satisfying. Buy two pairs.
