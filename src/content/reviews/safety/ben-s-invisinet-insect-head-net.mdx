## Overview

<cite index="8-1,8-2">
  The Ben's InvisiNet is a drop-front head net with a drawcord closure, elastic
  crown, and high-visibility mesh — and it's earned a recommendation as a
  high-performance head net, all for less than an ounce.
</cite>
Its defining trick is a 20-denier fiber knit that's genuinely close to invisible
in use, making it a strong option for hikers who can't stand the dark, gauzy
screen that most traditional head nets put between them and the scenery. At 20g
and a ~$15.99 retail price, it's one of the most cost-effective pieces of
bug-season gear you can throw in a pack.

## Performance

### Visibility

This is where the InvisiNet stands apart. <cite index="7-9">The 20 Denier fiber knit, 14 gsm fabric with its rhombus pore shape and 1.0mm pore size is described as "nearly invisible."</cite> That's not just marketing copy — <cite index="13-21,13-31">users consistently report that vision is not hindered in the slightest after a slight adjustment period, and that the netting is well made and very easy to see through.</cite> If you've ever tried to navigate trail in a traditional dark hex-mesh net, you'll appreciate what that actually means on a long descent.

It's worth being honest about the trade-off, though: <cite index="24-3,24-4">Ben's own UltraNet head net uses pores only 0.05mm wide, keeping out even no-see-ums, midges, and black flies.</cite> The InvisiNet's 1.0mm pores are what give it that clear view, but if your bug problem is primarily no-see-ums, the InvisiNet may not be the right tool. For mosquitoes, gnats, and black flies, it gets the job done.

### Fit and Compatibility

<cite index="3-13,3-14">
  The elasticized crown is designed to keep the net in place no matter your
  movement or wind speed, and the extra room means you can easily fit it over a
  hiking, gardening, or fishing hat.
</cite>
In practice, though, hat compatibility is where opinions start to diverge.

With a ball cap, the InvisiNet shines. <cite index="8-6">Compared to the Sea to Summit Mosquito Headnet, the InvisiNet secures around the tops of caps — such as baseball caps and Tilley hats — more effectively.</cite> The elastic crown cinches over the bill and stays put even in a stiff breeze.

With a wide-brimmed hat, it's a different story. <cite index="1-1">The elastic crown bunches up on top of wide-brimmed hats, reducing neck and upper chest coverage.</cite> <cite index="1-15,1-16,1-17">The elastic band wraps around the round part of your head when you wear a billed cap and is designed to keep the net centered — but many users find it uncomfortable because it squeezes the head, and the pressure increases with larger hat sizes.</cite>

Going completely hatless is possible, but comes with a specific caveat: <cite index="8-8,8-9">it's best used with a brimmed hat to keep the mesh away from your ears, because wearing the net over a bare head allows the mesh to contact the ears — and while mosquitoes can't fit their bodies through the net, they can bite through the pores at contact points.</cite> A ball cap solves this entirely.

### Drop-Neck Design and Ventilation

<cite index="7-10">
  The drop-neck design covers unzipped shirts and jackets to allow maximum
  ventilation in hot, buggy conditions.
</cite>
This is genuinely useful — being able to leave your shirt collar open while
still having neck coverage is a real comfort advantage in warm-weather bug
season.
<cite index="2-3">
  The neck area also has an elastic drawstring to keep it tight
</cite>
, sealing out insects even when you're moving around.

### Protection Scope

<cite index="1-9">
  The InvisiNet repels mosquitoes, ticks, ants, flies, chiggers, and midges
  (no-see-ums)
</cite>
when combined with a repellent. As a physical barrier alone — without permethrin
treatment — it stops anything that can't squeeze through a 1.0mm pore. For a
permethrin-boosted option, Ben's sells the InvisiNet XTRA with Insect Shield,
<cite index="24-7">
  which adds permethrin technology that both repels and kills bugs on contact.
</cite>
That version runs about $5 more.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Near-invisible 1.0mm rhombus mesh is genuinely easier to see through than any traditional head net
- 20g is featherweight — you won't notice it in your pack or on your head
- Elasticized crown holds securely on ball caps through wind and movement
- Drop-neck design enables shirt ventilation without sacrificing neck coverage
- Drawcord neck closure seals tightly
- Stuff sack included; packs down to almost nothing
- Hard to beat at ~$15.99

**Cons**

- Struggles with wide-brimmed hats: the elastic crown bunches up and reduces coverage
- Elastic band can squeeze uncomfortably on larger head sizes
- 1.0mm pores do not block no-see-ums — step up to the UltraNet for that
- Mesh-to-skin contact at ears or neck allows bites through — a hat is basically mandatory
- No permethrin treatment on the base model (the InvisiNet XTRA adds that for $5 more)

## Who Should Buy This

The InvisiNet is the right call for ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers who spend bug season in mosquito and black-fly country and primarily wear a ball cap on trail. It's also a solid emergency-weight addition to any pack — 20g is cheap insurance for a blueberry-bog section of trail you weren't expecting. If your headwear is a wide-brimmed sun hat, skip this one and look at the Sea to Summit Mosquito Head Net instead. If no-see-ums are your primary nemesis, step up to Ben's UltraNet or a similarly fine-mesh option.

## Verdict

The Ben's InvisiNet gets one thing exactly right that almost every other head net gets wrong: visibility. At 20g and $15.99, it's a nearly effortless piece of kit for anyone whose bug season is mosquito-dominated and whose hat has a bill. The wide-brim hat limitation is real and worth knowing before you buy, but for its intended use case, it's a well-engineered, honest-value piece of gear. **Rating: 8/10.**
