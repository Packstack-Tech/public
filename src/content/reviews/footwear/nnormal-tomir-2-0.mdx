## Overview

The Tomir 2.0 is Nnormal's all-mountain trail shoe. It keeps the original's silhouette but swaps the firm EVA midsole for EExpure supercritical foam, pairs it with a Vibram MegaGrip Litebase outsole, and adds a wider platform. At 307 g it's no featherweight, and its slim last rules out a lot of feet. The payoff is a shoe that owners describe as nearly indestructible, with grip that holds up on wet rock and loose descents.

## Performance

**Cushioning and ride.** The headline change from the first Tomir is the midsole. The original felt firm underfoot. The EExpure foam is lighter, softer and more responsive, and the stack grew by 2 mm to the current 33/25 mm. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers called the Nnormal midsole springy yet firm and trusted it from road to technical ridgeline. Believe in the Run's reviewer described a light squish over a mostly stable base, and expected the foam to last many miles. It isn't a bouncy, race-day foam. One reviewer at Meta Endurance found it precise and dependable despite the stack, but short on dynamism. Another reviewer, running on lumpy English trails, found the shoe clunky at first and grew to like its bounce once it broke in.

**Traction.** This is where the Tomir earns its reputation. The Vibram MegaGrip Litebase outsole carries 33 lugs, each 5 mm deep, with varied shapes and small nodules on the lug sides for micro-traction. Outdoor Gear Lab rated the grip excellent in dicey conditions. A Meta Endurance tester who found Nnormal's Kjerag sketchy when damp reported no fear of slipping in the Tomir. Reviews of the shoe's traction are consistently positive, whatever the surface: mud, rock or road.

**Durability.** Owners return to this point more than any other. Reddit runners report 800 km and more on a pair with little sign of slowing, and one reaching roughly 1,000 km. Another described the Tomir as strong enough to survive a nuclear blast. A r/Ultralight user wears the Tomir as a daily trail runner, then demotes it to backpacking duty once the foam is spent. That second life suits thru-hikers well. The TPE-heavy upper and nylon monofilament weave resist abrasion, and the lower stitching was reworked for strength. Even so, one reviewer worried about the midsole-to-upper seam and found no wear on it.

**Fit.** The fit is the sticking point. The midsole was widened by 4 and 7 mm on either side of the arch in response to feedback on the first Tomir, but the toe box is still narrow. Live For The Outdoors' tester, a woman with a wide forefoot, got rubbing at the inside of the big toe and ruled the shoe out for ultra distances. Outdoor Gear Lab found the midfoot too loose in testing, letting feet slide forward on descents. A reviewer who logged 400 km including part of the Grizzly 100 found the front too tight on very long days, partly because the size run jumps from 47 to 48.5. Others like it: one Reddit owner with a narrow foot said the stretchy material gives just enough, and a reviewer at Believe in the Run found it true to size, perhaps a touch long. The asymmetrical lacing helps lock the foot down, but runners with wide or swelling feet should try a half size up.

**Breathability and protection.** The weave is tight. It dries fast, but a Believe in the Run tester found it warm on a hot day. Toe and foot protection is a consistent plus. Another reviewer noted that a stubbed toe on a rock will still be felt, and the forefoot fold can feel rough for forefoot strikers.

**Weight.** At 307 g the Tomir sits well above the ultralight trail-runner norm. Live For The Outdoors called it relatively heavy for a trail shoe, and reviewers say it takes a while to break in. Reddit sentiment bears out the trade: the shoe is slightly heavier and cushier than comparable models, yet responsive.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional durability, with owners reporting 800–1,000 km
- Vibram MegaGrip outsole with 5 mm lugs grips well in wet, loose and technical terrain
- EExpure midsole is more cushioned and responsive than the original's EVA
- Protective upper shields toes and feet on rocky trail
- Worn-out pairs can be retired to camp or backpacking duty

**Cons**

- Narrow toe box and slim fit, which rules out many wide feet
- 307 g is heavy for a trail runner, and some testers report a break-in period
- Tight upper weave runs warm in hot weather
- Midfoot can feel loose on steep descents for some testers
- Not a fast or lively shoe

## Who Should Buy This

The Tomir 2.0 suits hikers and runners with average-to-narrow feet who put rough terrain ahead of low weight: scrambly ridgelines, wet rock, loose descents and long mileage on a high stack. It also suits thru-hikers who want a trail runner that will outlast two or three pairs of softer-soled competitors. Runners with wide forefeet should look at foot-shaped alternatives, and gram-counters or racers who want a lively, fast shoe should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Tomir 2.0 is a heavy, tough, grippy shoe that makes up for its weight with longevity and sure footing on bad terrain. If it fits, owners rate it among the most durable trail shoes they have worn. If the toe box pinches, nothing else about it will make up for that.
