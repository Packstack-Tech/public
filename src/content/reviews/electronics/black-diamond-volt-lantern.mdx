## Overview

The Black Diamond Volt is a flat, pocketable 250-lumen lantern that doubles as a small flashlight and a USB power bank. It runs on a built-in 2,600 mAh lithium-ion cell or, when that runs dry, three AA batteries. It suits car campers, basecamp users and weekend backpackers who want one device to light a tent and top up a phone. For gram-counters, 224 g with batteries is a lot of weight for a light that a headlamp can mostly replace.

## Performance

**Light.** Black Diamond calls the Volt the big brother of its Zip lantern, and the output backs that up. At 250 lumens it is enough to light a room or the inside of a tent. One owner describes it as ideal for a three- or four-person dome. A buyer who planned it for backpacking described the light as bright but not the brightest available. Owners who bought it for camp use and emergency kits generally say it does that job. The diffused, glare-free beam is the point of a lantern: it fills a space rather than throwing a spot, so it is easy on the eyes at a dinner table or inside a shelter. The dimming mode stretches runtime and keeps the tent from turning into a lighthouse.

**Power.** The dual-power design is the Volt's main selling point. The built-in battery handles routine use. The AA tray offers a fallback on longer trips, when a charge isn't available. Black Diamond rates burn time at up to 24 hours. The USB-out port can top up a phone or other small device, and owners cite this as the feature that justifies carrying it. The 2,600 mAh cell is a modest reservoir, so it is a top-up device rather than a multi-day phone solution.

**Reliability and the AA tray.** The weak spot is the AA compartment. One REI reviewer reported that the Volt burned through AAs quickly and that the hard plastic latch on the battery door was already cracking after a single trip. Another said the lantern would not recognize AAs at all while the internal battery worked normally. Those are minority reports in a pool of generally favorable ratings, which run around 4.6 to 4.8 out of 5 at Backcountry and Walmart but only 3.4 across seven reviews on REI. Still, the pattern suggests treating the internal battery as the primary power source and the AA fallback as a convenience. Another owner said the Volt could not charge a phone the way other Black Diamond models can, which conflicts with the product's listed charge-out port. Anyone buying it chiefly as a power bank should test that feature early, while a return is still possible.

**Weather and handling.** IPX4 means it shrugs off splashes and rain, but it is not built for submersion. The digital lock prevents the common pack-bottom disaster of a lantern switching itself on and draining overnight. The thin, flat body slides into a pack pocket more easily than most cylindrical lanterns, and the fold-out hook allows hanging from a tent loop or branch. When laid flat, it stands up on its own on a table or the ground.

**Weight.** Here the Volt struggles with ultralight standards. At 152 g without batteries, it weighs more than the dedicated lighting many thru-hikers carry for an entire trip. With AAs, it reaches 224 g. Running only on the internal cell avoids that extra weight, but even then, the Volt is a convenience carried on top of a headlamp rather than instead of one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 250 lumens of diffused light, enough to fill a tent or a camp table
- Built-in battery plus AA backup gives two power options
- USB charge-in and charge-out turns it into a small power bank
- Digital lock prevents accidental activation in a pack
- Flat profile packs easily; hangs or stands
- IPX4 weather resistance and a 3-year warranty

**Cons**

- 224 g with AAs is heavy by ultralight standards
- Some owners report problems with the AA compartment, including a cracking latch and poor AA recognition
- Reports of fast AA drain
- 2,600 mAh is a small power bank
- AAs are not included
- Some retailers have closed out the model

## Who Should Buy This

The Volt fits weekend backpackers, basecamp campers, kayak and raft trippers (it has IPX4 protection and a pack-friendly shape), and anyone who wants a single device for tent light, a backup flashlight and phone top-ups. It is also a sensible addition to an emergency kit or car trunk, where weight is irrelevant and the lock mode and AA fallback matter. Thru-hikers and sub-10-pound base weight packers should look elsewhere: a headlamp with a diffuser, or a lighter inflatable lantern, covers the same camp duties for far fewer grams.

## Verdict

The Black Diamond Volt does what the catalog description promises: a bright, glare-free light with the bonus of USB charging. The weight and the AA-tray complaints keep it from being a standout. Buy it for car camping or relaxed weekend trips; skip it if every gram counts.
