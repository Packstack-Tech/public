## Overview

Cocoon's Eco Travel Towel swaps the usual polyester-nylon microfiber for 100% lyocell, a cellulose fiber made from sustainably harvested eucalyptus, and ships in a recycled-nylon stuff sack. It's aimed at travelers and hikers who want a towel that feels like cloth instead of a wrung-out chamois, and who will pay a few extra grams for it. The trade-off is weight: it is a gentler towel, not a lighter one.

## Performance

**Feel and absorbency.** The pitch is skin comfort. Cocoon describes the fabric as skin-friendly and super-soft, and retailers consistently position the towel as highly absorbent and quick drying. Customers on European outdoor retailer Bergfreunde and its sister sites overwhelmingly say they would recommend it to a friend, and the two phrases that recur in their summaries are "compact" and "lightweight." In practice, that means a towel that behaves more like a small cotton hand towel than a slick synthetic sheet, which is the point of buying lyocell in the first place.

**Weight and pack size.** Lyocell is not a weight-saver. Run the numbers from Cocoon's listed specs and the towel comes out around 30 to 36 g per 1,000 square centimeters across the range, with the small sizes at the heavy end. The Large (120 x 60 cm) weighs 225 g by Cocoon's spec sheet, and some retailers list it at 240 g. That is a meaningful carry for an ultralight kit, where a 60 x 30 cm square of synthetic cloth can do the same job for a fraction of the mass. Cocoon's own synthetic Ultralight line, at the same dimensions, packs smaller and weighs a little less, though it absorbs less water.

**Packability.** The Large compresses to 18 x 8.5 cm in its sack, roughly the size of a large grapefruit cut in half. The small sizes shrink to 10 x 5.5 cm (S) and 15 x 6.5 cm (M), and the XL needs 21 x 9.5 cm. The sack doubles as a wet bag, which helps when the towel goes back in damp.

**Care and durability.** Cocoon and its retailers carry the same warning: the towel can warp in the wash, so it needs to be pulled back into shape while wet or dry. Machine wash at 40°C on a gentle cycle, skip the dryer, and wash colors separately. Expect to hand-reshape it after each laundering.

**End of life.** Cocoon says the towel is 100% biodegradable, and the lyocell is produced in a closed-loop process that the company says saves water and minimizes chemical use. For hikers who care about what ends up in a landfill, it's one of the few travel towels that makes the claim credibly, since the fiber is plant-based rather than petroleum-based.

## Choosing a Version

Four sizes are sold, and the weight penalty grows with the area.

- **Small (60 x 30 cm, 65 g):** A face-and-hands towel. It fits the ultralight case best, but it will not dry a whole body. Suited to a trail wash-up or a dunk in a lake before changing.
- **Medium (90 x 50 cm, 145 g):** Cocoon's own retail copy frames this as the pick when weight is a factor, and it is large enough for a quick full-body dry-off. The best fit for most backpackers.
- **Large (120 x 60 cm, 225 g):** Big enough to wrap around the waist and still compact at 18 x 8.5 cm. A good choice for hostel-and-trail itineraries, though heavy for a pure thru-hike.
- **XL (150 x 80 cm, 365 g):** A home-style bath sheet at the weight of two full-size cans of soup. Reserve it for beach days, van trips or camp-based travel where no one is counting grams.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft, cloth-like lyocell rather than synthetic microfiber
- Biodegradable, with a plant-derived fiber and a recycled-nylon stuff sack
- Packs small for its area, with a sack that works as a wet bag
- Four sizes, from face towel to bath sheet
- Strongly positive customer feedback on compactness and weight at retailers

**Cons**

- Heavier per area than synthetic ultralight towels
- Can warp in the wash and needs reshaping
- Not tumble-dryable
- Made in China, with no fair-trade or origin story to match the eco branding
- Larger sizes quickly outgrow the ultralight brief

## Who Should Buy This

Travelers, section hikers and gym-bag packers who want a soft towel with a lower environmental footprint, and who will use it daily. The Medium suits most of them. Gram-counting thru-hikers, who typically carry a bandana or a 60 x 30 cm synthetic cloth, should look at a lighter microfiber option instead.

## Verdict

The Eco Travel Towel is a pleasant, well-made towel for people who prefer the feel and the end-of-life story of lyocell over synthetic microfiber. It is not a base-weight tool, and its warping habit adds a small laundry chore. Buy the Medium for travel and camping; skip it for long-distance ultralight kits.
