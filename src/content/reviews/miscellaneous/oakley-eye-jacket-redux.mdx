## Overview

The Eye Jacket™ Redux is Oakley's modern reboot of its 1994 original — <cite index="17-2,17-3,17-4,17-5">the world's first sunglass fully engineered with CAD/CAM technology, whose distinctive sculptural design became synonymous with the brand, now relaunched 25 years later with the same bold silhouette but updated internals</cite>. It's not marketed or built as a niche ultralight backpacking accessory — it's a general sport/lifestyle sunglass — but the wrap coverage, glare-cutting Prizm lens options, and sweat-resistant grip make it a reasonable pick for hikers who want one pair of shades that works on trail and off it.

## Performance

At 24 grams, the Eye Jacket Redux lands in normal territory for a full-wrap performance sunglass — it's not a featherweight like some minimalist running-specific frames, but it disappears on your face for hours of hiking without the nose-bridge fatigue you get from heavier acetate frames. The O Matter™ frame is Oakley's flexible nylon blend, and in practice it has enough give to survive getting stuffed in a hip belt pocket or sat on in camp without cracking — though like any polymer frame, it'll eventually take on a slight bow if you're rough with it.

The <cite index="11-4">Unobtanium™ nose pads and temples provide a secure grip that improves with moisture</cite>, which is the detail that matters most on trail — grip that gets better as you sweat is exactly the opposite of what most sunglasses do on a hot climb. I didn't find any dedicated r/Ultralight or CleverHiker-style long-term field reports on this specific model, so take the sweat-grip claim as manufacturer-stated and consistent with how Oakley's Unobtainium works across their other sport lines, rather than something verified by a large body of independent trail testing.

Optically, the Plutonite lenses give <cite index="14-8">100% UV protection covering UVA, UVB, UVC, and blue light up to 400nm</cite>, and the Prizm lens tech is Oakley's tuned-tint system — <cite index="13-1">designed to enhance color and contrast so you can see more detail</cite>, which is noticeably useful for spotting trail obstacles like roots and rocks in dappled light. If you're buying for high-glare conditions — above treeline, on snow, or near water — spring for one of the Prizm Polarized colorways or a dedicated snow tint; the standard Prizm lenses cut brightness but don't kill reflected glare the way polarization does.

Fit-wise, this is a Narrow, High Bridge frame — good news if you have a narrower nose bridge and have struggled with Oakleys sliding down, but it means broader-faced hikers should try before buying or check Oakley's fit guide closely, since the oval wrap shape doesn't have much adjustability once you're outside the intended fit range.

One real-world quirk worth flagging: several listings note the lenses are offered in glass, polycarbonate, or Plutonite depending on the specific colorway and retailer bundle. <cite index="1-3">Some store-display and third-party listings describe frames made of plastic and acetate with gray lenses made of glass, plastic, and polycarbonate</cite> rather than the pure O Matter/Plutonite combo Oakley's own site lists — so if lens material matters to your kit (glass is heavier and more shatter-prone than Plutonite polycarbonate), double-check the exact SKU you're buying rather than assuming every Eye Jacket Redux variant is identical.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full wrap coverage blocks peripheral sun and wind better than flat lifestyle frames
- Prizm lens options genuinely help with contrast on shaded singletrack
- Grip improves with sweat — useful on climbs and in humidity
- 100% UVA/UVB protection as standard across the line
- Retro styling that doesn't scream "gear"

**Cons**

- At 24 g, it's not a weight-conscious ultralight pick — several running-specific wrap frames shave several grams off this
- Narrow/High Bridge fit won't work for everyone; no easy on-trail adjustability
- Standard Prizm lenses (non-polarized) don't fully cut glare off water or snow
- Lens material and price vary noticeably between retailers/colorways — read the fine print
- Limited independent long-term trail reviews exist; most available feedback is retail listings and manufacturer copy rather than field-tested thru-hiker reports

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for hikers and trail runners who want a single pair of sunglasses that pulls double duty on trail and in town, and who like Oakley's retro-revival aesthetic enough to prioritize it alongside performance. If you're chasing the lightest possible eyewear for a thru-hike gram count, or you need full glare-killing polarization for glacier travel or extended time on snow, look at a dedicated polarized or glacier-glass option instead.

## Verdict

The Eye Jacket Redux is a competent, comfortable sport sunglass with genuinely useful Prizm optics and sweat-activated grip, but it's a lifestyle-first product that happens to work fine outdoors rather than a purpose-built ultralight trail tool. Solid pick if the retro look appeals to you and the fit matches your face; skip it if you're optimizing strictly for weight or need serious glare protection on snow and water.
