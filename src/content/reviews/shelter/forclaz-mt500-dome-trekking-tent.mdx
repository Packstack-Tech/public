## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is Decathlon's freestanding, pre-assembled two-person dome. It has two doors and two vestibules, and it costs £129.99 at Decathlon UK. It's built for people who want a dependable three-season shelter without paying cottage-industry prices. The trade-off is plain: at 2.85 kg, it's a very good budget tent and a poor ultralight one.

## Performance

**Pitching.** The tent's best feature is how little it asks of the person pitching it. The inner and fly are joined, so the bedroom stays covered while it goes up. That matters in a downpour, when an inner-first pitch means rain falls straight onto the bedroom floor. The poles slide through colour-coded sleeves on the fly, and the dome stands on its own. Reviewers at Advnture called it simple to put up and pack away. One owner-style review found the printed diagrams confusing the first time, but said the process becomes straightforward with a little practice.

**Weather.** The 2,000 mm flysheet rating is typical for the price tier. The 5,000 mm floor is a sensible margin for wet ground. Decathlon says the pitched tent is lab-tested against 200 litres per square metre per hour for three hours, and that the design held up in wind tunnels at 70 km/h. In practice, testers have found it capable for the money. Advnture also took it out in snow, in conditions it wasn't designed for, and concluded that the tent is very capable for its price but was pushed past its limits. It is a spring-to-autumn shelter, and Decathlon sells it as one.

**Details.** Reviewers keep returning to the door zippers. They are tethered to the door fabric, so the panel rolls away as it opens instead of flapping in the wind or dripping fly water inside. The tent also has an internal drying line and two interior pockets. Attachment loops make it easy to add or remove guy lines. The poles are 7075 aluminium, and Decathlon stocks spare poles and bedrooms in store and online. A tent this cheap can be repaired instead of replaced.

**Space.** The MT500 is roomier than its weight suggests for one person. Reviewers describe it as comfortable for a solo hiker with gear. A second person makes it a squeeze, and the 60 cm per person figure explains why. That's barely wider than a standard sleeping pad. Two people will sleep shoulder to shoulder, and packs will have to go in the vestibules. The upside is that the two doors and two vestibules mean neither person climbs over the other. The 108 cm peak height gives enough room to sit up.

**Weight and pack size.** The 2.85 kg figure is the sticking point for anyone counting grams. Ultralight two-person shelters regularly come in at half that weight or less. The tent packs to 39 x 16 x 15 cm, which is compact and easy to stash in a pack. The wide compression bag also makes it easier to stuff in a hurry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Pre-assembled inner and fly keep the bedroom dry while pitching
- Freestanding, so it can be pitched on rock, gravel or platforms
- Two doors and two vestibules, so there is no climbing over a partner
- Tethered door zippers keep doors from flapping and dripping
- Compact 39 x 16 x 15 cm packed size
- Priced at £129.99 at Decathlon UK, with spare parts available
- Tested to Force 8 winds, with a 5,000 mm floor

**Cons**

- 2.85 kg is heavy for backpacking, and far from ultralight
- 60 cm per person makes it tight for two adults
- 2,000 mm fly with polyester fabric will sag and need re-tensioning when wet
- Three-season only
- Pitching diagrams are unclear on the first attempt

## Who Should Buy This

This tent suits budget-conscious backpackers, cyclists and first-time trekkers who want something quick to pitch and cheap to replace. It is also a good solo tent for hikers who value a big vestibule and a freestanding pitch over pack weight. It works best as a one-person tent with space to spare, or as a two-person tent for a couple who accept sharing close quarters.

Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. Over several weeks of carrying it, 2.85 kg adds up, and a trekking-pole or lighter freestanding tent will save well over a kilogram. Pairs who want room to spread out should also look at wider designs.

## Verdict

The MT500 is a well-considered budget dome that gets the practical details right: quick pitching, sensible doors, and spare parts. It is a good tent for short trips, bikepacking and casual three-season use. It is not a long-distance tent for anyone who counts weight.
