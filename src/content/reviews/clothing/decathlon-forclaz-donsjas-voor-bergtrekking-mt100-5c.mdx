## Overview

The Forclaz MT100 -5°C is Decathlon's women's hooded down jacket, a 260 g puffy filled with 800-cuin down that has become a staple of budget ultralight kits. It is a camp and rest-stop layer for three-season trips and mild winters, not an expedition parka. The trade-off is simple: premium-grade fill at a fraction of the usual price, in exchange for a thicker shell, a slim cut and a short list of features.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Decathlon's own testing certifies active comfort of 23°F (-5°C) when the jacket is worn as a third layer. For a standalone, stationary rating it is more modest: the brand lists a comfort band of roughly 23–41°F (-5 to 5°C). Reviewers' experience tracks the lower figures. A GreatMindsThinkHike tester wore it down to about 27°F over a long-sleeve base layer without trouble, and GearJunkie's reviewer called it suited to mild winters and summer trips where temperatures drop at camp. One owner who weighed the numbers pointed out that a medium holds only about 3.2 oz of down, against roughly 5 oz in a Patagonia Down Sweater, and concluded it is a cool jacket by comparison. Owners generally treat it as a layering piece. Below freezing, it wants a base layer and a shell for company.

**Weight and packability.** At 260 g with 800-fill down, the jacket sits in the ultralight conversation, and the r/Ultralight crowd has long cited its warmth-to-weight and warmth-to-price ratios. It stuffs into its own left-hand pocket, closed with a double-pull zipper, and one reviewer reported forgetting it was in the pack. The roomy hand pockets also earn praise.

**Shell and feel.** The 15-denier, 35 g/m² polyamide is more substantial than the gossamer shells on pricier down sweaters. FeedTheHabit's tester likened the first impression to a budget big-box jacket, and found the down a bit prickly against bare skin. That heft pays off in durability: GreatMindsThinkHike reported no rips or stray stitching after nearly two years of testing, and the lining is silky against the skin. Wear a base layer and the prickle is a non-issue.

**Wet weather.** The DWR is PFC-free and the down is not hydrophobic-treated. One reviewer's controlled shower test left the jacket mostly dry apart from some soak-through at the top of the zipper, but sustained rain is a different matter. Decathlon itself recommends a waterproof jacket over it in prolonged wet. In a wet climate, that is the plan, and untreated down is a liability when the shell loses.

**Features.** There are no pit zips, no two-way zipper and no mesh, so there is no way to dump heat on a climb. Owners report unzipping and sweating through steady uphill work, and FeedTheHabit's reviewer reserved it for low-output use. Some owners also find the main zipper stiff in the cold.

**Fit.** The cut runs small. CleverHiker advises sizing up, and one owner who normally wears a Patagonia medium bought a large. The weight listed here is for size M; larger sizes may vary slightly.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 800-fill down at 260 g, usually seen only on much pricier jackets
- Packs into its own pocket without much fuss
- Tougher shell than most ultralight puffies
- PFC-free DWR
- Hood and roomy hand pockets
- Often discounted; US pricing has ranged from about $40 on clearance to $110
- Machine washable (with tennis balls in the dryer)

**Cons**

- Runs small; most owners size up
- Down is not hydrophobic, and the DWR is basic
- No ventilation features
- Stiff zipper in the cold
- Less insulation than the "-5°C" label might suggest for sitting still
- Shell is stiffer and less supple than premium down sweaters

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want a camp and rest-stop puffy at a fraction of name-brand prices will get the most from it, as will travelers who need something that disappears into a bag. It suits cold sleepers less well if they plan to rely on it as a single layer near freezing. Anyone hiking in persistent rain, or planning to wear a down jacket while moving uphill, should look at synthetic insulation or a jacket with ventilation.

## Verdict

The women's MT100 delivers real 800-fill warmth at 260 g for a price that undercuts most of the field, and its shell is tougher than the money suggests. Size up, pair it with a rain shell and keep expectations at "camp layer" rather than "winter coat", and it is one of the best-value down jackets for backpackers.
