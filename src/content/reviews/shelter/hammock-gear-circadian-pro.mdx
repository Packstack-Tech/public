## Overview

The Circadian Pro is Hammock Gear's modular gathered-end hammock. It pairs a soft diamond-ripstop nylon body with a fully zippered bug net that comes off entirely, and it's sewn to order in Ohio. It's built for hangers who want a big, flat lay and a hammock that adapts to the season. The cost of that room and flexibility is weight: at 683 g in its lightest form, it sits well above the minimalist end of the category.

## Performance

**Comfort and lay.** The Circadian Pro's headline trait is room. The body is 58 inches wide, and the gathered ends are symmetrical, so there is no "right way" to lie in it. Sleepers can go diagonal or recline to either side. The fabric is a hexagonal-pattern diamond ripstop that Hammock Gear describes as soft and low-stretch, which helps keep the lay flat. CleverHiker's testers called it luxuriously comfortable and the largest hammock in their lineup. They also found the 12-foot version they tested a bit excessive in length, though they noted it could suit taller sleepers. Owners on Hammock Gear's own product page echo the praise. A 6-foot, 190-pound buyer who chose the single-layer 11-foot model described soft fabric, strong stitching and a good night's sleep on the first trip.

**The bug net.** The net is the reason to pick the Pro over the standard Circadian. On the standard model the net is attached. Here it zips off completely, which saves roughly an ounce on the standard model's weight and gives a bug-free option in some seasons. The 145 g net is high-visibility mesh. Four YKK zippers allow entry from either side and let a hiker reach gear stashed outside the hammock. The trade-off is that the zippers terminate mid-body so the net can detach. CleverHiker noted this means the hammock can't be zipped shut in one continuous motion, and there is no adjustability where the zipper ends meet. Hammock Gear's guidance for storage is to clip the carabiners together outside the double-ended stuff sack, which keeps hardware from snagging the netting.

**Weight.** This is where the Circadian Pro loses ground. The single-layer configuration with net comes in at 24.08 oz, and the double-layer builds run from 30.74 to 37.92 oz depending on options. CleverHiker's tested double-layer, 12-foot version weighed 30.8 oz as a hammock body, almost double the lightest hammock in their test. Their verdict was that it is too heavy to recommend as a general three-season hammock. An experienced hammock camper pricing out the category on Slickdeals offered a benchmark: a quality netted hammock tends to land around 16 oz. The Pro is 8 oz or more beyond that, even before suspension and a tarp.

**Winter use.** The double-layer option is where the design earns its weight. Sleeping in a double-layer body lets a hanger slide a pad or insulation between the layers to cut cold spots from underneath. CleverHiker, whose testers have logged cold trips in Minnesota's Boundary Waters, picked the Circadian Pro as its best winter hammock. Owners on Hammock Gear's page mention buying the winter top cover for shoulder-season and winter trips. Four quilt hooks hold an underquilt in place, and extra connection points on the body handle tie-outs and accessories.

**Durability and capacity.** The rolled hems and triple-stitched suspension channels are meant for long service life. The 40D body is rated to 350 lb. The 70D body is rated to 500 lb, and the double layer to 750 lb. That's far beyond what most solo hikers need, and it also means the Pro is heavier than a similar Dutch Chameleon, a comparison that came up on Hammock Forums. A 5'9", 150-pound hanger there noted that the extra load rating is wasted on him.

**Setup.** The hammock ships with Hammock Gear's basic ultralight suspension installed, so it's ready to hang out of the box. CleverHiker found setup straightforward.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide 58-inch body with a symmetrical gathered-end design that lets sleepers lie in any direction
- Bug net removes completely, so the net only travels when bugs are expected
- Double-layer option makes it a strong cold-weather platform
- Made to order in Ohio with triple-stitched channels and rolled hems
- High weight ratings: 350–500 lb single layer, 750 lb double layer
- Four-zipper layout allows entry from either side and easy access to gear
- Suspension is pre-installed

**Cons**

- Heavy: 24.08 oz minimum, and over 30 oz for double-layer builds
- Zippers end mid-body, so the net can't be closed in one pull
- Load ratings exceed what most solo hikers need
- Long body, especially in 12-foot form, is excess for shorter hikers
- Costs more than the standard Circadian, which has a fixed net

## Who Should Buy This

The Circadian Pro suits shoulder-season and winter hangers, tall sleepers, and anyone who wants one hammock that works with or without bugs. It's a good match for a hanger already invested in Hammock Gear's underquilts and top quilts, and for buyers who value US-made construction and long service life. Gram-counting thru-hikers should look at lighter hammocks that weigh close to 16 oz with a net. Those who always hike in bug season may prefer the standard Circadian, which saves about an ounce with its attached net.

## Verdict

The Circadian Pro is a roomy, well-built hammock that does its best work in cold weather and for sleepers who want space. It is heavy for 3-season ultralight use, and the mid-body zipper break is a small annoyance. Buy it for comfort and flexibility, not for a low base weight.
