## Overview

The Smartwater bottle has been the unofficial water vessel of the Appalachian Trail and Pacific Crest Trail for years, mostly because nothing else was as light and cheap. The Igneous NOBO is a $22 attempt to make that disposable bottle reusable. It is a 1-liter HDPE bottle made in the USA, with a tethered cap and measurement markings. The trade-off is a few extra ounces and a much higher price than a $2 grocery-store bottle.

## Performance

**Weight and carry.** At 64 g, the NOBO is among the lightest reusable bottles on the market. CleverHiker's testers hiked for miles with it and barely noticed it in the pack, even full. A disposable Smartwater bottle is still lighter, at roughly 1.2 oz by CleverHiker's figure. The NOBO's case rests on lasting longer, not on beating that number. The 240 mm height is shorter than most 1 L bottles, and one owner liked that it didn't knock into his arm while hiking. The cylindrical body slides into side pockets and cup holders easily.

**Filter compatibility.** The 28-410 thread fits the Sawyer Squeeze, Platypus QuickDraw, and most ultralight bidet caps. Igneous designed the body to be soft enough to squeeze water through a filter and firm enough to hold comfortably. Testers found it squeezes well. Bikepacking.com called it the softest and easiest to squeeze of the backpacking bottles it compared. One Garage Grown Gear buyer found it less squishy than Mazama's M!GO, which is made of softer LDPE and weighs 132 g.

**Durability.** CleverHiker's drop tests and regular backcountry use left the NOBO unharmed, and the site concluded that few bottles at this weight are as tough. The cap is the weak point. A Bikepacking.com reviewer broke one by dropping it onto the 3D-printed cap, and a Garage Grown Gear buyer described a replacement top as a little wonky and pricey at $8.99. Igneous sells replacement parts, and the TPU tether is replaceable too, but hikers who drop things should expect to carry a spare cap.

**Material and microplastics.** Igneous founder Ray Aldridge has a materials-engineering background. He chose HDPE because it is more rigid and abrasion-resistant than the plastic in disposable bottles, so it should shed fewer microplastics as it flexes. The HDPE is curbside recyclable and holds up to boiling water. Farlite notes that this lets the NOBO double as a hot water bottle in a sleeping bag. Its frosted finish also diffuses a headlamp into a soft tent lantern.

**Cap and fit quirks.** The thread pattern is not identical to Smartwater's. Garage Grown Gear's listing says the internal diameter is about 0.5 mm smaller, similar to a Cnoc Vesica. One buyer found that a sport cap sat crooked and sealed poorly unless cranked down hard. The same buyer found the body wider than expected and too thick for a shoulder-strap pouch. Standard filters and bidet attachments don't have this problem.

**Cleaning and convenience.** The narrow mouth makes the NOBO harder to clean than a wide-mouth bottle. CleverHiker and Bikepacking.com both say a brush helps. It also makes adding ice or drink-mix tablets a nuisance. The oz/ml markings are more useful than they sound. They let hikers measure exact water for a rehydrated dinner, which is tough to do with an unmarked Smartwater bottle.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 64 g, close to disposable-bottle weight
- Tough HDPE body that survived drop testing
- 28-410 thread fits Sawyer Squeeze, QuickDraw, and bidet caps
- Tethered cap keeps the lid from getting lost
- Carbonized oz/ml markings won't rub off
- Boil-safe and dishwasher-safe, with a lifetime guarantee
- Made in the USA, and curbside recyclable

**Cons**

- Roughly twice the weight of a Smartwater bottle, and about 10 times the price
- The cap can crack if dropped
- Narrow mouth makes cleaning and adding ice or tablets awkward
- Not insulated
- Some sport caps and strap pouches don't fit well
- Only available in a 1 L size

## Who Should Buy This

The NOBO suits thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who have run through a few Smartwater bottles and would rather buy one durable replacement. It also suits anyone who wants a reliable Sawyer Squeeze partner with measurement markings and a tethered cap. Hikers who want to drink ice-cold water, mix drinks, or clean easily should choose a wide-mouth bottle. Gram-counters who accept a bottle's short lifespan can stay with the disposable.

## Verdict

The NOBO does what it was designed to do. It replaces the disposable bottle with a reusable one that weighs about the same, fits the same filters, and adds useful details. The fragile cap and the price keep it from being a clear upgrade for everyone. For thru-hikers who want reusable gear, it is a strong first pick.
