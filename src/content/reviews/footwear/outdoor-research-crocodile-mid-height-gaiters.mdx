## Overview

The Crocodile Mid-Height takes the construction of Outdoor Research's long-running mountaineering gaiter and cuts it down to a shorter profile for hikers who want waterproof protection without a full calf wrap. It pairs GORE-TEX leg panels with an abrasion-resistant Cordura nylon foot section, a camlock top hem and replaceable instep straps. The trade-off is weight and price: at 235 g (8.29 oz) a pair and roughly $100, it is a snow-and-brush tool, not a summer thru-hiking accessory.

## Performance

The Crocodile design has changed little over the years. The leg sections are GORE-TEX over a tough face fabric that Outdoor Research says shrugs off devil's club and brush. Below that sits a Cordura boot panel meant to survive ski edges, crampon points and general boot-to-ground abuse. The family's reputation is built on that panel. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers called the full-height Crocodile's lower foot section super thick and resistant to abrasion from rocks and ice, and one tester's pair was still rock solid after several years.

The mid-height version carries that build into a shorter package. Owners who bought it specifically wanted a waterproof alternative to a mid-calf gaiter that isn't waterproof. One REI reviewer reported that no snow got into their shoes and that the gaiters stayed up without readjusting. Another described them as suited to foul-weather running and walking, which is a fair reading of the category: they work over low-cut shoes and trail runners in slop, mud and shallow snow, where a full-length gaiter would be overkill and a mesh trail-runner gaiter would be underdressed.

Fit is the variable. Gaiters in this line rely on a good seal rather than an elastic base band, and Crocodile owners have noted that snow can get pushed up between the gaiter and boot when the fit is off. One REI buyer of the mid-height model found the leg closure cumbersome and unable to cinch tight enough to keep snow out on size large gaiters over 10.5 Oboz boots. They returned them. Another reviewer called the fit too big. The sizing note matters here: OR recommends dropping a full size for lightweight boots, shoes or trail runners, and thru-hikers pairing these with low-volume footwear should heed it.

Durability across the Crocodile line is strong but not unanimous. Long-term owners report pairs lasting five years or more with only minor tears patched with duct tape, and one reviewer described using them daily all winter as a lineman. The recurring complaint concerns hardware: some owners have reported buckles, prongs or foot straps breaking, and one early-use shredding was reported on a full-height pair. Owners of the updated design say the buckle configuration can now be repaired with a quick bit of sewing, and the replaceable instep strap keeps a worn strap from ending a trip. A reviewer of the men's full-height pair also liked that the strap has no external buckle to snag.

As with any membrane gaiter, breathability has limits. Owners note some sweating inside on steep uphills. That's a minor annoyance in cold and wet weather, but it argues against wearing them on warm days.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fully waterproof GORE-TEX construction in a shorter, less bulky height than a full gaiter
- Cordura foot panel built for abrasion from rock, ice and brush
- Replaceable instep straps extend the life of the wear item most likely to fail
- Limited Lifetime warranty
- Camlock top hem and lace hooks offer a secure attachment system

**Cons**

- 235 g (8.29 oz) is heavy against the minimalist gaiters most ultralight hikers carry
- About $100, a lot for a shorter gaiter
- Fit-dependent seal; some owners could not cinch the top tight enough for snow
- Sizing runs large for low-profile footwear, so buyers need to size down
- Hardware complaints appear in reviews of the wider Crocodile line

## Who Should Buy This

This suits hikers who walk through snow, slush, mud or dense brush and want waterproof protection over low-cut boots or shoes, without the height of a full Crocodile. Shoulder-season backpackers, snowshoers, off-trail scramblers and winter day hikers get the most from it. Warm-weather thru-hikers who want to keep sand and pebbles out of trail runners should look at lighter, non-waterproof gaiters instead, since this pair adds weight and heat they won't use. Anyone with unusually wide or narrow calves should try a pair on before committing.

## Verdict

The Crocodile Mid-Height is a durable, repairable, waterproof gaiter that earns its place in cold and wet conditions and in rough country. It is too heavy and too costly for dry-trail use, and buyers need to size carefully, because the seal depends on fit. For snow and brush, it's a good buy. For summer miles, it's carried weight.
