## Overview

The Hohem iSteady Pro 4 is a 3-axis handheld gimbal built specifically for action cameras — GoPro Hero 5 through 13, DJI Osmo Action, Insta360 ONE R, and the Sony RX0 all fit its quick-release clip. It's aimed at vloggers, trail runners, and action-sports shooters who want smoother footage than a chest mount or handlebar clamp can deliver, without stepping up to a full-size mirrorless gimbal. At a street price hovering around $80–$100, it's positioned squarely as the budget pick in the action-cam stabilizer space.

## Performance

The headline feature here is the mounting system, and it's genuinely the thing everyone talks about. Instead of a thumbscrew, you get a <cite index="9-6,9-7">spring-loaded quick release clip that replaces the installation of tightening screws, so you don't have to struggle with installation</cite>. One reviewer summed up the trade-off well: it's <cite index="5-3,5-4">"both its best and worst feature"</cite> — great for speed, but if you need to change camera settings after mounting, you'll be wiggling the camera around a bit rather than pulling it fully off. It's a minor annoyance you learn to work around by dialing in your camera settings before clipping in.

Stabilization itself runs on Hohem's iSteady 5.0 algorithm across 3 axes, with mechanical ranges of 600° pan and 320° on both roll and tilt — enough headroom for inception-style rolls and follow-focus panning without hitting hard stops mid-shot. Testers running it through trail running, bike mounts, and low-light timelapses reported that <cite index="6-1,6-2">the Pro 4 feels sturdier than its price suggests, and the rubberised grip stays tacky when wet</cite>. That IPX4 rating isn't just a spec-sheet checkbox, either — the same review noted it <cite index="6-2">handled light rain and trail spray without drama</cite>.

Battery life is where this gimbal punches above its price bracket. The 3600 mAh cell is rated for 14 hours, and it doubles as a power bank — you can <cite index="6-3,6-4">top up your camera mid-shoot</cite> while it's still stabilizing. That's a genuinely useful feature for all-day shoots where you don't want to break down your rig just to plug in a battery pack. Charging the gimbal itself back up takes about 3.5 hours via USB-C.

Software is usually where budget gimbals fall apart, but multiple reviewers pushed back against that expectation here. One noted that <cite index="5-9,5-10">in a category full of buggy software and half-finished features, the iSteady Pro 4 feels like a complete package</cite>, and specifically called out the companion Hohem app as reliable, with solid connectivity and advanced controls. Camera control is handled over Bluetooth rather than Wi-Fi, which one source says <cite index="9-10">extends battery life by roughly 50% compared to the previous Wi-Fi connection</cite> used in the Pro 3.

Build quality is the honest weak point. It's made of ABS+PC composite plastic, and while it's held up to drops in testing — <cite index="5-15,5-16">the build quality feels okay but doesn't give off a premium feel, and it's taken a few dings and falls without any issue</cite> — you're not getting the metal-jointed feel of pricier rigs. At this weight and price point, that's a reasonable trade rather than a dealbreaker.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Screwless quick-release clip makes camera swaps fast — a real improvement over thumbscrew mounts
- 14-hour rated battery that can also charge your action cam mid-shoot
- IPX4 splash resistance held up in real rain and trail spray during testing
- Companion app reportedly stable and full-featured, which is rare at this price
- Broad compatibility across GoPro, DJI Osmo Action, Insta360, and Sony RX0
- Light at 365 g — won't fatigue your wrist on long handheld sessions

**Cons**

- Quick-release clip makes it awkward to tweak camera settings once mounted
- Plastic build doesn't feel premium, even if it's proven durable enough in testing
- Max camera dimensions (51mm height, 30mm depth) and 150g weight limit rule out some larger action cam setups
- No optical zoom or lens control — this is stabilization only, not a full remote-control rig

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for GoPro and action-cam shooters who want noticeably smoother handheld footage — running, mountain biking, travel vlogging — without spending $150+ on a higher-end gimbal. It's especially appealing if you shoot long sessions away from outlets, since the power-bank function and 14-hour battery mean you're less likely to be stuck mid-hike with a dead camera. If you frequently need to adjust camera settings on the fly while mounted, budget a little extra patience for the clip system, or consider prepping your shot settings before you clip in.

## Verdict

The iSteady Pro 4 delivers real functional upgrades — the quick-release mount, long battery life, and splash resistance — at a price point where budget gimbals usually cut corners on software and reliability instead. The plastic build and fiddly post-mount adjustments keep it from feeling premium, but for the money it's one of the more complete action-cam stabilizers out there. I'd put it at a 7.5/10: a clear recommendation for GoPro and action-cam users, with the caveat that this is a stabilizer, not a luxury tool.
