## Overview

Chiligryte, sold in export markets as Chili Stew with Beans, is Real Turmat's vegan answer to chili con carne. It is a freeze-dried, add-hot-water meal from the Norwegian company that also supplies expedition and military rations. It's built for backpackers who want a hot dinner with real beans and vegetables instead of paste, and who will pay a premium for it.

## Performance

**Calories per ounce.** At 568 kcal for 149 g, Chiligryte sits in the middle of the freeze-dried pack. It carries about 108 calories per ounce, which is respectable but not the leanest option for gram-counters. Fat does much of the work. Rapeseed oil puts 25 g of fat in the pouch, which is why a single serving feels like a full dinner. The 26 g of protein is also well above what most vegan freeze-dried meals deliver. Retailers classify the pouch in the 500–750 calorie "main meal" band. For most hikers it works as a complete dinner, not a snack with ambitions.

**Taste and texture.** Beans make up 40% of the recipe, and owners keep coming back to that. A German reviewer who hikes in northern Norway each summer wrote that the meal has "something to chew on" rather than undefinable mush. Another reviewer at a UK retailer noted that nobody at the table would have spotted the missing meat without being told. The testers there praised the texture and rehydration, and described a deep smoked-paprika flavor running through the whole dish. They scored it 7/10.

**Heat level.** Opinions diverge, and the divergence is useful. Some Finnish buyers call it fiery. Others find it mild and say so plainly. A UK taste-tester wanted more kick. The manufacturer pitches the dish at people who like "a proper kick," but the practical read is moderate warmth with smoke. Carry a small bottle of hot sauce or a pinch of chili flakes if the heat matters. Anyone sensitive to spice is unlikely to be bothered.

**Preparation and packaging.** The pouch is a vacuum-packed, resealable bag you fill with hot water and eat from directly. The company lists preparation at no more than 10 minutes. One reviewer noted a packing benefit: the vacuum-sealed pouch compresses into a very small space in the middle of a pack. A field tester for a UK retailer also reports that the meal cold-soaks well, which matters for stoveless trips, though cold soaking takes longer than the hot method. Some buyers dislike the packaging waste, a complaint common to every pouch meal.

**Salt.** The pouch carries 4.6 g of salt. That is helpful on a sweaty multi-day push and a lot for anyone watching sodium. Hikers on a long trip who already eat salty snacks should factor it in.

**Cost.** Price is the recurring complaint. Reviewers in Germany, Finland and the UK use phrases like "a little pricey" and "expensive" if you build every dinner from these pouches. Most then add that the taste justifies it. That is a fair summary of the brand overall: buyers consistently describe Real Turmat as one of the better-tasting freeze-dried lines available, with a price to match.

**Dietary fit.** The recipe is vegan, lactose-free and listed by the manufacturer as gluten-free. Soy is the headline allergen, since soy protein, soy sauce and bouillon all appear in the ingredients. Anyone with a soy allergy should skip it entirely.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 568 kcal and 26 g of protein in a 149 g pouch
- Beans make up 40% of the recipe, giving real texture instead of mush
- Smoky, well-seasoned flavor that even meat-eaters rate well
- Vacuum-packed pouch packs small; works hot or cold-soaked
- Vegan, lactose-free and gluten-free per the manufacturer
- 5-year shelf life suits resupply boxes and emergency kits

**Cons**

- Costs more than most freeze-dried competitors
- Heat level is inconsistent between reports; some find it too mild
- High in salt at 4.6 g per pouch
- Contains soy in several forms
- Calorie density is good, not exceptional, for the weight

## Who Should Buy This

Vegan and dairy-free hikers who are tired of bland or gummy pouch meals will get the most from Chiligryte. Section hikers and weekend backpackers who can afford a few premium dinners will appreciate a meal that tastes like cooking. It also suits resupply boxes, since the shelf life is long. Thru-hikers feeding a month of dinners from pouches should look at cheaper options or mix it with homemade meals. Anyone with a soy allergy, and anyone who wants a seriously hot chili, should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

Chiligryte is a bean-forward, smoky vegan chili that eats like a real dinner and weighs 149 g. It is not the cheapest or the leanest freeze-dried meal, but among vegan options it is one of the better-tasting. Buy it for the trips where dinner matters.
