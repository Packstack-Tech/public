## Overview

The Vesper is Therm-a-Rest's entry in the cottage-industry quilt market: a hoodless, zipperless 900-fill down blanket that tethers to a sleeping pad and leaves out the insulation that would only be crushed underneath you. It is aimed at three-season thru-hikers and gram-counters who want a mainstream brand's warranty and retail availability in place of a custom order and a long wait. The trade-off is the temperature rating. Owners and testers consistently say the number on the label runs warm-optimistic.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** This is where the Vesper earns its reputation. Ultimate Gear Lists put the 20°F Regular on the scale at 19.8 oz (563 g), about 13 g over claim, and found it compresses to roughly 2.8 liters in the supplied four-point compression sack. CleverHiker's testers ranked it among the lightest and most packable quilts available, at around half the weight of a comparable down bag. One alpine climber measured it about 250 g under Therm-a-Rest's own Parsec 20°F mummy. A Trailspace reviewer who owned the 32°F version said he had never carried a bag or quilt that packed so small.

**Warmth.** The ratings are the sticking point. CleverHiker's testers gave the 20°F model a 32°F comfort estimate and would not take it on sub-freezing trips. Adventure Alan argued it is not a true 20-degree quilt and described it as slightly underfilled, with some loft likely to fade after a season. The 32°F version draws the same verdict from different reviewers. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers noted that about 9 oz of down is modest for a 32-degree rating and called it more of a summer quilt for nights that stay above roughly 40°F. A Trailspace reviewer stayed comfortable into the mid-40s and argued it should be sold as a 41°F quilt. The workable rule: treat the 32°F version as a 40°F quilt and the 20°F version as a 30°F quilt that can be coaxed lower with a warm pad and a puffy.

**Design and comfort.** Box baffles and perimeter side baffles keep the down from migrating and cut cold spots. The snap-closed neck collar gives the quilt a snugness Ultimate Gear Lists said feels closer to a sleeping bag than a blanket. SynergyLink straps cinch it to a pad so it stays put through a restless night, and they loosen for venting. A PCT thru-hiker liked leaving it open on warm nights, poking out a leg, then cinching down when temperatures fell. The 10D ripstop shell is soft against the skin and breathes well, though it can feel slightly sticky in warm, humid conditions.

**Fit and hardware.** The insulated footbox keeps feet warm and, according to The Big Outside's reviewer, anchors the quilt so it doesn't slide off. Adventure Alan found it very narrow, so anyone who splays their feet should try it first. The 58-inch width suits back sleepers and moderate side sleepers; one reviewer, at 58 inches across the shoulders and 51 at the hips, found it only just wrapped fully around him. Hardware draws the most complaints: an REI buyer had a strap loop tear out of the hem on first use, and a Trailspace owner called the neck snap too small and out of step with the rest of the build.

**Moisture.** Nikwax Hydrophobic down and a DWR shell give some margin against tent condensation and damp air. It is a buffer, not wet-weather insulation. A dry bag is still needed.

## Choosing a Version

Three versions are available, and temperature rating changes weight more than length does.

The **32°F/0°C Regular (425 g)** is the summer and shoulder-season pick. It saves about 125 g over the 20°F Regular and packs smaller, but it is a warm-night quilt in practice. Hikers expecting nights near freezing should skip it.

The **20°F/-6°C Regular (550 g)** is the most useful of the three for a typical three-season hiker up to 183 cm (6 ft). It carries 390 g of down and handles shoulder-season nights in the 30s with a decent pad and clothing layers.

The **20°F/-6°C Long (610 g)** adds 60 g, 3 inches of width and 4 inches of length, and fits sleepers up to 198 cm. Anyone over six feet should take it rather than leave shoulders or toes exposed. Hikers of average height gain nothing from the extra weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 425–610 g for 900-fill down, with a compact packed size
- Box baffles, side baffles and an insulated footbox limit drafts
- SynergyLink connectors keep the quilt in place on the pad
- Hydrophobic down and DWR shell add margin against damp
- Soft shell fabric; sold widely with a limited lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- Real-world warmth runs about 8–10°F above the stated ratings
- Reviewers describe the fill as on the lean side
- Narrow footbox; the Regular is a tight wrap for large frames
- Strap loops and the neck snap have drawn durability complaints
- No hood, so cold sleepers need a hat or hooded layer
- The 20°F version lists at $460, though discounts are common

## Who Should Buy This

The Vesper suits three-season thru-hikers and section hikers whose nights hover around freezing or above, and who prefer a light, compressible quilt backed by a major brand's returns and warranty. Alpinists and bikepackers short on pack volume will appreciate the stuff size. Cold sleepers, hikers who camp regularly below freezing, and anyone with wide shoulders or feet should look at a cottage-brand quilt with more fill and a roomier cut.

## Verdict

The Vesper delivers on weight and packability, and its construction is thoughtful. Its ratings flatter it. Buy the 20°F version for 30°F nights, or the 32°F for summer, and it is a very good quilt. Buy either expecting its stated limit to be comfortable, and it will disappoint.
