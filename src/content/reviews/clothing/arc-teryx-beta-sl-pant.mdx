## Overview

The Beta SL Pant is Arc'teryx's stripped-down emergency shell pant: two-layer GORE-TEX Paclite Plus, full-length side zippers, and a weight of 330 g (11.6 oz) in the women's size small. It is built for hikers, skiers and mountaineers who want real storm protection that stuffs small and goes on without removing boots. The trade-off is plain: it costs $299 and weighs more than the thinnest rain pants a dedicated thru-hiker would pick.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The Beta SL's two-layer GORE-TEX Paclite Plus fabric is fully waterproof and windproof, which puts it in a different class from the DWR-coated nylon pants that dominate the ultralight rain-pant shelf. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers, a group that includes working mountain guides, called it one of their all-time favorite shell pants and the nearest thing they had found to a quiver-of-one design. Pants like these stay dry in sustained rain and sleet, and they block wind on exposed ridgelines, which is where cheaper shells tend to give up.

**The zippers are the point.** Full-length side zips are rare at this weight, and they are the reason to buy the Beta SL over a lighter pull-on pant. Reviewers describe the zips as the feature that makes the pant practical for emergency use: the pant can be pulled on over boots, crampons or skis without taking anything off, then opened from hip to ankle to dump heat on a climb. A retail review from Backcountry Skiing Canada listed the top-to-bottom zippers and the lace hooks among the pant's best features, alongside an excellent fit.

**Packability and weight.** At 330 g, it is light for a pant with this much fabric and hardware, and it compresses to a small bundle. It is not an ultralight rain pant in the thru-hiker sense. Outdoor Gear Lab itself noted that on big alpine objectives where grams matter, a flimsier and even lighter shell pant is a legitimate choice. For a three-season hiker, the extra weight buys durability and the zippers.

**Durability.** The main fabric is soft and supple N40r, and the hem and instep sit on tougher N150p GORE-TEX, which is where boots, brush and crampon points do their damage. A reinforced instep patch and lace hooks keep the hem from riding up or catching on boots. Testers expect Arc'teryx shells to last for years with decent care, and that longevity is the argument for the price.

**Fit and adjustment.** The fit is regular and mid-rise, with a 31-inch inseam listed by retailers for the women's pant. The waist uses low-profile external adjusters, and the hem has a drawcord, so the pant can be cinched over trail runners or boots. Simplicity is deliberate: the design carries no frills, in line with the "SL" (Superlight) brief.

**Breathability.** Reviewers rate it as breathable for a waterproof pant, though two-layer GORE-TEX is no substitute for open vents. The long side zippers do most of the real ventilation work.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-length side zips allow on-and-off over boots and rapid venting
- Waterproof and windproof GORE-TEX Paclite Plus
- Reinforced instep and hem fabric where abrasion happens
- 330 g and packs small for a fully zippered shell pant
- Lace hooks and hem drawcord keep the cuffs in place

**Cons**

- $299 list price; reviewers consistently flag the cost
- Heavier than the minimalist rain pants most thru-hikers carry
- No pockets, by design
- Limited colour choice; black only on the women's version

## Who Should Buy This

The Beta SL suits backpackers and mountain travelers who hike in cold, wet or snowy conditions: shoulder-season trekkers, alpine scramblers, ski tourers and anyone who wants a storm layer that can go on at a trailhead or windy col without a boot-removal routine. It also suits people who keep gear for years and want durable fabric at the high-wear points.

Thru-hikers counting every gram in warm, dry-ish climates should look at a lighter rain skirt or a basic DWR pant. Those on a tight budget will find cheaper waterproof pants that do 80 percent of the job.

## Verdict

The Beta SL Pant is a well-made, durable shell pant whose full-length zippers are the best argument for carrying 330 g of rain pant. It is overkill for fair-weather hikers and expensive for everyone, but for cold, wet, technical trips it is among the most convenient storm pants available. Rated 8 out of 10.
