## Overview

The NB10000 Gen3 is a 10,000mAh power bank with a carbon fiber shell, two USB-C ports and a weight of 150 g (5.29 oz). It is built for thru-hikers, trail runners and ski tourers who want roughly two phone charges without carrying a brick. The trade-off is price: it costs about double what a conventional 10,000mAh bank does, and it is no longer in Nitecore's current lineup, having been succeeded by the Gen4.

## Performance

**Weight and carry.** Weight is the whole argument. CleverHiker's testers put it on a scale at 5.3 oz and called it the lightest 10,000mAh bank they had tested, one of the lightest on the market. Outdoors with Max measured 154 g, within Nitecore's stated ±5 g tolerance. At 10.6 mm thick, it slides into a hip-belt or chest pocket beside a phone without adding bulk. Nitecore says the shell structure itself accounts for just 14.5 g, which is how the bank reaches an energy density of 257mWh/g.

**Real capacity.** Like every power bank, the 10,000mAh figure is cell capacity at 3.85V, not what comes out of the port at 5V. Nitecore lists the rated 5V energy in the 5,400–6,200mAh range depending on the listing, and it prints that caveat on the case. In practice that is still enough for a modern phone about twice with some left over. CleverHiker's testers measured output with a multimeter and a dummy load and concluded that the Nitecore beats larger, heavier banks on watt-hours delivered per ounce, even though those banks hold more in total. A cheaper owner-reported test: it survived a three-day camping trip with charge to spare.

**Charging speed.** The 22.5W output fills a phone at a reasonable clip, and the 18W input recharges the bank itself in about four hours over USB-C, according to CleverHiker. That matters on a town stop, where a short outlet window can leave a heavier bank half full. The catch is the shared output budget. Run both ports at once and the total drops to 5V/3A, or 15W, so charging a phone and a headlamp together is slower than charging either alone.

**Pass-through and small devices.** Either USB-C port can charge the bank while it powers a device, which lets a hiker plug a phone in overnight and wake up to both full. A low-current mode detects earbuds, watches and other small loads that cheaper banks sometimes shut off on. The included USB-C to USB-A adapter covers older headlamps and cables, though hikers with a USB-A-only kit should check the cable situation, since the standard box has the bank and adapter only.

**Durability and weather.** The carbon fiber sheets are held on by a composite frame, and testers reported it held up well in hands-on abuse. The corner cushions protect pack fabric and skin from sharp edges. IPX5 means it shrugs off rain and splashes from any direction, but it is not a submersion rating. A dunk in a creek crossing is a job for a dry bag. The Gen4 raised the rating to IPX7.

**Interface.** Four blue LEDs show remaining charge. That is coarse, and there is no numeric readout, but it is enough to decide whether to top up in town.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 150 g for 10,000mAh, among the lightest in its class
- Two USB-C ports, both bidirectional, with pass-through charging
- 22.5W output and 18W input make for quick top-ups
- Slim 10.6 mm profile pocket-carries easily
- Low-current mode handles earbuds and watches
- Rigid carbon fiber shell with corner protection

**Cons**

- Costs roughly double a standard 10,000mAh bank
- Total output falls to 15W with both ports in use
- IPX5 is splash-proof only, not waterproof
- Four-LED gauge gives only rough charge status
- Superseded by the lighter, IPX7-rated Gen4, so stock is dwindling

## Who Should Buy This

This is the bank for gram-counters who charge a phone, a watch and a headlamp across a week-long section and want the lightest 10,000mAh option with a proper fast-charge. It suits trail runners and ski tourers, who can carry it in a chest pocket where its thinness and weight pay off. Hikers who need more than two phone charges between resupplies, or who carry power-hungry devices such as a tablet, should look at a 20,000mAh bank. So should anyone who wants a full-sized waterproof rating or buys on price per mAh.

## Verdict

The NB10000 Gen3 remains one of the most weight-efficient ways to carry 10,000mAh, with fast, flexible USB-C charging and a shell that survives pack life. Buy it if the savings in grams matter more than the savings in dollars, and consider the Gen4 if a current-production unit with a 143 g weight and IPX7 rating is available at a similar price.
