## Overview

The REI Co-op Magma 15 is a three-season mummy bag built around a simple premise: stuff a lot of high-quality down into a well-fitted shell and keep the price reasonable. <cite index="4-1">REI pairs 850-fill down with a price that undercuts many similarly warm competitors</cite>, and the result is one of the better-value cold-weather bags on the market. <cite index="2-4">If you're looking for a warm, well-designed, compressible, and easy-to-carry down sleeping bag for cooler temperatures, the Magma 15 is for backpackers of every skill level.</cite>

## Performance

### Warmth — More Than the Name Implies

The "15" in the name undersells this bag. <cite index="4-10">With a 21°F Comfort Rating and a 9°F Lower Limit Rating, the REI Co-op Magma 15 ranks among the warmest ISO-rated backpacking bags in the "15-degree" class.</cite> Why? <cite index="4-12,4-13">The primary reason for the Magma 15's warmth is its fill weight — it packs a substantial 1 lb 7.3 oz of 850-fill down.</cite> <cite index="4-14">For comparison, the $549 Sea to Summit Spark 15 uses just 1 lb 0.9 oz of the same fill power down.</cite> That extra insulation means the Magma punches well below its stated rating in practice.

Real-world results back that up. <cite index="2-13">Gear analyst Ian Krammer at CleverHiker has used this bag for years in frigid temperatures in the Rockies, from backpacking in winter conditions above 10,000 feet to car camping in spring snowstorms.</cite> One Reddit user reported sleeping in it down to 22°F and finding it "WAY too warm still" — which is either a complaint or a badge of honor depending on how cold you run. <cite index="10-36">Cold sleepers with a comfort rating concern of 21°F and a lower limit of 9°F find it performs well with base layers and a warm meal before bed.</cite>

### Fit & Sizing — The Real Differentiator

<cite index="9-16,9-17">
  In an effort to accommodate an even wider array of users, REI introduced nine
  sizes for the redesigned Magma: Short-Narrow, Short, Short-Wide,
  Medium-Narrow, Medium, Medium-Wide, Long-Narrow, Long, and Long-Wide.
</cite>
That's not a gimmick — fit directly affects thermal efficiency, and dead air
space around your body is wasted weight.
<cite index="6-16">
  Having the right fit significantly impacts the warmth of a bag, and the Magma
  15 is the most customizable bag OutdoorGearLab has tested.
</cite>
Importantly, <cite index="3-15">larger sizes are not more expensive</cite>,
which is a genuine customer-friendly move.

I'd strongly recommend trying sizes in-store before ordering online. <cite index="9-25">While maybe a tad snug in the medium-width options, overall the fit is better than other "standard" bags.</cite>

### Packability & Weight

<cite index="2-18,2-19">
  The down insulation compresses very well into the included stuff sack,
  measuring only 7.8 inches wide and 16 inches long — meaning the Magma takes up
  minimal space in your pack, leaving more room for essentials.
</cite>
<cite index="6-27">
  For those who want to maximize space, an aftermarket compression stuff sack
  can reduce the packed size by almost half.
</cite>

Weight is the honest trade-off here. <cite index="4-15">The medium size weighs 2 lbs 3.6 oz, which puts the Magma outside true ultralight territory.</cite> If gram-counting is your religion, bags like the Therm-a-Rest Hyperion or Zpacks Mummy will save you meaningful weight — but at a significant cost premium and with less fill.

### Construction & Features

<cite index="5-5">
  Variable-sized baffle construction, a draft collar, zipper draft tube, and
  trapezoidal footbox are all high-end features commonly found on premium
  sleeping bags costing much more.
</cite>
<cite index="2-22">
  Two separate drawcords — one around the face opening, another around the neck
  area — can be adjusted from the inside, so you can dial in the fit exactly how
  you like it.
</cite>
<cite index="2-24">
  A large interior stash pocket easily holds small essentials like chapstick, a

<cite index="4-24,4-25,4-26">
  The Magma uses a recycled 15-denier ripstop nylon shell and liner. For

  reinforced panels at the hood and footbox, while lighter-weight bags like the

That said,
<cite index="4-29">
  because the Magma isn't reinforced, there are some durability concerns about

Handle with care on the ground.

On the zipper front: <cite index="4-28">the zipper felt high quality — wide, smooth, and confidence-inspiring — with no worrying about catching the fabric when opening or closing the bag.</cite>

### Ethical Sourcing

<cite index="7-29,7-30">
  REI only uses down certified to the Responsible Down Standard (RDS), which
  aims to ensure that the down feathers come from animals that have not been
  subjected to unnecessary harm or cruel treatment.
</cite>
The shell fabric is also bluesign® approved. For buyers who weigh supply chain
ethics alongside performance specs, that matters.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Generous 23.3 oz fill weight makes this bag warmer than its name implies — real-world lower limit closer to 9°F
- Nine non-gendered sizes give you a fit most mummy bags can't match
- Larger sizes don't cost more — a genuine rarity
- Competitive price for 850-fill down; undercuts Sea to Summit, Western Mountaineering at similar ratings
- Smooth anti-snag zipper, dual drawcords, interior stash pocket, draft collar — solid feature set
- RDS-certified down and bluesign® shell fabric

**Cons**

- At 2 lbs 3.6 oz, it's not truly ultralight — dedicated gram-counters will look elsewhere
- Can run too warm for summer use, even at altitude; the Magma 30 doesn't bridge the gap well for shoulder seasons
- 15d shell has no reinforced panels at hood or footbox; treat it gently
- Snug mummy cut won't suit restless sleepers or side sleepers who need room to move
- Down is not treated for wet conditions as aggressively as some competitors' hydrophobic fills

## Who Should Buy This

The Magma 15 is the right bag for three-season backpackers — especially cold sleepers — who want legitimate 15°F (and then some) performance without paying $500+. It's an equally strong pick for anyone who's struggled to find a bag that fits: the nine-size matrix solves a real problem. It's not the bag for dedicated ultralight hikers trying to shave every ounce, and it's overkill for summer desert trips. Think spring Sierra, fall Rockies, or PNW shoulder-season conditions where overnight temps routinely dip into the 20s.

## Verdict

The REI Co-op Magma 15 is one of the best values in cold-weather backpacking bags right now — not because it cuts corners, but because it genuinely overfills relative to its rated temperature and keeps the price at $399. The nine-size system is the kind of practical innovation that deserves more attention than it gets. The weight keeps it out of ultralight camp, and the thin shell demands respectful handling, but for three-season use from beginners to experienced thru-hikers, it's hard to argue with what you're getting. **Rating: 8.5/10.**
