## Overview

The Vesper is Therm-a-Rest's answer to the cottage-industry quilt: a hoodless, 900-fill down blanket that weighs roughly half what a comparable mummy bag does. It is built for three-season thru-hikers and weight-focused backpackers who want a big-brand warranty and wide retail availability instead of a custom order and a months-long wait. The catch is the rating. Testers and owners consistently describe the "20°F" label as optimistic.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** This is where the Vesper earns its reputation. Ultimate Gear Lists measured the regular at 19.8 oz (563 g) and found it packs to roughly 2.8 liters in the supplied compression sack. CleverHiker's testers called it one of the lightest and most packable quilts on the market, at about half the weight of a traditional down bag. One reviewer who carried it for alpine climbing noted it came in about 250 g lighter than Therm-a-Rest's own Parsec 20°F mummy.

**Warmth.** Here the story gets more complicated. CleverHiker's reviewers gave it a 32°F comfort estimate and said they wouldn't take it on sub-freezing trips, a complaint they level at much of the sleeping bag industry. Adventure Alan went further, arguing that the quilt isn't a true 20-degree product and that its comfort tops out at freezing. The same reviewer called it slightly underfilled and suspected it would lose some loft after its first season. Treat it as a 30°F quilt that can be coaxed lower with a warm pad and a puffy jacket, and the numbers add up.

**Design and comfort.** Box baffles and perimeter side baffles keep the down in place, and a snap-closed, padded neck collar gives the Vesper a snugness that Ultimate Gear Lists said feels more like a sleeping bag than a quilt. The SynergyLink connectors cinch the quilt to a pad so it doesn't slide off in a restless night, and the straps loosen for venting. A thru-hiker on the PCT liked leaving it open on warm nights, with the option of poking a leg out, and cinching it down when it turned cold. The 10D ripstop shell is soft but can feel slightly sticky against skin in warm conditions, according to Ultimate Gear Lists.

**Footbox and fit.** The insulated footbox keeps warmth where it's needed, but Adventure Alan called it very narrow. Side sleepers and anyone who likes to splay their feet should try it in a store before buying. The trim 58-inch width suits back and moderate side sleepers but leaves little tuck-in room for large frames.

**Moisture.** The down is treated with Nikwax Hydrophobic, and the shell carries a DWR finish. That gives some margin against condensation and damp tents, though it will not make the quilt a wet-weather piece. It still needs a dry stuff sack.

## Choosing a Version

The Standard (550 g) is cut for sleepers up to 183 cm (6 ft). The Long (610 g) fits up to 198 cm and adds 60 g, so it makes sense for anyone taller than six feet who would otherwise leave their shoulders or toes exposed. Both share the same fill, shell and packed footprint, so size to your height rather than shaving the extra 60 g. Hikers of average height should stay with the Standard.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 550 g for a 900-fill quilt, with a packed size of 200 x 140 x 100 mm
- Box baffles, side baffles and an insulated footbox cut drafts and cold spots
- SynergyLink pad connectors keep it in place on restless nights
- Hydrophobic down and DWR shell add some protection against damp
- Widely sold at major retailers, with a limited lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- Real-world warmth sits nearer 30–32°F than the 20°F rating
- Described as slightly underfilled by some reviewers
- Narrow footbox
- $460 list price, though discounts are common
- No hood, so cold sleepers need a separate hat or hooded layer

## Who Should Buy This

The Vesper suits the three-season thru-hiker or section hiker whose nights mostly hover around freezing and above, who wants a light, compressible quilt from a mainstream brand with easy returns and a warranty. Alpinists and bikepackers who need a small packed volume will appreciate it too. Cold sleepers, hikers who regularly camp below freezing, and anyone with wide feet or shoulders should look at a cottage-brand quilt with more fill and a roomier cut, or step up to a lower-rated Vesper.

## Verdict

The Vesper delivers on weight and packability, and its construction is thoughtful. It undermines itself with an optimistic temperature rating. Buy it for 32°F nights and above, and it is a very good quilt. Buy it expecting 20°F comfort and it will disappoint.
