## Overview

The Technical Merino 150 LS is a long-sleeved base layer from Danish brand Fusion, built from a two-sided knit with merino on the outside and polyester against the skin. It is aimed at runners, cyclists and day-to-day outdoor users who want a shirt that handles a wide temperature swing without the fragility of pure merino. The trade-off is built into the fabric: at 23% wool, it behaves more like a technical synthetic with a merino face than like a classic wool tee.

## Performance

**Next-to-skin comfort.** The fabric's central idea is that the itch-prone wool sits on the outside while a polyester face touches the skin. Retailers pitch this as a shirt that avoids the scratchiness some people get from merino, and Danish owners on Fusion's own site describe it as comfortable and soft. A bike-shop tester who wore the company's merino 150 base layer through cold rides called it very soft, with light stretch that follows the body.

**Moisture and warmth.** Wicking is the polyester's job. Fusion says the inner face moves sweat away while the merino exterior handles temperature regulation. Owners back this up in broad strokes. One reviewer calls it "breathable and insulating" for winter training, and a T-shirt version of the same fabric earns praise for moving sweat while offering more warmth than a standard tee. That profile suits stop-start effort: hard climbs, then a cold descent.

**Durability and care.** This is where the blend earns its keep. Fusion says the hybrid fabric holds its shape and resists abrasion better than wool alone, and the shirt can be machine-washed repeatedly. The seams use Fusion's PowerLock construction with four-thread overlock stitching, a reinforced build meant to survive pack-strap and hip-belt rub better than flatlock seams on thin merino. Fusion's claim about odor control comes from the merino, but with just under a quarter of the fabric made of wool, hikers planning multi-day wear without washing should treat it as a polyester-leaning shirt.

**Fit.** The cut is regular, which Fusion says most wearers find completely standard. It follows movement without feeling tight. At least one owner found the size slightly large, so those between sizes may want the smaller one. It is not a compression-fit layer, so the shirt will not sit snug against the skin in the way racing-style baselayers do.

**Ratings.** On Fusion's own store, the men's long-sleeve has 51 reviews, with 41 of them five-star. Those are brand-site reviews and skew toward Danish training and commuting use rather than backpacking, so they say more about comfort and build than about week-long trail performance.

**Weight.** At 150 g, the shirt is light but not remarkable for the ultralight world, where thin merino or synthetic long-sleeves can come in lower. The benefit is that fabric of this construction tends to be sturdier than whisper-thin wool.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Polyester inner face keeps wool away from the skin
- Machine washable, with good shape retention
- Reinforced PowerLock and four-thread overlock seams
- Mulesing-free merino, with fabric made in Denmark
- Wide stated temperature range (-20°C to 20°C) for a single layer
- Strong owner satisfaction: 41 of 51 reviews on the brand's site are five-star

**Cons**

- Only 23% merino, so it will not feel or smell-resist like a full-wool shirt
- 150 g is competitive but not class-leading for a thru-hiker's kit
- Regular cut, with at least one owner finding it a bit large
- Around €80, a premium for a blend
- Reviews focus on running, cycling and daily wear, not long-distance hiking

## Who Should Buy This

This shirt suits hikers and mixed-sport users who want one long-sleeve that works as a base layer in cold weather and a stand-alone shirt in mild weather, and who prefer to throw gear in the wash rather than baby it. Section hikers, winter day-trippers and runners who sometimes hike will get the most from it. Thru-hikers who go days between washes and want maximum odor resistance should look at a 100% merino shirt, as should anyone who wants the absolute lowest base weight.

## Verdict

The Technical Merino 150 LS is a well-built hybrid for people who want wool's comfort cues with polyester's durability. It is a sound choice for cool-weather training and everyday hiking, but long-distance hikers chasing odor resistance or the lightest possible kit have better-suited options.
