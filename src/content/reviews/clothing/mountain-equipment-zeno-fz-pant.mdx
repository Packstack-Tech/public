## Overview

The Zeno FZ Pant is Mountain Equipment's backup-plan rain trouser: a 2.5-layer recycled-nylon shell with full-length side zips, so it goes on over boots without a fight. At 8.1 oz (230 g) it sits in the middle of the rain-pant field, lighter than the mountaineering shells and heavier than the true ultralight bags-of-air. Its trade-off is a sturdier face fabric and a roomy, easy-on design in exchange for a few extra ounces and a zipper that some owners have found fiddly.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** A 20,000 mm hydrostatic head is a generous number for a rain pant, and owners report it holds up. European buyers describe the Zeno as waterproof and windproof, with reviewers using it for trekking, hillwalking and winter hiking. One wearer took the women's version through a Scottish Isles February and was heading to Everest Base Camp next. The 50D face fabric is thicker than the 7–15D films in ultralight shells, which is where much of the weight comes from. It also reads as the more forgiving choice for brush and sitting on wet rock.

**Zips and layering.** The full-length two-way YKK side zips are the defining feature. They open far enough to step into the pants with boots or trail runners on, and the internal and external flaps keep water off the zipper line. A two-way slider also gives a way to vent below the knee or at the thigh when a climb turns the inside of the pants into a sauna. Retailers who stock them single out the zips as the main selling point for changeable weather. The exception is a buyer who reported a faulty zip and called the hardware fiddly with gloves on, so the zippers are the first place to look for problems.

**Breathability.** A 12,000 g/m²/24hr RET figure puts the Zeno in ordinary PU-laminate territory rather than premium-membrane territory. One UK ultralight retailer rates the breathability as good for its class, with moisture managed effectively during steady effort. Side-zip venting does much of the real work here. It matters most on uphill sections, where rain pants are at their most miserable.

**Fit and mobility.** Mountain Equipment calls the cut Active, with articulated knees and a gusseted crotch. Backcountry lists the fit as semi-fitted, and European retailers describe it as slim. That mix explains the split in owner feedback. Most buyers praise the cut and range of motion, while one reviewer found it badly cut. Anyone wearing bulky insulated pants underneath should size up. The elastic waist with drawcord keeps the pants up without a belt, and the adjustable hem cinches the legs over shoes.

**Durability.** The fabric sits between very light and very durable, and the shell is sturdy enough for regular trekking. One retailer advises against sustained bushwhacking or scrambling, which is fair for a 230 g laminate. These are pants for the trail, not for off-trail scrub.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ only in leg length. The Standard weighs 230 g, and the M / Long weighs 257 g, a gain of 27 g (about 1 oz) for the extra length. Hikers whose legs run long and who would otherwise see ankle exposed with the standard inseam should take the Long. A longer leg also means more fabric to keep clear of mud and the heel of a boot. Everyone else, including most average-height hikers, should take the Standard and save the ounce. Buyers who wanted the long cut specifically cite it as a reason to choose the Zeno over competitors that don't offer one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-length two-way zips make on-and-off over footwear easy
- 20,000 mm waterproof rating
- 50D recycled face fabric is tougher than most ultralight rain pants
- Articulated knees and gusseted crotch for good mobility
- Long-leg version available
- PFC-free DWR; recycled fabric
- Solid value, which buyers cite repeatedly

**Cons**

- 230 g is not ultralight; some rain pants weigh far less
- 12,000 g/m² RET breathability is average
- Zipper hardware has drawn complaints of fiddliness and one reported failure
- Fit runs slim to semi-fitted, and one buyer found it badly cut
- Not built for heavy brush or scrambling

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season hikers and trekkers in wet climates (Scotland, the Pacific Northwest, the Appalachians) who want rain pants they can pull on without taking off their boots. It also suits shoulder-season hikers who expect to wear them often, and anyone who wants a long-leg option. Gram-counters chasing a sub-150 g rain kit, and people who value a heavy-duty fabric above all else, should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Zeno FZ Pant is a practical, fairly priced rain trouser whose full-length zips make it easier to live with than most. It is a little heavy for the ultralight crowd, and its zipper is worth checking before the first long trip. For wet-weather trekkers who value convenience and durability over the last ounce, it is a good buy.
