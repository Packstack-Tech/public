## Overview

The SoftTop Pillowcase is the foundation of Zenbivy's three-piece camp pillow: a removable, machine-washable shell that holds an inflatable bladder and a soft topper, whether that topper is Zenbivy's down pillow or a rolled-up puffy. It weighs 28 g in Regular and 42 g in Large, and it is built for backpackers who sleep on a Zenbivy bed. The trade-off is that it is only a case. The bladder and the topper are separate purchases, and the clips that keep it in place are designed for Zenbivy's sheet.

## Performance

The case solves a problem that all-in-one camp pillows share: they are hard to wash. Anyone who has put a few weeks of sunscreen, sweat and trail grime into a sealed inflatable knows the issue. Here the shell comes off and goes in a washing machine. The pillow is also built from parts that can be swapped or left at home.

The fabric choice follows the same logic. The sleeping surface is 50D polyester pongee, which Zenbivy describes as feeling like a pillowcase from home. The side panels drop to 25D to save grams. In practice the head rests on the heavier, softer cloth, and the panels the head never touches stay light. Neither fabric has a coating or DWR finish, so the case doesn't have the slick, crinkly feel of a coated dry bag.

The catenary cut-out is the 2026 redesign's main change. Older pillowcases were closed sleeves that had to be stuffed and then re-opened to tweak the bladder. The curved opening lets a hiker slide in a down jacket or fleece and add or remove air without taking the whole assembly apart. It also removes fabric, which helps explain the weight savings. Zenbivy's complete builds start at 2.3 oz for the Minimalist version (case and bladder), 4.1 oz for the Light version and 5.7 oz for the Overstuffed version.

The headboard clips attach the case to a Zenbivy sheet, so the pillow stays put on a slick pad instead of migrating off it overnight. This is a real advantage for restless sleepers and side sleepers, who move their pillow more than back sleepers do. The catch is that the clips are designed for Zenbivy's own sheet system. A hiker on a different quilt-and-pad setup gets a washable, soft case but not the anchoring.

Reviews of the full SoftTop system focus on support. Coverage in an outdoor-travel review praised its comfort for side sleepers and said it gives more support than most backpacking pillows while staying light and packable. A separate backpacking pillow guide also named it among the lightest and most affordable options in its class. Those verdicts describe the system, not the case alone, but the case is the part that makes the system modular.

## Choosing a Version

Regular (28 g, 14 x 9.5 x 6.5 in) is the standard backpacking pillow. It is the pick for most solo hikers, for back sleepers and for anyone counting every gram on a long trail. The Large (42 g, 18 x 10.5 x 7 in) adds 14 g and a noticeably bigger footprint. It suits side sleepers and broad-shouldered hikers who tend to slip off a small pillow, and it matches a roomier feel at camp. It also holds a bulkier topper such as a stuffed jacket more easily. Hikers who aren't sure should start with the Regular, since a bigger case also means a bigger bladder to carry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 28 g in Regular, 42 g in Large, with side panels cut from lighter 25D fabric
- Soft 50D pongee against the face
- Removable and machine washable
- Catenary cut-out makes it easier to load a topper and adjust the bladder
- Headboard clips keep the pillow on a Zenbivy sheet
- Works with a down jacket or fleece in place of a dedicated topper
- $19 for the case

**Cons**

- Case only; the bladder and topper cost extra
- Clips are designed for Zenbivy sheets and don't help on other setups
- Adds a third component to a system that a stuff sack of clothes already replaces
- Neither fabric has a coating or DWR finish, so it is not a moisture barrier
- Colors and sizes have been intermittently backordered

## Who Should Buy This

This is for Zenbivy bed owners who want a pillow that stays on the mattress, washes easily and can be built to a particular height and firmness. It also suits side sleepers who find a stuff sack of clothes too flat. Hikers who sleep on other quilt systems will get less from it, because the clips are the main feature and they have no sheet to attach to. Gram-counters who are happy with a stuffed puffy jacket in a stuff sack can skip it.

## Verdict

The SoftTop Pillowcase is a thoughtful, light and fairly priced shell that delivers on washability and stays put on a Zenbivy sheet. It is a clear recommendation for Zenbivy sleepers and a marginal one for everyone else.
