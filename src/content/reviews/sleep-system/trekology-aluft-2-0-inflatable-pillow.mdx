## Overview

The Trekology ALUFT 2.0 is a larger-than-average, ergonomically shaped inflatable pillow for backpackers who want real head-and-neck support without paying boutique prices. At roughly $18–20, it costs half or less of the better-known name-brand inflatables that perform about the same. The trade-off is weight: it is not the lightest pillow on the trail, and its generous 4-inch loft will not suit every sleeper.

## Performance

**Comfort and feel.** The ALUFT 2.0 fuses a rubber-like bladder directly to a stretch-nylon outer, rather than sandwiching a loose bladder inside a shell. The result is a pillow that is somewhat quieter than many budget inflatables and easier to wash and maintain. That matters: few things are more irritating in a tent at 2 a.m. than a crinkling pillow. The contoured shape is wide enough to cradle the head and sit comfortably around the neck and shoulders.

As with any inflatable, firmness is the whole game. Reviewers found the pillow comfortable when it is not fully inflated; topped off, it turns stiff and unkind to the neck, while a partial fill feels closer to a home down pillow. Users who report solid sleep typically settle around 80 percent inflation, enough to avoid a rock-hard surface. The click-button valve makes mid-night adjustment easy without a full deflation.

The 4-inch loft deserves attention. It is a boon for side sleepers, but some users found it too tall, particularly stomach sleepers and smaller-framed people. Letting out a little air to split the difference has a catch: the fill can migrate toward the edges and leave the center unsupported. The better strategy is to find the right firmness at the first inflation and stick with it.

**Valve and inflation.** The headline feature is the proprietary double-decked valve, which clicks once to inflate and clicks again to release air. It is a modest but real step up from the twist caps and fiddly valves common on cheap pillows. Inflation is quick, at three to five breaths for a full pillow, which spares the lightheadedness that comes with blowing up a large pillow at elevation after a long day.

**Stability and grip.** Raised rubber dots on the underside do a fairly good job of keeping the pillow from skating around, and the detachable elastic strap loops it to the sleeping pad. The system works best on slick pad surfaces and for active sleepers. Results vary with pad width and style, though, and restless sleepers on narrower pads report the pillow can still wander off in the night.

**Packability and weight.** Folded into thirds and rolled, the pillow shrinks to 5 × 2 inches, smaller than a soda can, which is impressive given the 16 × 12-inch inflated footprint. The included stuff sack is another matter. It runs snug, every bit of air has to be squeezed out before it will close, and re-rolling is common in the first few attempts. A slightly larger sack would help. On weight, the manufacturer claims 120 g, while reviewers' scales have put the pillow alone closer to 3.7–3.8 oz (105–108 g), with the strap and sack adding a little more. Either figure is competitive at this price, but the Sea to Summit Aeros Premium weighs roughly half as much.

**Durability.** Owners report long service lives. One Trailspace reviewer used the pillow for more than two years across trips in Alaska, Arizona, Nevada, California and Texas, and another logged 50-plus nights without trouble. The main long-term complaint is cosmetic: the fabric picks up stains from sunscreen, bug repellent, sweat and hair products, and it does not clean up as easily as buyers might hope. A thin pillowcase or a merino buff draped over the top is a practical fix.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Performance comparable to pillows that cost two to three times as much
- Click-button valve allows fast, one-handed inflation and deflation
- Contoured shape suits back and side sleepers
- Rubber dots and a pad strap keep it in place on most setups
- Packs to 5 × 2 inches, with stuff sack and strap included
- Bonded TPU and stretch-nylon construction runs quieter than many budget inflatables
- Multi-year durability reported across varied climates

**Cons**

- The 4-inch loft is too tall for some sleepers, and partial deflation can shift fill away from the center
- Comfort peaks near 80 percent inflation; fully inflated, it feels stiff
- Pad strap effectiveness varies with pad width and surface
- Fabric stains over time and resists thorough cleaning
- Stuff sack is a tight fit
- Heavier than the lightest premium inflatables

## Who Should Buy This

The ALUFT 2.0 suits backpackers who want a proper inflatable pillow rather than a stuff sack filled with a puffy jacket, and who would rather not spend $50 or more to get one. Side sleepers who like extra loft and a wide, contoured top get the most from it. Gram-counters chasing every ounce should look at the Sea to Summit Aeros or similarly minimal options. Stomach sleepers and anyone who prefers a low profile should also look elsewhere, as should car campers, who can bring far more comfortable bedding.

## Verdict

The ALUFT 2.0 is a capable, durable backpacking pillow that delivers more than its price suggests. The click-button valve, grip system and shaped top are touches most budget competitors skip. Buyers should plan to dial in the inflation on the first night, and accept the extra weight as the price of a bigger, more comfortable pillow.
