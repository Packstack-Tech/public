## Overview

Decathlon's Ultralichte waterdichte opbergzak is a flat, roll-top dry bag that holds 7 liters and weighs 40 g. It's sold under the retailer's Simond mountain line for €9.99, which makes it one of the cheapest ways to put a waterproof layer around a quilt, a down jacket or a pile of spare clothes. The trade-off is the waterproof rating. IPX5 covers rain and splashes. It doesn't cover a dunking.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** Decathlon rates the bag IPX5 and says it protects against rain for one hour. The seams are sealed, and the fabric carries a 5,000 mm coating. For a backpacker, that translates to a bag that will keep a sleeping bag or insulation layer dry through a day of rain, or a few hours of wet pack-liner duty. IPX5 is a water-jet standard, though. It isn't a submersion rating, and the bag shouldn't be trusted through a packraft capsize or a deep stream crossing. Hikers who need that should look at heavier welded dry bags.

**Weight and fabric.** At 40 g, the bag costs very little to carry. The fabric is a 40-denier polyamide with reinforced yarn. That's sturdier than the 10–20 denier silnylon sacks favored by gram-counters, and it's one reason Decathlon can sell it this cheaply. Decathlon says the bag was tested by 20 people across two treks, including a 16-day, 240 km route with 12,000 meters of climbing. Owners rate it 4.8 out of 5 across roughly 700 reviews on Decathlon's Dutch site. Those are retailer reviews, so they skew toward satisfied buyers. They do suggest the bag holds up to ordinary trekking.

**Closure and packing.** The roll-top is the standard three-fold design: push the air out, fold three times, clip shut. Squeezing out air is useful for compressible items such as a down jacket or quilt. The flat, pillow-like shape stacks well inside a pack, and it avoids the dead space a cylindrical sack leaves. A black pull tab at the base holds the bag in place while the contents are pulled out. The opening is wide, so stuffing a quilt in or pulling a jacket out doesn't turn into a wrestling match.

**Dual use.** The flat profile also lets the bag serve as a pillow case. Stuffed with a puffy and a fleece, it works for the campsite. A 7-liter bag is small, though. It suits a top quilt in a compressed state, a clothing kit or a food bag for a weekend. It won't hold a full sleeping system for a winter trip.

**Dust and sand.** Besides keeping out water, the bag is meant to guard gear against dust and sand. That's a useful side benefit on desert or coastal routes, where fine grit works into down and zippers.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 40 g for 7 liters
- €9.99 price makes it practical to buy several
- Sealed seams and a 5,000 mm coating handle prolonged rain
- Reinforced 40-denier fabric is tougher than most sub-50 g sacks
- Roll-top compresses contents and helps push out air
- Flat shape works as a pillow
- Two-year warranty

**Cons**

- IPX5 is not a submersion rating
- 40-denier fabric is heavier than the lightest silnylon sacks of the same volume
- Seven liters is too small for a full sleeping bag
- Bright yellow isn't subtle, though it does make the bag easy to find in a dark pack

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who want an inexpensive, light waterproof liner for a quilt, a spare layer set or electronics, and who expect rain rather than immersion. It also suits anyone building a modular pack with several color-coded sacks, since buying three or four costs about the same as one premium dry bag. Packrafters, canoeists and anyone fording rivers should skip it and buy a fully welded dry bag with a rated submersion depth. Hikers chasing the last few grams may also prefer a thinner, lighter fabric.

## Verdict

At €9.99 and 40 g, this is a sensible, well-reviewed rain-proof organizer for gear that must stay dry. It won't survive deliberate submersion, but that isn't what it's for. Buy it for wet-weather insurance on a trek, and buy a heavier bag if the water is going to be deeper than a puddle.
