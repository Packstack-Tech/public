## Overview

The AlpLight Down Jacket is Patagonia's answer to the ounce-counting alpine crowd. It is a hoodless, stripped-down puffy that weighs 270 g and stuffs into its own pocket. Reviewers describe it as a down jacket on a diet, and that fits. The cost of the diet is a shell fabric that needs gentle handling and a feature list that stops at the essentials.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The AlpLight is a cool-weather layer, not a cold-weather one. Switchback Travel's tester stayed comfortable in it over a thin merino base layer on trail breaks at roughly 50 to 60°F. The same tester also used it as a camp midlayer under a hardshell on colder nights. Its fill is competitive for the weight class. The women's version carries about 3 oz of 800-fill, and Switchback set that against rivals such as Arc'teryx's Cerium LT (2.6 oz of 850-fill) and Patagonia's own Down Sweater (3.4 oz of 800-fill). The men's jacket carries 100 g, a figure Patagonia confirmed to a Backcountry reviewer. Not everyone finds it warm enough. Advnture's reviewer rated it as not super warm, and one Backcountry owner wished it were thicker than his other Patagonia down jackets. Treat it as a breeze-and-chill layer for belays, summits and camp, not a stand-in for a belay parka.

**Weight and packability.** This is the main reason to buy it. Engearment pegged the jacket at 9.5 oz in a men's medium and called it one of the lightest down jackets commonly sold in North America. Only Montbell made lighter ones. At 270 g it sits close to that figure. The jacket compresses into its own pocket, and a loop lets it clip to a harness or the back of a pack. Owners say it packs very small. One notes that the loft takes a moment to return after a long squish, which is normal for a jacket this light.

**Weather resistance.** The shell is thin, and wet weather is where that shows. The DWR is PFC-free, so it sheds a light flurry or a brief sprinkle but not sustained rain. Switchback's testers flagged weather protection as subpar. Advnture listed the lack of water resistance as a drawback. Hikers who expect drizzle should plan on a rain shell over it. The overstuffed collar and hem drawcord do a good job of sealing out drafts, and Switchback found they kept the wind off in gusty Patagonian conditions. Without a hood, the neck is the only barrier up top.

**Durability.** A 10-denier shell is the price of a 270 g jacket. Engearment's assessment is that it is durable for its weight but won't last forever if worn roughly. Switchback also called the fabric delicate. One REI reviewer reported down leaking through the seams from the start. After three months, the seams themselves were coming apart. That is a single report, but it fits the profile of an ultralight shell. Pack it away from sharp edges and skip the bushwhacking.

**Features and fit.** The feature set is minimal. The pullover version has only a zipper, a pocket and a waist cinch cord. The full-zip jacket is similarly sparse. Owners generally call the fit true to size. Advnture suggests sizing up for a roomier cut, and one Backcountry buyer found it snug over a base layer and a long-sleeve shirt. Plan on a size up if layering it over a fleece.

**Sustainability.** The shell is made from recycled fishing nets (NetPlus). The down is traceable and the sewing is Fair Trade Certified. Backcountry notes that the recycled nylon, PFC-free water resistance and traceable down all meet Patagonia's sustainability standards.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 270 g with 100 g of 800-fill down
- Packs into its own pocket, with a harness loop
- Overstuffed collar and hem drawcord block drafts
- Recycled shell, PFC-free DWR, traceable down, Fair Trade sewn
- Layers cleanly under a shell

**Cons**

- 10-denier shell is delicate and snags easily
- Thin water protection; not for sustained rain
- No hood and few features
- Modest warmth for cold camps
- $299 is a lot for a fragile layer

## Who Should Buy This

The AlpLight suits alpine climbers, fast-and-light peakbaggers and weight-conscious backpackers who want a puffy that disappears in the pack and comes out for belays, summit stops and cool evenings. Three-season thru-hikers who carry a separate shell and treat their gear carefully will like the weight savings. Anyone who needs a hood, or who regularly brushes against rock and brush, should look elsewhere. The same goes for hikers who want real warmth in sub-freezing temperatures. Patagonia's Down Sweater is heavier but sturdier. A synthetic layer is the better call for persistent wet.

## Verdict

The AlpLight delivers on its premise: 270 g, small packed size, and enough loft to take the chill off. It is a specialist's jacket, and a pricey one, and it rewards careful handling. Buy it if grams matter more than ruggedness. Otherwise, a sturdier puffy will last longer.
