## Overview

The Pack-It III is Regatta's entry-level packable rain shell — a simple, no-frills jacket that stuffs into its own pocket and sells for around £20-25 in the UK. It's aimed squarely at casual walkers, commuters, and travelers who want a "just in case" layer in the bottom of a bag, not thru-hikers chasing grams and hydrostatic head numbers. If you're comparing this to a Patagonia Torrentshell or an OR Helium, you're shopping in the wrong aisle — this is a budget shell, and it performs like one.

## Performance

**Waterproofing:** Isolite 10000 puts this jacket well below the 20,000mm+ ratings you'd want for sustained mountain weather, and user experiences reflect that split. Several buyers report it holding up fine in everyday showers — one Trekkinn reviewer called it <cite index="10-9">"100% waterproof and very comfortable, with a small bag to store it and take up little space."</cite> Others weren't as lucky: one Regatta site reviewer flatly stated <cite index="5-2">"I wore this on a walk and it's not waterproof at all."</cite> My read on this split is that the DWR coating and taped seams do their job in light-to-moderate rain, but the fabric likely wets out or the coating degrades faster than a true hardshell once you hit sustained downpours or heavy pack-strap abrasion. This tracks with the Isolite 10000 rating — it's a step above a windbreaker, not a step below a 3-layer Gore-Tex shell.

**Breathability:** At 10,000 g/m²/24hrs, this is on the low end even for budget shells, and one detailed user review confirms it: a Bike24 reviewer testing it for cycling and walks noted it kept them dry twice in fairly heavy rain, but added it gets warm during intense activity because <cite index="8-5">it "dышит плохо"</cite> — breathes poorly. Translation for hikers: fine for a steady walking pace in cool weather, clammy fast if you're climbing switchbacks or moving with any real effort.

**Fit and cut:** This is the most consistent complaint across reviews, and it's all over the map. One German reviewer found <cite index="11-9,11-10">the S "too tight" while an M "fits tight at the bottom, chest loose, shoulders medium and the sleeves are rather short."</cite> Another found the opposite problem — ordering their normal size resulted in a jacket cut like <cite index="11-12,11-13">"far too large," with a size M measuring like an XL.</cite> A US buyer, on the other hand, was happy that <cite index="19-8">the sleeves were long enough, since "most jackets sleeves aren't long."</cite> Bottom line: if you're between sizes or have unusual proportions, this is not a jacket to order online sight-unseen without checking the return policy.

**Packability:** This is where the Pack-It III earns its name and its keep. It's reported to pack neatly into its stuff sack, and buyers consistently use it as a stash-in-a-daypack or stash-in-a-suitcase item — one review specifically mentioned it <cite index="1-9">fitting into a schoolbag without taking up a lot of space</cite>. At 298 g it's not ultralight by trail standards, but for the price it's a reasonable trade-off if packed size and low cost matter more to you than cutting every last gram.

**Hardware and details:** The zipper storm flap draws specific criticism — a German reviewer described the <cite index="8-11,8-12">sealing tape under the zipper constantly getting stuck on the inside of the zipper, requiring extra care when opening or closing it.</cite> Pocket configuration is also basic: no interior pocket, and <cite index="5-4">two outer pockets but no inner ones</cite> on some versions, with at least one reviewer noting the pockets lack zippers entirely.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely budget-friendly — often under £25
- Packs down small into its own stuff sack, easy to stash in a daypack or suitcase
- Taped seams and adjustable hood are nice touches at this price point
- Adequate for light rain, dog walks, festivals, and travel "just in case" use
- Long sleeve length praised by at least one taller reviewer

**Cons**

- Isolite 10000 / 10,000 g/m²/24hrs breathability is low for anything beyond casual walking pace
- Waterproofing performance is inconsistent across user reports — some stayed bone dry, others got soaked
- Sizing runs unpredictably; multiple reviewers report cuts that don't match the size chart
- Zipper storm flap tape reportedly snags and sticks
- No interior pockets, and outer pockets may lack zippers
- At 298 g, it's heavier than true ultralight rain shells without matching their protection

## Who Should Buy This

This jacket makes sense for casual walkers, commuters, festival-goers, or travelers who want a cheap insurance policy against a passing shower and don't want to spend $150+ on a technical shell they'll wear twice a year. It's a reasonable pick to toss in a school bag, suitcase, or car trunk. It is not the right call for backpackers or thru-hikers who need reliable weather protection over consecutive days, sustained exertion breathability, or confidence in a genuine downpour — for that, look toward a 20,000mm+ rated shell from a dedicated outdoor brand.

## Verdict

The Pack-It III does what its name promises — it packs away small and it's cheap — but the Isolite 10000 fabric and mixed user reports on both waterproofing and sizing mean it's a casual-use rain jacket, not a trail-ready shell. For the price, it's a fair "just in case" layer; for serious hiking in unpredictable weather, spend more elsewhere. I'd put it at a 5.5/10 — solid for what it is, but not what this audience is usually shopping for.
