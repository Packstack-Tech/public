## Overview

The Floorsheet Tyvek is a plain white sheet of 100% polyethylene Tyvek, sold in four sizes by the German brand Tatonka. It is aimed at backpackers and bike tourists who want a disposable-feeling layer between tent floor and dirt without carrying a branded, grommeted footprint. The trade-off is simple: very low weight and a PFAS-free material, but no corner tabs, no tie-outs and a price well above a roll of hardware-store house wrap.

## Performance

Tyvek's appeal on the trail is a short list: it is light, it sheds water, and it is hard to tear. Tatonka describes the sheet as water-repellent and tear-resistant, and the material is a nonwoven of high-density polyethylene fibers that resists abrasion and ageing. The company's own weights work out to about 44 g per square meter at every size, so the 180 x 220 cm sheet comes in at 175 g (6.2 oz) and the largest at 230 g (8.1 oz). Those numbers are for a sheet with nothing attached to it: no stuff sack, no webbing, no grommets.

The job is protecting a tent floor from abrasion, grit and moisture. Tatonka also points to a second use: a sheet under the tent keeps condensation from rising into the vestibules. A third tip from the manufacturer is to slip a sleeping pad between the groundsheet and the tent floor, which stops the pad from skating around inside the tent.

Tyvek is stiff and a little crinkly out of the package. Owners of Tyvek groundsheets in general report that the sheet quiets down after a wash without detergent, an air dry and some vigorous crumpling. They also note that one face is slick and the other soft, and that the soft side picks up twigs and dirt. Neither point is a deal-breaker, but both are worth knowing before the first muddy pitch.

Cutting is where the Floorsheet earns its keep. The sheet is sold as a single seamless piece and can be trimmed to match the inner tent. Trimming it a couple of inches inside the floor perimeter is the standard advice for any Tyvek footprint, because an oversized sheet can channel rain under the tent floor instead of away from it. A pair of scissors turns the 240 x 220 cm size into a custom footprint for a wide two-person shelter, or the offcuts into a pack liner or a pad for sitting on wet ground.

The material choice is also a selling point for some buyers. Tatonka states the sheet is free of PFCs and PFAS, which sets it apart from coated nylon and polyester footprints that depend on waterproofing treatments. Tyvek is also a single-polymer product, which simplifies disposal at the end of its life.

What the Floorsheet does not offer is attachment. Tatonka's listings mention no corner loops or grommets, so anyone who wants to stake the sheet down or pair it with a pole-pitched shelter needs to add their own tabs or simply let the tent's weight and stakes hold it in place. Cheaper US-made Tyvek kits arrive with grommet tabs, which is the clearest functional gap.

## Choosing a Version

All four sizes share the same 220 cm length; only the width changes, and the weight rises in lockstep.

- **180 x 220 cm (Tyvek III, 175 g):** The mid-large size, and the best match for a solo shelter that needs width for gear or a vestibule, or for a small two-person tent after trimming. At 175 g it costs about 60 g more than the narrowest sheet.
- **240 x 220 cm (Tyvek IV, 230 g):** The largest and the one to buy for wide two-person tents, or when the plan is to cut one sheet into a footprint plus a ground cloth for the vestibule. It is also the best value per square meter at €60.
- **120 x 220 cm (Tyvek I, 115 g) and 150 x 220 cm (Tyvek II, 145 g):** The narrow sizes cover a single sleeper on a bivy or tarp setup. The 120 cm sheet fits a bivy or the footprint of a slim solo tent. The 150 cm sheet covers most one-person tents with room to trim.

Anyone who owns a tent with a flared or tapered floor should buy one size up and cut. The cost of that extra width is a few dozen grams, the cost of a sheet that is too narrow is a replacement.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About 44 g/m², so the footprint adds little to base weight
- PFC/PFAS-free polyethylene construction
- Cuts cleanly to size from a single seamless sheet
- Four widths cover bivy to wide two-person shelters
- Tear- and abrasion-resistant, with water-repellent surface
- Packs small

**Cons**

- No grommets, loops or tie-out points
- Stiff and noisy until broken in
- €35–60 list price is steep for a sheet of Tyvek
- Soft side collects debris
- Plain white sheet shows dirt quickly

## Who Should Buy This

The Floorsheet suits backpackers who want to size their own footprint and keep the shelter system light, especially those who care about PFAS-free gear or who ride and hike in Europe, where Tatonka stocks are easy to find. It also works for bivy and tarp users who want a cut-to-fit ground layer. Anyone chasing the lowest cost per gram, or the convenience of a pre-grommeted kit, should look at a roll of house wrap or a US-made Tyvek kit. Hikers who rely on a tent-specific, fitted footprint will find this generic sheet more work than it is worth.

## Verdict

The Floorsheet Tyvek is a straightforward, lightweight groundsheet with a clean materials story and the flexibility to cut to the shape of any tent. It is not cheap for what it is, and it leaves attachment points to the buyer. For European ultralighters who want a PFAS-free option off the shelf, it is a sound pick.
