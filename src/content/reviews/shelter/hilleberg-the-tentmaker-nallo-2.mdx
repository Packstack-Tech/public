## Overview

The Nallo 2 is Hilleberg's do-it-all tunnel tent — a two-person, four-season shelter that's been a fixture of the Red Label lineup for decades. It's built for people who camp in genuinely bad weather (wind, sustained rain, shoulder-season snow) but still want to carry something reasonable for multi-day trips. Think Scottish Highlands, Scandinavian fjells, or Rockies traverses where you can't count on a calm night — not a fair-weather thru-hiking tent.

## Performance

**Weather resistance.** This is the Nallo's whole reason for existing. One reviewer who pitched it above Loch Lomond in February <cite index="11-1,11-2">described being forced to pitch up in wet and windy conditions where it held firm, providing a very welcome refuge</cite>. The Kerlon 1200 fly is the standout piece of engineering here — <cite index="11-13,11-14">a 10,000mm triple silicon coated flysheet made from Hilleberg's own Kerlon 1200 ripstop nylon 66 fabric that isn't going to leak</cite>. That confidence shows up across independent tests too: one long-term Trailspace reviewer called it <cite index="12-9,12-10">a weatherproof tent with a fly that's extremely strong and completely impervious to high winds and torrential, tropical rain</cite>.

**Pitching.** Tunnel tents have a reputation for being fiddly because they're not freestanding, but the Nallo 2 is genuinely quick once you know the drill. A Backpacking Light forum user who put real trail time into one wrote that <cite index="2-1">the Nallo 2 is incredibly simple to pitch</cite>, noting <cite index="2-2">tensioners at all four corners, a convenient center-rear staking point for foul-weather setup, and poles that are extremely easy to insert and tension</cite>. After some experimentation with staking order, they got a clean pitch down to <cite index="2-5">about four minutes, total</cite>. The trade-off with any tunnel design is that a good pitch depends on decent staking points — this isn't a tent you can freestand on a rock slab or a wooden platform without real improvisation.

**Space and livability.** For a tent with "2000g minimum weight" stamped on the spec sheet, the interior punches above its weight class. Reddit users summarized it as <cite index="1-1">very spacious, with a large vestibule, and really quick to pitch</cite>. A UK reviewer measured it out at <cite index="11-12">sufficient headroom, a generous 220cm length and over 30 sq ft of floor space</cite> — enough for two people and gear, though at ~100cm peak height you're sitting, not standing.

**Ventilation and condensation.** This is where the Nallo shows its age a bit compared to newer American ultralight designs. One Reddit summary of the Hilleberg lineup flagged that <cite index="1-14">the silnylon sags a bit when wet, and they're also not as well ventilated as a lot of newer, especially American tents</cite>. A Trailspace reviewer running the tent through near-freezing, wet alpine conditions in Taiwan found that <cite index="12-11">in freezing temperatures, this produced morning frost on the inside of the fly</cite> — and noted that in true winter use, <cite index="12-15">the condensation on the fly will freeze up</cite>. If you're camping below freezing regularly, budget extra time each morning for de-icing the fly before you pack it.

**Durability.** Multiple secondhand listings and long-term owners describe Nallo 2s still going strong after a decade-plus of use, which tracks with Hilleberg's reputation. The fabric itself has real numbers behind it too — Hilleberg's own materials info notes Kerlon 1200 carries roughly 12 kg of tear strength, well above typical "expedition-grade" fabrics, though the brand is upfront that the lighter 9mm poles and zippers mean the Nallo doesn't match the burlier Nammatj for true expedition abuse.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Bombproof weather protection for the weight — genuinely trustworthy above treeline or in sustained storms
- Fast, intuitive pitch once you learn the staking sequence (roughly 4 minutes with practice)
- Spacious interior and vestibule for a sub-2.5kg packed tent
- Kerlon 1200 fabric and DAC poles are built to outlast most owners' hiking careers
- Flexible pitching options (all-in-one, outer-first, fly-only)

**Cons**

- Not freestanding — needs real stakes and decent ground, which limits pitch locations
- Ventilation lags behind newer double-wall ultralight designs; expect condensation in cold, damp conditions
- Silnylon fly sags when wet and needs occasional re-tensioning mid-night
- Very expensive relative to ultralight competitors with similar weight
- At ~100cm peak height, it's a sit-up, not stand-up, shelter

## Who Should Buy This

The Nallo 2 makes the most sense for backpackers, hunters, and photographers who camp in genuinely variable or harsh weather and need one tent that works from summer alpine trips to shoulder-season snow. If your trips are mostly fair-weather three-season backpacking on established trails, you can find lighter, cheaper tents that will serve you just as well. But if you're planning a Cape Wrath Trail, a winter Highlands trip, or anything above treeline where wind and driving rain are a real possibility, the Nallo's weather chops justify the premium.

## Verdict

The Nallo 2 earns its reputation as one of the most trusted four-season backpacking tents available — it's fast to pitch, remarkably weather-tight for its weight, and durable enough to be a decades-long investment. It's not the lightest or best-ventilated option in its class, and the price will make budget-conscious hikers wince, but for anyone who prioritizes storm-worthiness over gram-counting, it's an easy 8.3/10.
