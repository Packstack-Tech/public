## Overview

The Exped FlexMat is a z-fold closed-cell foam pad. It's built for warm-weather minimalists, and for anyone who wants a puncture-proof layer beneath an inflatable. At 349 g (12.3 oz), it ranks among the lightest full-length foam pads on the market. The trade-off is the usual one for foam: it packs bulky and sleeps firm, and its R-value of 1.5 limits it to summer and shoulder-season use on its own.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** At 12.3 oz, the FlexMat undercuts most full-length foam pads, and the accordion fold gives it a compact 5 x 20.5 x 6 in footprint. Reviewers at Halfway Anywhere call it one of the most compact full-size foam pads available. Foam still packs like foam, though. It lives on the outside of a pack, strapped under the lid or against a side panel, and that's where it takes the abuse. Because it can't puncture, that's fine.

**Warmth.** The 1.5 R-value and 41°F rating put the FlexMat in summer territory. Exped calls it a three-season pad. Most testers would stretch that only in the sense that it works as an under-layer. Used alone, it asks a lot of a sleeper once overnight lows approach the 40s. Used beneath an inflatable, it does what Exped says: R-values are additive, so the 1.5 stacks on top of the air pad's own rating. For a shoulder-season trip, that's a noticeable lift from a 12-ounce add-on.

**Comfort.** At 0.7 inches, the molded dimples give a low-profile cushion, not a mattress. Reviewers note it's wider than the average regular-width foam pad, which helps restless sleepers, but side sleepers find it less comfortable than a thicker pad. Owners on REI describe the foam as a bit stiffer than Therm-a-Rest's Z Lite-style pads, and one buyer said they put the FlexMat on the bottom of a two-pad stack for that reason. Like all closed-cell foam, it compresses with repeated use, so a pad that feels springy in the first week will sleep flatter by season's end.

**Camp use.** The z-fold is the real selling point. There's no inflating, no leak check, and no pad repair kit. The pad unfolds in seconds and doubles as a dry seat at breaks. One REI owner uses it layered with a camp chair for extra padding and R-value. For anyone who has spent a cold dawn hunting a slow leak, a pad that simply can't deflate has real appeal.

**Modding.** The foam cuts cleanly. Gear retailer Trailbound Project suggests trimming a full-size closed-cell pad to about 14 inches wide and halving it, carrying one half in three-season conditions and both in winter. The FlexMat's uniform dimple pattern makes it a reasonable candidate for that kind of trail surgery, though it voids the full-length use.

**Materials.** The foam is IXPE (cross-linked polyethylene), PFAS-free and DWR-free, and Exped offsets the production and shipping emissions through myclimate. The pad sheds moisture as closed-cell foam always does, so it makes a sound ground sheet for a wet pitch.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 349 g, among the lightest full-length foam pads
- Can't puncture or leak
- Folds to 5 x 20.5 x 6 in and deploys in seconds
- Wider than most regular foam pads
- Adds 1.5 R-value and puncture protection beneath an inflatable
- Inexpensive, with a 5-year warranty

**Cons**

- R 1.5 is too low to use alone below the 40s
- Only 0.7 in thick; less comfortable for side sleepers
- Foam compresses and flattens with repeated use
- Bulky compared with any inflatable, and it needs external pack space
- Slightly stiffer than some competing z-fold pads

## Who Should Buy This

Summer and warm-shoulder-season backpackers who want a simple, fast-deploying pad will like it, as will first-time buyers who want something durable and cheap. It's also a good fit for anyone who stacks it under an inflatable for winter or for abrasive ground. Side sleepers and cold sleepers should look to a thicker, higher-R pad or an inflatable. Gram-counters who want maximum comfort per ounce will get more from a modern air pad.

## Verdict

The FlexMat delivers what it promises: a light, durable, unpuncturable pad that works as a minimalist summer bed or a cheap warmth insurance policy under an inflatable. It's not a comfortable pad, and it won't carry a hiker deep into the shoulder seasons alone. For its price and weight, it's an easy pick as a backup or a second layer.
