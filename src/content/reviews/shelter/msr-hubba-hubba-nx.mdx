## Overview

The MSR Hubba Hubba NX is a freestanding, double-wall, 3-season two-person tent that has been a fixture at trailheads for close to two decades. It is built for couples and hiking partners who want a livable shelter that pitches fast and lasts, not for gram-counters. The headline trade-off is plain: a minimum weight of 1,540 g buys interior space and ease of use that lighter tents struggle to match.

## Performance

### Livability

Interior space is the Hubba Hubba's calling card. The pole architecture is symmetrical, and the floor holds a 50-inch width from head to foot instead of tapering like many dome tents. That non-tapered rectangle is uncommon at this weight, and it shows up where it matters: two sleepers with wide pads aren't fighting over shoulder room, and the near-vertical walls keep a tall sleeper's bag from pressing against the fabric at either end.

At 39 inches of peak height, sitting upright is comfortable across most of the floor, which makes a long rainy afternoon far less claustrophobic than in a low-slung competitor. The trade-off is length. At 84 inches, the floor is a few inches shorter than some rivals, and hikers over 6'2" should measure their pad and bag before buying.

### Setup

The hubbed Easton Syclone pole system is the reason owners call this tent quick to pitch. A single hub-connected pole forms the freestanding skeleton, and the inner clips on. Color-coded clips match the pole to the inner eyelets, which removes most of the guesswork. Two people can go from stuff sack to staked out in about two minutes.

The sore spot is the rainfly attachment. Grommets at the pole ends are fiddlier than buckles, especially with cold, wet or muddy fingers, and MSR already uses a lightweight buckle on the stuff sack. The pitch is also inner-first, so in a driving rain the inner can pick up moisture before the fly goes on.

### Weather Resistance

The numbers look modest on paper: 1,200 mm on the 20D fly, 3,000 mm on the bathtub floor. MSR's Xtreme Shield coating is designed to resist breakdown longer than a standard polyurethane coat, and it is a large part of the tent's reputation for holding up season after season.

Buyers shopping used should check the production date. New Hubba-series tents sold in 2019 and early 2020 had a seam-sealing problem. MSR acknowledged it, offered free seam sealing, and has since corrected the issue, so current tents are not affected.

Wind is the tent's weaker suit. The tall, steep walls present a big profile, and the poles flex noticeably when gusts climb past 30 mph, though reviewers report the tent survived those conditions undamaged. Treat it as a tent for sheltered forest camps and benches, not exposed ridges or alpine cols. The fly also doesn't peg down flush to the ground. That gap leaves a path for wind-driven rain in a violent storm, but it also boosts airflow and keeps condensation down.

### Ventilation

Two opposing doors and a high, retractable fly vent move plenty of air. One reviewer saw no condensation on a calm night in the 30s. The large mesh inner panels that make this possible also make the inner drafty, which is welcome in July and less so in October.

### Floor Durability

The 30D floor is thin, and owners generally treat a footprint as mandatory on rocky or abrasive ground. MSR sells one separately for around $65. It also enables a fly-only pitch that drops carried weight, so budget for it from the start.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Non-tapered 29 sq ft floor with a consistent 50-inch width
- Hubbed pole system and color-coded clips make for an intuitive, fast pitch
- Two doors with a vestibule on each side (8.75 sq ft apiece) let either sleeper exit without climbing over the other
- Strong ventilation from dual doors and a high fly vent
- Freestanding, so it can be repositioned before staking and pitched on hard or sandy ground
- Xtreme Shield coating is built to outlast standard fly coatings
- Fly-and-footprint pitch available to cut weight

**Cons**

- Roughly 300 g heavier than the Big Agnes Copper Spur HV UL2 for comparable floor space
- Tall walls catch wind, and the poles flex in strong gusts
- Corner grommets are slower than buckles in wet or cold conditions
- Thin 30D floor makes a footprint a near-necessity, at extra cost
- The 84-inch length is tight for sleepers over 6'2"
- Mesh inner runs drafty in cold weather

## Who Should Buy This

The Hubba Hubba NX suits two people who value room to sit up, a fast pitch and a tent that survives years of use over shaving grams. Split between two hikers, the 1,540 g minimum weight comes to 770 g each, or about 965 g each at packaged weight, which narrows the gap with ultralight options considerably. It is a sound choice for general 3-season trips in forests, canyons and mid-elevation terrain.

Look elsewhere if the route runs along exposed alpine ridges, if the goal is a sub-10-lb base weight, or if the tent will mostly carry one person. A dedicated solo shelter saves far more weight than a two-person tent shared by one.

## Verdict

The Hubba Hubba NX has stayed a best-seller because the fundamentals are done well: livable geometry, a quick hub pitch and a two-door layout that pay off on night one and night one hundred. The weight penalty against the Copper Spur HV UL2 or NEMO Dragonfly OSMO 2P is real, but for most two-person 3-season trips it is a fair price. Buy it with a footprint, and pitch it out of the wind.
