## Overview

The Injinji Liner Mini-Crew is an ultra-thin, ankle-height toesock that wraps each toe in its own sleeve, so toes can't rub against each other. It's built to sit under a cushioned hiking sock, or to be worn alone on warm days. The headline trade-off is simple: it's very good at one job, between-toe friction, and it pays for that with a fabric thin enough that longevity is the weak point.

## Performance

**Blister prevention.** The five-toe sleeve is the whole argument, and it's the most consistent theme in owner reports. Separating the toes removes skin-on-skin contact, which is where many hikers' hot spots start. One Alpine guide logging more than 1,000 km every summer credits toesocks with effectively ending his blister problems. REI buyers rate the Mini-Crew 4.7 out of 5 across 21 reviews.

The limits matter, though. A Backpacking Light forum member who tried the CoolMax liner still developed blisters and ended up crediting a wool version for the improvement on that hike. Toe sleeves do nothing for heel slip or forefoot pressure. If the shoe fit or lacing is the real culprit, a liner won't fix it.

**Moisture and drying.** The CoolMax EcoMade blend, made with 100% recycled content, is a synthetic that moves sweat away from skin and dries quickly. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers, reviewing the crew-height sibling of this sock, described a snug, second-skin feel with strong wicking and breathability, and noted that synthetic socks often feel like a plastic bag on the foot while this one doesn't. Backpackers report toesocks drying within a few hours in the sun, which helps on stream-crossing days and when rinsing socks in camp.

**Mini-Crew height.** The cuff sits just above the ankle bone. That's enough to keep trail dust and grit from creeping in around the heel in low-cut shoes, while staying cooler than a full crew. It also pairs cleanly with the ankle-height trail socks most thru-hikers wear over a liner.

**Durability.** Thinness cuts both ways. The 200-needle knit gives the fabric more structure than its weight suggests, and some REI reviewers report no visible wear after long days at work and on runs. But Outdoor Gear Lab scored the Liner Crew 6.5 and pointed to the low price as the counterweight to limited durability. Hikers on forums describe holes appearing around the toes after hard multi-day use, particularly when the liner is worn alone. Worn under an outer sock, the liner takes less abrasion, and the outer sock absorbs most of the shoe's wear. The practical approach is to carry a spare pair and treat these as consumables.

**Fit and daily use.** The toe sleeves add a little fabric between each toe, so a roomy toe box matters. Hikers in snug shoes report the extra material feels crowded. Putting them on takes more time than a regular sock, since each toe goes in separately, and that's the one complaint that never really goes away. Owners generally say the routine gets quicker after a week or so.

**Packability.** At 31.2 g, a spare pair costs almost nothing in the pack. Outdoor Gear Lab suggests rotating a fresh pair for hiking and keeping another for camp or sleeping, which is a sensible use of a sock this light.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Eliminates toe-on-toe friction, the most consistently praised benefit
- Wicking, quick-drying CoolMax EcoMade fabric made from recycled content
- 31.2 g with no cushioning, so spares are nearly free to carry
- Mini-Crew cuff blocks debris without the heat of a full crew
- Roughly $12 at retail, so replacing a worn pair is painless

**Cons**

- Thin knit wears through faster than a standalone trail sock, especially at the toes
- Doesn't address heel or forefoot blisters caused by fit or lacing
- Extra fabric between toes feels tight in snug shoes
- Pulling on five toe sleeves takes longer than pulling on a normal sock
- No cushioning when worn solo

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who get blisters or hot spots between their toes, especially on wet, sandy or high-mileage days, should try this first. It also suits anyone building a two-sock system under a cushioned ankle sock, and warm-weather hikers who want a minimal sock in shoes with a roomy toe box. Hikers who want one sock to survive months of daily wear without a backup, or who wear narrow, tight-fitting shoes, should look at Injinji's thicker Trail or Outdoor midweight lines instead.

## Verdict

The Liner Mini-Crew does what it says: it separates the toes, wicks moisture and weighs next to nothing, at a price that makes spares an easy decision. It won't cure every kind of blister, and it won't last as long as a cushioned sock. As a cheap, light first line of defense against toe friction, it earns a 7.5 out of 10.
