## Overview

The CamelBak Crux is a full-featured hydration reservoir for hikers, mountain bikers and backpackers who want a bladder that works without fuss. It comes in 2-liter and 3-liter sizes, and both have a high-flow Big Bite valve, an on/off lever and a wide, handled fill opening. The trade-off is weight and bulk. At 198–211 g, it is built for durability and drinking comfort, not for a sub-10-pound base weight.

## Performance

**Flow and taste.** The bite valve is the Crux's best feature. CamelBak says it delivers 20% more water per sip than its older designs, and testers describe a free-flowing valve that self-seals after each drink. The lever locks and unlocks flow with one hand. That is useful on a bike and just as useful on a scramble with a trekking pole in the other hand. CleverHiker's testers ranked the Crux's drinking experience the best of the bladders they tried, and they found little of the rubbery aftertaste that plagues many competitors. Only the Platypus Hoser and Platy tasted better. Pricier bladders from Osprey and Gregory fared noticeably worse.

**Filling and pack fit.** The large opening and integrated handle make filling easy at a tap, and the reservoir fills from either a horizontal or vertical orientation. On a trail, a rigid handle gives a hiker something to hold under a trickle of stream water, which a floppy bladder can't. Internal baffles keep the profile low for loading into a sleeve and cut down on sloshing. The Quick Link system lets the hose stay routed through the pack while the reservoir comes out for a refill, and the port shuts itself off as the tube detaches.

**Sealing.** The screw-top cap draws the most complaints. Some owners report leaks, and CamelBak traces most of them to cross-threading. Its fix is to turn the cap counterclockwise until it clicks, then clockwise until snug. CleverHiker's testers had no leaks after many weeks of hiking with the reservoir stuffed into a full pack. The cap still takes more technique than a slide or zip closure.

**Weight and packability.** This is where the ultralight case fails. The plastic handle and wide cap add weight, and they keep the reservoir from rolling or folding flat when empty. CleverHiker also flags the lack of a rigid back plate. For scale, the HydraPak Velocity weighs around 3.8 oz at a comparable capacity, nearly half the Crux's 7 oz. Hikers watching ounces should look at the Velocity, the HydraPak Shape Shift or the Platypus Hoser.

**Durability and care.** The Crux is made from a heavier plastic that resists punctures better than thinner competitors. Owners report no mold or bacterial problems with normal use, and HydroGuard is credited with keeping odor down. Cleaning is the weaker side. The screw-top opening does not allow the inside-out drying that HydraPak's Contour and Velocity permit. A long tube brush and reservoir dryer make maintenance far easier, and the CamelBak Cleaning Kit covers both. CamelBak also endorses storing the reservoir in the freezer between trips, which keeps bacteria in check with almost no effort. Accessories from older Antidote-era CamelBak reservoirs do not fit.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes share the same listed footprint of 15 x 7.5 x 2.5 inches, the same valve and the same cap. The choice comes down to how much water a route demands, not what fits the pack. The 3L weighs 198 g and the 2L is listed at 211 g, so neither size has a weight edge worth planning around.

- **3L (198 g):** The pick for desert sections, long dry ridgelines and hot-weather carries of 20 miles or more between sources. It also suits hikers who want to fill once and stop thinking about water.
- **2L (211 g):** Better for cool-weather trips, day hikes and routes where a stream shows up every few miles. A smaller load rides lighter on the back, and a full 2L adds less sag to a pack.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- High-flow Big Bite valve with a one-handed on/off lever
- Among the best-tasting bladders in independent testing
- Handle and wide opening make refilling easy
- Internal baffles reduce sloshing and give a low profile
- Quick Link hose with auto shut-off port
- Durable construction that resists punctures
- HydroGuard treatment and lifetime warranty
- Roughly $42 for either size

**Cons**

- Heavy for its category at 198–211 g
- Rigid handle prevents folding flat
- Screw-top needs correct threading to seal
- Harder to clean and dry than zip or slide-top designs
- No rigid back plate
- Not compatible with older Antidote-era accessories

## Who Should Buy This

The Crux suits three-season backpackers, day hikers and mountain bikers who use a pack with a hydration sleeve and care more about drinking comfort and durability than about weight. It is a sensible replacement for a cheap bladder that leaked or tasted like plastic. Thru-hikers building an ultralight kit, and anyone who wants a reservoir that rolls flat or dries inside out, should look at the HydraPak Velocity or the Platypus Hoser.

## Verdict

The Crux offers some of the best flow and taste in the category, with a warranty to match, in both 2L and 3L. The weight penalty is real and the cap takes some technique, but neither will trouble most hikers once they are past the first few fills. At about $42, it is a dependable choice for anyone not counting grams.
