## Overview

The Suunto Charging Cable is the brand's own magnetic lead for its current GPS watch line, covering the Vertical, Race, Race S, Ocean, 9 Peak and 9 Peak Pro. It snaps onto the watch from any angle and, by Suunto's numbers, takes a dead battery to full in about an hour. The trade-off is the usual one for proprietary chargers: the connector is the point, and Suunto charges accordingly.

## Performance

**Charging speed.** Suunto's headline claim is a full charge from empty in one hour, and a 10-minute top-up that buys about two hours of training. For a thru-hiker, the second number is the useful one. A coffee-and-snack stop at a town restaurant with a free outlet, or a few minutes on a power bank while packing up camp, is enough to put a day's worth of tracking back in the watch. The one-hour figure matters less on trail than at home, but it means an overnight hostel charge is never in doubt.

**Attachment.** The cable's connector is a magnetic puck that Suunto says attaches to the watch automatically from all directions. In practice that means no fiddling with a dock in a dim tent or at 5 a.m. in a bunkroom: bring the puck near the back of the watch and it finds its seat. Magnetic pucks of this type are also easy to knock loose, so the sensible habit is to set the watch on a flat surface, not let it dangle off a pack strap or a bunk rail while it charges.

**Cable generations.** Suunto sells more than one cable under the "Watch Charging Cable" banner. The current US product page lists a newer USB-C version with a stronger magnet, a textile-covered cable and a microfiber base, specified for the Race 2 and Vertical 2. The cable covered here is the one specified for the earlier Vertical, Race, Race S, Ocean and 9 Peak family. The two are not interchangeable by assumption, so buyers should match the SKU to the watch before ordering.

**Third-party alternatives.** The proprietary connector has spawned a thriving trade in clones. Marketplace listings for Suunto-compatible magnetic cables run from roughly $17 to $33, many shipping from China, and often advertise 1 m lengths and over-current protection. Buyers who pick these up are trading Suunto's warranty and fast-charge rating for a lower price, and the claims on those listings come from the sellers, not from Suunto or independent testing.

**Pack weight.** A single short cable is a negligible load, but a thru-hiker's real charging kit is the cable plus a USB wall adapter or power bank, and that is where the grams add up. Hikers who carry one multi-device cable for a phone and headlamp will still need this dedicated lead; the watch cannot charge from a generic cable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fast charging: roughly an hour from empty, and 10 minutes for about 2 hours of training
- Magnetic connector self-aligns from any direction
- One cable covers the Vertical, Race, Race S, Ocean, 9 Peak and 9 Peak Pro
- Official part, with Suunto's fast-charge rating behind it

**Cons**

- $49 for a cable is steep, and clone leads sell for well under half that
- Proprietary connector: nothing else in a charging kit will substitute for it
- A single point of failure, since a lost or damaged cable on a long section means a dead watch
- Newer Suunto watches use a different cable generation, so compatibility has to be checked

## Who Should Buy This

Owners of a Vertical, Race, Race S, Ocean or 9 Peak watch who want a spare or replacement with Suunto's own fast-charge performance are the target. It suits anyone who has lost the original in a hiking-season pack reshuffle, and thru-hikers who want a second lead mailed to a resupply box. Buyers on a Race 2 or Vertical 2 should look at Suunto's USB-C version of the cable instead, and anyone for whom $49 is a sticking point may reasonably pick up a third-party magnetic lead as a backup while keeping the original as the primary.

## Verdict

The Suunto Charging Cable does the one job asked of it: a quick, fuss-free magnetic connection and an hour-long fill. It is overpriced for what it is, but cheap next to a watch that goes dark in the middle of a section. Buy it as the primary charger or as an official spare; skip it if the watch is a Race 2 or Vertical 2.
