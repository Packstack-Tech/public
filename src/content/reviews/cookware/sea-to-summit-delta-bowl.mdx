## Overview

The Sea to Summit Delta Bowl is a rigid, 800 ml Nylon 66 bowl for backpackers who want something sturdier than a silicone pancake and more purpose-built than a repurposed deli tub. At 79 g (2.8 oz), it won't impress the gram-counting crowd. The trade is a bowl that survives a stuffed pack, can be held with boiling food inside, and is easy to eat from one-handed.

## Performance

### Heat management

The headline feature is the Protex base, a hex pattern molded into the underside. Sea to Summit says it sheds weight and spreads heat across a larger surface area. Owners report it works in practice. Boiling water or a fresh ramen pour can be held by the base without the palm-scorching you get from bare metal. It buys a few extra seconds, not immunity, and a bowl that has just held something near boiling still deserves respect.

The other side of thin nylon is heat retention. Without a lid, food cools quickly in cold or shoulder-season conditions. That matters for anyone eating slowly at a windy camp or at altitude. Buyers of the lidded version say the cover helps hold in warmth, and a Scout leader who uses the bowl on cold campouts found it kept food reasonably warm. Nobody mistakes it for an insulated container.

### Ergonomics

The thumb grip is the detail owners mention most. It lets the bowl sit securely in one hand while the other works the spoon, and it holds well in either hand. The same loop takes a carabiner, so a wet bowl can hang off the outside of a pack to dry. The steep side walls keep soups and stews from sliding over the edge, which helps when dinner is eaten standing up or balanced on a log. Interior measurement increments make it easier to hit the water line on freeze-dried meals.

At 16 cm across and 5 cm deep, the bowl is roomy enough that some users store a utensil kit inside it. It also works as a plate for flatter food.

### Cleanability

Cleanup is quick. The smooth, rounded interior releases food more readily than silicone and doesn't take much water to rinse. Owners flag two caveats: turmeric will stain the nylon (cosmetic only, with no effect on durability), and egg yolk sticks hard once it cools. A prompt rinse or brief soak solves both. The hex pattern underneath can hold grime if ignored, so it needs an occasional scrub with a camp sponge.

### The lid

The Delta Bowl is sold alone or with a lid. Buyers like the lid for keeping bugs and heat out, and it can double as a plate. Its triple o-ring seal is watertight enough for cold-soaking a meal a couple of hours before dinner while the bowl rides near the top of the pack. The complaints are consistent, though: the lid can be stubborn to remove, and the o-rings take a wet toothbrush to clean after contact with food. The lid isn't part of the 79 g figure.

### Durability and packing

Nylon 66 stands up to being thrown in a pack without cracking or warping, and owners describe years of use with no sign of wear. It is more rigid and more durable than collapsible silicone, but it doesn't collapse. One buyer weighed their bowl at 87 g, a few grams over the claim. The bowls also nest poorly inside one another, which is a nuisance for anyone outfitting a group.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Protex base lets the bowl be held with hot food inside
- Thumb grip supports one-handed eating and clips to a pack
- Steep walls keep soups and stews contained
- Interior measurement marks help with rehydrating meals
- Rigid nylon resists cracking and cleans easily
- Dishwasher safe and BPA-free
- Optional lid enables cold-soaking and keeps bugs out

**Cons**

- Poor heat retention without the lid in cold weather
- Doesn't nest well with other Delta Bowls
- Hex base can trap food and grime
- Turmeric stains and egg yolk sticks if left to dry
- Heavier than titanium or silicone alternatives
- Lid can be hard to remove and the o-rings need attention

## Who Should Buy This

The Delta Bowl suits solo backpackers and campers who eat a lot of rehydrated, one-pot, or soupy meals and want a bowl they never have to think about. It also fits car campers and anyone who values a sturdy, hold-it-in-your-hand bowl over saved grams. Hikers building a sub-pound kitchen should look at a titanium bowl or a collapsible silicone option instead. Groups that want to stack bowls compactly should do the same.

## Verdict

The Delta Bowl does what it says: the base tames hot food, the thumb loop is a real ergonomic gain, and the nylon holds up. Its weaknesses are cold-weather heat loss, poor nesting, and a weight penalty over lighter materials. For a single hiker who values durability and ease of use over ounces, it earns a 7.5 out of 10.
