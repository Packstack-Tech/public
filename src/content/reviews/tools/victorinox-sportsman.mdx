## Overview

The Victorinox Sportsman is a two-layer, 84 mm Swiss Army knife that trades the second blade found on its siblings for a nail file, then adds a corkscrew, awl, tweezers and toothpick. It suits hikers who want a pocket-sized, do-it-all tool for camp chores, repairs and resupply-town picnics. The trade-off is weight: at 52 g it carries a corkscrew and nail file that few ultralighters will ever put to use.

## Performance

**Tool layout.** The front of the knife carries a large blade, a nail file with nail cleaner, and two combination tools: a can opener with a 3 mm screwdriver, and a bottle opener with a 6 mm screwdriver and wire stripper. The back holds a corkscrew and a reamer/punch with a sewing eye. Tweezers and a toothpick sit in the scales, and a split ring at the end takes a lanyard or key clip. Nothing is on the outside of the handle, so the knife stays smooth in a pocket or a hip-belt pouch.

**Cutting.** The main blade is roughly 64 mm, v-ground and spear-point, and owners say it arrives sharp. Knife enthusiasts describe Victorinox's stainless alloy as corrosion-resistant, reasonably good at holding an edge, and easy to touch up. That last point matters on trail, where a few strokes on a stone or a pack-strap-and-ceramic sharpener restore the edge in a minute. For slicing cheese, salami, tortillas, cordage and moleskin, the blade is more than enough. It is not a bushcraft tool, and with no lock and no one-hand opening, it rewards careful handling. The slip-joint design does keep it legal in places where locking blades are restricted.

**Repairs and camp tasks.** The awl and sewing eye handle pack-fabric repairs and punching new holes in straps. The screwdrivers cover stove fittings, eyeglass hinges and trekking-pole hardware, although the can opener's small screwdriver tip is better suited to tiny screws. One quirk, noted on knife forums: the 84 mm cap-lifter tool has no half-stop at 90 degrees, so applying real torque with the bottle-opener driver takes more care than on the larger 91 mm Victorinox models. Cyclists like the Sportsman as a companion to a bike tool, for levering brake pads, filing burrs and making holes.

**Carry.** The 84 mm frame is the smallest of Victorinox's larger-format knives and still fills the hand of all but the biggest users. Owners call it close to the ideal size for everyday carry, one that disappears in a pocket until needed. Reviewers at Three Points of the Compass put its weight at 52.1 g and compared it with the thinner, two-layer Cadet Alox at 46 g. The single-layer Alox Bantam, with a 63 mm blade and a can/bottle-opener combination tool, comes in at 29 g.

**The nail file.** It is the model's defining feature, and the reason enthusiasts split. Some owners say they prefer it to the small blade on the Tourist or Recruit. Others carry a Spartan or Tourist for the second cutting edge. On trail the file earns its keep on torn nails, rough blisters' edges and burrs on repaired gear, which is more than its reputation suggests.

**Durability.** Victorinox's lifetime warranty and an owner base that reports decades of use make the Sportsman a low-risk purchase. The Cellidor scales are light and smooth, if not as grippy as textured nylon. One owner noted that at this price, losing one to a pocket or a trail mishap is not a disaster.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 13 functions in a slim 84 mm, 52 g package
- Sharp, easily maintained stainless blade
- Awl with sewing eye, tweezers and toothpick are handy for field repair and splinters
- Cheap enough to replace, backed by a lifetime warranty
- Slip-joint blade is legal in many jurisdictions that restrict locking knives

**Cons**

- Heavier than blade-and-opener minimalist options such as the 29 g Alox Bantam or a small-blade knife
- Corkscrew and nail file are dead weight for most backpackers
- No blade lock and no one-hand opening
- Only one cutting blade; no scissors
- No half-stop on the cap-lifter screwdriver limits torque

## Who Should Buy This

The Sportsman suits section hikers, bikepackers and casual backpackers who want one small knife for the trail, the bike and the kitchen drawer back home. It is also a solid fit for anyone who values a file and awl over a second blade. Gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere: a Bantam or a bare razor blade does the cutting jobs for a fraction of the weight, and the Sportsman's extra tools rarely justify 20-plus grams. Anyone who needs a locking blade for heavier camp work should step up to a larger Victorinox or a dedicated folder.

## Verdict

The Sportsman is a well-made, inexpensive, versatile pocket knife that carries more tools than most hikers need and is lighter than it looks. For ultralight use, it is a comfort item more than a necessity, and shaving it for a 29 g Bantam is an easy call. For everyone else who wants a classic Swiss Army knife that disappears in a pocket, it is a good one.
