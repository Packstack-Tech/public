## Overview

The Sidas Trail Protect is a thin, mid-height synthetic sock from a French insole specialist, built for trail runners but equally at home in a fast-and-light hiker's trail runners. Its party trick is a silicone grip band that locks the sock to the foot and the foot to the insole. The trade-off is a slim, lightly cushioned sock at a price of about $20 (£21 in the UK) a pair, with padding only where Sidas decided you'd want it.

## Performance

The silicone band is the reason this sock gets talked about. Sidas designed it to work with its own Protect insoles, but it does the job in any shoe. A reviewer at Advnture, who had not seen the feature on a sock before, found it kept the sock from creeping down the foot and added grip inside the shoe on uneven ground. The same tester wore the Trail Protect through the Lakeland 50, a 50-mile ultra that took almost 15 hours of Lake District drizzle, and finished with no blisters. For anyone who has spent a wet afternoon watching a sock bunch under the arch, that is the relevant data point.

Fit appears to be a strength. A European buyer who ordered a 42-43 for a size 42 foot reported a snug fit with no slipping and no blister trouble, and noted that each sock is marked left or right inside the cuff. Customer reviews at Bergfreunde are small in number but lean positive, praising the quick drying and fit; the one dissenting reviewer called the sock thin.

That thinness is the defining character of the sock. Padding sits at the toes, heel and ankle, and the rest is a slim, responsive knit. Testers describe the placement as sensible, softening impact up front and sparing the ankle bone from the opposite foot's late-day clips. The ventilated arch panel keeps things breathable on hot days without making the sock feel flimsy. Runners and hikers who want a plush, cushioned sole underfoot will find the Trail Protect too minimal. Those who run snug shoes, or who want to keep their toe box roomy, will see that as a feature.

There is one fit quirk. The grip band is thinner and less cushioned than the rest of the sock, so the Advnture tester advises positioning it so the top lace doesn't sit directly over it. Otherwise it can cause rubbing. Once noticed, the fix is easy.

At 36 g a pair, the Trail Protect is light but not remarkable. The Advnture review weighed a UK size 6 pair at 34 g, so the claimed figure is consistent. Most of its savings come from how little fabric there is, not from exotic materials. It is entirely synthetic, so it will not offer the warmth or the odor behavior of a merino blend, and the 14 cm cuff will leave the lower leg exposed to brush and ticks. The reflective band matters more to a road-and-trail runner than to a backpacker, but it adds nothing in weight worth counting.

Durability reports are encouraging: the Advnture tester said the pairs stayed comfortable over many miles. Care is simple, with a 30°C wash inside out and no fabric softener.

## Choosing a Version

The only version choice is size, in five European ranges: 39-40, 40-41, 42-43, 44-46 and 47-49. The ranges are fairly narrow at the small end and wide at the top. The 44-46 range spans three shoe sizes, and the 47-49 range likewise, so wearers at the edge of a range should expect a looser or tighter fit than those in the middle. Because the silicone band depends on tension to work, a sock that is too big will lose some of its grip benefit. A buyer who ordered 42-43 for a size 42 foot said it fit well, which suggests sizing runs true. Anyone between ranges should choose the smaller if the shoe is snug and the larger if they wear thick-toed or roomy shoes. Retailers also stock a few smaller sizes for women, though 39-40 is the smallest listed on Sidas' own page for this model.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Silicone grip band keeps the sock and foot from shifting in the shoe
- Zero-blister result over a 15-hour wet ultra in independent testing
- Padding at toe, heel and ankle without bulk elsewhere
- Ventilated arch panel for hot days
- 36 g per pair, slim enough for snug shoes
- Left/right-specific construction; fit reported as true to size

**Cons**

- Roughly $20 / £21 a pair is steep for a thin sock
- Minimal cushioning for those who prefer plush soles
- Grip band can rub if it lands under the top lace
- Synthetic-only build with a mid-height cuff offers little warmth or leg coverage
- Wide size ranges at the top end

## Who Should Buy This

Trail runners, fastpackers and thru-hikers who wear low-volume trail runners and fight sock slippage or heel blisters will get the most from the Trail Protect. It also suits anyone running Sidas insoles, where the grip band is designed to work as a system. Hikers who need cushioning for a heavy pack, cold-weather warmth, or taller coverage against brush should look at a merino-blend crew instead. Budget-minded buyers can get a basic sock for half the price, though it won't have the grip band.

## Verdict

The Trail Protect solves a specific problem, sock slippage, with a clever bit of silicone, and it does so in a light, breathable package that held up through a 15-hour wet ultra without blisters. It is thin and pricey, and the grip band needs a moment of attention when lacing. For runners and fast hikers in trail shoes, it is a sound choice.
