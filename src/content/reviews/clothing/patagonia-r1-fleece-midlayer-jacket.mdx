## Overview

The R1 Fleece Midlayer Jacket is the full-zip, hood-free branch of Patagonia's long-running R1 family: a slim, grid-textured fleece built to be worn while moving, not while standing around. At 342 g it is light enough to live in a pack all day and pull on at the first cold saddle. The trade-off is the usual one for thin grid fleece: excellent venting, modest warmth, almost no wind protection, and a surface that can snag.

## Performance

**Warmth and breathability.** The R1's calling card is the grid face: small islands of insulation separated by channels that dump heat and moisture. Outdoor Gear Lab, which has long rated the R1 line its benchmark technical midlayer, scored the hooded version 9 out of 10 for breathability and weight, and called out its warmth-to-weight ratio. The same testers noted the flip side: stop moving or let the temperature drop and a heavier layer has to come out. A long-term reviewer at Better Trail, who logged 11 years in the pullover hoody, puts the sweet spot at fast-moving days below roughly 20°F, and notes the R1 runs warmer and less airy than newer ultralight grid fleeces such as the Senchi A90 and Black Diamond's Coefficient.

**The hybrid build.** This jacket differs from the classic R1 in how it is cut. The torso and sleeves use the heavier 6.9-oz Power Grid, while the sides and the undersides of the arms switch to a lighter 3.8-oz version. The idea is to keep the core warm and let the high-sweat zones vent. Owners describe it as warm for what it weighs and easy to toss in a pack. On a climb that means unzipping rather than stripping off the jacket.

**Fit and layering.** The cut is slim and athletic. The stretch in the Power Grid fabric gives full arm mobility, and buyers report no rubbing or chafing under a pack's shoulder straps or hip belt. The low-profile hand pockets sit above a hip belt without adding bulk, and owners single out the large interior mesh pockets as a good place for gloves or a beanie. The slim body slides under a shell or puffy cleanly, though anyone who wants to wear a thick base layer beneath it should check sizing. Without a hood, the jacket also avoids the bunched collar that bothers some people under a shell.

**Weather resistance.** There isn't much. The open grid that makes the R1 breathe also lets wind through, and Outdoor Gear Lab gave the hooded R1 only 6 out of 10 for weather resistance. It pairs with a wind shirt or rain shell; it does not replace one. Because the fleece wicks and dries fast, it handles sweat better than it handles a downpour.

**Durability.** Opinions split. The Better Trail reviewer's R1 survived hundreds of days of alpine climbing, ski touring and backpacking with tight seams and intact insulation. On Patagonia's own product page, though, which averages 4.15 stars across 40 reviews, some buyers complain that the fabric snags and pills. One reported pilling after a single backpacking trip. Another said the sleeves snagged and tore after light use. Carrying it near Velcro, rough rock and brush is the surest way to join that group.

**Odor and care.** The HeiQ Pure treatment and the polyester construction hold up well on multi-day trips. Reviewers describe the fabric as resisting stink better than most fleeces, which matters for anyone wearing it as a sleep layer on a week-long route.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 342 g for a full-zip jacket with five pockets
- Strong breathability and quick drying for a fleece with real warmth
- Stretchy, slim cut moves well under a pack and a shell
- Full zip vents quickly and is easy to get on and off with a pack
- Recycled fabrics and Fair Trade Certified sewing

**Cons**

- Thin fabric snags and pills for some owners
- Little wind resistance without an additional shell
- Not enough warmth for standing around in cold camps
- Slim fit leaves little room for bulky layers underneath
- No hood, so ears and neck stay exposed

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers, trail runners, alpine climbers and ski tourers who run hot, move fast and want one fleece that handles cold mornings, brisk climbs and cool evenings. It suits anyone who already carries a wind shirt or rain jacket and wants a breathable layer to wear under it. Hikers who spend long stretches stationary in camp, or who want a single layer to carry them through freezing, windy conditions, should look at a light synthetic puffy instead. Anyone who plans to push through thick brush or scramble on abrasive rock should also expect some cosmetic wear.

## Verdict

The R1 Fleece Midlayer Jacket is a well-made, light and highly breathable layer that has earned its place in thru-hiker and climber packs for years. It does best as an active midlayer under a shell, and it is a poor choice as a warm layer for sitting still. Buyers who accept some snagging and the need for a wind layer will find it one of the best-balanced fleeces at its weight.
