## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 Foam Trekking Seat is a Z-folding closed-cell foam sit pad from Decathlon's in-house trekking brand, built for lunch stops, rest breaks and bivouac evenings. It weighs 60 g, folds to roughly the size of a paperback, and puts a layer of insulation between a hiker and cold, wet ground. The trade-off is the usual one for sit pads: it does one job, and it does not do it any better than lighter or cheaper rivals.

## Performance

**Insulation.** The seat's R-value of 1.3 is more than a sit pad needs. A hiker sitting for 15 minutes on a cold log, a stone ledge or a patch of damp tundra loses heat mainly through conduction, and a layer of closed-cell foam with a reflective film on top cuts that loss sharply. For context, the brand's full-length MT500 folding pad, which uses the same foam family, is rated R-2.2. The r/Ultralight crowd has long treated Decathlon's foam as a benchmark for warmth per euro. The seat is a cut-down relative, so it is not a sleeping solution, but it is a real barrier between a rain-damp bottom and a cold surface.

**Moisture.** Closed-cell foam does not absorb water. Set it on wet grass, a snowbank or a dewy rock, and the surface stays wipeable and the hiker's shorts stay dry. A shake after a break is all the maintenance it needs. A fabric-covered inflatable seat cushion cannot say the same, and it is one reason foam keeps winning against fancier options in wet climates.

**Packability.** At 29 x 9.5 x 4.5 cm, the folded seat occupies about 1.2 liters. That is small enough to slide into a side pocket, a hip-belt pocket or the back sleeve of a frameless pack, where it can double as a bit of padding. The four-section fold means it opens in one flick, which matters when a break is 10 minutes long and the rain is closing in. Foam of this type is also forgiving about being stuffed, strapped and sat on, and owners of Forclaz's larger MT500 pad describe the foam as tough.

**Durability.** Foam pads of this kind compress and show crease lines at the folds over a season of use. That is cosmetic and expected, and a 60 g seat that costs less than a restaurant lunch is easy to replace when it flattens. Abrasion from rocks and granite is rarely a problem for polyethylene foam. Sharp, hot embers are a different matter.

**Weight.** At 60 g, the seat is not the lightest option. Generic four-fold sit pads from other sellers claim about 54 g. That 6 g gap is not going to decide anyone's base weight, but gram counters should know the number. What the Forclaz offers over those no-name pads is the Decathlon name and its retail network, which means easy replacement and a returns counter in most of Europe.

**Versatility.** Some hikers use a seat pad as a pack-frame stiffener, a kneeling pad for camp chores or a pot insulator. Others slip it under hips or shoulders as a mini-pad to add a little warmth for a cold sleeper. At 1.3 R it adds modest but real insulation under a pad on a freezing night.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 60 g and a compact 29 x 9.5 x 4.5 cm fold
- R-1.3 foam with a reflective aluminum film is warm enough for cold, damp ground
- Closed-cell foam takes on no water and wipes clean
- Four-section accordion fold opens quickly with one hand
- Cheap enough that replacing it is not a decision
- Decathlon's wide retail presence makes it easy to buy and return

**Cons**

- Slightly heavier than the lightest generic foam sit pads
- Folds crease and compress over a season of use
- Too small to serve as a shoulder-to-hip pad on its own
- Must be carried outside the pack's main compartment or in a pocket, or it can flop around

## Who Should Buy This

This seat suits three-season hikers, thru-hikers and weekend backpackers who stop often and want to sit somewhere dry, especially in wet or cold climates like the UK, Scandinavia and the Alps, where the ground is rarely hospitable. It is also a sensible add-on for anyone who already carries a Decathlon kit and wants a cheap, familiar piece of foam. Hikers who count every gram, or who prefer to sit on a rolled-up pad or a sleeping pad folded in the pack, can skip it. So can anyone who wants a pad to sleep on: this is a seat only.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 Foam Trekking Seat is a simple, sturdy and sensibly priced answer to cold, wet ground. It will not win a weight contest against a bare-bones generic, but it is easy to find, easy to carry and does what a sit pad should. Buy it for trail breaks; look elsewhere for sleeping insulation.
