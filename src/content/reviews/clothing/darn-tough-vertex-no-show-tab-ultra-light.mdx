## Overview

The Vertex No-Show Tab Ultra-Light is the thinnest sock Darn Tough makes: a merino-nylon blend with a fused True Seamless toe, a low-cut tab and 13 g per sock. It is built for runners, but the same properties appeal to hikers in trail runners who want a sock that disappears in the shoe. The trade-off is simple. It gives up nearly all cushioning, and with it some comfort on long, rocky days.

## Performance

**Weight and feel.** At 26 g a pair, this sock sits in a weight class most wool socks don't approach. Darn Tough describes it as its lightest sock, one meant for race day or anyone who wants a "lighter-than-air" feel. The knit is fine-gauge, and the heel and forefoot use a denser weave than the rest of the sock to improve durability without adding padding.

**Moisture and breathability.** This is where the sock scores best. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers logged more than 30 miles on trail and road in the women's version and rated wicking and breathability 9 out of 10. They called the breathability astounding for a merino blend. The nylon content helps it dry quickly, and the merino keeps odor down over repeated wears, a useful trait when a pair gets rotated through a multi-day trip.

**Fit and blister resistance.** The fused toe seam is the main comfort feature. Owners who run in Darn Tough's closely related no-show tab ultralight socks report that the tab keeps the Achilles from rubbing against the shoe collar. Some say heel blisters stopped on runs of 10 miles or more. Others report no hot spots on mixed hikes in trail runners, from cool mornings to hot afternoons. Outdoor Gear Lab scored fit 8 out of 10 and found the sock held its shape without rolling down.

**Where it falls short.** Outdoor Gear Lab gave comfort only 5 out of 10, saying the sock has no real comfort features. The merino-synthetic blend isn't itchy, but there is little between the foot and the footbed. On long, rocky descents, or in a stiff shoe with a thin insole, that shows. The same testers scored slip prevention 5 out of 10 and described the sock as slippery. That is a trade-off of the thin, smooth knit, and it matters most in wet shoes on steep terrain, where the foot can shift.

**Durability.** Outdoor Gear Lab gave durability a 10 out of 10, and the lifetime guarantee backs that up. Darn Tough's policy is a replacement with no conditions. At 13 g per sock, the Vertex is the least material Darn Tough puts between a foot and a shoe, yet it holds up as well as its heavier siblings. Thru-hikers who burn through socks tend to value that more than a plush footbed.

**Limits of the no-show cut.** The tab sits below the ankle bone. That keeps the look clean and the weight down, but it offers no protection from trail debris. Grit and sand can enter the shoe over the low collar, so this sock suits runners and hikers in low-cut shoes, not anyone crossing sandy washes or bushwhacking in gaiter-free trail runners.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 13 g per sock, the lightest in Darn Tough's range
- Strong wicking and breathability, rated 9/10 by Outdoor Gear Lab
- Fused True Seamless toe and heel tab reduce rubbing points
- Top durability score, with a no-conditions lifetime guarantee
- Merino blend resists odor across several days of wear
- Made in the USA at about $16–17 a pair

**Cons**

- No real cushioning; comfort scored 5/10 in testing
- Smooth knit can feel slippery inside the shoe
- Low cut lets debris in
- Not suited to cold, wet or high-mileage rocky terrain where a thicker sock earns its weight

## Who Should Buy This

Runners, fastpackers and day hikers in well-fitting trail runners, especially in warm weather, will get the most from it. It also suits anyone who wants a lightweight sock that is easy to wash and dry. Thru-hikers on long routes with abrasive terrain, or anyone who values underfoot padding, should look at Darn Tough's cushioned hiking socks instead. Cold-weather users should look elsewhere too, since this is a summer sock.

## Verdict

The Vertex No-Show Tab Ultra-Light does one thing well: it puts a durable, fast-drying merino blend on the foot at 26 g a pair. For hot-weather running and fast, light hiking it is a sound buy backed by a lifetime guarantee. Buyers who want padding or a slip-resistant grip should step up to a cushioned sock.
