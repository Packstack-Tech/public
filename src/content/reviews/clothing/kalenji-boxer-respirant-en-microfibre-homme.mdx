## Overview

The Kalenji Boxer Respirant en Microfibre is Decathlon's house-brand running boxer brief: a thin polyester-elastane pair with a long leg, mid-rise waist and seams moved away from the inseam. It sells for a few euros, which makes it one of the cheapest technical underwear options a long-distance hiker can buy. The trade-off is longevity. The fabric and fit win praise on the trail, but the elastic can relax and the garment can show wear sooner than pricier merino or nylon alternatives.

## Performance

**Moisture and drying.** Kalenji's pitch is a microfibre that moves sweat off the skin and dries quickly, and owners say it does. A walker on the Camino de Santiago forum who uses the same Decathlon running boxer briefs, which he puts at around 52 g, says they dry fast. He also reports they have survived a few thousand kilometres. He has used them for swimming a few times too, though he notes they were not designed for it. For anyone washing underwear in a sink and hanging it from a pack strap, a thin synthetic that is dry by morning matters more than anything on the label.

**Chafe control.** The relocated crotch seams are the main comfort feature, and long-run users back them up. One runner on a French review site wore the boxer through two marathons and long outings beyond two hours without friction or irritation. Another reviewer wears the pair for hiking, walking and the occasional trail run. That reviewer finds the fabric thin and discreet and reports no rubbing. The long leg keeps thighs from rubbing in the same way a short brief would not.

**Fit and hold.** The elastic waistband is built to hold without cinching, and owners generally agree that it stays put and doesn't ride up. The recurring complaint is that the garment loosens with use. Some reviewers say it relaxes fairly quickly, which leads to creasing at the groin and weaker support around the hips and thighs.

**Durability.** Opinion is split. A runner who has bought the model for years says the 2018 version showed no wear after six months, and notes that earlier versions started to fail at the seam between the waistband and the fabric. Others say visible wear appears after several washes. Decathlon recommends a 30°C wash in a laundry bag, liquid detergent, no softener and no tumble drying, since softeners and powders can impair the fabric's breathability. Those rules are easy to follow on a trail, where a tumble dryer is unlikely, but hotter laundromat cycles between resupplies will shorten its life.

**Value.** Reviewers keep returning to price. With a rating of 4.7 from more than 17,000 reviews on Decathlon's French site, it ranks among the most reviewed and best rated underwear there. At about 5 €, it can be replaced mid-trek without much pain, which is a more realistic durability strategy than buying one expensive pair and hoping.

## Choosing a Version

The only variable is size, from S to 3XL. The cut is the same across the range: a long-leg boxer brief with a mid-rise elastic waist. Because the most common complaint is stretch-out, owners are best served by choosing the size that fits snugly when new rather than sizing up for room. Hikers at the top of the range should note that fit reports from the 3XL end are less common than from the middle sizes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 50 g, with an owner-reported 52 g figure for the same Decathlon running boxer
- Dries quickly and handles sweat well
- Crotch seams are relocated, and long-run users report no chafing
- Costs a few euros, so spares are cheap
- Works for running, hiking and as a swim-short liner
- Sizes S to 3XL

**Cons**

- Elastic and fabric can relax with use
- Some owners see wear after several washes
- Care instructions rule out the dryer and fabric softener
- Basic styling, with no odor-resistant treatment

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers, runners and budget-minded section hikers who want light, quick-drying underwear and accept that it is semi-disposable. It suits Camino walkers and long-distance hikers who wash daily. Anyone who wants multi-week odor resistance, as with merino, or who expects years of use from one pair should look at pricier options.

## Verdict

This is the best-value synthetic boxer brief for hikers who want a pair that is light, dries fast and doesn't chafe. Buy two or three, size them snugly and replace them when the elastic gives up. Don't buy them if you expect them to last forever.
