## Overview

The 5-Pocket Shorts have been Yamatomichi's signature garment since 2013. They are a plain-woven, non-stretch nylon short built around a pocket layout that holds a phone, map and wallet without flapping against the thigh. They are not the lightest hiking shorts Yamatomichi makes, and that is deliberate. The trade is a few dozen grams for the toughest fabric in the brand's shorts lineup.

## Performance

**Pockets.** The pocket system is the reason these shorts exist. Yamatomichi spent two years testing and revising the pattern, and the result gives the map, wallet and phone each a defined place. The side phone pocket is the standout. The brand says it holds a phone firmly enough that it doesn't bounce even while running. Two front pockets are cut so hands can rest in them comfortably. On a long day, the practical effect is that snacks, keys and a phone stay reachable without a pack hip-belt pocket and without swinging against a leg on every step.

**Fabric.** The shorts use a high-density Taslan nylon, a textured air-jet yarn with a matte, cotton-like hand rather than the slick sheen of typical ripstop. Yamatomichi's own comparison chart rates its durability as excellent, the top mark across the three fabrics in its shorts range. The lighter Pertex Equilibrium options rate "good" for the DW version and "fair" for the Light. The company has said the plain-woven Taslan outlasted every alternative it tried when looking for a replacement, including a custom double-weave. That is a manufacturer's claim, but it matches the logic of the cloth: at 120 g/m², it is roughly twice the weight of the 62 g/m² fabric in the Light 5-Pocket Shorts.

**Drying and comfort.** The Taslan is rated fast-drying, not very fast. In Yamatomichi's testing, the shorts are almost completely dry after 15 minutes of spin-drying. The firmness and wrinkled surface keep the cloth from clinging to damp skin, though the brand rates comfort when wet as only "fair", behind the textured DW fabric. There is no stretch at all. The pattern relies on a generous hem opening to avoid overheating and to give the legs room, and the brand says it chose non-stretch, polyurethane-free nylon because stretch fabrics tend to dry slowly, wear out sooner and block wind. Wind resistance is high and breathability is lower than in the Light and DW versions. Hikers who run hot in humid, sticky climates may find the cloth a step warmer than the featherweight alternatives.

**Weight in context.** At 192–199 g, these are about 45–50 g heavier than the DW 5-Pocket Shorts (145 g in size M) and roughly 75 g heavier than the Light 5-Pocket Shorts (118 g in M). Those are meaningful gaps for a base-weight counter, but small next to the durability difference if the shorts will see brush, granite and repeated washing.

**Care.** Yamatomichi offers a repair service for the line, which matters for a garment meant to last several seasons rather than one thru-hike.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same fabric, pocket layout and construction. The only difference is scale: the L is 7 g heavier than the Standard (199 g versus 192 g) because it uses more cloth. Choose by fit. The L suits hikers with a larger waist or longer legs who would otherwise run out of room in the Standard. Everyone else should take the Standard. Because the fabric doesn't stretch, size up rather than hoping the cloth will give. The roomy hem and the webbing belt allow some adjustment at the waist, but not much in the seat and thigh.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Pocket layout built for hiking, with a secure side phone pocket that stays put on the move
- Dense Taslan nylon rated by the brand as the most durable in its shorts range
- Fast-drying, and wind-resistant for cool mornings and exposed ridgelines
- PFAS-free C0 DWR
- Matte, cotton-like texture that doesn't feel like typical shiny nylon
- 2026 update redesigned the belt webbing to resist twisting
- Repair service available

**Cons**

- Heavier than Yamatomichi's own DW and Light versions
- No stretch, so fit matters and deep lunges and high steps rely on the cut alone
- Less breathable than the lighter fabrics
- ¥13,500 is a premium price for plain nylon shorts
- Comfort when wet is only fair against the DW fabric

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who wear one pair of shorts for weeks of trail and want the pockets to do the work of a hip-belt pouch. Section hikers and thru-hikers on rough, brushy terrain will get the most from the heavier cloth. Anyone who has worn through ultralight shorts will understand the appeal. Trail runners, fastpackers and hikers in humid heat should look at the Light 5-Pocket Shorts or the DW version, which give up some toughness for less weight and better airflow. Those who want a stretchy, gym-style feel will not find it here.

## Verdict

The 5-Pocket Shorts are the durable workhorse of Yamatomichi's range. Their well-thought-out pockets and hard-wearing, fast-drying cloth earn a place in a long-haul kit despite the weight penalty over the brand's lighter shorts. Buy them for longevity and pocket design, not for gram-shaving.
