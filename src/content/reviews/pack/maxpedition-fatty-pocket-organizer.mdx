## Overview

The Maxpedition Fatty Pocket Organizer (model 0261) is a small clamshell pouch built to corral the loose EDC items — cash, cards, USB drives, meds, a mini flashlight — that otherwise rattle around the bottom of your pack. It's aimed less at thru-hikers optimizing base weight and more at overlanders, EDC/tactical carry folks, and hikers who want a dedicated grab-and-go pouch for a first-aid or admin kit that can drop into a backpack or mount externally on MOLLE/PALS webbing.

## Performance

At 5"x7"x2" and 136 g, the Fatty sits in an odd-but-useful middle ground — <cite index="3-1,3-2">it's an everyday carry twice the size of Maxpedition's smaller E.D.C. Pocket Organizer</cite>, but still small enough to disappear into the top pocket of a daypack or a hipbelt pocket on a bigger pack. I've seen it used two very different ways in the field and in owner write-ups: as a wallet/document dump and as a compact med kit, and it handles both reasonably well thanks to the layout.

The interior organization is the real selling point. On one side you get <cite index="16-14">a key leash with a clip, a tie-down loop, an elastic organizer with nine divisions and a slip pocket for a check book</cite>, and on the other <cite index="16-15">a zipper pocket and an elastic organizer with six divisions</cite>. That's 15 dedicated slots before you even touch the mesh exterior pockets — plenty for pill bottles, gauze packets, multi-tool bits, or a stack of cards and cash. One KnifeCenter reviewer described using it exactly this way, <cite index="9-1,9-2">carrying it in a day sack as a wallet, with a mix of currencies in the zipper pocket, USB sticks, passport, pens and cards in the main pocket</cite>. Another common use case I found echoed in gear write-ups is as a compact first-aid pouch — the clamshell layout means you can see everything at a glance instead of digging, which matters when you actually need the kit in a hurry.

The 1000D ballistic nylon shell feels appropriately overbuilt for something this small — it's the same fabric family Maxpedition uses on its full-size packs, so abrasion resistance isn't really a concern. The clamshell zippers are YKK, which is a small but meaningful detail; cheap zipper pulls are usually the first thing to fail on pouches like this. The dual-zipper clamshell design does mean two pulls to fully open rather than one, which is a minor inconvenience if you're trying to access it one-handed.

Where it falls short of "ultralight" territory is obvious: at 136 g empty, it's heavier than a comparable-capacity stuff sack or a simple zippered pouch from a cottage brand, and the elastic loops and multiple pocket layers add bulk you don't get with a single-compartment dry bag. If you're counting grams on a thru-hike, this isn't the organizer you reach for — it's built for someone prioritizing structure and durability over minimum weight. Available user reviews are thin (a handful of 4-5 star ratings across retailers, no real complaints beyond the usual size expectations), so I'd treat sample size here as small rather than the last word.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 15+ dedicated internal slots (9 + 6 elastic divisions) keep small items separated and visible
- Durable 1000D ballistic nylon construction with YKK zippers
- MOLLE/PALS compatible for external mounting, or drops loose into a pack
- Clamshell design gives full visibility of contents — no digging
- Doubles well as a wallet organizer, med kit, or tech/cable pouch

**Cons**

- 136 g is heavy for a pouch this size if you're weight-conscious
- Two zipper pulls to open fully, slower than a single-zip pouch
- No dedicated see-through mesh window on the interior compartments
- User review base is small, so long-term durability data is limited

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for hikers, overlanders, and EDC carriers who want a single dedicated pouch for admin items, meds, or small tools and value organization and MOLLE compatibility over shaving grams. If you're building a compact first-aid kit or just tired of loose gear floating in your pack's brain, the Fatty's compartmentalization is genuinely useful. Ultralight backpackers chasing sub-10-lb base weights should look at lighter stuff-sack alternatives instead.

## Verdict

The Fatty Pocket Organizer does exactly what Maxpedition designed it to do — keep small gear found and organized — with tough materials and a sensible internal layout. It's not a lightweight pick, but for a MOLLE-compatible admin or med pouch that can take a beating, it earns a solid 7.5/10.
