## Overview

The SnapBox Oval is a pair of stackable, snap-lid polypropylene boxes from Swedish brand Light My Fire, sold as a two-pack for carrying food or small gear. It is aimed at backpackers who are tired of finding a flattened tortilla or a leaking bag of couscous at the bottom of the food bag. The trade-off is simple: a hard plastic box protects its contents far better than a bag does, but it never gets smaller as you eat.

## Performance

**Organization and protection.** The oval footprint is the point. At 170 mm long, each box fits a handful of crackers, a stack of tortilla chips' worth of crumbs, a day's trail mix or a tangle of charging cables, and it is slightly roomier than the square SnapBox Original that Light My Fire sells alongside it. That model measures 110 x 110 x 40 mm and holds 170 ml. The Oval is the one to buy if the Original is too small to hold a real portion of anything.

**Closure.** The lid snaps down tight, and Light My Fire rates it waterproof when sealed. A pull tab on the edge lets it open with one thumb. Owners of the SnapBox line regularly report that the lids stay shut inside a loaded pack, which is the whole job here. One European buyer of the bio-based Oval described it simply as a good way to organize small portions of food or other items in a backpack.

**Measuring lines.** The molded lines at 100, 200 and 300 ml are a small but real convenience. They let the box double as a measuring cup for rehydrating oats or portioning couscous, a job that otherwise needs a separate cup or a guess.

**Cleaning and durability.** The corners are rounded, so there is nowhere for peanut butter to hide, and the polypropylene resists stains and odors. Curry-colored residue and garlic smell are the usual enemies of plastic food boxes, and this material is built to shrug them off. Both dishwasher and microwave use are approved, which suits a box that also lives in a kitchen drawer between trips. The boxes also float, a minor detail that matters if one goes overboard on a packraft or lake shore.

**Material.** The bio-based version is made from certified sugarcane and wood fiber from certified European wood, and the manufacturer says it can be recycled with standard polypropylene (no. 2) streams. The standard version is BPA-free polypropylene. Both are food-approved under EU and FDA rules.

**The ultralight objection.** A rigid box is dead volume. A zip bag collapses as the food disappears; this does not. Hikers counting every ounce will carry food in bags and reserve a box for the fragile or the sloppy: soft cheese, hummus powder mixed in camp, a day's worth of chips, or a first-aid kit that needs to stay dry. The nested design helps, since empty boxes stack inside one another's footprint only loosely, and in a bear canister the oval shape wastes some space compared with a bag that molds to the gaps.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Snap-lock lid seals waterproof and opens one-handed with a pull tab
- Oval shape holds noticeably more than the SnapBox Original
- Measuring lines at 100, 200 and 300 ml
- Stain- and odor-resistant; dishwasher and microwave safe
- Floats; stacks for storage
- Bio-based option available

**Cons**

- Rigid, so it doesn't shrink as food is eaten
- Awkward to pack around in a bear canister or tight pack
- No locking latch, only a friction snap lid
- Plastic gets scratched and cloudy over years of use

## Who Should Buy This

Section hikers, weekend campers and thru-hikers who want an organized food bag will get the most from the SnapBox Oval. It suits anyone who carries crushable snacks, wants a no-fuss place for spices and electronics, or prepares a cold-soak meal in camp. It is also a cheap way to bring lunch to work between trips. Gram-counters who live entirely on resealable bags and zip closures should skip it, as should anyone who needs a container that survives boiling water on a stove, since this is a storage box, not a cook pot.

## Verdict

The SnapBox Oval does one small job well: it keeps fragile and messy food intact and dry, and it can be washed in a dishwasher afterward. It is inexpensive, easy to open and sized for a real portion of food. It earns a place in the pack of anyone who has ever eaten crushed crackers, though it will stay home with the strictest ultralighters.
