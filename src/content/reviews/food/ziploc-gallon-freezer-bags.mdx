## Overview

Ziploc Gallon Freezer Bags aren't a "backpacking product" in any marketed sense — they're the same bags sitting in your kitchen junk drawer. But they've become a near-universal piece of ultralight kit for one simple reason: at 12 grams and a few cents apiece, they do a surprising number of jobs (food storage, electronics/document dry-bagging, trash compaction, even in-bag meal rehydration) better than almost anything purpose-built at that weight. This review covers the standard double-zipper gallon freezer bag, not the slider or "Stay Open" variants.

## Performance

**Food storage and organization.** This is the bag's bread and butter, literally. I use one per day's food when I'm portioning out a resupply, and a couple of larger ones as a full food bag liner inside my Ursack or hang sack. The double zipper seals well enough to keep out moisture and most odors, though it's not airtight enough to fool a bear's nose on its own — treat it as organization, not scent-proofing.

**Heat and rehydration.** One use that comes up a lot in backpacking forums is cooking Mountain House-style meals directly in the bag with near-boiling water. <cite index="14-1,14-2">Ziploc brand freezer bags, over storage bags, are considered more durable and better suited to taking on more heat, so if you're pouring near-boiling water into one, the freezer style is the way to go.</cite> I've done this a handful of times without issue, but I still wrap the bag in a pot cozy or a wool sock — the plastic gets soft and the seams are the weak point when hot liquid is sloshing around.

**Durability.** These aren't indestructible, and expectations should be calibrated accordingly. A long-running touring forum thread comparing freezer bags found <cite index="12-8,12-9">that thicker competitor bags may be more durable, or more puncture, tear, abrasion, and wear resistant</cite> than standard Ziplocs, and users noted <cite index="12-10">vibration and slow abrasion can be an issue when touring</cite> — the same applies to a bag getting jostled around in a backpack for days. One trail-focused review put it plainly: <cite index="15-8">they're not the most durable when compared to Loksak OPsaks but they're much cheaper.</cite> That tracks with my experience — I've had bags develop pinhole leaks after a week or two of daily use, especially if I'm cramming in anything with sharp edges (energy bar wrappers, granola clusters).

**Reusability.** The upside is these aren't strictly single-use. <cite index="15-8,15-9,15-10">They're highly washable and reusable and you shouldn't need to throw away and replace every single one when you resupply in town.</cite> I typically get 3-5 resupply cycles out of one bag before the zipper starts feeling gritty or a seam starts to fail.

**Seal quality.** The double-zipper closure on the standard freezer bag (as opposed to the newer slider versions) has held up well for me — I haven't had a zipper blow out the way testers reported with the slider-closure gallon bags, which is worth noting if you're choosing between Ziploc's product lines. Stick with the classic double-zip for anything you're relying on for days at a time.

**Non-food uses.** I've used mine as an improvised dry bag for a phone and paper map, and as an odor-reducing liner for a trash bag on multi-day trips. <cite index="15-1,15-2">They're so cheap and for their weight and price, they're actually durable and reliable enough to store things in on your thru-hikes, and plenty of hikers use them for IDs, passports, and electronics.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Essentially free and weighs almost nothing (12 g)
- Reusable for multiple resupply cycles if you rinse and dry them
- Heat-tolerant enough for in-bag meal rehydration
- Double-zipper seal is more reliable than newer slider versions
- Widely available anywhere with a grocery store — easy to restock on trail

**Cons**

- Not puncture or abrasion-proof; sharp food packaging can poke through
- Not truly odor-proof or airtight — don't rely on it alone in bear country
- Seams and zipper degrade with repeated heat exposure and reuse
- Clear plastic shows wear/grime quickly, which bugs some people cosmetically (doesn't affect function)

## Who Should Buy This

Pretty much every backpacker, honestly — this isn't a niche product. It's ideal for anyone who wants a nearly weightless way to portion food, organize a pack, or add a layer of moisture protection without paying for (or carrying) a dedicated dry bag or specialty pouch. If you need genuine odor-proofing for bear country or want maximum durability for a bag you'll reuse for months, step up to a Loksak OPsak instead — but for day-to-day resupply organization and general trail utility, there's no real reason to look elsewhere.

## Verdict

At 12 grams and pennies per bag, Ziploc Gallon Freezer Bags are one of the best value-per-gram items you can put in a pack, and the double-zipper version is durable enough for the abuse of daily trail use if you don't expect it to last forever. I'd rate it an 8/10 — it loses points only for lacking true odor-proofing and for a shelf life measured in days-to-weeks of hard use rather than months, but for the cost and weight, nothing else comes close.
