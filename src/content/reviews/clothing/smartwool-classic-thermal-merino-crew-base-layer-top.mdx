## Overview

The Classic Thermal Merino Crew, sold until recently as the Merino 250 Base Layer Crew, is Smartwool's warmest 100% merino top. It's a cold-weather piece for ski touring, winter hiking, and sleeping-bag or camp duty on shoulder-season thru-hikes. The trade is familiar for heavyweight merino: excellent warmth and odor control against slow drying, modest durability, and a price near $110.

## Performance

**Warmth.** This is the layer's reason to exist. CleverHiker's testers ranked it among the warmest base layers they wore. They said it keeps the wearer warm under a jacket when standing still, and works as a warm standalone layer once moving. Better Trail's testers also put it near the top of their warmth rankings. A women's tester for GearJunkie wore the matching set on 20°F ski days and found it warm enough with room to spare.

On trail, "midweight" turns out to mean "camp and cold-morning weight." A reviewer at The Trek wore the Classic Thermal as camp clothing alongside a 20°F bag. It stayed comfortable on chilly mornings but ran warm quickly once hiking. Most three-season walking happens in lighter fabrics. This one earns its ounces below freezing.

**Comfort.** Comfort is the most consistent point in the reviews. Better Trail gave it top marks, citing a soft, blanket-like hand feel, stretch that eliminates pinch points, and flat seams that don't press under a pack. Reviewers describe the merino as softer than expected and note it isn't itchy. The slightly extended collar adds warmth at the neck. A Backdrop Journal tester who toured in it described the sleeves and body as free of hot spots and bunching under a pack.

**Odor.** The odor resistance is better than any synthetic. The GearJunkie reviewer wore the set five days straight in the Dolomites with no smell, and the Backdrop Journal tester said it stayed fresh through multiple touring days. For a long winter trip where laundry isn't an option, that's a real advantage.

**Moisture and drying.** The weak spot is drying. Merino wicks well, but the dense 250 g/m² knit holds water. In CleverHiker's controlled lab test, the Classic Thermal took the longest of all the layers to dry, and the testers rated its breathability below the field. A sweaty climb in this shirt will leave it damp for a while. That's manageable in dry, cold conditions. On multi-day trips where the shirt gets soaked and must dry on the body, it's a drawback.

**Fit.** The cut is trim but not restrictive, with enough length to stay tucked under a harness or snow pants. Better Trail's tester found a medium close-fitting but comfortable, and the added length sits a couple of inches below the waist without riding up. A REI reviewer recommended sizing down for a truly snug fit. Smartwool's fit model wears a medium at 6'2" and 32" waist.

**Durability.** This is the mixed part. Better Trail scored durability 2.5 out of 5. It noted that the heavier fabric holds up better than Smartwool's 150 g/m² All Season line, but merino of any weight thins and forms holes over time, and it won't match a synthetic that can last a decade. Reviews on REI echo the pattern: small holes near the waist or at points of friction, and a critical review calling the longevity poor for the price. Some owners of recent women's versions also say the fabric feels lighter than earlier Merino 250 tops. The shirt still averages about 4.7 out of 5 stars on REI across more than 300 men's reviews, so it clearly satisfies most buyers.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Among the warmest 100% merino base layers; works as a standalone layer in the cold
- Very soft, with flat seams and raglan sleeves that don't bother under a pack or harness
- Odor resistance holds up over multi-day wear
- Slim fit with extra body length stays tucked
- Doubles as camp and sleep clothing

**Cons**

- At 275 g it's too warm for most active three-season hiking
- Slow to dry; the longest in CleverHiker's lab drying test
- Merino thins and holes with wear; durability rated 2.5 of 5 by Better Trail
- Around $110 is steep for a layer that may need replacing
- Not a good fit for hot or humid trips

## Who Should Buy This

Winter and shoulder-season hikers, ski tourers, and mountaineers who want a warm, odor-free next-to-skin layer. It also suits thru-hikers who want one soft, warm top for camp and cold starts in a 20°F bag, and who are willing to carry about 10 ounces for it. Buyers who need a fast-drying, hard-wearing layer for sweaty, high-output days should look at a lighter merino-synthetic blend or a grid-fleece synthetic. Warm-weather hikers have no use for it.

## Verdict

The Classic Thermal Merino Crew is a first-rate cold-weather base layer that delivers on warmth, comfort, and smell. It costs a lot, dries slowly, and will eventually develop holes. For below-freezing use it's a strong buy. For anything milder, a lighter layer is a better choice.
