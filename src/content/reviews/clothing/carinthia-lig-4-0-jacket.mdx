## Overview

The LIG 4.0 (Light Insulation Garment) is the thinnest, least insulated jacket in Carinthia's cold-protection line. It is built for people who want a rugged synthetic layer that works as a midlayer under a shell or as an outer layer in dry cold. The trade-off is weight: at 540 g in size M, it is a hard-wearing field jacket first and a gram-counter's puffy a distant second.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The numbers on paper are modest: 80 g/m² of G-LOFT through the torso and 60 g/m² in the sleeves. Owners nonetheless report the jacket running warmer than it looks. European buyers describe wearing it over a T-shirt at around freezing, and one Austrian owner reports getting by in a T-shirt down to about -5 °C. Another owner reported wearing it at -15 °C over a light fleece, which is a stretch of the design brief, but it shows how far a windproof shell and a snug body can carry a thin layer. Reviewers who compare it with a down or heavier synthetic jacket say the LIG 4.0 gives up some warmth. For a layer this thin, that is expected.

**Weather protection.** The polyamide shell blocks wind effectively, and owners treat that as the jacket's real strength. Water resistance is a more qualified story. Buyers describe it as fine for a short shower and report it shedding light rain, but one French owner stressed that it is repellent rather than waterproof, and a heavy downpour will get through. Synthetic fill keeps working when damp, which is the reason to choose G-LOFT over down here, but this is not a jacket to wear in sustained rain without a shell on top.

**Packability and the back pocket.** New for 4.0 is a large pocket in the lumbar area that doubles as the stuff sack. Packed, it becomes a small pillow, a trick several owners and a German retailer's write-up single out for camp use. The pack size is described as small, though not tiny: reviewers call it moderately compact. A few owners say that stuffing the jacket into its own pocket takes some persistence. 540 g also means it will never compete with purpose-built ultralight puffies on bulk or weight.

**Hood, collar and cuffs.** The hood stows away and is unlined. Owners find it works best over a fleece beanie, and one reports that the permanent collar is warmer when the hood is tucked away. The tall collar carries a soft fleece lining. The cuffs combine a loose outer band with an inner elastic wrist gaiter, which works with gloves. The zip is a plain single-slider this time, not the two-way of earlier versions, with a wind flap behind it.

**Durability and build.** Owners consistently describe the construction as robust. The jacket's military and police pedigree comes up repeatedly. Two small complaints recur: some buyers think the zippers could be stronger, and one wanted reinforced shoulders and elbows for pack straps, as on the heavier HIG 4.0. Fresh from the bag, the fabric can look glossy; one buyer found a first wash toned the sheen down. Owners also note that the jacket layers well under a hardshell and works as a base layer for the heavier MIG and HIG jackets in the same line.

## Choosing a Version

The LIG 4.0 comes in sizes S through XXL, and the only thing that changes across the range is the fit. The cut is slim and European, with a straight body and no longer back hem. Owners say jacket and sleeve length are tied to the size, and the sleeve length is the common complaint.

- **True-to-size for slim builds:** A buyer at about 177–185 cm and 77–85 kg recommended M, and found L bulky. Even then, the sleeves ran a touch short.
- **Between sizes:** Several owners wish for a regular/long option and note that a long-armed hiker will find the sleeves short.
- **Tall wearers:** A 6'4", 230 lb buyer reports that an XL gave a good length and fit under a rain jacket.
- **Layering:** Anyone planning to wear a thick fleece underneath should size up. Pairing the LIG with the HIG 4.0 works best when both are the same size.

Color (olive, black, urban grey) doesn't affect performance. One buyer found the grey much darker than the photos, almost charcoal.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Warm for its weight; owners wear it at freezing and below with little underneath
- Wind-tight shell with a high, fleece-lined collar
- Synthetic G-LOFT insulation keeps working when damp
- Built-in stuff pocket that doubles as a pillow
- Robust build quality; layers under a shell or with the heavier HIG/MIG jackets
- Wrist gaiter-style inner cuffs work well with gloves

**Cons**

- 540 g in size M is heavy for a light synthetic layer
- Water-repellent only; not suited to sustained rain
- Hood is unlined and not adjustable in the way a shell hood is
- Sleeves run short, and the sizing is tied to length
- Packing into its own pocket can be fiddly
- Zippers draw occasional durability complaints

## Who Should Buy This

The LIG 4.0 suits the hiker, camper, or bushcrafter who wants a rugged, wind-resistant insulating layer for shoulder-season and winter use. It is at home under a shell on cold, dry mornings, and also works as a stand-alone coat in camp. It is also a good fit for slim-to-medium builds who like a tailored European cut. Gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere: lighter synthetic or down jackets will carry more warmth per gram and pack smaller. Long-armed hikers should try it on first.

## Verdict

The LIG 4.0 is a tough, windproof, honestly warm layer with a useful pillow-pocket trick, and it earns its weight if durability matters more than grams. It is not the pick for weight-focused thru-hiking, nor for wet-weather use without a shell. A solid 7 out of 10 for the cold-weather camper and mixed-conditions hiker.
