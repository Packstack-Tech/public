## Overview

The Moab 3 GORE-TEX is the waterproof version of Merrell's long-running, do-everything low-cut hiker. It has a suede-and-mesh upper, a GORE-TEX bootie and a Vibram TC5+ outsole. It suits day hikers, shoulder-season walkers and weekend backpackers who want a shoe that needs no break-in and keeps puddles out. At 460 g per shoe it is not a thru-hiker's shoe. The trade is a protective, durable platform for a weight penalty and a warm foot.

## Performance

**Comfort.** Comfort is the Moab's reputation, and reviewers back it up. Testers at Advnture described it as comfortable straight from the box with no break-in. RunRepeat's testers called the shoe supple and pleasant on the foot from the first step. One Trailspace owner covered 15 miles on the first outing without trouble, and a Walmart buyer reported a 21-mile day with nearly 5,000 feet of gain and no blisters or lost toenails. The 11.5 mm drop and the stiff, supportive platform suit walkers more than minimalists. Reviewers who prefer flexible, low-drop shoes have found the Moab firm underfoot.

**Waterproofing.** The GORE-TEX bootie does what it promises. Owners report dry feet in snow, wet grass, shallow creeks and slush. One REI reviewer waded rivers on a backpacking trip and reported staying dry and blister-free. The membrane has two drawbacks. A Trailspace reviewer noted it crinkles audibly, and the same shoe is not forgiving once water gets in over the collar. Water that enters through the top stays in, because the bootie that keeps rain out also slows drying.

**Breathability.** RunRepeat's testers found the low-cut GTX warmer than earlier Moabs and not a breathable shoe on its own. A separate owner review called it too warm for summer daily wear. For desert miles or humid July trail days, a mesh trail runner will keep feet cooler and dry faster. The GTX earns its place in cold, wet or snowy conditions.

**Traction.** The Vibram TC5+ outsole gets mostly good marks. Advnture's testers found grip on wet roots and mud, and RunRepeat's testers reported confident footing on pavement, rock and snow. Winter users also praise it on ice and snow. The 5 mm lugs are not universally loved. One long-term reviewer found them weak on wet rock and said they clog in thick mud, and a Trailspace owner reported the tread collects debris. Moderate trail is where this shoe belongs.

**Protection.** Protection is a strength. Advnture's testers highlighted the rubber bumper, large toe cap and bellows tongue, which keeps trail grit out of the shoe. A Trailspace owner noted the wide sole stance feels stable and cuts down on ankle roll. The same reviewer called the shoe heavy, and a second weight comparison belongs here: the mid version runs about 1 kg a pair, so the low saves little.

**Durability and fit.** Experiences vary. Some Pennsylvania delivery drivers report wearing out a pair a year, and others praise the tread life. Complaints focus on the uppers and the membrane rather than the rubber. One Trailspace owner saw small leaks after several seasons and was told by Merrell that the warranty had been cut to one year with a receipt. Some REI buyers report heel-cup stitching and liner wear, and one reported sole separation. Fit reports conflict. Some reviewers say it runs true to size, and others needed a half size up. Merrell offers wide widths, and wide-footed hikers often rate the Moab among the better options.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Little to no break-in
- Reliable waterproofing for snow, slush and stream crossings
- Vibram outsole grips well on roots, trail and snow
- Toe cap, rubber bumper and bellows tongue protect feet
- Wide widths available
- Stable, supportive platform for pack carrying

**Cons**

- 460 g per shoe is heavy next to trail runners
- Runs warm, with slow drying once soaked
- Tread clogs in deep mud and is less sure on wet rock
- Upper and membrane durability complaints, with reports of leaks after several seasons
- 11.5 mm drop and firm feel don't suit minimalists

## Who Should Buy This

Buy this if you hike in cold, wet or snowy conditions, want a shoe that works out of the box, and carry a moderate pack on maintained trails. It also suits wide-footed hikers and anyone who values toe and sole protection over grams. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers should look elsewhere: a breathable trail runner dries faster, weighs less and handles warm-weather mileage better. Hikers who want a cheaper Moab and mostly face shallow puddles can consider Merrell's standard waterproof version over the GORE-TEX.

## Verdict

The Moab 3 GORE-TEX is a comfortable, protective, truly waterproof shoe that earns its popularity with day hikers and cold-season walkers. It is also heavy and warm, and some owners report durability trouble. It is a fine wet-weather choice, but a poor fit for long, fast or hot ultralight miles.
