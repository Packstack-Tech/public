## Overview

The Ferrosi Convertible is Outdoor Research's do-it-all softshell hiking pant with a zip-off leg, aimed at hikers who want one pair of pants to cover a warm-weather thru-hike, a scramble, and a post-trail dinner in town. It's built from a 90D nylon-spandex ripstop that's been a staple in OR's lineup for over a decade, and the convertible version just adds a knee-level zipper so you can drop the legs into shorts without stopping to swap pants entirely.

## Performance

The defining trait of the Ferrosi is stretch. Reviewers across the board single this out — Adventure Alan called them <cite index="13-1">"significantly more stretchy than the average hiking pants, and the stretchiest in our wardrobe"</cite>, and that stretch is doing real work on trail. OutdoorGearLab's testers found <cite index="9-4">the Ferrosi moves effortlessly through high steps and scrambling without restriction</cite>, and in slot canyon testing <cite index="9-9,9-10">the Ferrosi handled scrambling, chimneying, and wide step moves with ease, with the generous stretch and articulated design giving the pants a natural feel while climbing and navigating tight sandstone features</cite>. That's a pretty specific endorsement for a pant that isn't marketed as a climbing-specific piece.

Breathability is the other headline feature. CleverHiker notes the fabric is <cite index="14-12">extremely breathable, for those who sweat heavily or hike in warmer temperatures</cite>, and OutdoorGearLab's men's review echoes this, saying <cite index="11-16,11-17">the Ferrosi Pants were exemplary in the venting and breathability metric, particularly standing out for hikes that involved steep uphills and when hitting the trail in the heat</cite>. If you run hot, this is a big selling point over heavier softshells like the Prana Stretch Zion.

Where the Ferrosi gives ground is durability and weather resistance. Multiple reviewers flag it as a lighter-duty option: OutdoorGearLab's men's review states plainly that <cite index="11-1">as a lighter-weight model, the Outdoor Research Ferrosi Pants aren't the most durable option on the market</cite>, though they added that the pants <cite index="11-2">ultimately outperformed their numbers on the scale</cite>. CleverHiker's take is similar — <cite index="14-1">they may not provide the highest level of durability and protection from rocks and brush, but they come through with their superior breathing and quick drying abilities</cite>. Adventure Alan, who's tested various iterations for over a decade, agrees the fabric is durable for its weight but adds a caveat: <cite index="13-2,13-3">they don't sacrifice too much durability thanks to the 90d ripstop nylon, though they are more prone to snagging on pokey objects than other hiking pants</cite>. If you're bushwhacking through scrub oak regularly, expect some pilling and the occasional snag rather than bombproof armor.

Weather resistance is modest by design. The fabric sheds light rain and wind but isn't a rain pant substitute — Pack Hacker found <cite index="1-13,1-14">the material has a water-resistant coating evident from small spills to a torrential downpour, though liquids will eventually soak through with greater submersion</cite>. The upside is fast dry time: the same review noted <cite index="1-15,1-16">if they get soaked, they dry extremely quickly, and even straight out of the washing machine without drying, no moisture was detectable after just a half-hour</cite>.

On fit, expect a relaxed, athletic cut rather than a slim trail-runner silhouette. OutdoorGearLab's men's review liked the fit through the thighs but was less enthusiastic about the ankles, noting <cite index="11-14">the baggier cut around the ankles and feet wasn't loved as much, especially hiking through scrub oak or dense brush, though no testers reported tripping on the extra fabric</cite>. Sizing seems to run tighter than expected for some — one Trailspace reviewer found the pants <cite index="10-1">pretty tight in the waist</cite> despite matching their usual size, so it's worth trying before committing if you're between sizes, or leaning on that internal drawcord for fine-tuning.

Small gripes worth flagging: one long-term reviewer found the newer leg-cuff cinch design used a small plastic clip that <cite index="15-10,15-11">falls off easily, and I already lost one of mine</cite>. Another wrote that the drawcord placement is buried inside the waistband, adding it's a hassle to tighten mid-hike, especially with a pack on. Nothing dealbreaking, but the kind of detail that matters after a few hundred miles.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional stretch for a woven (non-knit) hiking pant — great range of motion for scrambling and high steps
- Strong breathability, ideal for warm-weather and high-output days
- Dries very fast after getting wet
- UPF 50+ sun protection built into a lightweight fabric
- Convertible zip-off legs add versatility without much weight penalty
- bluesign-approved recycled nylon blend is a nice sustainability touch

**Cons**

- Not a durable choice for heavy bushwhacking or off-trail abrasion — prone to snagging and pilling
- Water-resistant coating only holds up to light rain, not sustained downpours
- Ankle/leg cut runs baggier than some hikers prefer
- Some report tight waist sizing relative to other brands
- Minor hardware complaints (leg-cuff clips, awkward drawcord placement) on certain production years

## Who Should Buy This

The Ferrosi Convertible is best suited to hikers who prioritize mobility and breathability over bombproof durability — think desert scrambles, warm shoulder-season thru-hikes, or scenarios where you want one pant that can go from trail to town. If you're regularly pushing through dense brush, scree, or need serious rain protection, this isn't your pant; pair it with a separate rain shell for wet-weather trips instead.

## Verdict

The Ferrosi Convertible earns its reputation as a versatile, comfortable warm-weather pant — the stretch and breathability are genuinely excellent, and the convertible design adds real flexibility for variable temps. Just go in knowing it's a lightweight softshell, not an armor-plated trail pant, and budget for it to show wear faster than heavier-duty options if you're hard on your gear. I'd rate it a 7.8/10 — a strong everyday pick for most three-season hikers, with durability as the one deduction worth weighing before you buy.
