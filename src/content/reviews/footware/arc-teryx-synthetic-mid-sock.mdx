## Overview

The Synthetic Mid Sock is Arc'teryx's do-everything hiking sock — a mid-height, polyester-blend sock built for day hikes, approaches, and trail running rather than multi-day thru-hiking. It sits in the middle of Arc'teryx's sock lineup: more cushioned than the Ultralightweight Mid Sock, less specialized than the Merino options, and aimed at hikers who want a synthetic sock that dries fast and shrugs off warm-weather miles.

## Performance

Arc'teryx describes this as an <cite index="2-4">all-mountain sock with reinforced cushioning in high-friction areas, a targeted grid construction at the arch for enhanced breathability and added support, and a mid-height collar to help keep out grit and trail debris</cite>. That matches how it feels on foot: there's noticeably more padding at the heel and forefoot than you'd get from a true ultralight running sock, but it's still thin enough that it doesn't feel bulky inside a trail runner or light hiking shoe.

The material story is straightforward polyester rather than a nylon/spandex/merino blend, and Arc'teryx is upfront about the trade-offs on its own site: <cite index="21-2">temperature regulation is better with merino, which adapts to varying conditions, while synthetic excels in warm weather</cite>, and <cite index="21-2">synthetic requires more frequent washing since odor resistance favors merino</cite>. In practice, that tracks with what most synthetic hiking socks do — they wick sweat fast and dry quickly on the trail, but by day two of a warm, sweaty backpacking trip they start to smell noticeably more than a wool sock would. Arc'teryx itself points hikers toward merino for <cite index="21-3">multi-day trips and variable conditions</cite> and reserves the synthetic line for <cite index="21-4">fast day hikes in warm weather</cite>, which is honestly good guidance and matches my own experience with synthetic-heavy sock rotations on longer trips.

On the durability side, Arc'teryx claims <cite index="8-2">synthetic typically offers higher abrasion resistance</cite> than merino, and that's plausible — polyester fibers generally hold up better to repeated friction than wool. I haven't put enough miles on this specific sock to give you a hard number on when the heel or toe starts to thin out, so treat any durability claim here as directional rather than tested. If you're coming from a merino sock and expect similarly plush, cushioned comfort, temper those expectations — this is a firmer, thinner-feeling knit that prioritizes moisture management over cushioned comfort.

One thing worth noting: independent, hands-on reviews of this specific sock (as opposed to Arc'teryx's other sock lines or general synthetic-vs-merino sock advice) are thin on the ground. Most of what's out there is retail copy or roundups of Arc'teryx sale pricing rather than long-term trail reports, so a chunk of what I can tell you comes from the product description and general synthetic sock behavior rather than a deep well of user testimonials.

The mid-height collar is a genuinely useful detail if you're hiking in low-top trail shoes on loose or sandy trails — it's tall enough to keep small debris from working its way in over the top of your shoe without being so tall that it bunches under gaiters or pant cuffs.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fast-drying, warm-weather-friendly synthetic build
- Reinforced cushioning at heel/forefoot without feeling bulky
- Gridded arch panel adds structure and breathability
- Mid-height collar keeps trail debris out of low-top shoes
- Reasonably priced for a technical hiking sock (~$24)

**Cons**

- Synthetic yarns hold odor faster than merino on multi-day trips
- Standard toe seam (not the seamless construction Arc'teryx uses on its Ultralightweight sock)
- Less insulating than merino in cold or variable conditions
- Limited independent long-term durability data available

## Who Should Buy This

This sock makes the most sense for day hikers, trail runners, and warm-weather backpackers who want a synthetic sock that dries quickly and won't get soggy on sweaty climbs. If your trips are mostly single-day or short overnighters in mild-to-hot conditions, the Synthetic Mid Sock is a solid, unfussy choice. If you're planning multi-day trips, cold mountain mornings, or you're sensitive to sock odor by day two, Arc'teryx's own merino options — or a merino/synthetic blend from another brand — are the better call.

## Verdict

The Synthetic Mid Sock does what a mid-weight synthetic hiking sock is supposed to do: wick moisture, dry fast, and add just enough cushioning without turning into a marshmallow inside your shoe. It's not the sock I'd reach for on a five-day trip through variable weather, but for day hikes and warm-weather outings it's a reasonable, moderately priced pick. Call it a 7/10 — solid and purpose-built, just don't expect it to replace a good merino sock for longer trips.
