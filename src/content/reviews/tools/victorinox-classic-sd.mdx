## Overview

The Victorinox Classic SD is the smallest knife that still earns the Swiss Army name: seven functions in a 58 mm body that weighs 21 g. It has been a fixture on ultralight packing lists for years, not because it does everything, but because it handles a surprising share of trail chores without anyone noticing it is in the pocket. The trade-off is plain: a short, non-locking blade, and a slick handle that is hard to hold.

## Performance

### The scissors do the real work

Ask hikers who carry the Classic SD which tool they reach for, and the answer is rarely the blade. The spring-loaded scissors open food packages, trim Leukotape and guyline, and shape moleskin or gauze for blister care. Owners report that they stay sharp and precise even after repeated gear repairs on the trail. On a longer trip, they get more use than everything else on the knife combined.

### The blade: adequate, and no more

The blade measures roughly 1⁵⁄₁₆ inches, which sets a hard ceiling on its job description. Opening a freeze-dried meal pouch, slicing cheese or cutting thin cord is fine. Carving wood or any sustained cutting is not. Because the blade does not lock and cannot be opened one-handed, applying real pressure means closing a hand around an unsupported edge, a limitation worth respecting. The blade can feel flimsy when first opened, yet long-term owners describe years of use without failures, and it arrives sharp and holds its edge well.

### Handling in the field

The three main tools (blade, scissors and file/screwdriver) each have a fingernail nick and pull out smoothly with little pressure. They keep doing so after years of life in a pocket or on a keychain. The weak point is the grip. The small, smooth handle gives fingers little to hold, and the problem grows when hands are tired, wet or cold. Rain, snow and humidity all make it worse.

### The supporting cast

The nail file doubles as a 2.5 mm flat-head screwdriver, which has few occasions to prove itself in the woods. When it does, tightening an eyeglass screw or a headlamp battery door, it is the only tool that will do. On long trips the file's real job is toenails.

The tweezers are the sleeper. They sit in the handle scale at no weight cost, and they are precise enough for splinters and tick removal in poor light. Reviewers regularly rate them above what a knife at this price should carry. The toothpick sits next to them and needs no explanation.

### Durability

The Classic SD is a small, light knife and should be treated as one; abuse can damage it. Used within its limits, it tends to outlast its owners. The blade and most other metal parts are stainless, so rust is not a worry in wet conditions. The scale tools, including the toothpick and tweezers, are cheap and easy to replace if they wear out. The CleverHiker staff each carry one in an ultralight medkit, and the knife has stayed in their multitool guide for many seasons.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Seven functions for 21 g is hard to beat on weight efficiency
- Scissors are sharp, precise and the tool most often used
- Tweezers and toothpick ride in the handle with no weight penalty
- Swiss build quality well beyond what a roughly $15 price suggests
- Stainless parts shrug off wet weather
- Cheap to replace when airport security confiscates it

**Cons**

- Blade is too short for serious cutting
- No lock and no one-hand opening
- Smooth, small handle is slippery when wet
- 2.5 mm screwdriver has few backcountry uses
- Cannot go in carry-on luggage

## Who Should Buy This

The Classic SD suits ultralight and thru-hiking setups that need one compact tool for food prep, first aid and minor gear repair. Many hikers have carried the same one for a decade without wanting something bigger. It also works well as a keychain knife for town days. Anyone planning to process game, carve wood or cut thick rope should look elsewhere, or pair it with a dedicated fixed or locking blade such as an Opinel No. 6 and treat the Classic as the supplement.

## Verdict

At 21 g and about $15, the Classic SD is among the best-value pieces of gear in the ultralight kit. The scissors alone justify its place, and the tweezers and file are useful extras. Buy it if food prep and tape trimming are the extent of your knife duties. Skip it if you expect the blade to do heavy work.
