## Overview

The NB10000 Gen4 is the fourth iteration of Nitecore's popular ultralight power bank line, and it's aimed squarely at people who'll actually notice a 10-gram difference in their pack. It uses a silicon-carbon cell to squeeze 10,000mAh into a 143g package with an IPX7 rating, dual USB-C ports, and a carbon fiber shell — basically a power bank designed for thru-hikers, trail runners, and minimalist EDC carriers rather than someone who just wants to top off an iPad at the airport.

## Performance

The headline number here is weight-to-capacity ratio, and it holds up under scrutiny. <cite index="6-9,6-10">Most 10,000mAh power banks weigh between 220-280g, while NITECORE has engineered the NB10000 series to come in significantly lighter than that using high-density cells and a refined enclosure.</cite> CleverHiker's independent bench testing backs this up: <cite index="2-2">in their testing, the NB10000 emerged as one of the lightest and most compact 10,000mAh power banks in their lineup</cite>, and <cite index="2-8">it also took the top spot for efficiency per gram, which is great news for ounce-counting backcountry users who are intentional about what goes in their pack.</cite>

The bigger story with Gen4 specifically is usable capacity, not just rated capacity. Nitecore's own numbers show <cite index="13-13,13-15">7,200mAh (just over 36Wh) delivered in eco mode at 1A/5V, dropping to 6,800mAh (34Wh) at 3A/5V in regular mode</cite>. That's a real jump over the previous generation — <cite index="13-18,13-19">the Gen3 only listed a figure for 2A at 5V, which came in at 6,200mAh (31Wh), meaning the new generation offers up to 20% more usable energy in the best case</cite>. In practice, that Eco/Regular split matters on trail: eco mode trickle-charges more efficiently for phones and watches, while regular mode is there when you need a fast top-up and don't mind burning through capacity a bit quicker.

Nitecore added a few genuinely useful quality-of-life features for Gen4. <cite index="2-9">There's a knock-to-wake feature, glowing input/output ports, and a touch-sensitive power button</cite> — small touches, but useful when you're fumbling with a headlamp on at 6am trying to check remaining charge. Pass-through charging is also supported, so you can charge the bank and a device simultaneously overnight in the tent, which cuts down on morning wait time.

Build quality is where things get more nuanced. Pack Hacker's two-week test noted the shell itself held up well — <cite index="1-12,1-13,1-14">the NB10000 Gen4 keeps the carbon fiber panels that have become a staple of NITECORE's battery banks, and it still feels great in hand and holds up well to daily use</cite>. But they flagged a design regression at the corners: <cite index="1-16,1-17,1-18,1-19">instead of the rubberized bumpers from the previous version, NITECORE switched to hard plastic — not a huge deal, since they're not overly sharp, but it does change the feel slightly, making it a bit sleeker though arguably less impact-friendly.</cite> If you're the type who drops gear off rocks regularly, that's worth knowing.

On the output ceiling: 22.5W is fine for phones and small devices, but don't expect it to fast-charge a laptop or tablet at full speed. One review summed it up bluntly — <cite index="5-15,5-16">where the NB10000 Gen4 shows its limits is in the upper tier of power delivery, and with a 22W ceiling, it's not the right tool for anyone hoping to meaningfully</cite> push higher-wattage devices. That's a reasonable trade-off given the weight target, but it's a real limitation if you're trying to use this as your only bank for a laptop-heavy setup.

The included lanyard cable is a nice touch for a category where cables get lost constantly — it's designed to double as a carry strap, which is the kind of small design decision that actually gets used on trail rather than sitting in a drawer.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely class-leading weight-to-capacity ratio for a 10,000mAh bank
- IPX7 rating gives real peace of mind in rain or river crossings
- Eco/Regular mode split lets you choose efficiency vs. speed
- Knock-to-wake and illuminated ports are legitimately handy in the dark
- Pass-through charging works, useful for overnight tent charging
- Included lanyard cable doubles as a carry strap

**Cons**

- 22.5W ceiling means it's not suited for laptops or higher-draw devices
- Hard plastic corners are less impact-resistant than the previous generation's rubberized bumpers
- Dual USB-C only — no USB-A for older accessories
- Premium price relative to generic 10,000mAh banks

## Who Should Buy This

This is a strong pick for thru-hikers, ultralight backpackers, and trail runners who are charging phones, GPS units, headlamps, or small watches and want to shave every possible gram doing it. If your charging needs stop at phone-and-headlamp territory and you're already invested in USB-C gear, the NB10000 Gen4 is close to as good as it gets in this weight class. If you regularly need to charge a laptop, tablet, or higher-wattage device on the go, look elsewhere — the 22.5W cap will leave you waiting.

## Verdict

The NB10000 Gen4 delivers on its core promise: more usable capacity per gram than most of its competition, wrapped in genuinely useful features like pass-through charging and knock-to-wake. It's not the toughest bank in a hard fall and it won't fast-charge a laptop, but for its intended audience — gram-counting hikers charging phones and small electronics — it's one of the best options on the market right now. I'd rate it an 8/10.
