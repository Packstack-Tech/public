## Overview

The Pro Bar MEAL Bar in Peanut Butter Chocolate Chip is a dense, plant-based bar built from oats, peanuts, seeds and dark chocolate, sold as a stand-in for a real meal. It suits backpackers who want a no-stove lunch or a bailout dinner. The trade-off is a high sugar load and a price that adds up over a long trip.

## Performance

**Calories and weight.** At 400 calories in 85 g, the bar lands at roughly 133 calories per ounce. That is better than most tortilla-and-cheese lunches and in line with a good trail mix. Energy comes mostly from carbohydrate and fat, split roughly 43% carbs and 48% fat by calories. A retailer nutrition panel lists about 22 g of fat and 43 g of carbohydrate per bar. Protein is modest. At 11 g it will not carry a full recovery meal, but it is enough to take the edge off midday hunger.

**Satiety.** Owners often describe the bar as filling for its size. One reviewer noted it is about double the size of a typical granola bar. The oats, peanut butter and seeds give it more staying power than a gel or a chew. Reviewers who use it as a meal replacement say it keeps hunger away on long days. Many eat half as a snack and save the rest for later. The package is built for that: the nutrition panel lists a half-bar serving of 200 calories.

**Durability.** This is where the bar earns its spot in a food bag. Trail to Peak describes a square bar about three-quarters of an inch thick that packs into corners without crumbling or breaking apart. The same reviewer ate it at high elevation in cold temperatures and found it still chewed easily. Chewy bars like this can turn into bricks in the cold. A few minutes in a jacket pocket helps if the bar is stiff on a frosty morning.

**Taste and texture.** The peanut butter and dark chocolate flavor is Pro Bar's most popular MEAL bar, according to the company. It is chewy and dense, with swirls of peanut butter and chocolate chips. The sweetness comes from brown rice syrup, cane sugar and dates. Per-100 g figures from a nutrition database put total sugars near 25 g, and a retailer label lists about 21 g per bar. That is dessert-level sweetness. Days of eating these back to back will test the palate of anyone who prefers savory food.

**Cost.** At roughly $2.50 a bar in a 12-pack, a bar-only lunch for a week costs about $17.50. That is cheaper than most freeze-dried dinners. It is pricier than the same calories in tortillas, nut butter and oil.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 400 calories in 85 g, about 133 cal/oz
- Holds its shape in a pack and in cold weather
- Vegan, gluten-free and Non-GMO Project verified
- No cooking, no water, no cleanup
- Easy to split into two 200-calorie portions

**Cons**

- High sugar, roughly 21 g per bar
- 11 g of protein is low for a true meal
- Contains peanuts, so it is a no-go for allergy sufferers
- Single flavor fatigue sets in quickly on a long trip
- Costs more per calorie than bulk staples like nut butter or tortillas

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend and section hikers who want a lunch that needs no stove will get the most from it. Thru-hikers can use it as a rotation item, perhaps one or two a day alongside savory food. Vegans and gluten-free hikers get an easy option without a long label to read. Hikers who need high protein for recovery, who are sensitive to sugar, or who are allergic to peanuts should look elsewhere. So should anyone building a food bag on a tight per-calorie budget.

## Verdict

The Pro Bar MEAL Bar is a sturdy, calorie-dense lunch bar that survives a pack and the cold. It is sweet and its protein is modest, so it works best as part of a varied food bag rather than the whole thing. For no-fuss trail lunches it is a solid pick.
