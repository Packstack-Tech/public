## Overview

The Insulated AXL Air is Big Agnes's answer to the Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite: a 337 g inflatable pad that stacks up to 3.75 inches of air under a sleeper and packs to roughly the size of a one-liter bottle. PrimaLoft Silver insulation and a heat-reflective film handle the warmth. The trade-off is that the warmth is strictly three-season, and the pad's durability record is less settled than its comfort.

## Performance

**Comfort.** This is where the AXL earns its reputation. At launch, Andrew Skurka pointed out that it was 50 percent thicker than the XLite's 2.5 inches, and testers have consistently described the pad as cushy. CleverHiker found the dimpled interior leaves enough room that a sitting sleeper doesn't bottom out on a fully inflated pad. The larger outer chambers do what the catalog copy says: they cradle the sleeper toward the middle, which matters most for side sleepers who tend to roll off narrow 20-inch pads. Backpackers.com noted that the pad doesn't bounce much despite its thickness, and both CleverHiker and Skurka found it slightly quieter than the XLite, a real consideration for anyone who has crinkled through a night in a shelter.

**Warmth.** Here the story gets more qualified. The pad carries an R-value of 3.0, and Big Agnes originally rated it to 15°F. Testers were less generous. CleverHiker put its practical limit at about 32°F, and Skurka's verdict was that it is warm enough for three-season conditions only. In his use, nights in the high 30s and low 40s in the High Sierra were comfortable, and he considered late September about the seasonal limit in the Mountain West. Treat it as a spring-to-fall pad. Shoulder-season hikers expecting frost should add a foam pad or look at a higher-R pad.

**Weight and packability.** The mummy version weighs 10.6 oz, but the 20 x 72 rectangular pad described here is rated at 11.9 oz. Testers' scales ran a little higher: Skurka measured 12.6 oz (12.9 oz with the stuff sack), and CleverHiker got 12.1 oz for the pad alone and 12.9 oz with the stuff sack and repair kit. At 3 x 6.5 inches packed, it fits into a pack's corner or side pocket without much fuss.

**Inflation.** The low-profile two-way valve allows fine adjustment and quick deflation. A thick pad holds a lot of air, though. CleverHiker counted about 17 full breaths to fill it, on par with the XLite. Skurka suggested that at 10,000 feet a pump sack becomes more appealing.

**Durability.** This is the pad's soft spot. Big Agnes uses a patterned ripstop nylon that it says improves tear strength by 25 percent, with TPU lamination for weld strength. Even so, Backpackers.com noted that reports of durability problems keep appearing for the pad, which it called a recurring issue for Big Agnes. The review praised nearly everything else about the pad and said it came close to being the top pick for ultralight backpackers. Carry the repair kit and pitch on cleared ground.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very thick (up to 3.75 in) for its weight, with a cushioned feel that testers consistently praise
- Oversized outer chambers keep sleepers centered
- R-value of 3.0 covers most spring-to-fall nights
- Packs to 3 x 6.5 inches
- Quieter than the XLite, according to both CleverHiker and Skurka
- Fine-adjust two-way valve deflates quickly

**Cons**

- Practical limit nearer 32°F than Big Agnes's 15°F claim
- Takes around 17 breaths to inflate, and more at altitude
- Durability reports are mixed
- Real-world weights run about 0.2–0.9 oz above the listed figure once the stuff sack and repair kit come along
- At 20 inches wide, there's little margin for restless sleepers

## Who Should Buy This

Weight-conscious thru-hikers and section hikers who sleep on their sides, and who camp mostly between spring and early fall, are the target. For them, the AXL delivers more cushion per gram than nearly anything else in its class. Cold sleepers, shoulder-season and winter campers, and anyone who punishes gear on rocky or thorny ground should look at a higher-R pad or a sturdier build. Hikers who want maximum warmth for the weight will find the Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XTherm a better fit.

## Verdict

The Insulated AXL Air is among the most comfortable ultralight pads for three-season use, with a thick, stable platform in a package that disappears in a pack. Its warmth is honest but modest, and its durability record means a repair kit belongs in the pack. For mild-weather hikers who put comfort first, it's an easy pick.
