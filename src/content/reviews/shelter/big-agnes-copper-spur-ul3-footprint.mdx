## Overview

The Copper Spur UL3 Footprint is a custom-cut ground cloth for Big Agnes's three-person freestanding tent, and it does two jobs: it shields the tent floor from abrasion and moisture, and it turns the tent into a fly-and-poles shelter when the inner body stays home. It suits Copper Spur owners who want either floor insurance or a lighter pitch on dry, bug-free trips. The trade-off is price: $100 for 198 g of coated nylon is a premium over a sheet of Tyvek or polycryo.

## Performance

**Floor protection.** The Copper Spur's floor is thin, and that is a recurring theme in owner discussion of the tent on Reddit, where the UL3 is praised for space and headroom but flagged for a delicate floor. A footprint is the obvious answer. This one is cut to the tent's floor shape, so nothing overhangs to catch rain and funnel it under the tent, a common failure of oversized, DIY ground sheets. The 30D ripstop is heavier than the polycryo many ultralight hikers carry, and it shrugs off gravel, roots, and granite grit in a way polycryo does not. The 1500 mm coating and taped seams mean the footprint also works as a moisture barrier on soaked ground.

**Fast Fly pitching.** The reason to buy this over a generic sheet is the Fast Fly setup. Stake out the footprint, insert the tent poles into its corners, drape the fly over the top, and buckle it in. The configuration needs only the footprint, fly, poles, and stakes, so the inner tent can be left at home. Owners on r/WildernessBackpacking note that every Copper Spur can pitch fly-first with the footprint, which makes it useful in a downpour, since the fly goes up before the mesh body is exposed to the rain. The cost is that the fly-only mode gives up bug protection and a fully enclosed interior. It is a shelter for dry-ish, mosquito-free trips, or for shoulder-season outings where wind and rain matter more than insects.

**Weight and packability.** At 198 g, the footprint packs to 20 x 13 x 5 cm, about the size of a thick paperback. Add it to the tent for everyday use and it is a modest penalty, comparable to a long-sleeve base layer. Used as a Fast Fly floor with the tent body stripped out, it replaces the much heavier inner, which is where the weight savings come from.

**Owner satisfaction.** Buyers rate it well. REI lists the standard footprint at 4.9 out of 5 across 16 reviews, and a retailer review from a Copper Spur UL3 owner called it a footprint that "works great." The lower-scoring footprint in the line is the Bikepack version, which carries its own listing and weight.

**Caveats.** The fit is specific to the current Tiplok-buckle Copper Spur UL3 and its mtnGLO and Bikepack siblings. Owners of older Copper Spur HV UL3 tents with a different attachment system need to check compatibility before ordering. The standard footprint also doesn't fit the longer Copper Spur UL3 XL, which has its own 96 x 72 in footprint at $120. The footprint covers the tent floor only; there's no vestibule coverage.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Cut to the UL3 floor, with no overhang to collect water
- Enables Fast Fly pitching with fly, poles, and stakes
- 198 g and about the size of a paperback when packed
- Taped seams and a 1500 mm waterproof coating
- PFAS-free coating
- Strong owner ratings (4.9 of 5 at REI)

**Cons**

- $100 is steep next to Tyvek or polycryo alternatives, which cost a fraction as much
- Fits only the Tiplok-buckle Copper Spur UL3 family, not the XL
- Heavier than polycryo for owners who only want floor protection
- Fast Fly mode gives no bug protection
- Doesn't protect the vestibules

## Who Should Buy This

Copper Spur UL3 owners who want a pitch that can drop the inner body for dry trips, or who camp regularly on abrasive ground, will get real use out of it. Because it is cut to the tent, it is also the easiest way to protect the floor without trimming a sheet of Tyvek. Anyone who only wants cheap floor protection should look at a custom-cut Tyvek sheet, which costs around $29 from specialty sellers for the related HV UL3, or a polycryo sheet. Owners of the UL3 XL need the XL footprint instead.

## Verdict

A well-made, tidy accessory that does exactly what Big Agnes says, and the Fast Fly option adds genuine versatility to a tent that is otherwise on the heavy side for ultralight use. The price keeps it from being an easy recommendation for floor protection alone. Buy it for the Fast Fly pitch; otherwise a cheaper ground sheet does the job.
