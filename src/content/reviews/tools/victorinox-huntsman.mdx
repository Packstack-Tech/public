## Overview

The Victorinox Huntsman is the 91 mm Swiss Army Knife that most people picture when they hear the phrase: a Swiss-made folder with a large blade, a small blade, scissors, a wood saw and a corkscrew, plus a small drawer of screwdrivers and openers. It takes the long-running Officer's-style layout and adds the two tools that matter most outdoors, the saw and the scissors. The trade-off is weight. At 97 g it weighs several times what a minimalist trail knife does, and it earns its place only if a hiker will actually use more than the blade.

## Performance

**Cutting.** The large blade is a roughly 2.45-inch plain edge in 1.4110 stainless, a steel Victorinox tempers and polishes for corrosion resistance rather than edge-holding heroics. Owners use it for the jobs a camp knife actually gets: slicing cheese and salami, opening food packaging, trimming cord, and whittling kindling. It sharpens easily with a basic stone or ceramic rod, which suits a steel that wants regular touch-ups more than a long-haul edge. The small blade is useful as a second, cleaner edge for food or first-aid prep.

**The saw and scissors.** These are the reason to pick the Huntsman over the smaller, lighter Classic-style knives. The wood saw handles branches and tent-stake-sized stock well enough for fire prep, and it's a genuine step up from batoning with a 2.5-inch blade. The scissors are the more frequently used of the two on trail: cutting tape, trimming moleskin, snipping loose threads and opening stubborn packets. Hikers who carry a separate pair of tiny scissors in a first-aid kit can fold that weight into this tool.

**Everything else.** The can opener, bottle opener and corkscrew are conveniences rather than trail tools. The two screwdrivers cover trekking pole hardware, eyeglass screws and stove-fitting tweaks, and the 6 mm driver doubles as a light pry. The reamer and sewing awl are quietly the most valuable tools for repair work, punching through tough fabric, webbing or leather for gear fixes. Tweezers and a toothpick live in the scales and handle splinters and ticks.

**Handling.** The ABS/Cellidor scales are slim at 21 mm and slide into a pocket or hip-belt pocket without snagging. Nail nicks on the tools are stiff, and owners commonly note that opening the saw and larger implements takes a fingernail and some patience. That stiffness is the flip side of a tight build. Nothing opens one-handed, and the lack of a lock means the blade is for controlled cuts, not heavy prying.

**Weight in context.** In 97 g, the Huntsman carries more than most ultralight kits need. Gram counters typically bring a small blade, or a sub-30 g folder, and let a small pair of scissors and a repair needle fill the gaps. The Huntsman makes sense for those who value having every tool in one place, or who do trips where camp cooking, fires and repairs are a regular part of the day.

**Durability and support.** Victorinox backs it with a lifetime warranty against defects, and the stainless implements tolerate rain, river crossings and the damp interior of a pack. The plastic scales are less romantic than the wood-handled variants, but they shrug off drops and dirt, and the pouch-equipped versions offer a belt-carry option.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Scissors and a wood saw in a 91 mm frame, the two tools most useful beyond cutting
- Reamer, awl and screwdrivers make field repairs realistic
- Stainless implements resist corrosion and are easy to sharpen
- Lifetime warranty and Swiss-made build
- Slim 21 mm profile for a 15-function knife

**Cons**
- 97 g is heavy by thru-hiker standards for a pocket knife
- No one-handed opening and no blade lock
- Blade is short, at roughly 2.45 in
- Many functions (corkscrew, can opener) rarely earn their weight on trail
- Stiff nail nicks on some implements

## Who Should Buy This

The Huntsman suits section hikers, bikepackers, car campers and bushcraft-leaning weekenders who want a single tool that handles food prep, fire prep and small repairs. It also works as an all-purpose everyday carry that just happens to be happy outside. Thru-hikers and base-weight-obsessed backpackers should look elsewhere: a lighter small-blade Swiss Army model or a bare-bones folder covers 95 percent of trail cutting needs at a fraction of the weight. Anyone who wants a lockable, one-hand-opening blade for heavier work needs a dedicated folder.

## Verdict

The Huntsman is a well-built, well-equipped generalist whose 97 g mass is both its biggest selling point and its biggest liability. Buy it for trips where scissors, a saw and a repair awl will see use; skip it for fast-and-light mileage where a blade and a few grams of tape are all the kit requires.
