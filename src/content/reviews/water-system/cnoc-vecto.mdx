## Overview

The CNOC Vecto is a collapsible TPU water container with two openings: a wide mouth sealed by a slider, and a soda-bottle-style threaded neck that accepts a filter. It is made for backpackers who squeeze or gravity-filter and are tired of the narrow-necked pouches that filters ship with. The trade-off is weight and longevity. It runs roughly twice the weight of the thinnest soft bottles, and the TPU wears out eventually.

## Performance

### Filling

Filling is the reason the Vecto has a following. The wide mouth takes a swipe or two through a stream, where a Sawyer pouch, which fills only through its narrow neck, takes many. In a shallow trickle or a silty seep, that gap decides whether collecting water takes thirty seconds or ten minutes. Still water is fussier. Owners describe needing two hands, one to hold the mouth open and one to dunk the bag, and plenty of hikers just bail with a cook pot at ponds.

### Filtering

The threaded neck mates with 28 mm or 42 mm filters, depending on the version. Users report a tight, leak-free seal with Sawyer filters when the threads are started carefully and not overtightened. A few Trailspace reviewers had trouble getting the filter to engage. The fix is the same each time: seat the gasket squarely before tightening.

For gravity filtering, the loop on the slider lets the bag hang from a branch or a trekking-pole tripod. The slider seals watertight even when the bag is inverted, which the Platypus GravityWorks dirty bag cannot reliably do. Hang it, thread on the filter, and go pitch the tent. Squeeze-filtering is no strain, since the TPU takes hard pressure without the kinking that gives stock Sawyer bags their pinhole leaks.

### Carrying Water

The Vecto collects water better than it carries it. It tends to squirm out of external straps unless it is full, and CNOC's own FAQ advises against packing a full bag inside a pack if leaks are a concern. Most owners do it anyway without incident. A hard bottle still earns more trust next to a down quilt.

### Durability

The TPU wears over the long run. One longtime owner got close to two years from a Vecto before it sprang a leak after about 150 days on trail, a pattern that looks like fatigue more than puncture. CNOC says pinholes from UV, cold and abrasion are not covered by warranty unless they appear on a new bag. A round patch of Tear-Aid Type A or a drop of superglue handles the repair and weighs next to nothing.

The slider and cap are tethered, so neither gets dropped in the dirt or lost in the dark. A lost cap can be replaced by any standard PET bottle cap. The slider is the weak point. Owners report it getting stiff to open and close after several days of grit on trail, and a broken one cannot be fixed in the field. CNOC sells replacements, which is little help on day nine.

### Cleaning and Taste

The bag turns inside out, and the wide mouth lets a hand in for scrubbing. It dries in about twenty minutes after washing. New bags give water a faint plastic taste that fades with use. TPU is porous and not completely odor-free from the factory, so CNOC recommends washing and airing the bag before the first trip.

## Choosing a Version

The design is the same across the range. What changes is capacity and thread size.

**1L (69 g)** is the lightest and smallest, packing to 7 × 2 × 1.25 inches. It suits solo hikers who filter at the source and drink as they go, and anyone who wants a dedicated dirty bag with minimal bulk.

**2L, 28 mm (74–79 g across the 2L listings)** is the all-rounder. The Water Container 2L, 2L, 91 g 2L and 28mm listings span just 5 g, a gap no one will feel in a pack, so choose by price and availability. Two liters covers a solo hiker's evening cook water plus a refill and packs to 7 × 2 × 1.5 inches. This is the one for Sawyer Squeeze, Mini and Micro, HydroBlu Versa Flow and LifeStraw Flex users.

**2L (42mm) (87 g)** is the same bag with the wider thread for the Katadyn BeFree and other 42 mm filters. It costs about 8 g over the 28 mm 2L. BeFree owners have no other choice, because a 28 mm bag will not fit.

**3L (91 g and 105 g)** is the dry-camp and shared-camp size. It packs to 7 × 2 × 2 inches and stretches to about 17 inches when full, which hangs well from a tree. A couple's cooking and drinking water fits in a single filtering session. The 91 g bag is CNOC's standard 3L Vecto. The 105 g figure matches what CNOC lists for its heavier VectoX 3L, which uses thicker TPU and carries a 450 lb breaking point, so gram counters should check which bag a 105 g listing actually describes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide mouth fills quickly from streams, seeps and shallow sources
- Threaded neck fits 28 mm filters, with a 42 mm option for BeFree users
- Built-in loop and watertight slider make gravity filtering easy
- Cap and slider are tethered
- Turns inside out for fast cleaning and drying
- TPU resists the kinking and pinholes that plague stock Sawyer pouches
- Three capacities, from 69 g to 91 g in the standard bag

**Cons**

- Heavier than minimalist soft bags: a 2L Platypus Platy weighs about 37 g against roughly 75–79 g for the Vecto 2L
- Pinholes can develop with age, UV and cold, and are not covered by warranty after purchase
- CNOC advises against carrying it full inside a pack
- A broken or gritty slider cannot be fixed on trail
- New bags taste of plastic until washed and broken in
- No drinking hose

## Who Should Buy This

The Vecto fits backpackers who filter with a Sawyer, HydroBlu, LifeStraw Flex or BeFree and want a collection bag that does not fight them. The 1L and 2L suit solo hikers who filter at the source, and the 3L suits dry-camp hikers and pairs sharing a gravity setup. Anyone who sips from a hose on the move, or who wants the lightest possible bag for the least money, should look at a plain Platypus Platy or an Evernew bottle instead.

## Verdict

The Vecto does one job, collecting and holding untreated water for filtering, better than the pouches that come with most filters. It adds weight and wants a patch kit on long trips, but the fast fill and easy gravity setup justify both. For a Sawyer or BeFree water system, it is one of the better upgrades available at about $24–26.
