## Overview

The Terrebonne Joggers are Patagonia's answer to the question of what to wear when a hiking pant, a running tight and a pair of sweatpants all feel like the wrong choice. At 170 g (about 6 oz), they are a slim-cut, stretch-ripstop pant with a ribbed waistband and cuffs, built for trail miles, travel days and camp evenings. The trade-off is durability and warmth: this is a featherweight fabric, and it behaves like one.

## Performance

**Comfort and mobility.** This is where the Terrebonne earns its reputation. The ripstop has four-way stretch, the knees are articulated, and the ribbed cuffs keep the hems from creeping up the shin when sitting. A FeedTheHabit reviewer who took them trail running found they flex enough not to restrict movement, though they feel snug when pushing hard. A European retailer's customer testing turned up a similar theme from a buyer who wanted something for travel and trail running that wasn't tights, but could still pass in a restaurant.

**Weather and sun.** The DWR finish handles light moisture, and buyers describe the pants as surprisingly water resistant for something this thin. Gearweare's testers make the same point: polyester sheds water naturally, and the coating adds to it, but this is not a rain pant. UPF 40+ adds useful sun protection on exposed ridgelines. Warmth is minimal. The fabric cuts wind and adds a little insulation on a cold morning run, but a stiff breeze reminds the wearer how thin it is. Reviewers agree these suit warm and mild conditions, not deep cold.

**Fit quirks.** The slim cut is the most common source of complaints. Several owners with larger calves report the lower leg runs tight, and sizing up is the standard advice. One buyer who returned a pair found the seat and crotch baggier than the slim label suggests. The drawcord is another recurring note. FeedTheHabit's tester found the waist sits low in a squat unless tied snugly, and wished the cord were longer.

**Durability.** At 2.7 oz, the body fabric is built for comfort first. Owners report that the pants hold up to regular hiking and travel use, and one backcountry reviewer lived in them for six days of backpacking and called them seemingly durable. The limits show up in crashes and scrapes: a REI buyer reported a tear after a slow, low-speed bike fall. Brush, granite and talus will be harder on these than on a heavier nylon hiking pant.

**Packability and pockets.** The weight and fabric compress well, which is why owners compare them to a Houdini-style "just in case" layer for travel. Two drop-in front pockets handle the usual snacks and phone, and the zippered rear pocket keeps a key or card secure. Reflective branding adds a bit of visibility at dusk.

**Owner sentiment.** The pants average 4.3 out of 5 across 36 reviews at REI. Positive reviews cluster around feel, breathability and versatility; the critical ones around calf fit, waistband quality compared with some competing joggers, and tear resistance.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 170 g, compressible and breathable
- Four-way stretch and articulated knees move well on trail
- Quick-drying, with DWR for light moisture and UPF 40+ for sun
- Zippered pocket plus two drop-ins
- Comfortable enough to double as camp, travel and town pants
- Fair Trade Certified sewn, with a PFAS-free DWR

**Cons**

- Slim leg runs tight on larger calves
- Short drawcord; waist can sag when untied
- Thin fabric is vulnerable to snags and abrasion
- Little warmth, and no real storm protection
- Some buyers find the seat roomier than the slim label implies

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season hikers and thru-hikers on established trails who want a pant that works as both a hiking bottom and a camp or town pant will get the most out of it, especially in warm to mild weather. It also suits travelers who want one lightweight pair that handles a day hike and dinner out. Those who regularly push through brush, scramble on rough rock or want a tough long pant should look at a woven nylon hiking pant. Cold-weather hikers need a warmer layer, and anyone with large calves should size up or try on first.

## Verdict

The Terrebonne Joggers are a comfortable, packable, quick-drying pant that outperforms most athleisure bottoms on the trail, as long as the fabric is treated with a bit of care. The calf fit and abrasion resistance keep them from being a universal pick, but for easy-to-moderate trail use and travel, they are a strong choice at 170 g.
