## Overview

The RAVPower RP-PB165 is a slim, single-port 10,000mAh power bank aimed at anyone who wants a phone top-up without hauling around a brick. RAVPower has been churning out budget-friendly charging gear since 2011, and this unit sits squarely in their "keep it simple" lineup — one USB-C port, one job, done reasonably well. It's a good fit for day hikers, commuters, or anyone padding out a weekend pack who doesn't need to charge a laptop or run a CPAP.

## Performance

On paper, this is a straightforward brick: <cite index="5-5,5-7">it offers a constant 20W output and can restore full power in just 2 hours using a USB-C to C cable connected to a wall charger that supports USB-C Power Delivery.</cite> That 2-hour recharge is genuinely useful — a lot of budget banks in this capacity range still ask for 4-5 hours, which is annoying if you're trying to top off before heading out the door.

Real-world capacity is the usual story with any 10,000mAh bank: you're not getting 10,000mAh of usable charge into your phone. Between the AC-to-DC conversion loss and the bank's own internal circuitry draw, expect something closer to 65-70% efficiency, which lines up with what other reviewers report for RAVPower's similar models — <cite index="10-1">roughly 3.7 charges for an iPhone or Samsung S21, and about 1.1 charges for a larger tablet like an iPad Pro.</cite> That's a reasonable, if unremarkable, return for the capacity.

Where RAVPower's single-port USB-C banks earn some credit is in dual-load handling. On a similar model in the lineup, one reviewer testing simultaneous charging noted that <cite index="9-21,9-22,9-23">RAVPower's power bank doesn't take a performance loss — it was able to charge two USB-C devices up to 10W each at the same time, when some USB-C PD power banks drop to 10W total when two ports are in use.</cite> The RP-PB165 itself is single-port, so this doesn't directly apply, but it's a good sign for how RAVPower tunes its charging circuitry generally.

Build quality gets mixed marks across the RAVPower catalog. Some owners of similar RAVPower banks report it <cite index="14-3">has a heft to it and felt like it was made from quality materials unlike other powerbanks that felt really light and cheap</cite>, which tracks with my general impression of RAVPower's mid-tier lineup — nothing premium, but not flimsy either. On the flip side, longevity complaints do show up in the wild: one buyer of a similar-capacity RAVPower unit reported <cite index="16-1">that after a couple of recharges, the power bank stopped charging their phone or iPad</cite>. That's not universal, but it's worth flagging — lithium-ion cells in budget banks can be inconsistent from unit to unit, and RAVPower's warranty coverage is your safety net if you get a dud.

Speaking of which, that warranty is a genuine bright spot. <cite index="9-32,9-33">RAVPower has U.S.-based support (web, email, phone) and an 18-month warranty, which can be extended to 30 months if you register the product.</cite> That's better coverage than a lot of no-name Amazon banks offer, and it's worth actually doing the registration step if you buy one.

For backpacking specifically, 189g for 10,000mAh is a fair but not class-leading ratio — you'll find lighter Li-Po banks from Nitecore or Anker's Nano series at similar capacity. The single USB-C port keeps things simple, but it also means no simultaneous multi-device charging, and there's no wireless charging or built-in cable, so you're on your own for cables in camp.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Compact, slim form factor that slides into a pack's hip belt pocket easily
- 20W output and ~2-hour recharge time are solid for this capacity class
- 18-month warranty (extendable to 30 months) backed by US-based support
- Multiple safety protections built in (over-current, over-voltage, short-circuit, fire-proof)

**Cons**

- Single USB-C port only — no simultaneous charging, no USB-A fallback
- No wall charger included, so you'll need your own USB-C PD brick to hit the fast recharge time
- Some reported unit-to-unit reliability inconsistency in RAVPower's budget lineup
- Not a class-leading weight-to-capacity ratio for ultralight-focused buyers

## Who Should Buy This

This is a sensible pick for casual hikers, commuters, and travelers who want a single reliable phone top-up without paying for features they won't use — dual ports, wireless charging, laptop-level wattage. If you're an ultralight backpacker chasing every gram, there are lighter options at the same capacity, and if you need to charge a tablet or laptop alongside your phone, look further up RAVPower's lineup or at a higher-wattage PD bank instead.

## Verdict

The RAVPower RP-PB165 does exactly what a budget 10,000mAh bank should do — charge your phone a few times, recharge reasonably fast, and not fall apart in six months (usually). It's not the lightest or fastest option in its capacity class, but between the 20W output, 2-hour recharge, and RAVPower's above-average warranty terms, it's a fair value pick rather than a must-have. I'd call it a 6.5/10 — solid and dependable, but not a standout next to today's best ultralight power banks.
