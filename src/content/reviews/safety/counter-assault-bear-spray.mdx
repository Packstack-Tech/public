## Overview

Counter Assault's 8.1 oz canister is the smaller of the company's two bear deterrents. It's built for hikers who want EPA-registered protection without carrying the bulk of the 10.2 oz can. The trade-off is plain: 308 g of dead weight you hope never to use, in exchange for a shorter reach than the bigger can.

## Performance

The formula is the headline. At 2% capsaicin and related capsaicinoids, Counter Assault sits at the ceiling of what the EPA permits for bear deterrents, so the strength gap between brands comes down to delivery rather than heat. Delivery here means an atomized, high-volume cloud that reaches 32 feet and keeps going for 7 seconds. The company pitches that pairing as the sweet spot between range and duration. Seven seconds is long enough to correct a first miss or hit a second charge. Thirty-two feet is roughly three bus lengths of warning. The 10.2 oz sibling stretches to about 40 feet, and some hikers consider that extra margin worth the added weight.

Counter Assault also carries some history. The company markets this as the first EPA-registered bear spray, and says the canister works on all bear species. Its listings also cite mountain lions and coyotes. The company says every canister is leak-tested at the factory, which matters on a pack that spends weeks rattling around on a hip belt.

The safety mechanism is a small but useful piece of design. The wedge glows in the dark and is tethered to the can with a string, so it stays attached when pulled. On a dusk approach to camp, that means a visible, findable safety rather than a black plastic clip lost in the duff. Owners on retail sites rate the 8.1 oz can highly, with the holster-equipped version averaging about 4.9 stars across roughly 20 Walmart reviews.

On the trail, the canister is a tall, slim cylinder that rides at 8.5 inches by 2 inches. The optional nylon holster has a touch-closure flap and attaches to belts and packs, and the same holster fits the 10.2 oz can. Thru-hikers who skip hip belt pockets generally clip it to a shoulder strap, where the can can be drawn with one hand while walking. A can buried in a pack's main compartment is a can that won't be used in the two seconds a charging bear allows.

Two caveats apply to any canister in this class. Bear spray expires, and this one is rated for four years, so a can bought on sale and forgotten in a garage drawer is a can to replace. And a deterrent is only as good as the person drawing it. Counter Assault sells the 8.1 oz can bundled with an inert training canister that mimics the real range and duration, which is the sensible way to practice drawing and aiming before a trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Maximum legal 2% capsaicin concentration
- 32-foot range and 7-second spray give a workable margin for a second burst
- EPA-registered, with a four-year shelf life and factory leak testing
- Glow-in-the-dark safety wedge is tethered and hard to lose
- Holster option clips to belts and pack straps
- Inert training canister available for practice
- Shorter than the 10.2 oz can, which makes it easier to stow on a shoulder strap

**Cons**

- 308 g is a serious penalty on a base weight chased in single-digit ounces
- Shortest reach in the company's lineup; the 10.2 oz can reaches about 40 feet
- Expires after four years, so it can't be a one-time purchase
- Can't be taken on flights in a carry-on, which complicates trips that start with a plane ride
- Holster costs an extra $5 over the canister price

## Who Should Buy This

This is the can for backpackers, thru-hikers and trail runners heading into grizzly or black bear country who want a short, easy-to-carry canister and accept a 7-second discharge. It suits hikers who carry it on a shoulder strap and have practiced the draw. Anyone whose route is dominated by open terrain, or who expects to meet a bear at distance rather than at a campsite, should consider stepping up to the 10.2 oz can for the added reach. Hikers in regions with no bears or cougars can leave it at home and save the weight.

## Verdict

Counter Assault's 8.1 oz spray does the one thing it's for, with the strongest legal formula, a reasonable range and a safety wedge that's hard to lose. It's heavy by ultralight standards, but bear country isn't the place to shave grams. Buy it with the holster, practice with the inert can, and mount it where the right hand can reach it.
