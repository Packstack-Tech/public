## Overview

The Camp Cutlery Spork is Sea to Summit's cheapest way to get food from a pot to a mouth: a single molded piece of glass-reinforced polypropylene that sells for about a dollar at most retailers. It is aimed at campers, families and newer backpackers, and Sea to Summit itself lists its best use as camping, car camping and entry-level backpacking. The trade-off is plain. It costs next to nothing and shrugs off abuse, but it gives up a few grams and some reach to the brand's aluminum sporks.

## Performance

Start with the number that matters to this category: 10 g. That is a hair more than the 9 g Sea to Summit quotes for its AlphaLight aluminum spork, and 1 g is a rounding error in any pack that isn't already a spreadsheet. Where the Camp Spork differs is in the hand. Sea to Summit describes it as a full-sized design that works bare-handed or with gloves, and the shaped handle is meant to nest against other pieces in the Camp Cutlery line for compact packing. At 18 cm it is a conventional table-style length, which suits a bowl or a mug. A deep bag of rehydrated dinner is another matter: the 21.6 cm AlphaLight Long that testers at 99Boulders singled out for eating from bags has a clear reach advantage.

Durability is the point of the glass fibre. The material is polypropylene loaded with 30 percent glass, a recipe meant to stiffen a part that would otherwise flex and snap at the neck. Owners describe it as sturdy. One buyer who bought several for a toddler called them extremely sturdy and noted they washed easily in the dishwasher, adding that at a dollar apiece, even a six-month lifespan would justify a repurchase. European retail reviews are similarly warm: a 4.6 average across 22 reviews at Bergfreunde, with 91 percent recommending it, and comments that focus on the low weight, the comfortable shape and the sense that it is stronger than it looks.

Heat is where plastic utensils have a reputation to live down. Cheap sporks can soften or warp when they are left in a hot pan, and testers who cook with them in camp have watched them melt. Sea to Summit rates this one as heat-stable for stirring meals, which covers the usual job of working a spork through a pot of freeze-dried food or instant noodles. The practical limit is still the same as for any plastic tool: use it to stir and eat, and don't rest it on a stove burner or against a hot pot wall.

Smaller details are well judged. The carabiner hole lets the spork hang off a pack strap, a mug handle or a loop of cord, which helps with the real hazard to a $1 utensil, which is losing it at a lunch stop. The 0.5 cm thickness is slim enough to slide into a pot, a cook-kit pouch or a hip-belt pocket without taking up room. The spork is also microwave safe and clean enough in the dishwasher, which makes it useful at home between trips and a sensible pick for car camping or the family kit.

What it lacks is polish. There is no long handle, no titanium, no carbon-fibre swagger, and the plastic bowl does not have the thin edge of a metal spoon for scraping the bottom of a bag. Anyone who has spent time with a titanium spork will notice.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 10 g (0.4 oz), light enough that it doesn't register in a base weight
- Costs about $1, so losing one barely matters
- Glass-reinforced polypropylene is tough, and the spork is dishwasher and microwave safe
- Heat-stable for stirring hot meals
- Carabiner-compatible hole makes it easy to clip to a pack or cook kit
- BPA-free, and Sea to Summit lists the cutlery as free of PFAS and PFOA

**Cons**

- 18 cm handle is short for eating from deep freeze-dried bags
- Plastic bowl lacks the thin edge of a metal spork for scraping out pots
- Not for use over a flame or against a hot pot wall
- Sea to Summit positions it as entry-level, not a long-haul tool

## Who Should Buy This

This spork is for the hiker who wants a dependable utensil without spending money on one: weekend backpackers, families outfitting kids, car campers, and anyone who keeps a spare in the glovebox or desk. Thru-hikers who eat mostly from bags, or who want the reach of a long handle, should look at the AlphaLight Long or a titanium long-handled spoon. Gram-counters who want a stiffer, thinner metal bowl for scraping will also be happier elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Camp Cutlery Spork does one job at a price that makes it hard to argue with. It is strong, easy to clean and light enough, and it is a sensible backup for any kit. For a primary long-distance utensil, a longer handle is worth the extra few dollars.
