## Overview

The Buff Merino Wool Multifunctional Neckwear is a seamless tube of 100% merino that weighs 48 g and can be worn more than a dozen ways: neck gaiter, beanie, headband, balaclava, sun shield, pirate-style head wrap. It suits thru-hikers and three-season backpackers who want one small item to cover sun, wind and chilly mornings. The trade-off is that wool is not for everyone's skin, and the thin fabric only goes so far as a cold-weather layer.

## Performance

**Temperature range.** At 125 g/m², this is a light merino, and it is meant to modulate rather than insulate. Reviewers on Trailspace credit it with managing heat well enough to be tolerable in hot conditions while still giving real thermal protection when it's cold. Its best work is at the edges of the day. One REI buyer on a hike doubled the tube over his head through a cool morning, then flipped it back to a pirate-style wrap once the day warmed up. Anyone expecting a ski-day neck warmer should look at Buff's heavier merino options. Reviews of Buff's merino tubes in snowy conditions describe them as comfortable but quick to chill.

**Sun and wind.** The tube is long enough to cover the neck, ears and face. Experienced long-distance users say its most valuable job is keeping the sun off the neck, face and head. A Trailspace reviewer called it essential gear across nearly a decade and thousands of miles. Another noted that the 26.5-inch length lets it be folded in half for extra warmth without leaving a gap between collar and chin. The fabric is fine enough to sit low on the neck as a one-inch band, or to be pulled up over the whole head.

**Odor and moisture.** Merino's main selling point on a long trip is that it doesn't get funky. Owners report that it wicks sweat and stays fresh well past the point where a polyester tube would need a rinse. Another trick is to dunk it in a cold stream and wear it as a neck cooler. Merino stays comfortable when wet and still cools the skin on a hot climb.

**Durability.** One Trailspace reviewer wore a merino Buff through the entire Te Araroa (2,000 miles) and Pacific Crest Trail (2,700 miles), plus part of the Continental Divide Trail, before retiring it. That is a high bar for a sub-2-ounce accessory, and one that cotton bandanas and cheap synthetic tubes don't clear.

**Care and comfort.** Owners say the tube survives machine washing, but it should stay out of the dryer; the fine knit is nearly dry straight out of the wash. Itch is the recurring complaint. A few REI reviewers found the fabric scratchy against the skin and returned it or wore it only over something else. Most owners describe it as soft and forget it's on. Buyers with sensitive skin should assume a small risk, particularly at the neck and jaw.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 48 g for a sun scarf, beanie and neck warmer in one item
- Merino resists odor over multi-day wear and handles sweat well
- Proven mileage on long trails, including full PCT and Te Araroa thru-hikes
- Seamless construction and stretch let it sit flat under hats, helmets and pack straps
- Works wet as an evaporative neck cooler
- Dozen-plus wearing configurations cover most weather changes

**Cons**

- Some wearers find merino itchy against bare skin
- Too thin to be a primary cold-weather layer
- Needs a gentle wash and air dry
- Priced well above a synthetic tube, at roughly $30 at retailers
- Lacks the UPF-rated sun protection some synthetic tubes advertise

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and multi-day backpackers who want a single multi-purpose item in the lid of the pack should buy it. It suits desert and alpine trips where sun protection and cool-night warmth both matter. It also suits trips long enough that odor becomes a concern. Anyone with sensitive skin should try it on first or look at a synthetic tube. Winter hikers, skiers and others who need serious wind protection should buy a heavier merino or fleece option.

## Verdict

The Buff Merino is one of the best weight-to-versatility trades in a thru-hiker's kit. It is cheap by gear standards, durable, and doesn't smell after a week. Just make sure the wool doesn't itch before committing it to a long hike.
