## Overview

The Hannah Walk II is a high-cut merino hiking sock from the Czech outdoor brand Hannah. It is knit and sewn in the Czech Republic from Schoeller merino yarn, and it is aimed at hikers who want a conventional, durable trail sock at a modest price. Its trade-off is plain: it uses a nylon-and-polypropylene backbone to hold its shape, so it is a more traditional sock than a fine-gauge, ultralight merino.

## Performance

The fiber breakdown is the most useful thing to know about the Walk II. At 71 percent merino, the sock sits well above the wool content of many "merino blend" socks, and Hannah's other Walk Lite II, at 64 percent merino, gives a sense of where this one sits in the range. That share does the work on the trail: wool moderates temperature across a wide range of conditions and resists the sourness that builds up over multi-day trips without a wash. Hannah credits the blend with moisture wicking, odor resistance, strength and elasticity.

The remaining 29 percent holds the sock together. Polyamide, the largest synthetic share at 15 percent, is what gives merino socks their abrasion resistance. The 11 percent polypropylene is a hydrophobic fiber that moves moisture away from skin and dries quickly. The 3 percent elastane gives the sock its recovery, so it should return to shape after each wear rather than sagging into the boot.

Fit is addressed by the "fixation zones," bands of tighter knit across the instep and around the ankle. Their job is to stop the sock creeping down or bunching under the arch, which is the usual cause of hot spots on long descents. The reinforced toe and heel put extra material where socks wear through first and where boots rub hardest. Those are the two places a sock fails on a long trail, and they are the main reasons to choose this over a plain knit.

The high cut suits mid-height boots and trail runners worn with gaiters or in cooler weather. It protects the ankle from boot collars and trail debris. In summer heat, the extra height is more sock than many ultralight hikers need, and the lower-cut options in Hannah's range will suit them better.

What the Walk II doesn't promise is also worth noting. Hannah doesn't market it as a cushioned or heavily padded sock, and at 59.7 g it falls into the everyday hiking weight range rather than the featherweight or expedition categories. It works as a three-season sock for most walkers. Anyone chasing the lightest, thinnest merino for hot-weather thru-hiking, or the plush warmth of a thick winter sock, will find it sits between the two.

Hannah sells the sock mainly through European retailers and its own site. It is typically priced between about €13.50 and €15, which is low for a sock with this much merino and local manufacture.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- High merino content (71%) for odor resistance and temperature regulation
- Fixation zones at the instep and ankle designed to keep the sock in place
- Reinforced heel and toe where hiking socks wear out first
- Made in the Czech Republic from Schoeller yarn
- Priced around €14, undercutting many comparable merino hiking socks

**Cons**

- High cut adds warmth and bulk that hot-weather hikers may not want
- Polypropylene and polyamide content means it is not a pure-merino feel
- Not marketed as a cushioned or expedition-weight sock
- Distribution is concentrated in Europe, so North American hikers may need to import

## Who Should Buy This

Trail hikers and trekkers in Europe who want a durable, everyday merino sock for boots or mid-height trail shoes should pick this up, especially if they are replacing cotton blends or cheap synthetics. It also suits shoulder-season hiking, when the high cut and merino share help with temperature swings. Ultralight thru-hikers who run low-cut trail runners in hot conditions, and anyone who wants heavy cushioning for winter, should look at a lower-cut or thicker option.

## Verdict

The Walk II is a straightforward, sensibly built hiking sock. Its high merino share, targeted reinforcement and fixation bands are backed by a price that makes buying several pairs easy. It earns a solid recommendation for three-season boot hikers, though not a place in the kit of anyone chasing the lightest possible setup.
