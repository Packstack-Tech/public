## Overview

The Extra Quilt 32F Standard is the entry-level down quilt from Ice Flame, a Chinese cottage-scale maker that sells direct and through AliExpress. It pairs 700 FP DWR-treated duck down with 20D nylon on both faces, and it lands at 554 g with 360 g of fill. The trade-off is plain: it is far cheaper than cottage-brand quilts from the US and Europe, but it is not as light as the maker's own premium 7D goose-down models.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Ice Flame lists a comfort range of 0–20°C with a lower limit of −5°C, and it sets those numbers up with a caveat. The limit is intended only for physically strong sleepers, and leaner people are told to add 5–10°C to the rating. Read that way, the quilt works as a true freezing-point quilt for average sleepers and a roughly 5–10°C quilt for cold sleepers. The 32°F label is the maker's own figure, so buyers who sleep cold should plan around the conservative end. The maker sells the quilt for "3000m-level mountaineering" in spring, summer and autumn, which is a fair description of a shoulder-season bag. It is not a winter one.

**Weight and fill efficiency.** At 554 g with 360 g of down, fill makes up about 65 percent of the total weight. That is a respectable ratio for 20D fabrics, which are sturdier and heavier than the 7D and 10D shells on premium quilts. Ice Flame's own Extra Quilt LT, with 800 FP goose down and a 7D shell, is listed at about 500 g. The Standard gives up roughly 50 g to get a tougher shell and a lower price. The 700 FP duck down is less lofty per gram than the 800–900 FP fills in the pricier models, so it also compresses a little less.

**Construction.** The central body uses box baffles, with a breathable mesh layer between the inner and outer fabrics. Box construction is the standard way to keep down from shifting and to avoid cold spots. The quilt has no hood, and the maker recommends a separate down hood in cold weather. Ice Flame also notes that a quilt takes more skill and experience to use than a mummy bag. The quilt attaches to a sleeping pad with the two included straps. Draft control depends on a good pad fit and a sleeper who stays put.

**Water resistance.** The down carries a PFOA- and PFOS-free water-repellent finish, which Ice Flame says stays dry 90 percent longer than untreated down. That helps with condensation and tent-wall drips during a damp night. It does not make the quilt a rain garment. Down quilts of any kind lose loft when soaked, and this one is no exception.

**Value and customization.** Buyers can add 100 g or 200 g of extra down at checkout, which turns the 32°F quilt into a colder-rated one for a modest weight gain. A reviewer at Nomad Hiker, who tested an Ice Flame 20D quilt of the same family, rated it as strong value for budget-conscious hikers. That reviewer also praised the zippered footbox with a cinch cord, which lets the bottom close up like a sleeping bag for cold feet. Both a compression sack and a larger storage sack come in the box. The storage sack is meant to keep the down uncompressed between trips and extend its life.

**Caveats.** Ice Flame is a direct-order brand, so buyers should expect international shipping times and no local service. The product page has sometimes shown the quilt as sold out. Rated temperatures come from the manufacturer rather than from a published lab test, which is common among quilts at this price.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 554 g with 360 g of fill is a good weight for a freezing-rated quilt at this price.
- 20D shell and lining are more durable than the ultra-thin fabrics on pricier quilts.
- DWR-treated down gives a margin against condensation and damp nights.
- Box baffles and a 140 cm width give usable coverage for side sleepers.
- Extra down can be added at checkout, and a compression sack and storage sack are included.
- Priced around $127–157, well under US cottage-brand quilts.

**Cons**

- 700 FP duck down is bulkier and heavier per unit of warmth than 800–900 FP goose down.
- No hood, so cold nights call for a separate down hood.
- The rating is the maker's own, and cold sleepers need to add 5–10°C.
- Ordering direct brings long shipping times and no local warranty support.
- A quilt takes more skill to manage drafts than a sleeping bag does.

## Who Should Buy This

This quilt suits the budget-minded 3-season hiker who wants to move from a bag to a quilt without spending $300 or more. It also fits a gram-counting beginner who wants a 32°F quilt with some durability and room to add down. It works best for people who already own a decent pad and sleep with a beanie or hood. Cold sleepers, winter campers and anyone who wants a published lab-tested rating should look at a name-brand quilt or buy the extra-down version. Hikers chasing the lowest possible base weight are better off with Ice Flame's own 7D goose-down models.

## Verdict

The Extra Quilt 32F Standard is a well-built, sensibly priced down quilt that does what its spec sheet says, as long as buyers apply the maker's own advice and shave a few degrees off the rating. It earns a solid score for value and weight, and it loses points for 700 FP duck down and an unverified temperature rating. Shoulder-season hikers on a budget should buy it. Cold sleepers should add down or pay for a warmer quilt.
