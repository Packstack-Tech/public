## Overview

The Optimus Polaris Optifuel is a remote-canister/liquid-fuel hybrid stove built for people who don't want to think about fuel logistics — it'll run on LPG canisters, white gas, gasoline, kerosene, diesel, or jet fuel, all through a single jet with no swaps required. It's aimed squarely at expedition users, international travelers, and cold-weather backpackers who need one stove that works regardless of what's available at the local shop, rather than weight-weenie 3-season thru-hikers.

## Performance

The headline feature here is the single-jet design, and it's not just marketing spin — multiple long-term users confirm it actually works as advertised. <cite index="4-7,4-8,4-9">Stoves that can use different fuels have been around for many years, but with most you need to change the jet when you change fuels, which is a fiddly business — the Polaris is different as the fitted jet will work with all fuels.</cite> That's a meaningful practical difference if you're the type who loses tiny jets in the dark at 12,000 feet.

Boil times land where you'd expect from a pressurized multi-fuel burner — <cite index="6-7,6-8">the official spec is about 4 minutes per liter, depending on climate, altitude, etc.</cite> I'd treat that as a best-case number; wind, altitude, and pot choice will push it out, especially on liquid fuel where you're also managing pump pressure and priming.

Build quality is the thing nearly every reviewer circles back to. <cite index="6-1,6-2">At 475g it isn't the lightest multi-fuel stove on the market, but the build quality is excellent, and the pump unit is solidly engineered — one of the best pump units testers have used.</cite> That pump quality matters more than it sounds — a mushy or leaky pump is the single most common failure point on liquid-fuel stoves, and it's reassuring that this is consistently called out as a strength.

Stability and simmering are where the Polaris earns its keep over lighter alternatives. <cite index="4-10,4-11">The combined legs/pot supports fold inward for a compact package, and when opened up the low-profile stove is very stable and will support heavy pots.</cite> Out of the box, simmer control is decent but not great — this is a known weak point of the stock flame spreader. <cite index="6-10,6-11,6-12">The aftermarket 'PolarDawg Silent Cap' makes a considerable difference, not only reducing noise but also letting you turn the stove down to a really low simmer level without it blowing out like it does with the standard flame plate.</cite> If you plan to actually cook (not just boil water for freeze-dried meals), budget for that accessory — several long-term users treat it as close to mandatory. On the plus side, this one at least _can_ simmer reasonably well without mods, which distinguishes it from a lot of liquid-fuel competitors. <cite index="6-15">A lot of multi-fuel stoves are great at boiling water but generally rubbish for actual cooking due to most not being able to turn the flame down to simmer.</cite>

Noise is the other recurring complaint, though it's endemic to the category rather than unique to Optimus. <cite index="4-2,4-13">In use the Polaris roars loudly, as many multi-fuel stoves do, even with cartridges.</cite> If you're used to a quiet canister-top stove, the first cold start will surprise you.

Cold-weather performance is a genuine strength. <cite index="4-1,4-12">The burner has a pre-heat tube and there are supports for an inverted cartridge for use in cold weather</cite>, which lets you run an upside-down gas canister like a liquid-fuel stove when temperatures drop below where standard canisters perform reliably — a trick you can't do on most canister-only stoves.

Fit and finish details that owners appreciate: <cite index="3-16,3-17">there's a windshield and heat reflector to mitigate damaging fragile ground and any fuel spillage, plus a full repair kit, pump lubricant, and a magnetic cleaning needle</cite>. One long-term liquid-fuel user on the Backpacking Light forums noted their older Optimus liquid-only version <cite index="8-2">worked like a charm for over a decade</cite>, which speaks to the durability of Optimus's burner lineage generally, though it's not a direct data point for this specific hybrid model.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Single jet runs all supported fuels — no swapping tiny parts in the field
- Excellent pump build quality, widely praised across owner reviews
- Genuinely stable, wide-folding pot supports handle heavy cookware
- Cold-weather capable via inverted-canister support and pre-heat tube
- Comes with a genuinely useful accessory kit (windscreen, reflector, repair tool, cleaning needle)

**Cons**

- At 475g, it's heavier than dedicated canister stoves or ultralight liquid-fuel options
- Loud — a known trait of the category, but worth setting expectations
- Stock simmer control is mediocre until you add the aftermarket Silent Cap
- Liquid fuel operation still requires priming/pumping know-how — not plug-and-play like a canister stove
- Overkill (and extra weight) for straightforward 3-season canister-only trips

## Who Should Buy This

This stove makes sense for expedition travelers, international trekkers who can't count on finding the right gas canister, and cold-weather or high-altitude users who need liquid fuel reliability but still want the option to run clean LPG when it's available. <cite index="2-3,2-4,2-5">The main market for a stove of this quality, capability, and price is people going off on big trips or expeditions where they're unsure what fuel will be available — run it on gas for weekend trips, then switch to petrol for a multiday hike abroad.</cite> If your trips are all domestic weekend trips with easy canister access, a lighter dedicated gas stove will serve you better and save you a few ounces plus a lot of noise.

## Verdict

The Polaris Optifuel delivers on its core promise: one jet, any fuel, and hardware solid enough to trust on a real expedition. It's not the stove to bring if all you care about is shaving grams off a weekend canister trip, but for anyone whose travel plans include fuel uncertainty or serious cold, it's one of the more dependable multi-fuel options out there — especially once you add the Silent Cap for better simmering. I'd rate it an 8/10, docked slightly for the stock simmer performance and the noise that comes standard with the category.
