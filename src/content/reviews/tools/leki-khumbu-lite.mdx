## Overview

The Khumbu Lite is Leki's entry point for hikers who want the brand's best-known features without the carbon-fiber price. It's a three-section aluminum pole with an external lever lock and an angled cork-and-rubber grip. At 246 g (8.6 oz) per pole, it isn't an ultralight pole. It trades some grams for stiffness, durability and a locking system that owners say doesn't slip.

## Performance

**Grip.** The Aergon grip is the reason to buy this pole. Section Hiker's Philip Werner calls the grips what make these poles great. They tilt forward at an 8-degree angle, so the wrist bends less on each plant. They also have articulated thumb and finger rests for climbing, and a rounded top pommel to rest the palm on descents. Owners who tested the poles on long hikes report no rubbing or blisters, and one Appalachian Trail hiker says the grips get tackier when wet. Cork absorbs moisture less aggressively than foam, which is a minor point against it on sweaty alpine climbs. A few buyers coming from other Leki models find the top of the grip a little small.

**Locking.** Speed Lock Plus is a flip lever that can be worked with gloves on. Reviewers describe it as quick to adjust and quick to collapse on the move. REI customers report levers that never slipped or released under heavy load. One reviewer leaned on them for most of their body weight down a descent after a knee injury. Another owner who had switched from budget Amazon poles said the locks hold without sliding. Those poles are rated 4.7 out of 5 across 69 REI reviews.

**Stiffness and feel.** HTS 6.5 aluminum is heavier than carbon, but it flexes less and tolerates abuse better. Section Hiker's testers found the pole solid and stiff under load. Switchback Travel, which lists the pole among its picks for trekking-pole-supported tents, describes a sturdy build that doesn't flex or bend the way cheap poles or ultralight carbon models can. It also doesn't shatter when it wedges between rocks. That durability is the practical argument for aluminum on talus, scree and rooty New England trail.

**Weight and packability.** Here the pole gives ground. At 246 g, the pair comes to 492 g, and carbon rivals that cost more are lighter. As a three-section telescoping design, it also packs longer than a folding Z-pole. UK reviewer Coast and Fell is blunt that it is neither an ultralight nor a folding pole. For hikers who carry poles in a hand all day, the swing weight is fine. Anyone counting grams for a sub-10-pound base weight will notice the difference.

**Details.** The carbide tips are replaceable, and Leki supports the line with spare parts, so a worn tip or bent lower section doesn't mean replacing the pair. Section Hiker notes that rubber tips and snow baskets aren't included. Switchback also notes the poles come without extra baskets or a hex tool, which some competitors bundle. The anti-shock Khumbu Lite AS is a separate model, and its reviews are more mixed because of a firm DSS spring. Those complaints don't apply to the standard pole.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Angled Aergon cork-composite grip that reduces wrist strain and stays comfortable over long days
- Speed Lock Plus levers that are easy to adjust with gloves and hold under load
- Stiff, durable HTS 6.5 aluminum that tolerates rocks and abuse
- Wide 100–135 cm range suits tall hikers and shared poles
- Replaceable carbide tips and baskets, with spare parts available
- Costs much less than premium carbon poles

**Cons**

- 246 g per pole is heavier than carbon ultralight options
- Telescoping three-section design packs longer than folding poles
- No rubber tips, snow baskets or hex tool in the box
- Grip top is on the small side for some hands

## Who Should Buy This

The Khumbu Lite suits backpackers and section hikers who care more about grip comfort and lock reliability than the last 50 g. That includes first-time buyers moving up from throwaway poles, hikers with sore wrists, and anyone who wants a pole that can take a beating on rocky terrain. Its 135 cm maximum also suits taller hikers. Thru-hikers with sub-10-pound base weights, anyone who wants to fold poles into a pack, and shoppers who want a fully carbon, all-cork build should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Khumbu Lite is a well-made, comfortable aluminum pole that delivers Leki's best features at a mid-range price. It gives up weight and pack size to carbon and folding competitors, and the ultralight crowd will feel that. For everyone else, it's one of the easiest trekking pole recommendations around.
