## Overview

The Cerium Jacket, known until recently as the Cerium LT, is Arc'teryx's trim-fitting hybrid puffy: 850-fill goose down in the places that matter for warmth, Coreloft synthetic in the places that get damp. It's built for alpine climbers, shoulder-season backpackers and anyone who wants a packable layer that slides under a shell. The trade-off is familiar for the category: a lot of warmth per gram, wrapped in a thin 15D shell that asks for some care.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Warmth-to-weight is the Cerium's calling card, and reviewers keep returning to it. Mountain Massif's testers describe a surprising amount of insulation without bulk. Outdoor Gear Lab found the line warmer than most jackets of similar thickness, crediting high loft and quality goose down. Owners go further: one Arc'teryx customer called it the warmest jacket they'd owned, and a camp-bound sleeper on REI called it an "ultimate overnight jacket" that worked under a shell in single-digit temperatures. It is a standalone jacket for cold, dry days and a midlayer under a hardshell once the weather turns.

**Hybrid insulation.** The synthetic panels are there to hedge against moisture where breath, sweat and drizzle collect. That helps, but it doesn't turn the Cerium into a wet-weather jacket. Switchback Travel's testers note that it can't match a fully synthetic piece in damp conditions, and that it would fall short as a belay jacket when cold and wet. A shell is part of the system.

**Breathability and activity.** This is a static-warmth jacket. Mountain Massif reports it can run too warm during high-output work, and one owner who wore it on a sunrise hike over a T-shirt sweated quickly and found it hard to peel off afterward. Walking and easy hiking in cool air are fine. Uphill grinds are better handled in a lighter layer, with the Cerium coming out at the summit or the break.

**Fit and movement.** The cut is slim. Switchback Travel calls it the most athletic fit of any down jacket the testers have worn, which pays off under a pack or beneath a shell. Articulated sleeves keep cuffs from riding up when arms go overhead, and the hem stays put. The flip side: larger-chested users who want to layer a thick fleece underneath should size up, and a reviewer on a 39–41 inch chest found a medium comfortable with a fleece but no more than that.

**Durability.** The 15D Arato shell is what keeps the weight at 300 g, and it's also the weak point. Mountain Massif's reviewer wouldn't take it bushwhacking or scrambling, and the design prioritizes low weight over toughness. Long-term reports are mixed. One two-year user with periodic multi-month trips saw only a few feathers poke through and a jacket that still looked new. A climbing-gear reviewer reported a Cerium that survived festivals, a cigarette burn and ice-screw tears, and still lofted well. Critical REI owners report small tears and escaping feathers after about two years, along with isolated complaints of baffles with little down and a failed zipper. At roughly $400 in this class, those reports sting. Treat it as a jacket for careful use, and carry a few patches.

**Details.** The jacket stuffs into its own inside pocket, a feature owners like. The main zip is a lighter design than the pull-open Vislon zipper found on many Arc'teryx pieces, a likely weight-saving choice, according to one reviewer. The shell carries a PFAS-free DWR, which will shed light moisture but isn't a substitute for a rain jacket.

**Weight in context.** At 300 g, the Cerium isn't among the lightest down jackets. Switchback Travel notes it sits toward the heavier end compared with true ultralight options. What the extra grams buy is a more durable-feeling build, a better cut and the synthetic hedge.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Strong warmth-to-weight from 850-fill goose down
- Synthetic insulation in moisture-prone zones adds a margin of safety
- Athletic cut with articulated sleeves; layers easily under a shell
- Packs into its own pocket
- PFAS-free DWR and recycled shell fabric
- Clean looks that work in town as well as on the trail

**Cons**

- Expensive, with most reviewers flagging the price
- 15D shell is vulnerable to snags and abrasion; some owners report tears and feather leakage after about two years
- Runs warm and breathes poorly during hard exertion
- Heavier than the true ultralight down jackets
- No hood on this model
- Slim cut leaves little room for bulky layering

## Who Should Buy This

The Cerium suits backpackers, alpinists and ski tourers who want one dependable insulating layer for cold mornings, camp and summit stops, and who'll mostly wear it under a shell or in dry air. It rewards careful users who keep it away from brush and rock. Thru-hikers counting every gram, or anyone who wants a rugged jacket for bushwhacking and scrambling, should look at a lighter, sparser down jacket or a sturdier synthetic piece. Those wanting hood coverage should step up to the Cerium Hoody.

## Verdict

The Cerium Jacket earns its reputation: a sleek, genuinely warm and packable hybrid puffy with a fit that works under a shell. Its thin shell and high price keep it from being a universal recommendation, but for dry-cold alpine and shoulder-season use, it's a very good jacket.
