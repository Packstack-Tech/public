## Overview

The Meindl Antelao GTX is a mid-weight, B-rated hiking boot built for hikers who spend multiple days on rough mountain trails carrying a real pack — think Alps, Dolomites, or multi-day Himalayan foothill treks rather than ultralight thru-hiking. It's part of Meindl's Comfort Fit line, which means it's cut noticeably wider through the forefoot than most hiking boots on the market, making it a go-to recommendation for hikers who've struggled to find boots that don't crush their toes on long descents.

## Performance

**Fit and comfort.** This is where the Antelao GTX earns its reputation. Meindl builds it on a wide last with a specifically roomy toe box — <cite index="3-5,3-6">the Meindl Antelao GTX walking boot is a lightweight boot suitable for people with wider feet, and being part of Meindl's Comfort-Fit range it offers excellent support and a natural feeling, relaxed fit.</cite> The footbed backs that up: <cite index="3-10,3-11,3-12">the footbed is extra comfortable, made from cork with a wider surface area at the front of the boot, allowing for more movement and support as the foot — particularly the toes — can lay straight, with a fleece covering that guarantees stability and comfort.</cite> One long-term owner who bought a pair for Himalayan trekking noted the boots <cite index="4-10">felt instantly good and comfortable</cite> right out of the box on a store test area, which lines up with what most Comfort Fit reviewers report — this isn't a boot that demands a brutal break-in period the way some stiffer leather boots do.

If you have narrow or average-width feet, though, take that "wide fit" reputation seriously. Meindl's own retail guidance stresses that <cite index="3-16,3-17,3-18">achieving the right fit is not just about length — the shape, width and overall volume of the boot must match your foot, and the widest part of your foot should align with the widest part of the boot.</cite> Skip a half size down from your usual measurement if you're used to narrower boots, or you'll end up with heel slip.

**Waterproofing and breathability.** The GORE-TEX Performance Comfort lining does what you'd expect — it's a well-proven membrane, and nobody in my research reported wet feet on this boot even after extended wear. The suede/mesh upper combo helps with breathability compared to a full-leather boot, though it does mean slightly less abrasion resistance than Meindl's tougher leather models.

**Support and protection.** As a B-category boot, the Antelao GTX sits in the middle of the stiffness spectrum — supportive enough for loaded multi-day hikes on uneven terrain, but not a crampon-compatible mountaineering boot. <cite index="9-17">The combination of technical features makes the Antelao GTX a sturdy mountain shoe, with which you can enjoy hiking during a multi-day trip on uneven terrain or in the mountains.</cite> One user comparing it against Meindl's stiffer Pro GTX model (a C-rated, crampon-compatible boot) specifically chose the Antelao for standard alpine and Himalayan trekking, concluding the Pro would have been overkill for that kind of use. That's a useful data point: if you're not doing glacier travel or need crampon compatibility, the Antelao's lighter B-rated build is the more sensible — and more comfortable — choice.

**Traction and cushioning.** The Meindl Multigrip 3 by Vibram outsole paired with dual EVA and PU shock absorbers gives a noticeably cushioned ride for a boot with this level of support. <cite index="3-13">The Antelao GTX boasts a Meindl Multigrip 3 (by Vibram) with EVA shock absorber and PU shock absorber meaning reduced impact upon each step and lots of cushioning.</cite> On rocky, technical trails this translates to noticeably less foot fatigue by the end of a long day compared to a minimalist trail-runner-style boot, though you're paying for that cushioning in weight — 660g per boot is solidly in mid-weight hiking boot territory, not ultralight.

**Durability.** A small front rubber rand protects against toe-stubbing and abrasion, though it's worth noting this is a smaller rand than you'd find on Meindl's beefier Alpine-focused models — <cite index="3-9">a small rubber rand at the front further protects against abrasion on rougher terrain.</cite> For rock scrambling or heavy scree, that's a modest amount of protection; for standard maintained trail, it's plenty.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely wide, accommodating toe box — a rare find for hikers who've been squeezed by standard-width boots
- Comfortable more or less out of the box, minimal break-in drama
- GORE-TEX lining keeps feet dry without obvious breathability complaints
- Cushioned ride thanks to dual EVA/PU shock absorption in the sole
- Solid mid-weight support for multi-day trips with a loaded pack

**Cons**

- 660g per boot is heavy relative to modern lightweight hiking boots — not a candidate for ultralight setups
- Narrow-footed hikers will likely find it too roomy; sizing down is often necessary
- Smaller toe rand than heavier-duty Meindl models means less protection on very rocky terrain
- B-rating means no crampon compatibility — not suitable for glacier travel or technical alpine routes
- Suede/mesh upper is less rugged long-term than full-leather alternatives

## Who Should Buy This

The Antelao GTX is best suited to hikers with wide or high-volume feet who do multi-day hikes on maintained mountain trails — Alpine hut-to-hut routes, UK hill walking, or moderate Himalayan trekking — and want a supportive, waterproof boot without stepping up to a stiffer, heavier mountaineering model. If you need crampon compatibility or you have narrow feet, look elsewhere in Meindl's lineup (the Pro GTX or a narrower-lasted model, respectively).

## Verdict

The Meindl Antelao GTX delivers on its core promise: a genuinely comfortable, wide-fitting hiking boot with reliable waterproofing and enough underfoot support for multi-day trips. It's not for ultralight hikers chasing grams, and narrow-footed buyers should size carefully or skip it — but for its target audience of wide-footed trekkers on B-category terrain, it's a dependable, well-cushioned option. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — solid, honest gear with a clearly defined niche rather than a do-everything boot.
