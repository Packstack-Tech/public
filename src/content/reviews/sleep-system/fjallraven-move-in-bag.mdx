## Overview

Most mummy bags assume a sleeper who lies still. Fjällräven's Move In Bag Long is built for the person who spends the night flipping from back to side to stomach, and wants the bag to stay put on the pad while they do it. It is a 195 cm down mummy for summer and shoulder-season trips, and its headline trade-off is plain: the cut and the baffling are clever, but the weight is closer to a conventional three-season bag than an ultralight one.

## Performance

**Design.** The idea behind the Move In Bag is that the sleeper rotates inside a stationary bag, so the back of the bag always faces the ground. Fjällräven builds that into the insulation. The front of the torso gets box baffles with 700 cuin down, which keep loft from migrating and minimize heat loss where the sleeper's chest and shoulders sit. The back and legs use cheaper, lighter sewn-through channels filled with 600 cuin down. Sixty percent of the down sits on top. The logic holds up: the underside of any bag is compressed by body weight, so the down there does little work.

**Warmth.** Fjällräven rates the bag for warm summer nights, cabin-to-cabin treks and longer trips, and the numbers back that up. The brand's figures put the women's comfort temperature at +9°C and the men's limit at +5°C. In practice that makes it a bag for July in the Alps or a Scandinavian summer, not a shoulder-season bag for frosty mornings. Pair it with a warm liner and a decent pad and it stretches a little, but cold sleepers should expect to wear layers once temperatures approach the single digits.

**Fit and comfort.** A few extra centimeters of width relative to a standard mummy, 80 cm across the shoulders and 53 cm at the feet, make turning over easier. The hood is described as very warm, and its opening can be cinched with a visible yellow cord at the side. A two-part, full-length zipper allows venting, and the bag can also be opened at the foot and used as a blanket. A down-filled draft flap runs along the zipper, with a band along it to keep fabric out of the teeth. One Backpacker reviewer who logged more than 30 nights in the Move-in, a broad-shouldered ranger working summer and fall stints at Channel Islands National Park, reported staying warm and comfortable. That reviewer also praised the way the zip turns the bag into a blanket for warm nights and camp lounging.

**Weight and pack size.** This is where the bag gives ground. At roughly 730 g for the Long, it is about 60 g heavier than the 670 g Regular, and well above the sub-600 g down bags that thru-hikers carry for similar temperature ratings. The roomy cut and the box baffles cost ounces, and the 20D shells are sturdier than the 10D fabrics common in ultralight bags. Fjällräven does call it ultra compressible, and the Regular packs to about 14 x 27 cm.

**Details.** The fill is traceable down, and the DWR is fluorocarbon-free. Both are welcome, though the fluorocarbon-free finish means damp tents warrant the usual care with a down bag.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Torso box baffles and 60/40 down split are built for sleepers who roll inside the bag
- Wider cut moves easily without the bag twisting on the pad
- Full-length two-part zipper, draft flap and foot opening make it a flexible bag in warm weather
- Long version fits users up to 195 cm
- Traceable down and fluorocarbon-free water repellent

**Cons**

- About 730 g is heavy for a bag with a +5°C limit rating
- Warm-weather rating limits it to summer and mild shoulder-season trips
- Hybrid fill and sewn-through back add complexity without saving much weight
- List price of €279.95 buys less warmth per gram than many quilts and mummy bags

## Who Should Buy This

The Move In Bag Long suits tall, restless sleepers who like a hooded mummy but find narrow bags twist around them, and who do most of their camping in warm conditions: hut-to-hut routes, summer trekking, car-camping with a hike in. The zip-open blanket mode adds value for those who camp in variable summer weather. Gram-counting thru-hikers, cold sleepers and anyone planning trips below the single digits should look at a lighter quilt or a warmer bag instead.

## Verdict

The Move In Bag Long solves a real problem for side-to-stomach sleepers with careful baffle design and a generous 195 cm length. It does so at a weight and price that cost it in the ultralight category. Recommended for tall, active sleepers on warm-season trips; not a first pick for thru-hikers.
