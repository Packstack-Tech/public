## Overview

The Sarek Gear EVA Pad is a bare-bones closed-cell foam pad made from 3mm Evazote, produced in Sweden. At 118g for a 48 x 190cm sheet, it's built for hikers who want a torso-length or full-length foam layer without the bulk of a traditional foam pad like a Z Lite Sol — think supplemental insulation under an air pad, a sit pad for breaks, or emergency backup, rather than a complete sleep system on its own.

## Performance

Let's start with the obvious: an R-value of 0.5 and 3mm of foam is not going to keep you warm on its own on anything but a hot summer night. This pad exists in a specific niche — European Evazote foam has a reputation among ultralight sewers and gear tinkerers as a lighter, more durable alternative to the polyethylene foam used in cheap blue pads or generic torso pads, and Sarek Gear appears to be filling a gap for buyers who can't easily source that material without importing it. One Backpacking Light forum thread flagged this exact product as one of the only European retail sources for 3mm Evazote, <cite index="1-3">noting it's a useful bit of kit though re-importing similar products from the US is uneconomic</cite> — which tells you this is very much a specialty item rather than something you'll find at your local outfitter.

In practice, that thinness means the pad's real strength isn't cushioning — it's abrasion resistance and puncture protection. Evazote is a denser, tougher foam than the eggshell-thin blue foam pads you see at big-box stores, so a lot of buyers use it as a ground-sheet buffer layer directly under an inflatable pad, extending the life of an air mattress on rocky or debris-strewn tent sites. At 3mm, it packs down small and disappears in weight terms, which is the whole point — you're not trying to replace your inflatable pad's warmth, you're adding a small margin of puncture insurance or a sit-pad-sized bit of comfort for lunch breaks.

I found limited independent, detailed testing of this specific pad — reviews from actual thru-hikers running long-term durability numbers are scarce, which is typical for a small cottage-industry product like this. What surfaced was mostly anecdotal and enthusiastic: one Swedish backpacking blogger who used Sarek Gear's 3mm EVA pad on a week-long Northern Scandinavia trip called it <cite index="2-1,2-2">the most comfortable sleeping pad they'd ever slept on, and recommended it for anyone looking for better sleep in the mountains</cite>. That's a strong claim for 3mm of foam, and it's worth noting the same trip report mentions the pad doubling as bedding for a dog in the tent — a good sign for durability under load, but not a substitute for a controlled comfort test. I'd treat that kind of praise as one data point rather than a verdict; comfort at 3mm thickness is going to depend heavily on what you're sleeping on top of (a soft duff campsite versus bare rock will feel very different) and body type.

Because there's so little third-party lab data — no ASTM-standardized R-value testing that I could find beyond the manufacturer's own 0.5 figure — I'd treat the R-value as a rough guide rather than gospel. If you're relying on this pad in isolation for anything below a mild summer night, you're likely undercutting your own warmth margin.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely minimal weight (118g) for a full 190cm-length foam pad
- Evazote foam is tougher and more tear-resistant than typical polyethylene camp-store foam
- Doubles well as a sit pad, ground-sheet buffer, or pack frame stiffener
- Sourced and made in Sweden — useful for EU buyers avoiding import costs/duties on US cottage brands
- Packs essentially flat; easy to trim to custom shapes or sizes

**Cons:**

- R-value of 0.5 means negligible standalone insulation — this is a supplement, not a sleep system
- Limited independent long-term durability or comfort testing available
- Small brand with limited distribution outside Scandinavia/Europe
- At 3mm, doesn't offer much cushioning on uneven ground for side sleepers
- Not a fair substitute for a full-length foam pad if that's what you actually need

## Who Should Buy This

This pad makes sense for a narrow but real use case: ultralight hikers who already run an inflatable pad and want a cheap insurance layer against punctures, or hikers who want a packable sit pad for breaks and camp chores without carrying a dedicated foam pad. European buyers who've been importing Evazote sheets from the US or UK cottage brands will appreciate having a local source. It's a poor choice if you're looking for a standalone three-season sleeping pad — the R-value just isn't there.

## Verdict

The Sarek Gear EVA Pad is a well-targeted niche product: light, tough Evazote foam in a useful size, made accessible to European buyers who'd otherwise be paying import fees for the same material. It's not trying to be your only sleeping pad, and if you buy it expecting that, you'll be disappointed. As a supplemental puncture-guard or sit pad, it does its job for a very reasonable weight penalty. I'd put it at a 6.5/10 — solid for its actual purpose, but the category itself is a "nice to have" rather than "need to have" for most kits.
