## Overview

The Abisko Summer Hat is Fjällräven's wide-brimmed sun hat, built from the same G-1000 Lite fabric the brand uses in its trekking pants and jackets. It's aimed at hikers, paddlers, and anyone who wants serious, no-compromise sun coverage rather than a floppy packable boonie — the trade-off being a stiffer, slightly heavier build than most ultralight sun hats.

## Performance

The standout feature here is the brim. At roughly 7 cm wide and noticeably stiffer than the mesh or nylon brims on most sun hats, it doesn't fold or flop in the wind, and it holds its shape even after being crushed in a pack. <cite index="8-4,8-5,8-6,8-7">Multiple users report the hat returns to its original shape after being flattened repeatedly for transport, and holds up well to getting whacked by branches without fading despite lots of direct sun exposure.</cite> That durability is a recurring theme — <cite index="1-10">it's expensive, but it's also incredibly durable and should last for years, making it good value as long as you don't lose it.</cite>

Sun protection is exactly what you'd expect from a UPF 40, full-coverage design. <cite index="5-3,5-4">Fjällräven developed and tested the hat during Lapland's Arctic summer specifically to shield hikers from intense sun, and the wide brim folds onto the sides for less flapping while an adjustable chinstrap keeps it secured</cite>. On exposed ridgelines or open water, that's a real advantage over a ball cap or a narrower-brim hat that leaves your ears and neck exposed.

Breathability is the hat's most consistent criticism. The G-1000 Lite fabric is a tight-weave polycotton, and it doesn't move air the way a mesh-paneled sun hat does. <cite index="8-9,8-10,8-11,8-12,8-13">One long-term owner said the hat needs ventilation grommets, otherwise it gets rather hot, and rarely wore it for years until adding four vent grommets themselves — after which they were shocked Fjällräven hadn't updated the design with ventilation.</cite> Another reviewer echoed this, saying <cite index="8-17,8-18,8-19,8-20,8-21">it looks good, but the material on top isn't the best choice, is rather thick, isn't very breathable, and got them overheating on a hot hiking day</cite>. That said, experiences vary — some owners report the opposite. <cite index="5-1">One noted it's comfortable, breathes easy, and did quite well in a bit of snow despite the G-1000 fabric containing cotton</cite>, and another simply called it <cite index="8-15">"perfect hat for full sun coverage, great materials, proper fit, and doesn't get too hot"</cite>. My take: if you run hot, or have a lot of hair trapping heat underneath, expect this to feel warmer than a mesh trucker-style sun hat, especially standing still. It's noticeably better once you're moving and getting some airflow.

Weather resistance is a genuine perk of the G-1000 fabric — you can rub in Fjällräven's Greenland Wax to boost water repellency before a wet trip, and the weave blocks wind well, which matters more for a hat than most people realize (nothing ruins a hike faster than chasing your hat downwind). <cite index="9-1,9-2">One long-term reviewer noted the hat continues to wear well even when wet — it's not waterproof at all, but does keep the rain</cite> off reasonably well for a short shower.

Sizing is where I'd flag the most caution. Feedback is all over the map: <cite index="4-17,4-18,4-19">one buyer with a 22-inch head found the small too tight and the medium tight too, ending up in a large that also fit their 23-inch-headed partner comfortably</cite>, while another <cite index="10-15,10-16">used the size guide, ordered a large, and found it wasn't even close to fitting properly — way too small</cite>. Measure your head circumference carefully and check Fjällräven's specific size chart rather than guessing based on typical hat sizing — and if you're between sizes, size up.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide, genuinely stiff brim that holds its shape and doesn't flop in wind
- UPF 40 protection covers face, neck, and ears far better than a cap
- G-1000 Lite fabric is durable, fade-resistant, and re-waterproofable with Greenland Wax
- Adjustable chin strap keeps it secure in gusty conditions
- PFAS-free water resistance treatment

**Cons**

- Breathability is inconsistent — several long-term owners find it runs hot with no vents
- Sizing runs inconsistent between S–XL; easy to end up with the wrong fit
- Heavier and stiffer than packable mesh sun hats, so it doesn't stuff down as small
- Premium price for a sun hat

## Who Should Buy This

This hat makes the most sense for hikers and paddlers who prioritize durability and maximum sun coverage over ultralight packability — think desert trekking, high-altitude ridgelines, or long days on open water where a flimsy boonie hat won't survive the trip. If you overheat easily or hike in humid, still conditions, look at a mesh-paneled or vented alternative instead.

## Verdict

The Abisko Summer Hat delivers on its core promise: durable, wind-resistant, wide-brim sun protection that will outlast most sun hats on the market. The breathability complaints are real and worth weighing against your climate and how much you sweat, but for exposed, sunny, or windy trips, it's a solid buy. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — excellent coverage and build quality, docked for ventilation and inconsistent sizing.
