## Overview

The CoEfficient Fleece Hoody is Black Diamond's answer to a question alpine climbers and ski tourers ask every morning: what do I wear when I'm working hard and it's still cold? It pairs a gridded Polartec Power Dry fleece with a slim, long-tailed cut and a hood sized to slip under a helmet. The trade-off is typical of the category. It breathes and moves well, but it is a layer for effort, not for standing around, and it wears faster than its $199 price suggests.

## Performance

**Breathability and warmth.** The grid pattern is the point. Each raised square traps a little air while the thin channels between them let moisture and heat escape, which is why this style of fleece dominates on skin tracks and approach hikes. Reviewers who have worn it for winter climbing, ski touring and shoulder-season walking describe a layer that holds a steady warmth without the clammy build-up of a flat fleece. Better Trail's tester rated it a little warmer than The North Face Futurefleece and Outdoor Research's Vigor Grid, though still behind Patagonia's thicker R1 Air. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers put it plainly: on its own it isn't the warmest layer on the market. It works best as the piece that stays on while moving, and it wants a shell or puffy once the pace drops.

**Fit and mobility.** The cut is the hoody's signature. FeedTheHabit's reviewer praised a long, trim body that stays put under a harness and doesn't ride up on a bike, and Outdoor Gear Lab named it a favorite for unrestricted mobility, crediting both the pattern and the stretchy fabric. The slim fit is genuine, but owners who normally avoid close-cut clothing report it doesn't feel constrictive. Under a shell it adds almost no bulk, and the thumbholes pin the sleeves in place while a jacket goes on over the top.

**Hood and details.** The hood is low-profile, sits comfortably under a helmet, and reviewers note it is unobtrusive when down around the neck. The zipper runs smoothly without snagging. The single zippered chest pocket is enough for a phone or lip balm, though one owner-reviewer flagged the lack of hand pockets as the one missing feature. Fleece of this type carries a DWR finish, but it is there to shed flurries, not to stand in for a shell.

**Durability.** This is the weak spot. Outdoor Gear Lab's verdict says outright that the CoEfficient isn't the most durable option in its class. Better Trail's tester logged visible wear after roughly 15 days of hiking and running from January through May, concentrated where the fabric rubbed against hook-and-loop closures. The construction itself (stitching, zipper) earned praise; it's the face fabric that fuzzes and abrades. Packing it against a pack's Velcro-fastened pockets or a harness's gear loops will speed that along.

**Packability.** The fleece compresses flat enough to serve as a spare layer in a pack, according to owners, but it takes more room than a thin grid fleece or a synthetic active-insulation piece. Better Trail's verdict was that it suits colder pursuits where it is the primary active layer, not a get-it-out-for-camp item.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Gridded Polartec fabric breathes well during sustained effort
- Long, slim cut stays tucked under a harness or hip belt
- Stretchy, with strong freedom of movement
- Helmet-compatible hood and thumbholes that work under other layers
- Warmer than most thin active fleeces

**Cons**

- Face fabric abrades and shows wear sooner than expected, especially against Velcro
- Pricey at $199 for a fleece that needs replacing early
- Not warm enough as a standalone layer when stopped
- Only one chest pocket; no hand pockets
- DWR offers little protection in sustained rain

## Who Should Buy This

Ski tourers, alpine climbers and winter hikers who run hot and need a close-fitting layer that works under a shell and a harness. It also suits shoulder-season hikers who want one fleece to wear from trailhead to summit without constantly shedding and adding layers. Those who prioritize durability, who spend long hours in camp, or who want pockets for hands should look at a thicker fleece such as the R1 Air or a synthetic insulated hoody instead.

## Verdict

The CoEfficient is a well-cut, breathable mid layer that does exactly what its climbing pedigree promises when the pace is high. Its limited durability and modest standalone warmth keep it from being a bargain at full price, but for active cold-weather use it remains a strong choice, ideally bought on sale.
