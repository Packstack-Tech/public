## Overview

The WXP 3L is Source's military-issue hydration reservoir, a 100 oz bladder with a wide slide closure, a quick-disconnect hose and a no-bite push-pull valve. It was built to survive soldiers, firefighters and law-enforcement officers, and that shows in the thick film and the hardware. The trade-off for backpackers is weight: at 300 g it is a tough, low-maintenance bladder, not an ultralight one.

## Performance

**Taste and cleanliness.** This is the WXP's strongest argument. Source's polyethylene film is a different material from the TPU used in many competing reservoirs, and users consistently report that it doesn't pick up plastic flavor or hold odors. Reviewers describe water that tastes clean after sitting in the bladder for days, and the manufacturer says the liner's smooth surface resists biofilm. The antimicrobial treatment is built into the bladder and tube rather than applied as a coating. A hunter who had swapped from a Camelbak 3 L on Trailspace judged the plastic tougher and the bladder the better of the two. Rinsing and hanging the bladder open to dry is the manufacturer's care routine for water-only use. Anyone putting electrolyte mix or sugary drinks in it will still want to clean it properly.

**Filling and cleaning.** The Widepac slide closure is the feature that converted many Source owners. It opens the whole top of the bladder, so a hand fits inside to scrub, ice goes in without fuss, and the bladder dries flat. The WXP also keeps a conventional screw cap, which is useful when filling from a spigot or a gravity filter. Owners report no leaks from the closure, even after bags were thrown down or the bladder was carried on long days.

**Valve and hose.** The Storm valve is a push-pull design with no biting required, and Source claims about 25% more flow than bite valves. The valve rotates 360 degrees, so the hose can be shorter, and a hose cover insulates the water and blocks UV. The QMT fitting lets the tube come off with a button press, even with a full bladder, because the bladder side seals itself. That makes it easier to slide a full reservoir into a pack, and the fitting accepts compatible filters. The failure points owners mention are small: the valve can be stiff to open, one owner replaced a bite valve that had started leaking, and the cord on the valve cap can fray.

**Durability.** Owners describe the film as thick and abuse-tolerant, with no pinholes or seam failures after tossed packs, rough handling and long use. Source's tactical lineage is more than marketing here.

**Fit and weight.** Dimensions are generous, and Source owners note that the wide bladder can be a tight fit in slim daypack sleeves. Sloshing and a bulging shape are the usual price of a high-volume bladder, and a full 3 L adds roughly 3 kg of water high on the back. At 300 g the empty bladder, tube and valve weigh more than the simple bottles many thru-hikers carry on the shoulder straps. For a gram-counter, that is the case against it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Water stays free of plastic taste, even after several days
- Wide slide opening makes cleaning, drying and adding ice easy
- Push-pull valve needs no biting and shuts off when closed
- QMT quick-connect allows tube removal without draining the bladder
- Insulated, UV-blocking hose cover
- Tough film that holds up to hard use
- Priced in the mid-$30s at several retailers, cheaper than many premium reservoirs

**Cons**

- 300 g is heavy for an ultralight kit
- Wide body doesn't fit every slim pack sleeve
- The valve can be stiff, and the valve cap cord can wear
- Two openings and a quick-connect add parts that can fail or need maintenance

## Who Should Buy This

The WXP suits section hikers, hunters, guides, bikepackers and anyone who carries a hydration bladder daily, sometimes in a pack that sees hard use. It's also a good choice for people who got burned by the taste or mildew of cheaper reservoirs. It fits best in a pack designed for a 3 L reservoir and works well in dry regions where water carries run long.

Thru-hikers whose base weights are measured in grams should look at bottle-based setups or lighter bladders with a smaller capacity. The same goes for anyone using a minimalist frameless pack with a narrow sleeve.

## Verdict

The WXP 3L earns its reputation for taste, cleanability and ruggedness, and its Widepac opening is still among the easiest to live with. It is a durable workhorse at a fair price, though not a weight-saving one. Buy it if reliability matters more than ounces.
