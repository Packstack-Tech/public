## Overview

The Matador NanoDry is a full-length nanofiber towel that ships in a silicone case with a carabiner. It is aimed at backpackers who want to dry off after a river dip or a hostel shower without carrying a damp brick. The headline trade-off: the towel itself is barely there, but the case that comes with it weighs more than the fabric.

## Performance

**Weight and pack size.** A tester at Camping Guidance weighed the pieces separately and found the towel at about 2.4 oz and the silicone case at 2.6 oz. That puts the whole kit at roughly 5 oz, matching the 142 g spec. Packed, it is a squat cylinder about the size of a large coffee mug. The case earns some of its keep: Garage Grown Gear's reviewer admitted at first that it was dead weight for ultralight use, then came around because it kept a damp towel away from dry layers inside a pannier. Hikers who care about every ounce can leave the case at home and stuff the towel into a pocket or shoe.

**Absorbency.** Nanofiber is not terry cloth, and it doesn't behave like it. Matador says the fabric holds 2.3 times its weight in water, and Camping Guidance's bench test came in close at 2.2x, or about 5.1 fl oz. That is enough to dry an adult after a swim or shower, as long as the towel gets wrung out partway through. After wringing, the same tester found 43% of the water still in the towel, slightly better than the 46% left in a cotton comparison towel. Reviewers who treat it like a cotton towel and rub their skin with it tend to be disappointed. Pressing and blotting works better, the same as with any microfiber-style travel towel.

**Drying speed.** This is where the NanoDry is strongest. In the same test, a cotton towel took 2 hours 15 minutes to dry outdoors and 27 hours indoors. The NanoDry was the fastest-drying towel the tester owned. On the trail, that means a towel clipped to a pack by the carabiner will usually be dry by the time the next stop arrives. Garage Grown Gear's reviewer noted the large size dries quickly in sun or warm, dry air.

**Dirt, sand and smell.** The fabric's low-pile weave sheds grit well. Camping Guidance reported that it picked up no dirt after being dragged across grass and only a little sand at the beach, though the reviewer still wouldn't lay it out as a beach towel. After a seawater soak the towel stayed odor-free, while the cotton comparison smelled like spoiled food. The antimicrobial coating is the likely reason it stays fresh.

**Coverage and hang features.** At 120 cm or 132 cm long and 60 cm wide, the towel wraps around a torso for changing in public or for warming up after a chilly dip. The snap loop sits in the middle of the towel rather than at one end, so it hangs shorter from a branch or tent guyline.

**Durability.** Matador backs the towel with a one-year warranty, and the Camping Guidance reviewer reported the Trek (small) version held up through five washes. Long-term thru-hike durability data is thin, so owners should expect to baby the towel somewhat around rough rock and Velcro.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ by 18 g and by length. The 142 g version measures 120 cm x 60 cm, and Matador lists the 160 g version at 132 cm x 60 cm. Both share the same width, fabric and silicone case.

- **142 g (120 x 60 cm):** The better pick for gram counters. It covers the torso and dries a body without trouble, and it dries slightly faster because there is less fabric.
- **160 g (132 x 60 cm):** The better pick for taller hikers or anyone who wants to wrap the towel fully around the waist. The extra 12 cm costs 18 g, which is a fair price for the added coverage.

Hikers who want something much smaller should look at Matador's separate Trek Towel, a 39 cm square that holds only 1.4 fl oz and needs frequent wringing when used on a whole body.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-length coverage at about 5 oz including the case
- Dries far faster than cotton and stays odor-free
- Sheds dirt and most sand
- Case, carabiner and center snap loop make it easy to carry and hang
- Machine washable, with a one-year warranty

**Cons**

- The silicone case weighs more than the towel
- Absorbency is modest (about 2.2–2.3x its weight), so it needs wringing mid-use
- Slick, thin feel takes adjustment for people used to a cotton towel
- About $39 is steep for a towel
- Not a beach towel; the fabric holds some sand

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers and thru-hikers who shower at hostels, swim in rivers, or want a real towel for hot springs and campground showers will get the most from it. It also suits travelers who need one towel that can clip onto the outside of a pack and dry on the move. Anyone who only needs to wipe their face and hands can skip it and carry the smaller Trek Towel or a bandana. People who like a plush towel feel, or who want a wearable beach blanket, will find the NanoDry too thin.

## Verdict

The NanoDry is one of the lightest full-size towels a hiker can buy, and its drying speed and dirt resistance hold up well against cotton and standard microfiber. Skip the silicone case if weight matters, pick the 142 g version for the lowest base weight or the 160 g version for extra length, and expect to wring it out once mid-shower. An easy recommendation for ultralight travel and trail use.
