## Overview

Decathlon's Nabaiji microfibre towel began life on the pool deck, but it has become a quiet favorite with campers, hostel travelers and budget-minded hikers. The Large size, 80 x 130 cm, is big enough to wrap around a torso and light enough at 186 g to justify a spot in a trip kit. The trade-off is texture: it dries fast and costs little, but it does not feel like a bath towel and carries none of the hang loops or stuff sacks of dedicated backpacking brands.

## Performance

**Drying speed.** This is the towel's main job, and owners praise it for it. Buyers across Decathlon's regional storefronts describe it as quick to dry and quick to be ready for reuse. That matches the polyester-heavy construction. Cationic polyester takes dye well and, like most synthetic fibers, holds little water in the yarn itself. On a damp trail morning, a towel that is dry by the next break matters more than one that feels plush.

**Absorbency.** Reviews are more mixed here, and the pattern is consistent. One buyer called the fabric good with "medium absorbency." Others say it soaks up water well. The usual microfibre technique applies: pat rather than rub, as Decathlon itself advises. A towel this thin will not wick a soaked body in one pass the way terry cloth does. Two passes, one wring-out, and the job is done.

**Feel.** The fabric is the common complaint. A European buyer wished the otherwise attractive towel were made of a softer material, and that fits the construction. This is a flat, slightly slick weave, not the suede-like finish of pricier travel towels. For rinsing off after a swim or drying hands and face at camp, it is fine. For anyone sensitive to the clammy-microfibre sensation, it is a minor annoyance that the low price partly forgives.

**Packability.** The sewn-in elastic band is the one design touch worth noting. Roll the towel, snap the band around it, and it stays together without a separate sack. Buyers who camp with it call out this feature specifically, saying it lets the towel shrink to a size that fits any pack or bag. It will not compress to the tennis-ball size of a small hiking towel, but at 130 cm long it stays easy to pack.

**Durability.** Decathlon rates the fabric as wear-resistant and tolerant of repeated washing, and owner behavior backs this up: the 30,000-plus reviews on the US listing average 4.7 stars, and UK listings show 4.8 across roughly 27,000 votes. Many reviewers say they have bought it again. Care is simple: cold wash, tumble dry low. For the Nabaiji line, Decathlon warns against fabric softener, which can degrade microfibre.

**What's missing.** There is no hang loop, no snap, no mesh bag, no antimicrobial treatment. Hikers who dry a towel on a pack strap will need to improvise with a safety pin or carabiner through a corner.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 186 g for a full 80 x 130 cm towel
- Dries quickly and is ready for reuse soon after
- Elastic band keeps the roll together
- Machine washable and resistant to repeated washing
- Priced around £7.99 in the UK, so a loss or wear-out costs little
- Exceptionally strong owner ratings across tens of thousands of reviews

**Cons**

- Fabric feels coarse and less soft than premium travel towels
- Medium absorbency; needs patting and a wring-out
- No hang loop or stuff sack
- Larger than most thru-hikers need for a trail wipe-down
- Fabric softener is off the table

## Who Should Buy This

The Large Nabaiji suits section hikers, bikepackers, hostel travelers and campers who want a real towel for showering, swimming or a lake dip, and who would rather spend less than ten pounds than forty. It also works as a shared-trip towel or a backup for a family bag. Gram-counting thru-hikers who need only a face-and-hands cloth should look at the smaller sizes in the same Nabaiji line or a dedicated packtowel. Anyone who wants a soft, plush finish or an integrated hang loop should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Nabaiji Large is a plain, honest piece of gear: 186 g, quick to dry, cheap enough to ignore, and well-liked by a very large number of owners. It will not impress anyone's skin, but it handles wet duty reliably. For general camping and travel, it is an easy buy.
