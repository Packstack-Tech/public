## Overview

The Permanent Home Repair Kit is Therm-a-Rest's stronger, slower answer to a leaking sleeping pad: a tube of wet adhesive, fabric patches and the prep supplies to apply them. It is aimed at anyone who owns a NeoAir or other Therm-a-Rest pad and wants a repair that will last the life of the pad. The trade-off is time. The glue needs 8 to 12 hours to cure, so this is a kitchen-table fix, not a trailside one.

## Performance

The method is the oldest in the book, and it works for the same reason it always has. Cleaning the area with an alcohol pad, spreading a thin layer of adhesive and pressing a fabric patch over it produces a bond that becomes part of the pad's shell rather than a sticker sitting on top of it. Therm-a-Rest positions this as its most durable repair option and the one to choose for larger tears, a step above the peel-and-stick patches that ship with new pads.

Those larger repairs are where the kit earns its place. A pinhole from a stray thorn can be handled by almost anything. A slice from a rock edge or a seam-adjacent tear is a different job, and fabric patches set in wet adhesive can be cut and shaped to cover a wider area than a stick-on circle. The patch assortment includes clear and colored pieces, so a repair to a pad with a light, thin shell does not have to be a flag-colored one.

The cure window is the main constraint. Plan on leaving the pad flat and untouched overnight, and inflating it only after the full 8 to 12 hours. Older retailer listings and some reviews cite a 4-hour cure for this kit, but the current Therm-a-Rest figure is 8 to 12, and the longer window is the one to follow. A pad still tacky at breakfast is a pad that may leak again by dinner.

Finding the hole matters as much as sealing it. The usual routine of inflating the pad, submerging sections in a tub or brushing soapy water over it, then marking the leak with a pen before drying the area carries over directly. The alcohol prep pads handle the surface cleaning, but the surface needs to be completely dry before any adhesive goes on. Because the adhesive is solvent-based (Therm-a-Rest carries a Prop 65 toluene warning on the product), it also belongs in a ventilated room.

On a thru-hike the kit is a poor fit for the same reason it is good at home. A wet repair in a vestibule, in cold or damp conditions, with a pad that has to be usable by bedtime, is the situation it was not built for. A UK retailer's assessment of the kit reaches the same conclusion, calling it a home-repair product and pointing hikers toward Therm-a-Rest's Instant Field Repair Kit for the trail. That kit uses adhesive dots rather than wet glue, so the pad can be used immediately, and it weighs roughly 20 g. Even for field repairs, the usual advice is to treat them as temporary and finish the job properly once back home. That second step is this kit's role.

At 60 g, it is not something most ultralight hikers would carry. Packed at 10 cm x 15 cm x 2 cm, it is flat and compact, which suits a gear-repair drawer more than a lid pocket.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Strongest repair option in the Therm-a-Rest lineup, with a bond suited to larger tears
- Works on every Therm-a-Rest inflatable pad, NeoAir models included
- Patches in clear and colored fabric can be cut to fit the damage
- Includes alcohol prep pads and an applicator, so nothing else is needed
- Inexpensive compared with replacing a $150–200 pad

**Cons**

- 8–12 hour cure rules out same-day use of the pad
- Wet, solvent-based adhesive is awkward in cold, damp or cramped trail conditions
- 60 g is heavy for a repair item when a 20 g dot-based kit exists
- Tube of adhesive has a finite shelf life once opened, which is a consideration for a kit used once every few seasons

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who owns a Therm-a-Rest pad and plans to keep it should have one of these at home. It suits owners of expensive NeoAir XLite or XTherm pads who would rather fix a leak than replace the pad, and it suits gear tinkerers who want a lasting repair after a trip that ended with a field patch. Hikers who need a fix they can use on trail should buy the Instant Field Repair Kit instead, and carry this one only on very long trips with a guaranteed multi-day stop, if at all.

## Verdict

The Permanent Home Repair Kit is a cheap, effective way to extend the life of a pad that costs many times more. It is the right tool for the job it was designed for and the wrong one for anything else, so buy it for the shelf and pair it with a lighter field kit for the pack.
