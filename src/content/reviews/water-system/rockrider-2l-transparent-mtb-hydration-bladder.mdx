## Overview

Decathlon's in-house mountain bike brand sells this 2-litre reservoir for the price of a sandwich and a coffee, and it weighs 105 g empty. It is built for riders, but the formula is the same one day hikers and weight-conscious section hikers want: a wide slider opening, a lockable bite valve and a hose you can trim to fit. The trade-off is polish. This is a budget bladder with a few sharp ideas, not a premium system with every convenience.

## Performance

**Filling and cleaning.** The sliding closure is the main selling point. The opening is wide enough to get a hand inside for scrubbing and to dunk under a stream or a spigot without fuss. Decathlon's one caution applies in practice: the slider has to click fully into place, or the bladder will leak. A road.cc reviewer testing the same bladder bundled with a Rockrider pack made a similar point, saying the top works well enough if the user takes a moment to seal it properly. Owners report the usual new-bladder plastic taste, which a thorough wash before first use clears.

**Drinking.** The silicone bite valve sits at 45°, which puts it in the mouth without craning the neck. Rotating the valve 180° locks it, and Decathlon says it will not drip even when open. Decathlon's own advice for good flow is to flip the filled bladder upside down and suck the air out of the tube before setting off. An owner in India, who used an earlier version for close to three years before a crash destroyed it, noted that the valve has no dust cover, which is a nuisance on dry, gritty trails.

**Hose.** At 100 cm, the tube is long enough for most pack and vest layouts, and it can be shortened: pull the bite valve, soften the tube in hot water, cut it, and refit the valve. BikeRadar's tester, reviewing the Rockrider Explore pack that ships with the same 2L reservoir, found the hose thin and overly flexible, and another BikeRadar tester called it easy to kink on a different Rockrider pack. Those comments came from packs, but the hose is the weak point to watch.

**Durability.** Decathlon says it lab-tested the bladder, found no defects in burst and high-pressure tests, and recorded a puncture-resistance increase of more than 74% over the previous design. That is the manufacturer's claim, not independent testing. The practical care rules are the standard ones: do not fold it, do not fill it with hot liquid, and do not carry it upside down or by the tube. Decathlon lists a 2-year warranty on the Indian listing.

**Capacity.** Decathlon rates two litres for roughly three hours of mountain biking. For hiking, that translates to a half-day carry in moderate temperatures or a desert-section top-up between water sources, not a stand-alone solution for a dry 25-mile stretch. Reviewers describe it as a notch below CamelBak or HydraPak in quality, while noting that the price makes that easy to accept.

**Compatibility.** Decathlon lists the bladder as fitting its Explore 7L and ST900 hydration backpacks. Most packs with a standard hydration sleeve and a hose port should take a 35.5 x 17 cm reservoir, though the hose connection is not a quick-disconnect type.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 105 g for 2 L, light enough that it does not compete with a Sawyer-and-bottles setup for weight
- Wide slider opening makes filling and scrubbing easy
- Lockable 45° bite valve that does not drip
- Trimmable 100 cm hose
- BPA-free and made in France
- Priced around £13 / ₹1,199, so replacing it is no hardship

**Cons**

- Hose is thin and prone to kinking, according to testers of Rockrider packs
- No dust cover on the bite valve, per an owner
- Build quality does not match CamelBak or HydraPak, according to reviewers
- Slider must be seated fully or it leaks
- Durability claims come from Decathlon's lab, not independent testers
- 2 L is the only size

## Who Should Buy This

Day hikers, section hikers and mountain bikers who want a cheap, light reservoir for a pack they already own will get what they need here. It also suits anyone building a spare or loaner kit, or who wants to try a bladder before spending three times as much. Thru-hikers on long, dry stretches should look at larger bladders. Anyone who wants a quick-disconnect hose, an insulated sleeve or a covered bite valve should buy from a dedicated hydration brand.

## Verdict

This bladder does the basics well: it fills easily, cleans easily and drinks without drips, at a weight and price that are hard to argue with. The thin hose and the unproven long-term durability keep it from a higher score. For short outings and budget builds, it is an easy buy.
