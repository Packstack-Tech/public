## Overview

The Adotec Ultralight Food Locker is a soft-sided, IGBC-certified grizzly-resistant food bag made in Canada from 6 oz/sqyd woven Dyneema Composite Fabric. It's aimed at thru-hikers who want grizzly-country compliance without a bear canister's bulk, or the water-soaked weight of a fabric Ursack. The trade-off is a stiff, break-in-required fabric and a price that sits at the Ursack's level, not below it.

## Performance

**Bear resistance.** The 14 L bag passed the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee's live-bear protocol with captive grizzlies. The Trek's reviewer described it as built to withstand more than an hour of a grizzly's attention. Adotec's own account is that bears tend to lose interest within 10 to 15 minutes. It attributes this to the hydrophobic fabric, which holds in food smells that would otherwise leak out and keep a bear motivated. IGBC certification means resistant, not proof, and the Trek's reviewer made that distinction explicit. A determined bear can still crush the contents.

**Weight and pack fit.** At 187 g, it undercuts comparable fabric bags. A Backpacking Light forum member ran the numbers against the Ursack Almighty (10.5 L, 9.6 oz) and the Ursack Almighty Grizzly (20 L, 14 oz). The Adotec gives more volume than the former at less weight, and it's far lighter than the latter. Owners also praise the shape. The push-in corners give the bag a flat base, so it packs more like a stuff sack than the long tube of an Ursack. It also weighs less than the usual hanging kit of nylon bag, throw rope and rock sack. A hiker who tested it for the Arizona Trail made that comparison.

**Water.** Dyneema doesn't absorb water, and owners note the bag isn't heavier after a rainy night, doesn't dampen neighboring gear and wipes clean easily. That's the clearest advantage over an Ursack, which one forum user said soaks up roughly 6 oz of water in rain. The nuance is the closure. With the rope cinch, water can still find its way in through the top opening, so the grizzly version is water-shedding rather than submersible. The waterproof claim is strongest with a roll-top closure.

**Capacity.** Adotec rates the 14 L for four to five days of food for one person. Testers have stretched that. The Trek put it at five to six days, and another reviewer fit six days of his own food, with a warning that bulkier or larger appetites will get less. Hikers who have been running calorie deficits to squeeze a week into a 10.5 L Ursack describe 14 L as a more realistic size. Dense, repackaged food is what makes a week workable.

**Break-in.** Reviewers consistently flag a stiff fabric out of the box. One owner needed several attempts before the top would close tight enough, and the manufacturer's retail listing warns that the cinch must be worked in the first time. The fix is unglamorous but effective: scrunch the bag, then seal and unseal it repeatedly until the DCF softens. Expect a few evenings of wrestling before it behaves.

**Rodents.** Owners rate it more rodent-resistant than an Ursack and far more so than a nylon hang bag, but not at a canister's level. Mice and marmots are a lesser concern here than bears, but a hiker in rodent-heavy terrain should know the limits. Some retailer listings go further and state the bag is not rodent-proof.

**Rules.** Certification doesn't override land-manager regulations. Some parks and forests require hard-sided canisters regardless of IGBC listings, so check local rules before relying on a soft bag. Only the 14 L size carries the grizzly certification.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 187 g for IGBC grizzly certification, among the lightest options in its class
- 14 L is enough for roughly five to six days for most hikers
- DCF doesn't absorb water, so no extra weight in rain
- Push-in corners give a flat base that packs better than a long tube
- Holds up to extended mauling in testing, and leaves less odor to attract bears
- Replaces a hanging kit's bag, cord and rock sack

**Cons**

- Stiff fabric needs a deliberate break-in before the cinch closes cleanly
- Cinch closure isn't fully waterproof
- Costs roughly what a same-size Ursack does, in the $130+ range
- Rodent resistance is good, not canister-grade
- Certified in the 14 L size only
- Not accepted where hard-sided canisters are mandatory

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and section hikers in grizzly country who need a certified container but don't want a canister's volume, and hikers heading into wet terrain such as the Pacific Northwest or the Canadian Rockies, where a fabric bag that doesn't soak up water earns its keep. It also suits anyone with an Ursack and a dislike of its weight when wet. Those hiking in places that mandate hard canisters, anyone who needs a guaranteed rodent barrier, and shoppers who'd rather not pay a premium for 14 L of fabric should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Adotec is the lightest IGBC-certified soft food bag at its capacity. It also solves the wet-Ursack problem. Pay for it if grizzly rules apply to the route, and plan on a few evenings of fabric softening before the first big trip. A hiker who never sees grizzly country can buy a cheaper hang kit.
