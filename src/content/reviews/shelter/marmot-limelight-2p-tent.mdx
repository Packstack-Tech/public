## Overview

The Limelight 2P is a freestanding, double-wall, 3-season tent built around pre-bent poles that push the walls close to vertical. The result is more headroom and more usable floor than a conventional dome of the same footprint. It's aimed at weekend backpackers and campers who want room to live in, and it makes no pretense of being an ultralight shelter. At 2,251 g, the trade-off is weight.

## Performance

**Livability.** This is where the Limelight earns its reputation. Marmot's zone pre-bend construction keeps the poles straight at sleeping height before they arch overhead, and owners say it "raises the roof" in a way a standard dome doesn't. Side wings add interior pockets that hold gear without eating into sleeping space, and one REI reviewer reported fitting two people, their gear and a dog. Two doors with two vestibules mean neither sleeper climbs over the other, and wet boots stay out of the bedroom.

The caveat is that "2P" still means two people who like each other. A Trailspace reviewer called it a tight fit for two average-sized men. Another owner said that at one-person occupancy it works well as a comfortable solo tent with a pile of gear.

**Weather.** Owners describe the Limelight as stable when properly guyed. One Trailspace reviewer said it held firm in strong, gusty winds on the Croatian coast, while tents nearby flattened. Others report heavy rain and even hail without leaks. The fly's 1,200 mm rating and the 2,000 mm floor are conventional numbers for this class, and the included footprint adds protection on rocky ground. Reports on the factory stakes are less flattering: they bend easily, and owners recommend swapping in sturdier ones such as MSR Groundhogs.

**Ventilation and condensation.** Plenty of mesh keeps air moving. One long-term owner who replaced a decade-old Limelight with the current version says condensation is rarely a problem. The same airiness has a cost in cold, wet weather, when one reviewer felt wind pushing through the tent. Others noted condensation in damp conditions, so the Limelight is best used in warm to moderate temperatures. Some owners do push it into winter use, but that's the exception.

**Setup.** This is the feature owners mention most often after space. Two color-coded poles, a freestanding frame and a footprint with a matching shape make pitching simple, even solo in wind. Reviewers also note that the tent can be pitched with the fly over the footprint. Without the inner tent, that gives a lighter, more open shelter on dry nights.

**Weight and packability.** At 2,251 g, or just under 5 lb, the Limelight is average for a two-person backpacking tent and well above the ultralight standard. The footprint is included in that figure, so the real gap to cutting-edge shelters is somewhat smaller than it first looks. The pack size is 62 x 14 cm, which fits inside a pack but is long for a tent of this class. Reviewers who bought it for backpacking often report it's better suited to short carries or shared loads. One owner said he felt every pound on his first overnight with the larger 3P. Wilderness Times, which scored the tent 7.8/10, makes the same point, praising its livability over any ultralight credentials.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Near-vertical walls and wing pockets create real headroom and storage
- Two doors and two vestibules for two people and their gear
- Fast, intuitive setup with color-coded poles
- Footprint included
- Stable in wind when fully guyed, with no leaks reported in heavy rain
- Plenty of mesh for warm-weather airflow

**Cons**

- 2,251 g is heavy for anyone counting grams
- Packed length of 62 cm is long for a tent this size
- Stock stakes bend easily
- Mesh-heavy design can feel drafty in cold, wet weather
- Heavy owners report cramped space when two large people share it

## Who Should Buy This

The Limelight 2P suits couples, friends or hikers with a dog who want a comfortable freestanding tent for weekend trips, short backpacking days and summer car camping. It also suits solo hikers who want room to spread out and don't mind carrying the extra weight. Thru-hikers and anyone chasing a base weight under about 10 lb should look at trekking-pole shelters or lighter freestanding designs. Cold-weather campers should also look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Limelight 2P is a comfortable, easy tent with a good footprint, strong wind performance and thoughtful storage, but it is not a long-distance backpacking tent. Buy it for livability and quick setup. Skip it if weight is the first thing you look at.
