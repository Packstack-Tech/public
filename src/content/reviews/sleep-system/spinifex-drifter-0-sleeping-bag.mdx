## Overview

The Spinifex Drifter 0° is Anaconda's house-brand synthetic sleeping bag: a rectangular, hooded, hollow-fibre bag aimed at school camps, family car-camping and spare-room sleepovers. It typically sells for AU$49.99 on club pricing and AU$99.99 at full price. It is a bag for people who park next to their tent. At 1,905 g, it asks a lot of a backpacker's shoulders.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The Drifter's insulation is 400 GSM of hollow-fibre fill, a heavy, lofty, old-school synthetic. Anaconda describes the 0°C figure as a comfort rating. Owners using the bag on mild Australian camp nights report sleeping warm. A scout group's gear list recommends it for younger sections and says the zip draft system holds warmth better than competing bags. The same list suggests adding a liner and a camp blanket for extra warmth. That is sensible advice. A rectangular bag with a lot of internal volume has air to heat, and cheap hollow fibre doesn't trap heat as efficiently as modern continuous-filament or down insulation. Backpackers who sleep cold should treat 0°C as the bag's limit and plan on a 5–8°C night for real comfort. That is a judgement call from the bag's construction, not a lab figure.

**Weight and packed size.** This is where the Drifter parts ways with the Packstack audience. At 1,905 g, it weighs about as much as an entire ultralight shelter-and-sleep setup. Down bags at a similar temperature rating often come in at roughly half that weight or less. The packed size of 45 x 23 cm is respectable for a hollow-fibre bag, and the drawcord sack lets you cinch it down. It will still take up a large share of a 40-litre pack, and in a frameless ultralight pack it would dominate the load.

**Fit and features.** At 218 x 84 cm, the rectangle is roomy for most adults, and the shape lets you roll over without dragging the bag with you. The hood is a plain drawcord affair. The full-length zip opens the bag flat so it doubles as a quilt or a blanket for warm nights, a genuine selling point for car camping and shared tents. The draft collar is there to keep heat from leaking through the zip. An inner pocket holds a phone or headlamp.

**Durability and care.** The shell is a 210-denier polyester that is advertised as tear-resistant. One listing describes a water-resistant, PFAS-free ripstop finish. The polyester-cotton lining feels more like a bedsheet than a technical fabric. That is comfortable against the skin, but cotton blends hold moisture and dry slowly. Buyers on Anaconda's site, where the bag has nearly 400 reviews, mostly praise it for value, comfort and being easy to wash. A minority of reports mention quality-control problems, such as a lining that tore on first use. Machine-washability is a real plus for a bag that will spend its life with kids.

**Value.** At around AU$50 on sale, the Drifter is hard to argue with as a casual-use bag. Reviewers repeatedly land on the same phrase: good for the price.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low price, with frequent sales at AU$49.99
- Roomy rectangular cut with a full-length zip that opens flat as a blanket
- Draft collar and hood add warmth for a budget bag
- Easy to machine-wash and maintain
- Includes a drawcord storage sack and an inner pocket

**Cons**

- 1,905 g is heavy by any backpacking standard
- 45 x 23 cm packed volume is large for a hiking pack
- Rectangular cut and polyester-cotton lining are less thermally efficient than a mummy or quilt with a technical liner
- The 0°C rating is best treated as a survival limit, not a comfort one
- Reports of early lining failures on some units

## Who Should Buy This

The Drifter suits parents kitting out kids for school camps, car campers on mild nights, and anyone who wants a durable-enough spare bag for guests or the back of the car. It is for use within walking distance of a vehicle.

Thru-hikers, ultralight backpackers and anyone counting grams should look elsewhere. A budget down bag or a synthetic quilt will save close to a kilogram. Cold sleepers planning real near-freezing nights should also shop up the range.

## Verdict

The Spinifex Drifter 0° does what its marketing says: it keeps a family warm at a campground for the price of a pizza dinner for four. It does not belong on a trail, and the rating reflects that. Buy it for the car boot, not the backpack.
