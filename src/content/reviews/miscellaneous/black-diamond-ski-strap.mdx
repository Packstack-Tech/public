## Overview

The Black Diamond Ski Strap is a 15-inch loop of stretchy thermoplastic with a steel hook-and-pin buckle. It was built to hold a pair of skis together, but it earns its spot in an ultralight kit by doing the jobs a stuff sack can't: lashing a tripod, snowshoes or a foam pad to the outside of a pack, and fixing broken gear in the field. The trade-off is that at 27 g it is heavier than a length of cord. In return, it grips better and is much faster to use.

## Performance

**Grip and adjustability.** The design is simple. The strap wraps around the load, stretches, and the metal pin locks into one of several holes in the band. Owners keep returning to how forgiving that is. One reviewer on Black Diamond's own site says it can be adjusted and secured with bulky gloves on. They add that it holds skis together "firmly and gently". Another buyer, who used the longer 25-inch version, found the rubber could be pulled tight and locked by the pin. They also noted that the looped end lets excess length be tucked away. That addresses a common annoyance with ski straps, where the buckle works loose and the strap goes slack.

**Stretch and feel.** Reviewers who have used both describe the Black Diamond as more supple and more packable than the Voile strap, a long-standing alternative. They also say the rubber covering the hook keeps it from scratching what it's lashed to. Skimo.co's retail testers, writing about an earlier 19-inch version, found it stretchier than the Voile and liked that the metal buckle holds well. They also found it offered more ways to adjust the fit. That give is the point. The strap stays snug on a bundle that shifts or compresses, which a rigid webbing strap won't do.

**Length.** At 15 inches, this is the short option. Black Diamond rates it for skinny to extra-skinny skis, which makes it a fit for touring setups, poles and small bundles. One owner calls 15 inches a good length for smaller bundles of backcountry skis and poles. Wider skis, or anything you want to wrap twice, call for the 20- or 25-inch versions.

**Beyond skis.** This is where the strap is useful to backpackers. Buyers report using it to hold snowshoes and skis to a pack, to strap down loads in an SUV, and to bundle odds and ends. One REI reviewer says they could see taking it backpacking to attach gear to the outside of a pack. Black Diamond itself pitches it for a camera tripod on a pack's exterior. The repair stories are the most convincing. A Backcountry.com buyer used one as an improvised toe strap to get a partner down a descent after a binding piece snapped. Another owner says the straps have saved more than one snowboarder's toboggan ride after a binding strap broke. Black Diamond also suggests lashing skis and poles into a makeshift emergency sled.

**Durability.** Ratings are consistently high. Black Diamond's own page shows 62 reviews averaging 5 out of 5 for the 15-inch strap. REI's 25-inch version has 95 reviews averaging 4.8. Backcountry.com's description calls the thermoplastic abrasion-resistant and says it holds up in cold temperatures. One first-season owner said they hope the rubber lasts, a reminder that any elastomer will eventually age.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Easy to tighten and release with gloves on
- Stretch keeps tension on loads that shift or compress
- Rubber around the hook protects gear from scratches
- Doubles as a repair tool for broken buckles, straps and bindings
- Cheap enough to carry several

**Cons**

- At 27 g, it weighs more than cord or a thin cinch strap
- The 15-inch length is too short for fat skis or big bundles
- Elastomer will eventually age and lose stretch
- Sized and marketed for skiing, so the buckle is more than a hiker-only user needs

## Who Should Buy This

This strap suits backcountry skiers and ski mountaineers first. It's also a good pick for ultralight hikers who carry snowshoes, an ice axe, a tripod or a foam pad on the outside of the pack. Anyone who wants a repair item that works on a broken pack buckle, a failed binding or a loose boot buckle will find it useful. Buy two or three, and consider a 20-inch one for bigger loads. Thru-hikers who never see snow and who count every gram should carry a length of cord instead.

## Verdict

A $10 strap that does one job very well and a dozen others adequately. The stretch, the gloved-hand adjustment and the protective hook coating make it better than most budget straps, and the 27 g weight is easy to justify for the repair options it adds. Buy it for winter and shoulder-season kits. Skip it if your pack never leaves summer trails.
