## Overview

SiS Hydro+ is a single-serve electrolyte sachet from the British sports-nutrition brand Science in Sport. It weighs 4.1 g, contains no sugar or carbohydrate, and covers five electrolytes plus vitamins C, B6 and B12. It is a good fit for hikers who want flavored, low-calorie hydration for almost no pack weight. The trade-off is a sodium dose that is moderate rather than aggressive, so it works better for everyday and moderate-effort use than for the saltiest days.

## Performance

**Electrolyte profile.** SiS says each sachet delivers 1,576 mg of electrolytes. That total is the sum of potassium, chloride, calcium, magnesium and sodium. Chloride is the largest component at 750 mg. The figure that matters most to a hiker is sodium. At 350 mg in 500 ml, the mix works out to about 700 mg per liter. That is a reasonable baseline for steady walking in mild to warm weather. It is not enough for a heavy sweater grinding through a hot, exposed climb. Those hikers would have to use two sachets in a liter, or add salt from elsewhere. That also doubles the vitamin dose.

The potassium (300 mg), calcium (120 mg) and magnesium (56 mg) amounts are meaningful for a drink mix at this weight. They are not a substitute for a real diet. SiS's own tablet range sits at a similar sodium level. 220 Triathlon's testers found the brand's Hydro tablets offered 345 mg of sodium with a good spread of electrolytes plus B vitamins, and praised them as budget-friendly. Hydro+ is a different product, but the sodium targets line up.

**Calories and fueling.** There are no carbohydrates here. That is the point, and also the limit. Hydro+ will not fuel anything. On a long day it needs to be paired with real food or a separate carbohydrate drink. SiS sells a GO Electrolyte powder for that job, with 36 g of carbohydrate per serving. For hikers who already get calories from bars, tortillas and candy, a zero-calorie electrolyte drink is easier to slot in. It also won't add to the sugar load that makes many hikers tire of sweet drink mixes by day three.

**Taste.** Flavor is where owner opinion splits. Buyers on SiS's own product pages praise the taste and say it works well after long runs. Some drink it hot in the evening as a recovery drink. The less enthusiastic reports describe the orange-grapefruit style flavor as sweet enough to make them wince. SiS directs users to mix a sachet with 500 ml of cold water, and cold is clearly where it works best. Hikers who dislike sweet drinks should try a single sachet before buying a box. Diluting it with more water is the obvious fix, though that also weakens the electrolyte dose per sip.

**Vitamins.** The 80 mg of vitamin C and the B6 and B12 are mostly label copy. SiS pitches them for normal energy metabolism and reduced tiredness. Nobody should plan a resupply around them. They do no harm, and they are a small bonus for anyone using the mix as a daily drink rather than only on trail.

**Packing.** This is where the product earns its place in a gear list. At 4.1 g, a week's supply of seven sachets is about 29 g. It is flat, packs into a ziplock or a hip-belt pocket, and needs no scoop or measuring. SiS markets it for trail running and bikepacking as well as lower-key activity. Sachets are also easy to ration, which is not true of a tub. The cost is trash. Each sachet is a small piece of single-use packaging to carry out. A tablet tube is more compact in that respect.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 4.1 g per serving, one of the lightest ways to flavor water and add salts
- Zero sugar and zero carbohydrate, so it won't compete with food calories
- Five electrolytes, including 350 mg sodium and 300 mg potassium per 500 ml
- Single-serve sachets are easy to ration, pocket and count out per day
- Some owners also drink it hot as an evening recovery drink

**Cons**

- Sodium is on the low side for heavy sweaters or hot-weather mileage
- Taste divides owners; some find it very sweet
- Provides no energy, so it needs to be paired with calories on long days
- Sachet packaging adds trash
- Added B and C vitamins are of minor practical value on trail

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who sweat at an average rate, walk in temperate conditions, and want a light, low-calorie way to make water more drinkable and replace salts. It also suits day hikers, trail runners and bikepackers who want flavor and electrolytes without fuel, and anyone who wants a daily electrolyte drink off trail as well as on it. Heavy sweaters, desert hikers and anyone who gets cramps in the heat should look for a mix with a higher sodium dose. Hikers who want one product to do hydration and calories should look at a carbohydrate-based drink mix instead.

## Verdict

Hydro+ is a competent, extremely light electrolyte mix with a balanced mineral profile and no calories to speak of. The sodium level and a polarizing flavor hold it back from being a default pick for serious mileage. It is a good choice for moderate days and flavor-starved water bottles, and a weaker one for long hot slogs.
