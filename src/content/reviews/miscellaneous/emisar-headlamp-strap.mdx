## Overview

This is Hank/Noctigon's official headlamp strap for the Emisar D2, DW3AA, DW4, and DW4K — a handful of dollars' worth of elastic and a light-retention cradle that turns a right-angle EDC flashlight into a functional headlamp. It's aimed squarely at people who already own one of these compact Anduril-powered lights and want hands-free use without buying a dedicated headlamp.

## Performance

At 48 g and under $6, expectations should be modest here, and for the most part the strap clears that low bar. The elastic itself is unremarkable in a good way — it stretches enough to go over a beanie or buff without feeling like it's about to snap, and Hank's added a light-reflecting logo on the band, which is a nice small touch for anyone using this on a dog walk or road-adjacent errand after dark.

The more interesting story is in the retention system, and it's worth knowing that this isn't the first strap Emisar has sold for these lights. According to a detailed writeup on Candlepower Forums, the older strap that used to ship alongside the DW4 relied on <cite index="3-10,3-11">pre-sized rings around the cylindrical body as a retention method, and with the DW4 body the headlamp retention rings didn't really line up with any of the contours of the light</cite>. The result, per that same user, was that <cite index="3-1,3-2">the headlamp felt like it jutted out and was ungainly</cite>.

The current strap being reviewed here appears to be Hank's fix for that problem. The same CPF poster, after picking up the newer version, reported it <cite index="3-14">handily replaces the DW4 strap that originally came with it</cite>, and that <cite index="3-15">it fits the DW3AA exceptionally well, also</cite>. They went on to describe a real-world stress test — extended close-up work requiring extra light — during which <cite index="3-16">the DW4 carried comfortably the entire time</cite>. That's about as good a practical endorsement as you'll get for a $6 accessory, and their closing take was blunt: for anyone stuck with an old strap that used pre-sized rings on a right-angle light, <cite index="3-17">the Emisar strap is worth the $7 he charges for it</cite>.

I'd treat that single account as a strong data point rather than gospel — user reviews on this specific accessory are thin online, and I couldn't find a broader sample from r/flashlight or BPL-style forums to corroborate long-term durability. The 18mm–25mm tube range on paper covers the D2's slim AA-cell body up through the beefier DW4K, but "covers the range" and "fits snugly" aren't always the same thing, and the CPF account suggests fit quality varies enough between light bodies that Hank felt the need to redesign the cradle at least once already.

Practically speaking, this is not a tilting, ratcheting headlamp mount like you'd get on a Petzl or BioLite — it's an elastic sling that holds a cylindrical flashlight against your forehead at whatever angle the light's own right-angle head points. That's fine for the D2/DW3AA/DW4 family since they're designed with a 90-degree head specifically for this kind of use, but don't expect fine-tuned beam angle adjustment once it's strapped on.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Dirt cheap at $5.95 — basically an impulse-buy accessory if you already own a compatible light
- Reflective logo adds a small margin of visibility for night use
- Newer retention design reportedly fixes the poor fit of the original bundled DW4 strap
- Covers a wide 18mm–25mm tube range, spanning the D2 through the DW4K
- Lightweight at 48 g, so it won't weigh down a pack or hip pocket

**Cons**

- Independent user reviews are scarce — most of what's out there is a single detailed forum account, not a broad consensus
- No tilt or ratchet mechanism; you're limited to the light's built-in head angle
- Elastic-only construction means long-term stretch/durability is unproven from what I could find
- Only works with Emisar's specific right-angle lights, not a universal headlamp mount

## Who Should Buy This

If you already carry a D2, DW3AA, DW4, or DW4K and find yourself wishing you had a spare hand while camping, doing gear repairs, or walking the dog, this is a no-brainer add-on given the price. It's not a replacement for a dedicated headlamp with a proper article ratchet band and tilt bracket — it's a way to get more utility out of a light you already own.

## Verdict

For $5.95, this strap does what it needs to: it gets one of Emisar's compact hotrods onto your head without much fuss, and the redesigned retention cradle sounds like a real improvement over the strap that used to ship with the DW4. I'd call it a solid, low-risk accessory buy rather than a headlamp replacement — 7.5/10, with the caveat that broader long-term durability data just isn't out there yet.
