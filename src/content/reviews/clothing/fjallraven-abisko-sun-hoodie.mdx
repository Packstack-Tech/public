## Overview

The Abisko Sun-Hoodie is Fjällräven's answer to the Sahara-style hiking hoodie: a long-sleeve polyester jersey with a roomy, well-shaped hood, built for trekking in warm, sunny weather. At 180 g in size M, it covers head, neck, arms and wrists without much of a weight penalty. Owners praise the coverage and the breathability. The recurring complaints are odor and a fabric that some find scratchy against bare skin.

## Performance

**Sun coverage.** The hood is the main selling point. Buyers describe it as generous and semi-structured, with enough brim to shade the face and enough volume to pull over a ballcap or slide under a helmet. Its geometry also keeps it from blowing down in a breeze. Owners say it stays put without a drawcord, though UK retailer Ultralight Outdoor Gear notes that it can feel a bit loose in stronger winds. Thumb loops extend coverage over the backs of the hands. One REI reviewer wore them all day against trekking-pole grips and saw no wear at the thumb joint.

One caveat comes up repeatedly. The Abisko carries no official UPF rating, and Fjällräven doesn't claim it blocks UV to a measured standard. Most wearers report that it shields them well in full sun, from a Florida humidity-and-bugs report to a spring snowshoe day at altitude. One buyer who spent whole days exposed said it failed to protect them adequately. Anyone with a strict sun-safety requirement should note that no number backs up the claim.

**Breathability and drying.** The jersey knit is the other strength. Reviewers describe it as airy and fast to dry, and the loose cut lets air circulate. One wearer said it wicked sweat and let wind pass through for cooling during two days of intermittent sun. Another called it a good fit for humid conditions. It runs a little warmer than the thinnest sun shirts. A Finnish buyer was surprised by that, while others count it as a plus on cool mornings or as a base layer on colder days. In sustained desert heat, a lighter, more open weave may be more comfortable.

**Comfort and odor.** Opinions on the fabric are split. Some owners find it softer than most synthetics. Others describe it as itchy or rough at the edges, particularly for the women's cut, where one reviewer wanted a softer hand. Odor is the more consistent weakness. A backcountry reviewer reported that smell built up after a single day compared with other base layers, which matters on multiday trips. Another owner said five days passed without trouble, so results vary by wearer and conditions. Still, polyester with no odor treatment will not match merino or treated fabrics for a long stretch between washes. The material does rinse out and dry fast, which helps.

**Fit.** The cut is regular but runs roomy, with a long body. Several European buyers found it too long even after sizing down. One reviewer at 5'10" in a medium called it slightly oversized. Another, also in a medium, thought a large would have fit better because the arms felt slim. Taller or longer-limbed wearers should test sleeve length before buying, since women's-cut buyers have reported short sleeves.

**Durability.** Reports are mostly positive, and owners cite Fjällräven's reputation for lasting garments. One REI reviewer had the stitching on an elastic wrist band fail on a 14er trip after limited use, so the cuffs may be the weak point.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 180 g for full coverage from hood to wrists
- Roomy hood that fits over caps and helmets and stays in place
- Fast-drying, breathable jersey
- Thumb loops hold up under trekking-pole use
- Made without PFAS; 88% recycled polyester
- Doubles as a light layer on cool mornings

**Cons**

- No UPF rating or manufacturer UV claim
- Odor builds quickly for some wearers
- Fabric edges feel scratchy to some
- Long body and sleeve fit are inconsistent
- Reported cuff stitching failure
- Pricey next to basic sun hoodies, with a list price around $95

## Who Should Buy This

The Abisko suits hikers who want a hood with real coverage and who spend long days on exposed trail, in places like the Sierra, the Colorado high country and humid Southeastern forest. It also suits anyone who wears a cap or helmet and wants the hood to fit over it. The relaxed cut helps with airflow, and it works for fishing and mountain biking as well as hiking.

Skip it if you need a certified UPF rating, if you rarely wash on multiday trips and are sensitive to smell, or if you want the lightest, most open weave for desert heat. Thru-hikers counting every gram might also look at thinner shirts.

## Verdict

The Abisko Sun-Hoodie is a well-cut, comfortable hood that does what a hiking sun layer should, and it weighs 180 g. Its drawbacks are odor retention, a fit that can run long, and the missing UPF number. It is a good choice for hikers who value the hood and breathability, and less so for those who need measured UV protection or odor resistance on long trips.
