## Overview

The Zpacks Belt Pouch is a small zippered bag that clips onto a padded pack belt, adding roughly a liter of snack-and-gadget storage for under an ounce. It is built for thru-hikers who want food, a phone and a headlamp within reach without stopping to open the pack. The trade-off is straightforward: it costs extra, and it only works on a padded belt.

## Performance

**Capacity.** One liter sounds modest, but the pouch is 3D-shaped rather than flat, and reviewers note that it is larger than the sewn-in hip pockets on most ultralight packs. Zpacks says it takes a headlamp, folded maps, snacks, a hat and gloves, or small gadgets. In practice, a day's worth of bars, a phone and lip balm fit comfortably. A full-size smartphone in a case, plus food, fills it quickly.

**Attachment and stability.** The pouch clips to the belt with loop toggles instead of being stitched on. The design is aimed at padded belts like the one on Zpacks' own Arc packs, and a reviewer of an early Arc Haul kit counted the belt pouches among the more stable accessories on that pack. Because the attachment is a clip-on system, it is worth checking the toggles now and then, as a tester of the broader Zpacks accessory range advised.

**Weather resistance.** The zipper is water-resistant, not waterproof. Zpacks itself suggests lining the pouch with a zip-top bag if it will carry electronics. In steady rain, food and a headlamp will be fine. A phone gets a bag.

**Zipper and one-handed use.** The zipper is reinforced with a stiffer material so it can be opened and closed with one hand while walking. That matters more than it sounds. Hip belt zippers on lightweight packs are often the first part to fail, and Zpacks' fabric is meant to withstand being dragged along the ground each time the pack is set down.

**Replaceability.** This is the pouch's strongest argument. Since it is removable, a failed zipper means replacing a roughly $33 pouch and not retiring or repairing the whole pack. Adventure Alan's reviewers make the same point, calling the replaceable design a clear advantage over the built-in pockets found on most other packs.

**Weight.** At 23 g, a pair adds about 46 g to the pack. That is the cost of two bars' worth of snacks being reachable at all times. For comparison, an ounce and a half buys hands-free access to food, which for many hikers means fewer stops and more steady miles.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 23 g for about 1 L of belt-level storage
- Larger and more three-dimensional than most sewn-in hip pockets
- Replaceable if the zipper wears out
- One-handed, reinforced zipper
- Abrasion-resistant fabric that tolerates being set down on the ground
- Fits Arc-style padded belts, and loop toggles make it removable

**Cons**

- Sold separately from the pack, so a pair is around $66
- Water-resistant, not waterproof; electronics need a bag
- Requires a padded belt, so webbing-belt packs are out
- The clip-on attachment needs an occasional check
- One liter is tight for large phones plus food

## Who Should Buy This

Owners of Zpacks Arc packs, and anyone with another padded-belt pack whose own pockets are small, will get the most from it. It also suits hikers who have killed a hip belt zipper on a long trail and want a fix that doesn't involve a sewing kit. Anyone using a pack with a thin webbing belt should look elsewhere, as should hikers who want a phone-first pocket; Zpacks' shoulder pouch or a dedicated phone holder suits that better. Hikers who don't mind a sewn-in pocket and won't pay for add-ons can skip it.

## Verdict

The Belt Pouch does one job well: it puts snacks and small essentials at hand for next to no weight, and it can be replaced when it wears out. The price is steep for what is essentially a small zippered bag, and the water resistance stops short of waterproof, but for a padded-belt pack it is the best hip storage in the ultralight category.
