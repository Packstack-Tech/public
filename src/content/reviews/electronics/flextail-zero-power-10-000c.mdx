## Overview

The FLEXTAIL ZERO POWER 10,000C is a 10,000mAh USB-C power bank built around a forged carbon fiber shell, aimed squarely at ultralight backpackers, trail runners, and thru-hikers who want a full-size battery capacity without the usual brick-in-your-pack feel. At 145 g and just 9.9 mm thick, it's marketed as the world's lightest and thinnest 10,000mAh power bank on the market, and after digging through both the spec sheet and independent field tests, that claim mostly holds up.

## Performance

The headline number here is 145 g for a 10,000mAh cell, and that's a real, verifiable figure — CleverHiker weighed it themselves and got <cite index="12-2">"just under 5 ounces on our scale (it's listed at 5.1 ounces)"</cite>, confirming the manufacturer spec rather than the usual "up to" marketing fudge. For comparison, the popular Nitecore NB10000 sits in the same general weight class, and one reviewer who tested both noted <cite index="7-18">the Nitecore is slightly more water-resistant (IPX5 vs IPX4), but the Flextail is marginally lighter and often a few dollars cheaper</cite>.

The dual-mode charging system is the other big differentiator. Fast Charge Mode pushes the full 22.5 W, and one reviewer's real-world test had a phone <cite index="9-8,9-9">down to 3% just as the sun was setting, plugged in and turned on Fast Charging, and within about 15 minutes, back up to 55%</cite> — a legitimately useful recovery speed if you're racing to catch golden hour or just don't want to be phoneless at camp. Switch to Efficiency Mode (5V/1A) and you trade speed for total output; FLEXTAIL's own testing frames this as unlocking meaningfully more usable capacity per charge cycle, which lines up with the rated 7,000mAh (Efficiency) vs 5,200mAh (Fast Charge) split in the spec sheet. In plain terms: if you're topping off overnight with no rush, use Efficiency Mode and you'll squeeze noticeably more juice out of the bank over a multi-day trip.

Where the ZERO POWER shows its one real weakness is cold weather. CleverHiker's testing found <cite index="12-9">the only real limitation we discovered in testing was cold-weather performance, with the FLEXTAIL losing about 8% of its efficiency when placed in the fridge</cite>. FLEXTAIL does list a -10°C operating floor, so it's not going to brick on you in a frosty vestibule, but don't expect full-rated output on a cold morning — sleeping with it in your bag on shoulder-season trips is still good practice.

Durability has held up well in independent testing too. CleverHiker <cite index="12-16,12-17">subjected the device to a drop test by letting it fall about eight feet into dirt, and it emerged with no signs of damage, with the carbon fiber shell and slightly rounded corners helping with overall longevity</cite>. FLEXTAIL also claims the frame uses <cite index="8-17,8-18">aerospace-grade nylon reinforced with fiberglass and an added flame retardant that resists ignition when exposed to open flame</cite> — a nice-to-have if it ever ends up near a camp stove, though I'd treat that as a marginal safety buffer rather than something to test on purpose.

One quirk worth flagging before you buy: the included lanyard cable is genuinely part of the system, not an optional accessory. It doubles as your USB-C charging cable, and at least one owner pointed out that <cite index="14-9,14-10">the weight claimed is a bit misleading because it does not include the lanyard, and it would be silly to leave home without that part so add a few grams to the base weight</cite>. It's a smart design — no separate cable to lose — but budget an extra few grams in your pack spreadsheet.

Battery efficiency (watt-hours delivered per gram of bank) is where this thing separates itself from cheaper 10,000mAh bricks. CleverHiker called it <cite index="12-7,12-8">one of the most efficient power banks we tested, packing quite a punch for its size, though it's on the pricier end, with the low weight and excellent efficiency helping justify the cost</cite>. That tracks with my expectations for a premium ultralight electronics purchase — you're paying for grams saved, not just mAh.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely class-leading weight-to-capacity ratio, independently verified at ~5 oz for 10,000mAh
- 22.5 W Fast Charge mode delivers real, usable speed (55% in ~15 minutes from near-dead in one field test)
- Efficiency Mode meaningfully stretches total usable power for multi-day trips
- Forged carbon fiber shell held up to an 8-foot drop test with no damage
- Built-in lanyard/cable design means one less thing to lose or forget

**Cons:**

- IPX4 water resistance is a step below the Nitecore NB10000's IPX5 — fine for splashes, not for submersion
- Loses roughly 8% efficiency in cold conditions based on independent testing
- Premium price relative to bulkier competitors like the Anker 523
- True carry weight is a few grams over the advertised 145 g once you factor in the required lanyard cable

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right pick for ultralight backpackers, trail runners, and thru-hikers who've already dialed in the rest of their base weight and are hunting for grams in their electronics kit specifically. If you're charging a phone, GPS watch, or headlamp on multi-night trips and don't want to carry a 20,000mAh brick "just in case," the ZERO POWER 10,000C's efficiency modes and low weight make it a smart trade. It's less compelling for casual car campers or anyone who prioritizes bomber water resistance over shaving ounces — for those users, a heavier IPX5+ bank will probably serve better and cost less.

## Verdict

The FLEXTAIL ZERO POWER 10,000C backs up its "world's lightest" marketing with independently verified weight and efficiency numbers, and the dual-mode charging system is a genuinely useful feature rather than a gimmick. Cold-weather performance and IPX4 (rather than IPX5) water resistance are the main trade-offs, but for gram-counters who need real capacity without the bulk, this is one of the better ultralight power banks on the market right now. I'd rate it an 8/10.
