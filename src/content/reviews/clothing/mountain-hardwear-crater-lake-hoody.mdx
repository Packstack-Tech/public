## Overview

The Crater Lake Hoody is a thin, stretchy UPF 50+ pullover that works as a stand-alone sun shirt or a single-layer base on cool, bright days. It has been a fixture on trails, rivers and climbing crags for years, largely because it feels better against the skin than most of its rivals and costs about $70. The trade-off is a tight-knit fabric that gives up some airflow for sun protection, and a stretch that some owners say doesn't fully come back.

## Performance

**Comfort.** This is where the Crater Lake wins its reviews. The jersey knit is silky and smooth, and testers at DuskHiker named it their most comfortable sun shirt, noting that it's pleasant enough to wear in the shade. Switchback Travel's reviewers singled out the fabric's feel and the freedom of movement it allows. REI buyers describe an athletic cut that sits close without flapping at the midsection, which keeps it from catching on a pack's hip belt or climbing harness.

**Sun protection.** With a UPF 50+ rating, the fabric blocks the vast majority of UV on exposed arms, neck and shoulders. The hood is the practical advantage over a standard sun shirt. It pulls up over the ears and neck, and wearers say it sits comfortably under or over a cap. DuskHiker's testers did note that the hood works best when paired with a hat. Thumbholes keep the cuffs over the backs of the hands, which matters on exposed ridgelines and long paddling days.

**Breathability and drying.** The knit is breathable for a fabric this sun-proof, and reviewers describe it as quick-drying. It is not the airiest option, though. One REI owner expected a lighter, more open fabric and found some breathability had been sacrificed for UV protection. The same reviewer said it clings when soaked with sweat. Wildland Trekking's guide, who wore it across Vietnam and the US Southwest, found it impractical only on the hottest, most humid, overcast days. The fabric dries fast enough that it never developed a smell over consecutive days of wear.

**Odor.** Experiences differ. Switchback Travel flagged it as not the most odor-resistant option for heavy sweaters, and an aggregate of reviews shows some owners reporting lingering odor after washing. Like any untreated polyester, it rewards a rinse on multi-day trips.

**Durability.** Reports split. The Wildland Trekking tester saw no pilling, abrasion or discoloration after extended use in rough conditions, and one REI owner got nearly 14 months as a go-to base layer. At the other end, a critical REI reviewer said the shirt grew by a size in every direction within two months. Seam fraying around the thumbholes shows up in some owner reports, mainly on the women's version. Treat this as a shirt for sun and travel, not one to be dragged through brush, and wash it gently.

**Packability.** At 168 g in a fabric thin enough to stuff into a pocket or knot around the waist, it takes up almost no room. It costs a bit more weight than a bare tee, but replaces a hat's neck coverage and a layer of sunscreen.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptionally soft, smooth fabric that's comfortable all day
- UPF 50+ with a hood and thumbholes for full neck, ear and hand coverage
- 168 g and packs down very small
- Athletic cut moves well and doesn't flap
- Fast drying; reasonable price for the performance

**Cons**

- Less breathable than open-knit or mesh-panel sun shirts in hot, humid weather
- Clings when soaked with sweat
- Some owners report stretched-out fit and odor build-up
- Durability reports are inconsistent
- Hood works best when worn with a hat

## Who Should Buy This

The Crater Lake suits hikers, paddlers and climbers who want a soft, no-fuss sun layer for dry, bright conditions, from desert days to alpine glare to cool-morning base-layer duty. It also suits thru-hikers who put comfort next to the skin ahead of everything else. Hikers who run hot, sweat heavily, or work in humid, sticky climates should look at a more open-weave shirt. Anyone hard on gear through thick brush or heavy pack straps should expect to replace it sooner.

## Verdict

The Crater Lake Hoody is a comfortable, well-priced sun shirt at a trail-friendly 168 g, and its soft hand and sun coverage make it an easy recommendation for most three-season hikers. Heavy sweaters and humid-climate hikers will get more from a looser-knit option. Buy it for comfort and coverage, and wash it gently.
