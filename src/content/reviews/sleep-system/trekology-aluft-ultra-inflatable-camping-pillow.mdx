## Overview

The ALUFT Ultra is the big sibling in Trekology's inflatable pillow line: a 24 x 15 in air pillow with a removable fabric cover, built for people who want a bed-sized headrest in the backcountry and will pay in grams to get it. It suits side sleepers, restless sleepers and car-camp-to-trail crossover users. The headline trade-off is plain. At 265 g it weighs about two and a half times as much as the brand's own ALUFT 2.0.

## Performance

**Size and comfort.** The 24-inch width is the reason to buy this pillow. Most backpacking pillows run 15 to 18 inches across, and a head that wanders in the night tends to slide off the edge. Here it has room to roam, and the 4.5-inch loft gives side sleepers a real gap to fill between ear and shoulder. Trekology pitches the ergonomic shaping as neck support and compares the feel to memory foam. That is marketing language, but the combination of width, height and a fabric-faced cover does put the Ultra closer to a bed pillow than the slim, slippery-skinned designs that dominate the category. Because it is air, firmness is adjustable. Back sleepers can let a few breaths out to drop the loft.

**Weight and pack size.** This is where the Ultra gives ground. At 265 g, it is roughly 155 g heavier than the ALUFT 2.0 (110 g, 16 x 12 x 4 in inflated) and about 95 g heavier than the ALUFT Plus (170 g). A thru-hiker who trims toothbrush handles will feel it. Packed, it reaches 7.5 x 4 x 4 in, a water-bottle-sized cylinder that eats a side pocket or a chunk of the pack's main compartment. Many hikers use a stuffed jacket or a spare clothes sack as a pillow for zero added grams, and the Ultra cannot compete on that math.

**Staying put.** The detachable strap is meant to cinch the pillow to an inflatable pad, a sensible answer to the nightly problem of a pillow sliding off slick nylon. It is an upgrade on the rubber dots used on the smaller ALUFT 2.0. A Trailspace reviewer who put more than two years on that model praised its durability and comfort but reported that the dots did not grip a sleeping bag, a pad or even a table. A strap that wraps around the pad does not depend on friction. Pairing it with a pad that has a pillow-lock or sleeve-style strap system adds further security.

**Durability and care.** The core is thickened TPU, a welded, airtight material widely used in inflatable gear, and Trekology markets the pillow as leak-proof. The textile cover comes off for washing. That is a practical benefit on trips of a week or more, when a sweat-and-sunscreen-soaked pillow becomes the dirtiest item in the tent. Separating the cover from the bladder also keeps the pillow from feeling clammy against the face, a common complaint with bare plastic-skinned air pillows. The two-way valve is designed for quick inflation and deflation, and the pillow's size means more breaths to fill it than a compact model needs.

**Beyond sleep.** The size lets it double as lumbar support in a camp chair or on a long flight, though a 265 g pillow is a poor choice if that is the only reason to carry it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roughly 24 x 15 in surface leaves room for head movement
- 4.5 in of loft suits side sleepers
- Removable, washable cover
- Strap helps anchor the pillow to a sleeping pad
- Thickened TPU bladder; adjustable firmness
- Priced well below premium backpacking pillows from the big names

**Cons**

- 265 g is heavy for a pillow, and well over the weight of other models in Trekology's own line
- Packs to water-bottle size, not soda-can size
- More lung work to inflate than a compact pillow
- A jacket-in-a-stuff-sack costs nothing and weighs nothing extra
- Foam-like comfort is a marketing claim; air will not feel like foam

## Who Should Buy This

This is the pillow for side sleepers and light sleepers who rank a good night's rest above base weight: weekend backpackers, section hikers on short trips, and campers who walk a mile or two from the car. It also suits anyone moving from a full-size bed pillow who finds compact pillows too small to use. Gram counters, thru-hikers with base weights under 10 pounds and fastpackers should skip it and look at the ALUFT 2.0 or ALUFT Plus, or at a stuffed-clothing pillow.

## Verdict

The ALUFT Ultra delivers what it promises: a wide, tall, washable air pillow that sleeps larger than almost anything else that packs to the size of a bottle. It is a comfort piece, not an ultralight one, and buyers who accept the 265 g price will likely be happy. Anyone chasing every ounce should step down to the smaller ALUFT models.
