## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is Decathlon's entry-level merino underwear for trekkers: an 86% wool boxer-brief with a nylon-wrapped yarn for durability. It sells for roughly the price of a sandwich at a trailhead café, which is the main reason to consider it. The trade-offs are a snug, compression-style cut, no fly, and a fabric that is practical rather than luxurious.

## Performance

**Comfort.** At 18.5 microns, the wool sits in the fine range where itch is rarely an issue, and Decathlon pitches the fabric on softness. Owners say the first impression can be rough, though. One long-term reviewer who compared the MT500 against Icebreaker's Anatomica boxers found the Forclaz pair itchy and far too tight out of the packet, with a feel closer to compression shorts than underwear. After a couple of weeks of wear the fit relaxed. After about a year of frequent use the same pair felt normal and, if anything, slightly baggy. Decathlon's own advice is to wash them before the first wear, which helps soften the fabric.

**Fit and cut.** The name says boxer, but the shape is a boxer brief. Decathlon describes the cut as fitted so moisture can move to the outside of the fabric, and that is the part to take seriously: size up only if between sizes, because the fabric stretches with wear. There is no fly, and the pouch has drawn criticism for being less comfortable than the best merino competitors. For some hikers, a missing fly is a deal-breaker. For most, it's a minor annoyance that costs a few seconds at every trailside stop.

**Moisture and odor.** This is where merino earns its place in a pack. Wool absorbs moisture vapor and releases it, so the damp-cling feeling common to cheap synthetic briefs is largely absent, and the 190 g/m² fabric stays warm in cool conditions while remaining breathable in summer. Decathlon backs the odor claim with an ISO 17299-3 laboratory test. In practice that means multi-day wear without a smell problem, which is the reason merino underwear works for thru-hikers carrying two pairs rather than five.

**Durability.** Merino underwear is notoriously fragile at friction points. The MT500 addresses this with Beta-Spun yarn, where polyamide threads coat the wool fibers to protect them from rubbing and breaking. The 10% nylon share is modest next to some blended competitors, and the pairing works: the long-term reviewer reported good yarn and stitching quality after a year of frequent use. Pilling on the waistband showed up early, though, which is cosmetic rather than structural. Drying is the soft spot. The fabric is wool-heavy, so it dries more slowly than a pure synthetic pair when washed in a sink, and a tumble dryer can shrink it by a size or two.

**Weight.** At 79 g, the MT500 is not the lightest merino underwear available, and the 190 g/m² fabric is on the heavier side for the category. Gram counters in hot climates will find lighter options. For three-season hiking, the weight is a fair price for durability and warmth.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Real merino content (86%) at a budget price, around £20 at list
- 18.5-micron wool is fine enough to avoid itch for most wearers
- Nylon-wrapped yarn gives better abrasion resistance than plain merino
- Strong odor control, with lab testing behind the claim
- Traceable, non-mulesed wool supply
- Machine washable at 30°C
- Available in sizes S to 2XL

**Cons**

- Snug and slightly harsh during break-in
- No fly, and the pouch is less comfortable than the best-in-class alternatives
- Waistband pilling reported relatively early
- Heavier fabric than ultralight-focused merino briefs
- Stretches out over a year of wear
- Must be dried flat; shrinks if tumble dried

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want merino's odor resistance without paying premium-brand prices, and who are willing to endure a short break-in. It also suits budget-minded travelers who want underwear they can wear for several days between washes. Hikers who put softness first, those who insist on a fly, and gram-counters building a hot-weather kit should look at Icebreaker's Anatomica line or a lighter 150 g/m² merino brief.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 doesn't pretend to be the most refined merino underwear on the market. It's a durable, odor-resistant, inexpensive pair that works well once it has been worn in. Buy two for a long trip, wash them before the trailhead, and accept the missing fly.
