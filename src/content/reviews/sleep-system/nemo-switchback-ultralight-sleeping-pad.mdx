## Overview

The Switchback is NEMO's answer to the Therm-a-Rest Z Lite Sol: an accordion-fold closed-cell foam pad with hexagonal, dual-density nodes and a metallized film layer meant to bounce body heat back at the sleeper. It's built for thru-hikers and ultralighters who want a pad that can't leak, can't fail and doubles as a camp seat. The trade-off is familiar to anyone who has carried foam: it's bulky on the outside of a pack, and it's firmer than any inflatable.

## Performance

**Weight and packing.** The Regular weighs 14.5 oz (415 g), which puts it a half-ounce above the 14 oz Z Lite Sol. GearJunkie's testers found the Switchback about 0.15 in thicker than the Z Lite, and its tighter-nesting hexagonal pattern packs at least an inch smaller. The reviewer at Halfway Anywhere still describes it as large when packed, as any full-length foam pad is. Most hikers strap it to the outside of the pack, which means it has to be moved aside to reach the pack's contents. It also adds a little width that can snag on brush.

**Warmth.** An R-value of 2 is about the ceiling for a single foam pad. That makes it a fit for three-season use, with NEMO's own range running from 20 to 35°F. The taller nodes leave more room for a sleeping bag or quilt to loft underneath the sleeper, and the metallized film adds some radiant reflection. Reviewers at The Trek tested it in conditions from the mid-30s into the 80s and found the insulation good for its rating. In real cold, the pad's second job comes into play. NEMO pitches it as a layer to stack over an inflatable, and the foam backs up a pad that has leaked or is underperforming.

**Comfort.** This is the Switchback's soft spot. Its extra thickness over the Z Lite is noticeable but modest. Back and stomach sleepers generally get along with it. Side sleepers are less well served: reviewers at Halfway Anywhere say that despite the added height, it's still not great for hip and shoulder pressure, and Reddit sentiment points the same way, with side-sleeper discomfort the most common complaint. Hikers who've moved from inflatables should expect a firmer, flatter night. Foam also compresses with use, and Halfway Anywhere reports the nodes flatten over repeated nights.

**Durability and ease of use.** There's nothing to inflate, patch or puncture. Unfolding takes seconds and so does packing it up. Thru-hikers on r/Ultralight frequently cite durability and the absence of failure modes as the reason foam outlasts any inflatable on rough ground, and the pad doubles as a sit pad at breaks and a yoga mat at camp. NEMO covers it with its lifetime warranty.

**Cut it down.** The accordion panels are a gift to gram counters. One r/Ultralight hiker carried six panels on the John Muir Trail in July, at a reported 167 g (about 6 oz), and said it was warmer and lighter than a thin inflatable, with no repair kit or inflation time. A torso-length Switchback is a common pairing with a pack's framesheet or a spare pair of shoes under the legs.

## Choosing a Version

Only the length differs. Both sizes share the 20-inch width, the 0.9-inch thickness and the R-value of 2.

- **Short (300 g):** At 51 in, it covers shoulders to hips. Hikers who put their legs on a pack, shoes or a backpack liner can save 115 g over the Regular, and its 4 in packed height makes it the easier one to stash. Cold sleepers should expect cold feet and calves on bare ground.
- **Regular (415 g):** The 72 in length reaches from head to heels, so it suits hikers who want a single pad with no other gear underneath them, and anyone who sleeps in snow or on cold ground. It's also the size to buy if the plan is to cut it down yourself, since the extra panels are easy to trim off.

There is no long or wide version, so taller or broader sleepers will find the Regular snug.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Cannot leak, puncture or fail mid-trip
- R-value of 2 is about as warm as single-layer foam gets
- Thicker and plusher than the Z Lite, with a smaller packed footprint
- Fast to deploy and pack, with nothing to inflate
- Panels can be cut to a custom torso pad of around 170 g
- Two sizes cover torso-only and full-length sleepers

**Cons**

- Heavier than the Z Lite Sol by half an ounce
- Bulky on the outside of a pack, and snags on brush
- Still a firm bed, and poor for side sleepers
- Nodes flatten with extended use
- Only two sizes, with no long or wide option

## Who Should Buy This

The Switchback suits thru-hikers and weight-conscious three-season backpackers who value reliability, back or stomach sleepers who can handle a firm pad, and anyone who wants a foam pad to cut down, layer over an inflatable or use as a camp seat. It's also a sensible winter add-on. Side sleepers, people who sleep cold below freezing as a stand-alone pad, and hikers who hate carrying anything outside the pack should look at an insulated inflatable instead.

## Verdict

The Switchback is a well-thought-out foam pad that edges out the Z Lite on thickness and packed size, at the cost of a little weight and some extra bulk. For hikers who want a bombproof pad that can also be cut to size, it earns its spot. For anyone who counts on a soft night's sleep, an inflatable remains the better choice.
