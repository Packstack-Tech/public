## Overview

UCO's Stormproof Matches are oversized, chemically treated sticks that keep burning in wind, rain and snow, and relight after a dunk in water. They are a first-aid-kit-style backup for hikers, campers and anyone building an emergency kit. The trade-off is simple: a box is more fire than most backpackers will use in a season, and it's still just a match.

## Performance

The pitch is a match that behaves less like a match and more like a small flare. UCO rates each stick at up to 15 seconds, which works out to more than six minutes of cumulative flame per box. That's long enough to nurse a fire-starter cube or a damp pile of tinder into life, or to light a stove with a fussy igniter, without racing the clock.

Independent testing backs the claim. The Prepared bought and tested nearly 50 types of matches and containers, and named UCO's Stormproof the best match for emergency preparedness. The reviewers described them as big enough to burn like a small flare for more than 15 seconds and noted they stayed lit underwater. TruePrepper also ranked them its top pick, praising the way they struck easily and stayed lit through its submersion test. Its testers measured a burn of 16 seconds, just past the stated figure. Titan, UCO's larger and pricier line, clocked 28 seconds in the same testing, so the standard version is the middle option, not the endurance one.

Owner reports line up. Reviewers who dunked a lit match and pulled it out report the flame dulls underwater and returns to full strength in the air. One outdoor writer couldn't blow a match out with a breath, and took them into the Adirondacks, where they lit on every strike. Buyers in survival and bug-out forums describe them as reliable and compact enough to tuck into a pack or roll.

The 2.75-inch length is a practical advantage. It keeps fingertips well back from the flame when lighting a canister stove, a lantern or a campfire in cold, clumsy hands.

Durability depends on how they're stored. UCO says matches and strikers last for many years as long as they stay dry in a waterproof case or sealed plastic bag. The retail box is cardboard, and the occasional buyer has complained about it. A pack of 25 loose in a hiker's pocket is not a robust system, so most users move them into a sealed container. UCO sells its own match kit for this: an ABS case that weighs 1.7 oz (48 g) and holds up to 40 matches. A few sticks in a zip bag cost far less weight.

One dissenting review on a retailer page called the burn too brief to light a lantern. Against that, testers have consistently timed the matches at or above their stated burn, and most complaints trace back to packaging or storage, not flame performance.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Burn times of 15–16 seconds, longer than ordinary waterproof matches
- Relights after submersion and holds up in wind, rain and snow
- 2.75-inch length keeps fingers clear of the flame
- Cheap: about $8 for 25, or roughly 30 cents a match
- Top pick in tests by The Prepared and TruePrepper
- Long shelf life when kept dry

**Cons**

- 22 g a box is more than a mini lighter, and only a few matches are ever needed
- Cardboard retail packaging needs a waterproof container
- Each match is single-use; a lighter gives hundreds of ignitions
- Standard version burns about half as long as UCO's Titan matches

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want a dependable backup fire source in the 10 Essentials, from weekenders to winter campers. Emergency-kit builders, hunters, boaters and stove users who want something that works with wet or numb hands will also like them. Ultralight hikers can carry a handful, five or ten sticks and a cut-down striker in a small zip bag, for a few grams. Anyone who needs a primary ignition tool should carry a lighter first and treat these matches as the backup.

Hikers who never light campfires and carry a stove with a built-in igniter have less need, though one dry backup is cheap insurance. Anyone who wants many hours of burn from a single stick should look at UCO's Titan line instead.

## Verdict

UCO's Stormproof Matches have earned their spot at the top of the waterproof-match category. They light, they stay lit, and they cost next to nothing. Buy a box, repack a few into a sealed bag, and keep the rest in the emergency kit.
