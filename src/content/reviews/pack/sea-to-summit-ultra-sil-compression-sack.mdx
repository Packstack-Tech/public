## Overview

The Ultra-Sil Compression Sack is Sea to Summit's dedicated volume-crusher — not to be confused with their roll-top dry sacks. It's built for one job: taking something bulky (a quilt, a puffy, a synthetic bag that refuses to pack small) and squeezing it down so it actually fits in your pack. If you've ever wrestled a synthetic sleeping bag into a stuff sack that was clearly designed for down, this is the product that solves that specific headache.

## Performance

**Compression.** This is the whole point of the product, and it delivers. Sea to Summit rates it at up to two-thirds volume reduction, and that lines up with how these sacks behave in practice — cinch the three straps down evenly and you feel the fabric go from baggy to taut fairly quickly. One <cite index="11-21,11-22,11-23,11-24">REI reviewer noted the sack "does exactly what it's supposed to - compress," using evenly spaced straps, and that even though it's sil-nylon, "it compresses quite easily."</cite> The three-strap version (the current spec) trades a bit of even pressure distribution for weight savings versus older four-strap models, but I haven't seen complaints that this meaningfully hurts compression in practice.

**Where it really shines** is with synthetic insulation. Down compresses fine in almost any stuff sack; synthetic fill is the stuff that laughs at flimsy sacks. A reviewer who used it specifically because <cite index="11-3,11-4,11-5">their synthetic quilt didn't pack down like a down one would said they "couldn't have fit the bulky quilt into my backpack without the Ultra-Sil compression sack."</cite> That's a common enough use case — synthetic bags/quilts for wet climates or budget builds — that it's worth calling out as this product's sweet spot.

**Durability.** Reports across retailers point to the sack holding up well over repeated compression cycles. One long-term user summed it up plainly: <cite index="11-14,11-15">it "works perfectly, is built to last, weighs next to nothing," and while pricey, "it's worth it."</cite> That tracks with Sea to Summit's broader Ultra-Sil line reputation — <cite index="1-6">gear writers have praised the fabric and seam construction for the weight class, calling it best-in-class for ultralight backpacking</cite>, though that specific commentary was about the dry sack line rather than the compression sack.

**Water resistance — manage expectations here.** This is not a dry bag. Sea to Summit's own product description is explicit that <cite index="14-11,14-12">the fabric is water-resistant enough for storage inside a pack, but the seams are not tape sealed and the top doesn't use a roll-top closure — so it's water-resistant rather than waterproof</cite>. If you're expecting the submersion protection of Sea to Summit's Ultra-Sil Dry Sack or eVent Compression Dry Bag, you'll be disappointed — this is a flip-top-and-drawcord closure, which is fast and easy to use but leaves an obvious gap for driving rain or a dunk in a creek. Pair it with a pack liner or trash-compactor bag if you're hiking somewhere wet and can't risk a soaked quilt.

**Fit and sizing.** The line runs from 5L up to 35L, so it's genuinely used for everything from a spare puffy to a full winter sleeping bag. Buyers on REI's product page have flagged that Sea to Summit previously had a similar-looking product (the Ultra-Sil Evac Compression Dry Bag) that's since been discontinued and replaced by this simpler compression-only version — worth double-checking which one you're ordering if you're shopping older listings, since <cite index="11-7,11-8">one reviewer bought this to replace an Evac bag they'd lost, not realizing Sea to Summit had split that into a separate dry bag line</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely compresses bulky gear — especially synthetic sleeping bags/quilts that resist stuffing
- 82g is negligible added pack weight for what it does
- Flip-top lid keeps straps from tangling, which sounds minor until you've fought a snagged buckle at 6am
- Six size options cover everything from a stuff sack replacement to full sleeping bag duty
- Sea to Summit's lifetime guarantee backs the product

**Cons**

- Water-resistant only — no taped seams, no roll-top, so it's not a substitute for a real dry bag in wet conditions
- Three-strap compression on larger sizes can feel less evenly distributed than four-strap designs on bigger loads
- Pricier per liter than a basic stuff sack if all you need is storage, not compression

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right call if you're packing a synthetic bag or quilt that just won't compress on its own, or if you're chronically fighting for space in a smaller pack and need to shave liters off your bulkiest item. It's less necessary if you're already running a down quilt that packs small naturally, or if you need guaranteed waterproofing — in that case, step up to one of Sea to Summit's dedicated dry bags instead and treat this as a volume tool, not a weather shield.

## Verdict

The Ultra-Sil Compression Sack does one job — shrinking bulky gear — and does it well at a genuinely low weight penalty. Just don't mistake it for a dry bag: pack it inside a liner if rain or river crossings are a real risk. For synthetic-fill sleeping systems especially, it's close to essential kit. I'd put it at an 8/10 — reliable, well-made, and priced fairly for what it solves, docked slightly for the water-resistant-not-waterproof asterisk that trips a few buyers up.
