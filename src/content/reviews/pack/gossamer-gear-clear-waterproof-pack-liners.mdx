## Overview

<cite index="1-1">
  The Gossamer Gear Clear Waterproof Pack Liners are ultralight, watertight bag
  liners — and they've recently been made taller for more storage coverage.
</cite>
<cite index="4-3,4-4,4-5">
  You insert one into your pack's main compartment before loading your gear, so
  you're essentially packing everything inside the liner itself — which creates
  a waterproof wall between your kit and the outside world.
</cite>
They're a no-frills solution aimed at weight-conscious hikers who'd rather keep
the rain out than carry around a rain cover.

## Performance

**Waterproofing**

These liners do the one job they're asked to do reliably well. <cite index="3-22,3-23">SectionHiker used Gossamer Gear's clear plastic pack liners for many years, going through a pair per year, and found them lighter than trash compactor bags and easy to patch with clear tape if punctured.</cite> The waterproofing isn't some laminated membrane — it's just polyethylene doing what polyethylene does. That means it's effective as long as the material stays intact, which requires a bit of care around sharp tent stakes and trekking pole tips.

<cite index="3-24">
  The bags are open at the top, so you have to fold them over or use a twist-tie
  to hold them closed
</cite>
— this is the biggest real-world limitation. In moderate rain it's a minor
inconvenience; in a genuine downpour you want that fold sealed tightly before
shouldering your pack. A rubber band or a couple of clip-style bag ties weighing
fractions of a gram solve this entirely.

**Durability**

<cite index="15-15">
  For how thin these plastic bags are, they're surprisingly durable and
  effective.
</cite>
The material isn't indestructible — it's ultralight plastic, after all — but the
consensus across reviewers is that a pair lasts a full season of regular use
before needing replacement. If you do get a nick,
<cite index="21-1,21-2">clear tape patches work well</cite>, and you can usually
nurse a liner through the rest of a trip without issue.

**Visibility**

The clear material is genuinely useful. <cite index="8-5">The clear polyethylene makes finding gear a cinch</cite>, especially inside dark-interior packs where colored items disappear. That said, <cite index="3-26">because they're clear, the liners inherit the color of your pack's interior, so they have a neutral (rather than enhancing) impact on visibility</cite> — don't expect the contrast boost you'd get from a white compactor bag.

**Sustainability**

<cite index="1-3,1-4">
  GG has updated the material to PolyEarthylene®, a biobased polyolefin that is
  USA-made and can be recycled in existing curbside systems or biodegrades
  completely in an active landfill.
</cite>
<cite index="1-5">
  Major grocery stores also offer specialty bins that guarantee proper plastics
  recycling when local curbside options vary.
</cite>
It's a genuine step forward over standard virgin polyethylene film, though this
is still a consumable product you'll replace annually.

**Pack Liners vs. Rain Covers**

<cite index="15-16,15-17">
  Compared to rain covers, these liners are a fraction of the cost and weight,
  and work significantly better — they also won't blow away or make it harder to
  access pockets.
</cite>
The one functional gap:
<cite index="15-18">they don't protect external storage areas</cite> like hip
belt pockets or exterior mesh pockets. If you rely on those for quick-access
items like a rain jacket or snacks, you'll still want to keep those things in
their own small dry bags or accept they might get damp.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Extremely light at 34–40 g per liner
- Clear material lets you see pack contents without unpacking
- Patchable with clear tape — easy trailside repair
- Eco-friendly PolyEarthylene® material is recyclable and biodegradable
- <cite index="8-4">Sized specifically for ultralight packs</cite>, unlike
  generic compactor bags
- Comes as a 2-pack — one for your current trip, one as a backup
- Made in the USA

**Cons**

- Open-top closure requires a fold or twist-tie; no integrated roll-top seal
- Does not protect external pockets or hip belt compartments
- Noisy and crinkly inside the pack — a minor annoyance you'll notice
- Consumable: <cite index="3-22">expect to go through roughly a pair per year</cite> with regular use
- <cite index="8-3">
    At their price point, they're not as casually disposable as a compactor bag
  </cite>
  — you'll want to treat them with some care

## Who Should Buy This

<cite index="8-1,8-2">
  Poly pack liners like these are a good fit for hikers who want a Goldilocks
  blend of durability and affordability
</cite>
— lighter and better-fitting than a trash compactor bag, but far cheaper and
simpler than a silnylon or DCF roll-top liner. They pair particularly well with
Gossamer Gear's own packs (the Mariposa, Gorilla, etc.), which lack built-in
waterproofing. If you're already carrying a GG pack and want to complete the
system without a heavy rain cover, this is the obvious answer.

## Verdict

The Gossamer Gear Clear Waterproof Pack Liners are about as unsexy as ultralight gear gets, and that's entirely the point. They work, they're light, they're cheap enough to replace without wincing, and the new PolyEarthylene® material gives you a cleaner conscience when they finally wear out. The open-top closure is the only genuine friction point, and it's easily managed. At a rating of **8/10**, the only reason this isn't higher is that the lack of an integrated seal means you need to stay disciplined in heavy weather — a roll-top version would be near-perfect.
