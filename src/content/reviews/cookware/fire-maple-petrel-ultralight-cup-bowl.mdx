## Overview

The Petrel Ultralight Cup-Bowl is Fire Maple's answer to the age-old "do I really need a separate mug and bowl" debate. It's a 300ml hard-anodized aluminum vessel that's wide and shallow enough to eat oatmeal or ramen out of, but still comfortable to sip coffee from, and it comes with a removable neoprene sleeve so you're not juggling a branding-iron-hot cup at 6am. At 34.5g it's aimed squarely at solo ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers who run a one-pot, one-vessel kitchen and want that vessel to pull double duty.

## Performance

This is a new-ish release, and independent long-term reviews on Reddit, BackpackingLight, or Section Hiker are basically nonexistent as of this writing — most of what's out there is Fire Maple's own product copy and a handful of unboxing videos. So take the following as an assessment based on the spec sheet, the manufacturer's own FAQ answers, and the one substantive buyer testimonial I could find, rather than a deep well of crowd-sourced trail reports.

**As a mug:** The 94mm diameter and 71mm height put it firmly in "generous coffee cup" territory rather than a tall, narrow mug. One buyer testimonial posted on Fire Maple's own site called it a <cite index="7-1,7-2,7-3">"Lightweight and durable... perfect cup of coffee size."</cite> That same reviewer flagged the one real ergonomic weak point: the bare aluminum rim gets hot right along with the rest of the cup, and they specifically recommended pairing it with a silicone lip protector because <cite index="7-9,7-10">"it's hard to complain about this cup getting so hot because that's what it's supposed to do... but the hot lips accessory is critical to me fully enjoying it."</cite> That tracks with what you'd expect from thin-walled hard-anodized aluminum — the sleeve handles your palms, but your lips are on their own.

**As a bowl:** The shallow, wide geometry is clearly the point here. Fire Maple's own FAQ pitches it as good for <cite index="11-1">coffee, oatmeal, soup, and simple solo meals</cite>, and the dimensions back that up — at 71mm tall with a wide mouth, it's noticeably more bowl-shaped than most backpacking mugs, which tend to be taller and narrower for insulation reasons. If your dinner routine is instant mashed potatoes or ramen eaten straight from the vessel, this shape works better than a traditional 12oz camp mug.

**Heat handling:** The neoprene sleeve is removable, which matters for two reasons. First, you can pull it off and set the bare aluminum cup directly over a flame — Fire Maple confirms <cite index="11-34,11-35">"Yes, but remove the neoprene sleeve first. The aluminum cup itself is safe to place over a stove flame,"</cite> so in a pinch it can function as a secondary pot for reheating or melting snow, not just a passive vessel. Second, the sleeve can be swapped or replaced independently if it wears out, without junking the whole cup. Neoprene insulation isn't going to compete with a double-wall titanium mug for heat retention, but it does what it's designed to do: make the outside touchable while the inside stays hot.

**Packability:** This is where the Petrel ecosystem angle shows up. Fire Maple designed the cup to <cite index="11-25">fit inside compatible Petrel pots and compact cooking setups</cite>, specifically nesting into the <cite index="3-12,3-13">G2 and Ramen pots, though not perfectly — they recommend the G3 pot and Quickboil Pro system for the best fit</cite>. If you're already running Fire Maple's G3 or Quickboil Pro, this cup slots into your kit for basically zero added pack volume. If you're pairing it with a different brand's pot, expect it to just be a standalone item you stuff a spoon into.

**Weight:** 34.5g is genuinely competitive in the ultralight drinkware category — it's in the same range as popular titanium options, and lighter than most enamelware or hard plastic cups of similar capacity, though it doesn't beat the very lightest bare-bones titanium cups that skip a sleeve entirely and just accept the burnt-fingers tradeoff.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely light at 34.5g, competitive with titanium cups in this capacity range
- Wide, shallow shape works well as an actual eating bowl, not just a mug
- Removable neoprene sleeve means it's stove-safe when needed and washable/replaceable independent of the cup
- Nests neatly into the Petrel G3/Quickboil Pro ecosystem for zero-volume packing
- 3-year warranty from Fire Maple, which is unusually generous for a $15–20 accessory

**Cons**

- Hot rim: the sleeve protects your hands but not your lips — expect to blow on it or sip carefully with boiling water inside
- Independent, long-term user reviews are scarce right now, so durability over months of trail use is largely unverified
- Best packing efficiency is tied to owning other Petrel-series cookware; it's a fine standalone cup with other brands' pots but loses its nesting advantage
- Hard-anodized aluminum, while tough, will show scratches and dings over time more visibly than titanium

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for solo ultralight hikers who want one vessel instead of a separate mug and bowl, especially if you're already running (or considering) Fire Maple's Petrel G3 or Quickboil Pro cook system. It's also a reasonable pickup for anyone who just wants a wide-mouthed, packable coffee cup that can double as a ramen bowl without adding meaningful weight to a kit.

## Verdict

The Petrel Cup-Bowl does exactly what it says: a light, dual-purpose vessel that nests well with Fire Maple's own cookware and handles hot liquids reasonably comfortably, provided you're careful with the rim. Given the lack of extensive independent trail reviews so far, I'd call this a solid, low-risk buy for the price rather than a must-have — 7.5/10, with points held back mainly for the unprotected rim and the still-thin body of user feedback.
