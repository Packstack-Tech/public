## Overview

<cite index="2-8">
  The Osprey Talon 33 is a lightweight pack with an adjustable torso length
  that's good for extended day hiking, peakbagging, and multi-sport use.
</cite>
<cite index="12-7,12-8">
  It's a streamlined top-loader for light-and-fast overnights or committing day
  trips, built around an injection-molded AirScape backpanel and a
  continuous-wrap harness and hipbelt that allow the pack to move with you.
</cite>
It sits squarely in the "heavy day hiker or ultralight overnighter" category —
not a true ultralight pack, but one of the more capable and comfortable options
at 33 liters.

## Performance

**Comfort and Fit**

This is where the Talon 33 earns its reputation. <cite index="8-3,8-4">With all the qualities of a multiday pack shrunk down to just 33 liters, it was one of the cushiest daypacks tested by _Backpacker_ in 2025, and its comfort and high degree of adjustability won it best-in-test honors.</cite>

The big story on the updated version is the torso adjustment system. <cite index="8-14,8-15,8-16">The pack offers a whopping five inches of adjustable length — a full inch more than previous editions and more than any other daypack _Backpacker_ tested that year. The system does add weight, but it allows Osprey to make just one version, which helps keep costs down. Testers ranging from 5'4" to 5'8" were able to dial in their perfect fit.</cite> I want to flag what reviewers consistently say about this: <cite index="18-29,18-30,18-31">fit matters more with the Talon 33 than most people expect, because it's a relatively lightweight, close-fitting pack where even small sizing issues can show up quickly once loaded. If you can, try it on with some weight inside rather than judging it empty on a shop floor.</cite>

<cite index="8-17">
  Behind the adjustable backpanel sits a stiff plastic framesheet, molded to
  follow the curves of the spine and integrated into the hipbelt for more
  effective load transfer.
</cite>
<cite index="8-18">
  One tester was able to carry 24 pounds of snacks and gear on a 43-mile
  hut-to-hut trek in the Italian Dolomites without issue.
</cite>
That said,
<cite index="2-5">Osprey's officially recommended max load is 20 lbs</cite> —
worth respecting for long days.

**Ventilation**

<cite index="11-24,11-25,11-26">
  The AirScape suspension system gives you a close-to-body, stable carry without
  turning your back into a swamp — something you really start to appreciate on
  long hikes in warmer climates. It's one of the better ventilation setups in
  this size range.
</cite>
<cite index="8-24,8-25">
  Even after trail-running in Colorado's Flatirons, one tester's back was barely
  damp — credit goes to a set of horizontal cutouts behind a translucent mesh
  panel.
</cite>
It's not a suspended-mesh trampoline back like the Osprey Exos, so some contact
with your back is inevitable, but the channeling does real work.

**Organization and Pockets**

<cite index="16-9,16-10">
  The Talon 33 is a top-loading pack with a large top lid pocket, including a
  hidden mesh pocket under the lid with a key fob — a classic Osprey design that
  provides convenient access to maps, snacks, and daytime essentials without
  opening the main compartment.
</cite>

<cite index="16-11,16-12,16-13">
  There are two large hip belt pockets that close with zippers. They're big
  enough to store a large smartphone, point-and-shoot camera, hats, gloves, and
  snacks.
</cite>
<cite index="8-10">
  The dorsal shove-it sleeve felt a touch narrow to some testers, but fit a rain
  shell and light fleece in a pinch.
</cite>

The one pocket that consistently frustrates users is the side bottle holders. <cite index="2-18">Water bottles stored in the side pockets are only accessible through the tops and are not reachable while the pack is worn.</cite> If you rely on a water bottle rather than a reservoir, plan to stop and take the pack off every time you hydrate. <cite index="2-19,2-20">The external hydration pocket is located behind the shoulder harness — not inside the main bag — which makes it easier to pull out the reservoir to refill and eliminates the risk of a leak drowning your gear.</cite>

**Trail and Technical Features**

<cite index="16-22,16-23,16-24">
  The Talon 33 comes with dual ice axe loops including elastic shaft holders,
  making it useful for ice climbing, ski mountaineering, and winter hiking above
  treeline. Osprey's Stow-on-the-Go trekking pole holder system lets you tuck
  your poles along the side of the pack without removing it.
</cite>
For a daypack, that's a genuinely impressive feature set.

**Durability and Weather Resistance**

<cite index="21-7">
  The pack is constructed with bluesign®-approved recycled high-tenacity nylon —
  a sustainable build that's tough and durable.
</cite>
<cite index="9-14,9-15,9-16">
  After more than two years of testing including a summit of Kilimanjaro, one
  reviewer noted the pack barely showed signs of wear, calling it "tougher than
  it looks."
</cite>

Weather resistance is a known limitation. <cite index="18-7,18-8,18-9">The fabric does have a degree of water resistance and will handle light rain or a quick shower, but this is not a waterproof pack, and it doesn't come with a rain cover included.</cite> For serious wet weather, pack dry bags inside or budget for a separate rain cover.

**Warranty**

<cite index="10-28,10-29">
  Osprey's All Mighty Guarantee covers repair or replacement of any damage or
  defect for any reason, for free, spanning every generation of gear back to
  1974.
</cite>
<cite index="7-12">
  At least one REI reviewer received a brand-new replacement after 13 years of
  use.
</cite>
In a world of limited warranties and asterisks, this still stands out.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Best-in-class torso adjustability at 5 inches — one pack fits a wide range of body sizes
- AirScape suspension is genuinely comfortable under real loads, even at 20+ lbs
- Dual ice axe loops and Stow-on-the-Go pole attachment add real alpine utility
- Generously sized hip belt pockets hold a full-size smartphone and snacks
- bluesign®-approved recycled nylon construction; durable for the weight class
- Osprey's All Mighty Guarantee is the best warranty in the business

**Cons**

- 1262 g (2 lbs 12.5 oz) is substantial — dedicated ultralight packs at this volume weigh half as much
- Side water bottle pockets are completely inaccessible while wearing the pack
- No rain cover included at a $205 price point
- Top lid is fixed (non-detachable), adding weight you can't shed on shorter trips
- Shoulder strap stretch pocket is barely snack-bar sized — not phone or GPS compatible
- 33L is a realistic ceiling of 1-2 nights; three-day trips will require strict discipline

## Who Should Buy This

The Talon 33 is the right call for hikers who push long, demanding days and want a single pack that can double as an ultralight overnight bag without feeling like a compromise. <cite index="4-24,4-25">It's designed for those moving fast and packing smaller loads across mountain biking, trail running, fastpacking, peak bagging, and adventure racing.</cite> If you're a base-layer-and-snacks day hiker who wants something bombproof and comfortable up to 20 lbs, this is an easy recommendation. If you're an ounce-counter looking to shave grams on a sub-10-lb base weight setup, look at frameless or semi-frameless alternatives — the Talon carries weight well, but it costs grams to do it.

## Verdict

The Osprey Talon 33 is one of the most refined and feature-complete packs in its volume class, and the 2025 update pushed it further with improved adjustability, recycled fabrics, and an Extended Fit option. The carry comfort and torso-fit system are genuinely best-in-class for a 33L daypack. The main trade-off is weight: at 1262 g, it's a full-featured hiking pack, not an ultralight one. If that trade-off fits your style, it's hard to beat.

**Rating: 8/10**
