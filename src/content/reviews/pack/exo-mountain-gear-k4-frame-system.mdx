## Overview

The K4 Frame System is the chassis of Exo Mountain Gear's modular pack line: a USA-built external frame with custom carbon fiber stays, a plastic-backed hip belt and shoulder harness, and a load shelf that accepts four interchangeable bags from 2,200 to 7,200 cubic inches. It was designed for backcountry hunters who need to carry a 70-pound kit in and a 100-pound elk quarter out. The trade-off is plain: a frame that weighs about 3 lb and costs $384.99 before a bag is added makes no sense for a 15-pound thru-hiking base weight, but it is very good at what it was built for.

## Performance

Comfort under load is where the K4 earns its reputation. Rokslide's reviewer, who had used earlier Exo frames, called it the most comfortable pack he had tested after more than a month and hundreds of miles of carrying heavy loads. He said it seems to lock onto the hips and stay put. Owners on the Rokslide forum say the same thing in different words: weight feels like it lands on the hips and legs instead of the lower back and shoulders, the belt doesn't slip, and a slim 6'4", 160 lb user reported that 70 to 80 lb felt fine on a pack where others turned miserable past 60. One owner who has used both frames offered a useful tip: a pack's comfort only shows up past 100 lb, because many packs feel alike at 50.

The design explains some of this. The hip belt's plastic backbone spreads weight around the hips and reduces pressure points. The carbon stays are a clear departure from the older K3 frame. Backwoods Pursuit's one-year test noted that the K4 frame is considerably narrower and more tapered than the K3. Owners also point to better head clearance than earlier Exo designs. Carryology, assessing the whole system, argued that above roughly 60 lb an external frame beats an internal-frame pack on comfort, and that the weight penalty is worth accepting at that point.

Weight is respectable for the job. Backwoods Pursuit weighed the frame alone at 2 lb 15 oz. Paired with the 5000 bag (2 lb 6 oz), the complete system came to 5 lb 13 oz. Carryology listed the 2200 pack system at 4 lb 9 oz and the 3600 at 5 lb 8 oz. Owners who switched from Kifaru report the Exo is noticeably lighter while carrying equally well.

Fit is the other strength. The K4 combines four frame heights, five hip belt sizes and micro-adjustable harness and load-lifter positions. Owners who visited Exo's Montana shop describe being fitted with 50 lb in the pack. Complaints are few and mostly specific to the bags: one owner found the narrow bottom of the main bag awkward for packing a bulky sleeping bag, and another said a lumbar issue under load was solved when Exo made a custom lumbar pad. Another owner agreed that the tallest 26.5" frame carries load better.

Build quality gets consistent praise. Forum owners comparing the K4 with Stone Glacier's frames describe the Exo's laser-cut finish and stitching as more refined, and one calls the shoulder strap foam stiffer in a good way.

## Choosing a Version

The frame comes in four heights (22", 23.5", 25", 26.5") and five hip belt sizes. Frame height should match torso length, since it sets where the load shelf and harness sit relative to the shoulders. The belt size is chosen separately to match the hips. Taller frames add torso range, and owners say the 26.5" carries heavy loads better, though that is for tall torsos, not a general upgrade. Because the two choices are independent, a tall, narrow hiker and a short, stocky one can both end up well fitted. That flexibility is the main selling point.

The bag decision comes second. The 2,200 and 3,600 cu in bags suit short or ultralight hunts, and Exo's retail partners position the 3600 for trips of three to four days. The 5000 is the most popular, while the 7200 is for long, cold-weather or multi-animal trips. Owners packing a bulky winter kit report the 5000 gets tight beyond a week.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional heavy-load comfort, with reviewers comparing it favorably to Kifaru and Stone Glacier
- Four frame heights and five hip belt sizes, plus micro-adjustments
- Carbon stays keep the frame at about 3 lb with a 150 lb+ rating
- One frame works with four bags from 2,200 to 7,200 cu in
- Built in the USA, with a reputation for clean construction and responsive service

**Cons**

- At roughly 3 lb, the frame alone outweighs many complete ultralight packs
- $384.99 before a bag; full systems run $649.99 to $729.99
- Overbuilt for typical thru-hiking loads
- Some owners find the main bag shape narrow at the bottom for bulky sleeping bags
- Fit depends on picking the right frame and belt size, with no in-store try-on for most buyers

## Who Should Buy This

Hunters, game packers and anyone who regularly hauls 60 lb or more over rough country. It also suits owners of an existing K4 bag who want to adjust their fit or replace a frame. Thru-hikers carrying 20 to 30 lb total should skip it and look at a frameless or light internal-frame pack, which will be far lighter and cheaper.

## Verdict

The K4 Frame System is a very good load hauler. It's comfortable, adjustable and well made, and its weight is reasonable for what it carries. It isn't an ultralight thru-hiking pack, and its price and heft only make sense if you regularly carry heavy loads.
