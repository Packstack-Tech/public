## Overview

The DH Camping Stove 205 is a folding stainless steel gas stove from Dhaulagiri, an Indonesian outdoor brand better known for trekking poles, tents and cook sets. It is built for car camps, base camps and group trips where a big pan needs a stable seat. It is not built for anyone counting grams: at 1.3 kg, it weighs more than most complete ultralight cook systems, stove, pot and fuel included.

## Performance

**Weight and pack size.** The number that matters is 1,300 g. A typical backpacking canister-top stove weighs a small fraction of that, and the 205 carries about as much as a loaded day pack's worth of water. Folded, it measures 27.5 x 8 x 10.5 cm, a flat brick about the size of a hardback paperback. That slots easily into a car-camping bin or a duffel. It is awkward in a frameless pack, where a rigid 27.5 cm slab will find your spine.

**Stability and pan support.** This is the stove's reason to exist. Unfolded, it spreads to 34 cm deep, which gives the pan holder a wide footprint. The wide holder is meant for frying pans, large pots and group cookware that would tip off a small three-prong stove. Anti-slip rubber feet grip the table or tailgate, and a magnetic lock keeps the folded halves closed in transit instead of rattling open in the bag.

**Flame and controls.** Dhaulagiri markets the burner as a blue, high-output flame. It has an adjustable valve and an onboard ignitor, so lighting and simmering need no lighter. The maker lists a gas-container safety feature to manage canister heat during use. It does not publish a power rating in watts or a boil time, so burn-rate comparisons with Jetboil, MSR or Soto stoves can't be made from the spec sheet.

**Wind and fuel efficiency.** The 205 has no integrated windscreen and no heat exchanger. Dhaulagiri sells a separate windproof model, the CS 203, for that job. Anyone cooking outdoors in a breeze will want a screen of their own, and fuel economy will lag behind pot-integrated systems.

**Ownership and value.** Buyers on Indonesian marketplaces rate it highly. One Lazada listing shows a 4.9 average, and Tokopedia shows a perfect score on a handful of ratings. The review pool is small and skewed toward casual campers, not long-distance hikers. At Rp 550,000 it sits above Dhaulagiri's simpler stoves, such as the CS 202 at Rp 145,000, so the premium buys width, build and fold-flat storage, not weight savings.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide folding pan support suits large pots and frying pans
- Anti-slip feet and a magnetic lock add stability and tidy transport
- Adjustable burner with a built-in ignitor
- Folds to a slim 8 cm profile
- Stainless steel construction is sturdy and easy to wipe clean
- Carry bag included

**Cons**

- 1,300 g is several times the weight of a backpacking stove
- Rigid 27.5 cm body suits vehicle storage better than a pack
- No published output or boil-time figures
- No integrated windscreen
- Primarily sold through Indonesian retailers and marketplaces, so shipping and canister compatibility abroad need checking

## Who Should Buy This

Car campers, overlanders, basecamp cooks and group trips in Indonesia and Southeast Asia who want a stable, folding gas stove for real pans. It suits anyone who has watched a skillet slide off a tripod stove at dinner. Thru-hikers, ultralight backpackers and anyone carrying the stove more than a few hundred meters from the car should look elsewhere. A canister-top stove at a fraction of the weight does the same job on trail.

## Verdict

The 205 is a sensible, sturdy table-top camp stove, and an unsuitable one for the trail. Its wide pan holder, locking fold and onboard ignitor are well chosen for basecamp cooking. For a Packstack reader, though, the 1.3 kg weight rules it out for anything you carry on your back.
