## Overview

The Black Diamond Trail is a three-section aluminum trekking pole with Dual FlickLock adjusters, an EVA foam grip with a choke-up extension, and both trekking and snow baskets in the box. It is built for hikers who want a pole they can stop thinking about, not one they have to protect. The trade-off is weight: at 481 g for the standard pair and 560 g for the Trail Back, it sits well above the carbon folding poles that dominate thru-hiker packs.

## Performance

**Locks.** The FlickLock is the reason owners keep recommending these poles. The levers sit flush with the shaft, snap shut with or without gloves, and owners report they hold under a loaded pack on long descents. Twist-lock poles on the same trips reportedly slipped. The locks are plastic, not the aluminum FlickLock Pro used on Black Diamond's pricier poles. Even so, testers at BetterTrail found them more dependable than twist-locks and the hardware on budget poles like the REI Co-op Trailmade. A small screw on each lever lets owners re-tension the clamp if it loosens. One Trailspace reviewer logged roughly 1,000 miles in six months without needing to touch it.

**Durability.** This is where the Trail earns its rating. CleverHiker named the Trail Back one of the most durable poles in its test, with cosmetic scratching but no structural damage, and its testers trusted the aluminum shafts enough to ski with them. Outdoor Gear Lab describes the design as easy to repair and built to last for decades. The reason is simple. Aluminum bends before it breaks, and a bent section can often be straightened well enough to finish the trip. Carbon tends to fracture outright, especially when a tip wedges in talus. Black Diamond sells replacement sections and tips, so one damaged shaft does not mean a new pair.

**Grip and comfort.** The EVA foam grip is serviceable but not a standout. Some users find it rough over long days, and several report it turns slick when sweaty. New poles can also leave a dark residue on the hands until the first wash. The extended foam below the handle partly makes up for this. On rolling terrain, hikers can slide down for a short climb instead of stopping to shorten the pole. Switchback Travel, which named the Trail Back its best overall pole, credits the choke-up extensions for steep or technical ground.

**Weight and packability.** The poles are heavy. Outdoor Gear Lab notes the Trail Back is heavier than many folding poles, and Black Diamond's own Distance Carbon Z weighs about half as much and packs much smaller. For a telescoping pole, though, the Standard collapses to around 23 inches. That is shorter than the 25–26 inches typical of rivals. The Trail Back collapses to about 25 inches, which CleverHiker counted among the longer collapsed lengths in its test group. Both extend to 140 cm, long enough to pitch most trekking-pole shelters.

**Four-season range.** Snow baskets come in the box, which budget competitors like the Leki Spirit Lite and REI Trailmade do not offer. That makes the Trail usable for shoulder-season hikes and snowshoeing without extra purchases.

## Choosing a Version

Both versions share the same basic design: three aluminum sections, Dual FlickLocks, EVA foam grips with extensions, and a 100–140 cm adjustment range.

- **Standard (481 g):** About 79 g (2.8 oz) lighter than the Trail Back and a couple of inches shorter when collapsed. It suits hikers who strap poles to a pack or fit them in luggage and want the lowest weight in this family.
- **Trail Back (560 g):** The heavier version, and the one most independent testers have reviewed. Outdoor Gear Lab, CleverHiker and Switchback Travel all reported on it, and REI shoppers rate it 4.5 out of 5 across 55 reviews. It suits buyers who want the proven option and don't mind the extra weight and slightly longer collapsed length.

Neither version is for gram-counters. Between the two, the lighter pair is the better pick unless the Trail Back's track record matters more to you than the weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- FlickLocks are secure under load, glove-friendly and adjustable in the field
- 7075 aluminum bends instead of snapping, and replacement parts are sold
- Foam grip extension is useful on rolling and steep terrain
- Trekking and snow baskets included
- Standard version collapses shorter than most telescoping rivals
- 100–140 cm range suits tall hikers and trekking-pole shelters

**Cons**

- 481–560 g is roughly double the weight of carbon folding poles
- EVA foam grip gets slick when sweaty and does not breathe like cork
- Plastic FlickLocks, not the aluminum Pro version
- Trail Back collapses longer than many hikers will want for pack strapping
- No shock absorption

## Who Should Buy This

These poles suit three-season and shoulder-season hikers, first-time pole buyers, and anyone who works poles hard on talus, scree or snow. They also work as shared or loaner gear because the locks are easy to learn and the shafts are forgiving. Thru-hikers watching base weight, trail runners and anyone who wants a folding pole that vanishes into a pack should look at carbon options such as the Distance Carbon Z.

## Verdict

The Trail does the basics well. It locks reliably, takes abuse and costs about $100. The weight keeps it out of the ultralight conversation, but for hikers who value toughness over grams it is one of the safer buys in the category. The Standard is the better choice for most people, and the Trail Back is for those who want the pole the major reviewers have tested.
