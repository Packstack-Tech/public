## Overview

The XA Pro 3D V8 is Salomon's long-running trail runner/hiking hybrid, now in its eighth major revision. It's built for hikers and backpackers who want boot-like stability and rock protection without the weight or break-in time of a traditional hiking boot, and it's just as popular with trail runners logging technical miles as it is with thru-hikers using it as their primary footwear.

## Performance

**Traction and stability.** This is where the V8 earns its reputation. The Contagrip MA outsole with the "Premium Wet Traction" compound is genuinely confidence-inspiring on wet rock and loose scree — one long-term user <cite index="14-6,14-7">noted the XA Pro version provides optimum stability on mixed and rocky terrain, with rugged stability and durability coming from the Contagrip traction and low-profile chassis combined</cite>. That stability comes from the 3D Advanced Chassis, a plastic cradle wrapping the heel and midfoot, and it does its job: multiple reviewers independently landed on the same word to describe the ride — stiff. Adventure Alan's team found <cite index="12-1,12-2">the rubber soles excellent for off-trail and tricky terrain, with midsole design providing extra stability that makes the shoe feel stiffer than many similar shoes, intentionally, to keep feet secure during long days</cite>.

**Fit — read this before you buy.** The V8's biggest change from prior generations is a lighter, thinner upper, and that shift hasn't been universally loved. A Zappos reviewer who'd worn multiple generations found <cite index="11-9,11-10,11-11,11-12,11-13,11-14">the shoe significantly lighter than the previous version and breathing really well, but because of the lighter materials it doesn't fit the same way — a size that was 10.5 in the old model now feels like an 11, so sizing down is worth considering</cite>. More concerning is a report from a long-distance hiker who'd logged years and thousands of miles in prior XA Pro 3D versions: on the V8 they found <cite index="11-26,11-28,11-29,11-30,11-31">this new version caused a problem on top of the foot they'd never experienced before, feeling every step on the Tahoe Rim Trail, with the only fix being to lace it only very loosely — which works on-trail but not for off-trail walking, the shoe's actual strength</cite>. That's a pattern worth taking seriously if you have a higher instep or have loved older XA Pros — try before you commit to a long trip.

**Durability and weather resistance.** Salomon's build quality track record shows up in most of the feedback, with several buyers on their third or later pair of XA Pros over 15+ years praising their durability and balance of stability and cushion. That said, durability isn't universal — one reviewer reported the upper starting to separate after seven months of use, so expect some batch-to-batch or usage-pattern variability rather than bulletproof consistency. On the non-GTX version, breathability is solid and dries fast; multiple reviewers who covered long distances (including one who walked nearly 500 km of the Camino) reported the shoes staying comfortable and drying quickly even after crossing muddy sections. RunRepeat's aggregated user sentiment on the GTX version lines up with this: <cite index="10-1,10-2">people are generally happy with the shoe because it offers confident traction, a secure and waterproof wrap, and steady underfoot experience</cite>, though <cite index="10-5">the stiffness of the platform and the restrictive upper are criticized</cite>.

**Weight and ride.** At 363 g it's not ultralight by trail-runner standards — Backpackers.com called it <cite index="13-5">"on the heavier side for true trail runners"</cite> — but it's dramatically lighter than a leather boot, and the 11 mm drop with EnergyCell midsole gives a stack that's cushioned enough for road-to-trail transitions without feeling mushy underfoot.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent wet-rock and loose-terrain traction from the Contagrip MA outsole
- 3D chassis delivers genuine ankle/midfoot stability for a shoe in this weight class
- Fast-drying, breathable mesh on the non-GTX version
- Good track record for long-term durability across generations
- QuickLace system is fast on and off at camp

**Cons**

- Narrower, lower-volume upper than previous XA Pro generations — sizing runs differently
- Stiffer ride than most trail runners; some feet (especially higher insteps) report pressure/discomfort on top of the foot
- At 363 g it's heavier than minimalist trail-runner competitors
- Isolated durability complaints (upper separation) suggest some batch inconsistency

## Who Should Buy This

This shoe fits hikers and backpackers transitioning away from traditional boots who still want a stable, structured platform — think rocky alpine terrain, rim-to-rim day hikes, or long-distance trails where you're carrying a moderate pack (sub-30 lb) and want boot-like security without boot-like weight. It's also a strong pick for trail runners who overpronate or want extra stability on technical routes. If you have a wide or high-volume foot, or you loved the roomier fit of older XA Pro models, try these on in person first — the V8's slimmer upper is the most polarizing change in this generation.

## Verdict

The XA Pro 3D V8 delivers on Salomon's core promise: confident traction and stability on rough terrain in a package far lighter than a hiking boot. It's not the shoe for minimalist-fit lovers or anyone chasing the lowest possible trail-runner weight, and the tighter, thinner V8 upper is a real fit change from earlier generations that's worth trying before you buy for a big trip. For hikers who want a stable, durable, weather-resistant workhorse and don't mind a stiffer ride, it earns a solid 7.5/10.
