## Overview

The Leatherman Signal is the brand's dedicated "outdoor survival" multitool — a 19-in-1 built around a core of full-size pliers and a 420HC drop-point blade, then padded out with camp-oriented extras like a ferro rod, diamond sharpener, and emergency whistle. It's aimed at car campers, overlanders, and hikers who want one tool that covers mechanical repairs and basic wilderness-survival needs, rather than dedicated ultralight thru-hikers counting grams.

## Performance

At 7.5 oz and 4.5 inches closed, the Signal sits in an odd middle ground — noticeably heavier and bulkier than a Leatherman Skeletool or Squirt, but still small enough to belt-carry or toss in a hip belt pocket. One Bob Vila reviewer who carried it for a month <cite index="11-2">found it comfortable to belt-carry or tote in a backpack as a counterpart to a pocket knife.</cite> That's really the right frame for this tool: it's a backup/survival kit in your pocket, not a lightweight EDC blade.

The pliers and wire cutters are standard Leatherman quality — sturdy, well-aligned, and the part of the tool most owners praise without reservation. The 420HC blade arrives reasonably sharp and, per long-term users, <cite index="11-16">the knife and saw blades arrived sharp, and they have held their edges very well after all kinds of cutting.</cite> The included diamond sharpener is a nice touch for touch-ups in the field, though it's slow going on a heavily used edge.

Where the Signal earns its name is the survival-kit layer bolted onto a standard multitool chassis: the ferro rod throws a solid shower of sparks, the whistle is loud enough to be genuinely useful for signaling, and the hammer head — while clearly an afterthought — does the job of pounding in tent stakes. One reviewer confirmed <cite index="11-14">the shape of the hammer head did a fine job installing tent anchors</cite>, though it also <cite index="11-14,11-15">prevents using the bit driver or can opener with the handles closed, which is a little inconvenient to deal with on a regular basis</cite>.

Durability is mostly solid but not bulletproof. A five-year owner who used the Signal on hurricane relief deployments reported that <cite index="15-1,15-2">a tiny part on the tip of the primary knife chipped off while slicing open packages of bottled water, and cutting through the plastic wrapping became noticeably different after the tip broke off</cite>. The same reviewer also had the belt clip snap off, and credits Leatherman's warranty service for making it right — <cite index="15-22,15-23">Leatherman replaced the blade and belt clip and returned it, with the knife looking brand new</cite>. That 25-year warranty is worth factoring into the price; Leatherman clearly stands behind the tool even when parts fail.

Not everyone comes away impressed with fit and finish. Reviews on Walmart and REI flag inconsistent quality control — one buyer reported <cite index="13-1,13-2">fit and finish wasn't typical Leatherman, with a locking mechanism that was stiff and difficult to use</cite>, and another on REI's site said <cite index="12-2,12-3">the pliers came scratched at the base, with paint rubbing off the handles onto the pliers, feeling like cheap garbage</cite>. These complaints aren't universal, but they show up often enough to suggest some batch-to-batch variance, especially on the painted color variants versus the DLC black finish.

A couple of usability gripes worth flagging before you buy: there's no printed manual, and the tool-opening sequence for some of the less obvious implements isn't intuitive out of the box — <cite index="12-18,12-19">one reviewer had to watch a YouTube video to figure out how to open or close several of the tools, since there was no instruction manual</cite>. And if you're hoping for scissors, you won't find them — a notable omission on a tool this size, according to at least one long-term reviewer.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Full-size pliers and wire cutters that feel like a "real" multitool, not a keychain gadget
- Genuinely useful survival add-ons: loud whistle, functional ferro rod, onboard diamond sharpener
- 420HC blade holds an edge well through hard use
- Leatherman's 25-year warranty backs up hard failures (chipped blade tips, snapped clips)
- Hammer head actually works for tent stakes in a pinch

**Cons**
- At 7.5 oz / 212.6 g, it's heavy for anyone counting grams — this is a car-camping or basecamp tool, not a fastpacking one
- Reported fit-and-finish inconsistency: scratched pliers, chipping paint on some units
- Hammer head geometry blocks the bit driver and can opener when handles are closed
- No scissors
- No printed manual; some tools aren't intuitive to open the first time

## Who Should Buy This

The Signal makes the most sense for car campers, overlanders, and disaster-preparedness kits where the whistle, ferro rod, and hammer genuinely get used, and where 7.5 oz doesn't register on a base weight spreadsheet. If you're a mileage-focused thru-hiker, this isn't your tool — a Leatherman Squirt PS4 or a simple razor blade covers 90% of on-trail repair needs at a fraction of the weight. But for weekend trips, vehicle kits, or as a glovebox/emergency-bag staple, the Signal's blend of mechanical and survival tools is hard to match in one package.

## Verdict

The Signal is a well-conceived survival multitool let down slightly by inconsistent build quality on some units — the core pliers/knife/wire-cutter combo is genuinely good, and the ferro rod and whistle aren't gimmicks. It's not an ultralight pick, but for basecamp, car camping, or emergency-kit duty backed by Leatherman's 25-year warranty, it earns a 6.5/10.
