## Overview

The Minimus Lite is Montane's stripped-down rain shell for people who move fast and carry little: trail runners, fastpackers and ultralight hikers who want a waterproof layer that disappears into a hip belt. At 215 g it is built around a 20-denier Pertex Shield fabric and a short feature list. The trade-off is plain: excellent breathability and packability, but a thin face fabric that is not meant for hours of standing around in a storm.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The 20,000 mm rating and fully taped seams, zips and pockets put the Minimus Lite in credible waterproof territory, and reviewers largely confirm it on the trail. One tester logged a 20-mile trail run in torrential rain without complaint. Others describe a jacket that handles moderate rain, short downpours and snow well, but that wets out on the surface in sustained heavy rain. The membrane keeps water out, though. Testers describe ending up damp rather than soaked, with the sleeves clinging to the body once the outer fabric saturates. The non-fluorocarbon DWR is the usual weak point on a jacket this thin; it beads water early on and needs periodic reproofing to keep the face fabric from flooding.

**Breathability.** This is where the jacket earns its place. The 20,000 g/m² MVTR figure is high for a rain shell, and wearers who keep moving report that the jacket manages sweat well on climbs and in steady rain. It is not a miracle: one tester on a fast uphill run still overheated. Because the only ventilation is the open hand pockets, which are less effective than pit zips, the jacket is best treated as an on-and-off layer, not something to wear all day. Climbing Gear Reviews likened it to a beefed-up windshell, which is a fair read of how it behaves.

**Fabric feel and fit.** Pertex Shield in this weight is soft and fairly quiet, and the 2.5-layer construction avoids the crinkly plastic feel of many ultralight shells. Reviewers who compared it with Gore-Tex Active found the Pertex softer and quieter. The cut is trim and stretches for running motion, and testers report room for a light midlayer but not a puffy underneath. It sits well beneath a running vest or pack straps, and the articulated sleeves move without riding up. One caveat: a tester on the women's version found the arms short, and reviewers have asked for fully elasticated cuffs.

**Packability and weight.** Montane quotes 215 g; Climbing Gear Reviews put its sample at 224 g on a scale. Either way it rolls down small enough to hold in two hands and fits into a waist pack. That is the point of the jacket. At this weight, it is easy to justify carrying as a mandatory-kit or "just in case" shell.

**Durability.** The thin 20D face fabric is the question mark. TrekSumo's tester found abrasion resistance better than expected, with robust seam taping and stitching, and judged it fine for rocky trails and repeated stuffing into packs. Others caution that it is less durable than heavier shells, and that long-term wear is still unproven. Pack straps rubbing on the shoulders over a long thru-hike will be harder on it than a few weeks of running. Montane backs its products against faults in workmanship and materials for life.

**Features.** The hood has three points of adjustment and holds its shape, which adds some structure to an otherwise minimal jacket. There are two zipped hand pockets and no chest pocket, a frequent request from runners who want to stash a phone.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 215 g and packs to a very small bundle
- High breathability for a waterproof shell
- Fully taped seams, zips and pockets
- Soft, stretchy, relatively quiet fabric
- Structured, adjustable, helmet-compatible hood
- Lifetime guarantee against faults in workmanship and materials

**Cons**

- Surface wets out in sustained heavy rain, leaving sleeves clinging
- Thin 20D fabric is less durable than heavier shells
- No pit zips; pocket venting is modest
- No chest pocket; cuffs are not fully elasticated
- Trim fit leaves little room for insulation underneath
- Pricey at £185 / $235 for a shell with this much compromise

## Who Should Buy This

Trail runners, fastpackers and fast-and-light hikers who want a rain jacket they will actually carry every day, and who mostly use it while moving, will get the most from it. It also suits summer and shoulder-season trips where rain is intermittent and the shell spends more time in the pack than on the body. Hikers expecting days of steady rain, anyone who wants to layer insulation beneath it, or anyone who plans to sit in camp wearing it should look at a heavier, roomier shell with pit zips and a tougher face fabric.

## Verdict

The Minimus Lite does what Montane says it will: it delivers real waterproofing and good breathability at 215 g, in a soft fabric that moves well. It is a specialist's jacket, not an all-weather workhorse, and the price is steep for that. For runners and ultralight hikers who prioritize weight and airflow, it is a strong choice.
