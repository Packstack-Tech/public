## Overview

The Featherstone UL Granite 2P is a freestanding, double-wall 3-season tent aimed at backpackers who want a lightweight-ish shelter without paying cottage-industry prices. It's built around a single hubbed aluminum pole, 20D sil-nylon fabrics, and a no-see-um mesh canopy — the same basic recipe you'll find in tents costing two to three times as much. This one's for beginners building out a kit, budget-conscious weekend backpackers, and anyone who wants a "real" backpacking tent without spending $400+.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** At 2090 g packaged (or 1220 g if you leave the stuff sacks, stakes, and footprint at home), this isn't going to turn heads at an r/Ultralight gear list swap, but it's competitive with mid-tier 2P doubles-wall tents from bigger brands, and noticeably lighter than most car-camping-grade tents in this price range. The packed size of 16 x 4 in is compact enough to strap under most 50-65L packs without a fight.

**Setup.** This is where the Granite earns its keep. The single hubbed pole design means you're not fumbling with multiple pole sections in the dark — <cite index="9-5">durable 7001 aluminum poles are pre-attached with a hub system for fast, foolproof setup and a stable, freestanding frame</cite>. Several owners echoed this in their own words, describing the setup as extremely easy and something they could manage solo without much trouble, and both ends of the tent are functionally identical so there's no guessing which side the fly goes on.

**Weather resistance.** For a budget tent, the Granite has held up better than expected in the field. One long-term owner reported the tent surviving <cite index="8-1,8-2">at least eight setups, half of those with weather, without a drop getting inside, and the tent still looking damn near brand new</cite> years later. Another described the tent withstanding a wind storm with gusts up to 50 mph and staying dry through a subsequent rainstorm, though I'd treat any specific wind-speed claim from a single anecdotal test with some skepticism — that's not something Featherstone or most reviewers can consistently verify. What's more reliably corroborated across multiple reviews is that the tent sheds light-to-moderate rain and wind without leaking, though nobody in the reviews I found had pushed it through a genuinely nasty multi-day storm. Older reviews (some dating back several years, before Featherstone appears to have upgraded the floor coating) mentioned the floor material felt thin and not fully waterproof, so if you're buying new stock with the current 2000mm PU-coated floor, that older complaint may no longer apply — but it's worth pitching on a groundsheet or your own footprint if you're pitching on anything rocky or abrasive.

**Livability.** This is the tent's strongest selling point. Reviewers consistently call it roomy for a 2P — one comparison review argued it offers <cite index="4-1">significantly more volume than the NEMO Hornet OSMO 2P</cite>. The dual doors and dual vestibules (65 cm deep) mean two people don't have to crawl over each other to get in or out, and there's enough vestibule space to stash boots and a pack out of the rain.

**Ventilation.** The no-see-um mesh canopy combined with vents under the fly's lower lip gives reasonable airflow — several owners specifically praised the ventilation, though as a single-wall-mesh-plus-fly design, expect some condensation on cold, still mornings, as with virtually any tent in this class.

**Minor gripes.** A couple of recurring nitpicks: the included guylines are on the short side for fully staking out every tie-out point, and at least one reviewer bent a stake in rocky ground (it bent back fine, but it's not a confidence-inspiring stake). Neither is a dealbreaker, but budget an extra $10-15 for better stakes and a length of paracord if you plan to use this somewhere consistently windy.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely fast, foolproof freestanding setup thanks to the hubbed pole
- Roomy interior and dual vestibules for a 2P tent at this weight class
- 20D sil-nylon and real aluminum poles — materials well above what you'd expect at this price
- Multiple configurations (full setup, fly + footprint only, mesh-only) for different trips
- Strong anecdotal durability track record, including multi-year owners reporting no leaks

**Cons**

- Not actually "ultralight" by strict UL standards — it's a lightweight budget tent, not a sub-2-lb shelter
- Stock stakes and guylines are underwhelming; plan to upgrade both
- Only 3-season; owners note it runs cold in winter conditions
- Peak height of 109 cm is on the low side — expect to duck, not stand
- Some older units reportedly had thinner, less waterproof floors (seems addressed in current spec sheet)

## Who Should Buy This

This tent makes the most sense for new backpackers assembling a first kit, budget-minded weekend hikers, or anyone who wants two full doors and vestibules without paying premium-brand prices. If you're chasing the lightest possible base weight for a long thru-hike, there are lighter (and pricier) options out there — but for car camping, section hikes, or a first few backpacking trips, the Granite 2P punches well above its price point.

## Verdict

The Featherstone UL Granite 2P isn't going to win any base-weight competitions, but it delivers a surprisingly livable, easy-to-pitch, weather-resistant shelter for a fraction of what comparable name-brand tents cost. Swap the stakes, maybe add some guyline, and it's a smart buy for anyone getting into backpacking without wanting to spend cottage-industry money on their first tent. I'd put it at a 7/10 — great value, with a few small compromises that are easy to work around.
