## Overview

Iniu's Palm-Size 10000mAh PD 45W is a pocketable battery that runs phones, tablets and other USB-C gear at up to 45 watts. It targets hikers and travelers who want one bank to cover a phone, a headlamp and the occasional tablet or handheld console. The trade-off is weight: at 180 g it is a sensible carry for a long weekend, but gram-counters on a multi-week thru-hike will find lighter ways to store the same energy.

## Performance

**Capacity per gram.** The numbers work out to roughly 0.2 Wh per gram on paper. That is respectable for a 10,000 mAh bank, and Iniu leans on a high-density cell design across its 10,000 mAh range, which the company says makes the packs 30 percent smaller and 15 percent lighter than conventional banks of the same capacity. One Slickdeals commenter who measured actual watt-hours against weight on an Iniu bank reported that it beat every similar-capacity pack he had tested, Anker's included. Owners of this class of bank typically see about two full iPhone charges, which matches Iniu's own claim for its 10,000 mAh line of up to 2.0 iPhone charges or 1.2 iPad Air charges.

**Output.** The 45 W ceiling is the real draw. Buyers of Iniu's 45 W 10,000 mAh banks report steady power into a Nintendo Switch and a Steam Deck, and one Galaxy S25+ owner confirmed Samsung's PPS super-fast-charging negotiation at 45 W. For a hiker, that means a phone tops up quickly during a lunch stop instead of hanging off a cable all afternoon. A 45 W port will also run some low-power laptops, though a 37 Wh pack will not go far doing it.

**Heat and sustained output.** Owner reports across Iniu's 45 W line are mixed on this point. Some buyers of the thinner, cheaper models say the output throttles as the pack warms, so 45 W is only a temporary figure. Others report that Iniu's more compact, higher-priced 45 W model holds 45 W from start to finish at normal temperatures. In a pack pocket in summer sun, expect output to drop before it does in a cool tent.

**Recharging.** Input is slower than output. One critical buyer of Iniu's pocket-size 45 W bank complained that it refills at about 20 W at best, which means a few hours on a wall charger or a hut outlet. Pair it with a 30 W-plus USB-C wall brick and plan on topping it up overnight rather than during a resupply lunch.

**Reliability.** Experiences vary. Several owners praise Iniu's customer service and say it replaced faulty units promptly, and the brand markets a three-year warranty on its 10,000 mAh banks. A minority report failures or cable problems, and Iniu has recalled a different 15 W 10,000 mAh model. As with any lithium pack, use a reputable cable and keep it out of a hot car.

**Dimensions.** At 11 x 7 x 1.8 cm, it is a bit larger than a deck of cards and slips into a hip-belt pocket or the side pocket of a running vest. The 1.8 cm thickness is on the chunky side beside Iniu's slimmer 0.5-inch models, but it keeps the bank pocketable and easy to fit into a pack's lid.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 45 W USB-C output handles phones, tablets and handheld consoles
- 10,000 mAh covers roughly two phone charges
- Pocket-size footprint, 11 x 7 x 1.8 cm
- Iniu's brand-wide service reputation is generally good among owners

**Cons**

- 180 g is heavy against ultralight-oriented 10,000 mAh banks; Iniu's own Carry P50 is listed at 5.6 oz (about 159 g), roughly 21 g lighter
- The $49.99 list price is steep next to Iniu's own 45 W 10,000 mAh models, which sell for $27 to $35 and are often discounted further
- Slower input than output
- Sustained 45 W may throttle when the pack heats up
- Not waterproof, so it needs a bag in wet conditions; Iniu's Outdoor Rugged 10000 is the IP67 option

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend and section hikers, and anyone who wants one bank that can charge a phone on the trail and a tablet or handheld at camp or the hotel. It also suits travelers who want a flight-friendly capacity with real fast-charge speed. Thru-hikers who only need to keep a phone and headlamp alive should look at lighter dedicated ultralight banks. Hikers who want weather protection should step up to the rugged Iniu model, and anyone shopping on price should look at the cheaper 45 W Iniu banks.

## Verdict

The Iniu Palm-Size 10000mAh PD 45W delivers what the spec sheet promises: fast, flexible USB-C output in a package that fits in a hand. At 180 g and $49.99, though, it is neither the lightest nor the cheapest way to carry 37 Wh, and Iniu's own lineup undercuts it on both counts. Buy it if the 45 W output matters; otherwise a lighter or cheaper 10,000 mAh bank does the job.
