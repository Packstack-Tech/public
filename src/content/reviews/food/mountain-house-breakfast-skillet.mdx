## Overview

The Mountain House Breakfast Skillet is a freeze-dried pouch of hash browns, scrambled egg, crumbled pork sausage, peppers and onions, rehydrated with boiling water in under 10 minutes. It has been on trail-food shelves for years and remains one of the brand's better-known breakfasts. The trade-off is familiar: easy, hearty and dishwashing-free, but salty, and the eggs can be temperamental.

## Performance

**Calorie density.** At 520 calories in 105 g, the pouch works out to roughly 5 calories per gram. That is respectable for a freeze-dried hot meal, though it sits below what a stack of nuts, olive oil or a tortilla-and-peanut-butter breakfast delivers per gram. The 28 g of protein in the full pouch is the stronger number. Eaten by one person, it is a real breakfast. Split between two, each serving is closer to a side dish.

**Portions.** The pouch is labeled as two servings, and a Trailspace reviewer found the 4-ounce package could stretch to two people if paired with toast, a tortilla or a bagel and coffee. For a solo hiker burning calories on a long day, the whole pouch is the sensible portion.

**Flavor.** Reviewers describe a savory, well-spiced breakfast with a noticeable smoke note. Treeline Review's testers called it a repeated favorite on long section hikes. A Trailspace tester compared it to the inside of a breakfast burrito and suggested a tortilla and hot sauce for anyone who can spare the weight. Opinion is not unanimous: some buyers have found the potatoes and egg bland or the smoke flavor off-putting, and REI's 227 reviews average 4.1 out of 5, which is good rather than raving.

**Texture.** The eggs are the usual variable. Trailspace's tester found them a bit runny, while a side-by-side comparison of old and new recipes on REI's site reported that the current version can leave a squishy, under-rehydrated center in the egg unless an extra half cup or so of water goes in. Another reviewer said the sausage pieces rehydrate slightly tougher than the rest. Stirring thoroughly, letting the pouch sit the full time and erring toward more water are the standard fixes.

**Sodium.** At about 710 mg per serving and 1,420 mg per pouch, this is a salty breakfast. Trailspace's reviewer flagged it for anyone who watches sodium. On a hot, sweaty day that is arguably a feature. On a cold, easy morning, some eaters will find it heavy-handed.

**Convenience.** This is the category's main selling point. Boiling water goes into the pouch and the meal is eaten out of it, with no pot to scrub. One reviewer noted that the sealed pouch doubles as a hand warmer while the meal rehydrates, which counts for something at a frosty camp. The recipe is certified gluten free, which is uncommon among hot backpacking breakfasts, though it contains milk and egg.

**Storage.** Mountain House rates the pouch for a 30-year shelf life. That matters less to backpackers than to anyone building an emergency cache, but it does mean a spare pouch can live at the bottom of a pack or a car trunk without worry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Real breakfast food: eggs, sausage and potatoes in a single pouch
- 28 g of protein and 520 calories per pouch
- No cookware to clean; eaten straight from the bag
- Ready in under 10 minutes with boiling water
- Gluten free
- 30-year shelf life

**Cons**

- High sodium: about 710 mg per serving
- Egg texture can be inconsistent and may need extra water
- Smoke flavor divides eaters
- Two-serving pouch is really one serving for a hungry solo hiker
- Calorie density is only moderate for its weight

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want a hot, savory breakfast and are willing to carry a stove for it. It suits hikers on weekend and multi-day trips who value zero cleanup, and anyone who needs a gluten-free hot option. It is also a reasonable pick for people who stock long-term emergency food.

Stoveless hikers, sodium-conscious eaters and gram-counters chasing the highest possible calories per ounce should look elsewhere. Cold-soak oatmeal, nut-and-seed mixes or a tortilla breakfast will weigh less or carry more energy for the weight.

## Verdict

The Breakfast Skillet delivers a hot, protein-heavy breakfast with almost no effort, which is exactly what it promises. Add a little extra water, expect a salty finish, and it earns its place in a food bag. It is dependable rather than exceptional.
