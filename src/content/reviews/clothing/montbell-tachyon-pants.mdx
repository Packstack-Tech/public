## Overview

The Tachyon Pants are Montbell's 7-denier wind shell trousers. At 54 g, they weigh about as much as a couple of AA batteries and fold into their own internal stow pocket. They're built for runners, fastpackers and ultralight hikers who want a just-in-case layer for exposed ridges. The trade-off is that the fabric is gossamer-thin, so durability and fit are the price of the weight.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** The headline number holds up. Will Rietveld, who tested an earlier generation for Backpacking Light and Ultralight Insights, measured the pants at the manufacturer's stated 1.9 oz. He compared them with Montbell's Versalite rain pants, which weighed about 4 oz in their earlier version. Stuffed into the internal pocket, the pants become a small pouch that disappears into a hip belt pocket or a running vest. That's the whole point: a layer light enough that nobody leaves it at the trailhead to save weight.

**Wind protection and breathability.** Montbell redesigned the Tachyon line to improve breathability. The company says it changed the weave and finishing of the 7-denier nylon to let humidity escape while still blocking wind. Owners notice the difference. A Backpacking Light forum member who owns both generations of Tachyon wind gear called the newer material "way better" and said the old fabric felt like wearing a plastic bag. Reviewers describe the current pants as surprisingly breathable for a fabric this thin. They are still a shell, so a hard, sweaty climb will fog the inside. They work best as a layer to put on when the wind picks up and take off when the pace does.

**Weather resistance.** The Polkatex DWR sheds light precipitation and wet brush. It isn't a rain pant, and Montbell doesn't market it as one. Testers who have used the pants for brief showers and wet vegetation found the weight savings over conventional rain pants significant. Anyone expecting a sustained downpour should carry a waterproof shell instead.

**Fit and cut.** The legs aren't tapered, and at least one UK retailer review notes they can feel wide and flappy in strong wind, particularly over bare legs. The elastic waist and thin drawcord do most of the fitting. There are no zippered ankles. Rietveld found the leg openings wide enough to pull over size 12 trail runners, though the cuffs may not clear bulkier shoes without a struggle. Some owners remove the drawcord to save a fraction of an ounce.

**Durability.** At 7 denier, the fabric rewards care. Reviewers warn that sitting on rough rock or pushing through heather and other thick vegetation can damage it. Treat the pants as emergency and transitional gear, not as trail pants for bushwhacking. A roll of repair tape belongs in the same pocket.

**Looks.** iRunFar named the Tachyon its top wind pant for 2026, praising it as lightweight and effective, and cheerfully noting its main drawback: it looks like a garbage bag. That's fair. Nobody wears these for style.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 54 g, about 1.9 oz, among the lightest wind pants available
- Packs into its own internal stow pocket
- Redesigned fabric breathes better than earlier Tachyon generations
- Polkatex DWR handles light drizzle and wet brush
- Reflective details at no meaningful weight cost
- Named top wind-pant pick by iRunFar

**Cons**

- 7-denier nylon is vulnerable to abrasion, snags and rough seats
- Untapered legs flap in strong wind, especially over shorts
- Not a substitute for waterproof rain pants
- Price of $110 is steep for 54 g of fabric
- No ankle zips, so putting them on over larger shoes takes effort
- The look is strictly functional

## Who Should Buy This

The Tachyon Pants suit trail runners, fastpackers and thru-hikers who run cold on high, windy sections and want a layer they'll never notice in the pack. They also make sense as a tent-camp warmth boost, pulled over base layers or shorts at camp, and as a mosquito barrier.

Hikers who push through brush, scramble, or sit on abrasive ground should choose a sturdier 15-denier pant instead. Anyone who needs genuine rain protection should carry a true waterproof pant. Hikers who want a snug fit over shorts or tights will do better with a tapered cut.

## Verdict

The Tachyon Pants do one job: they cut the wind and weigh almost nothing. Redesigned fabric makes them more comfortable than earlier versions, and the price is fair for a purpose-built emergency layer. Buy them for ridgelines and cold mornings, and don't expect them to survive rough treatment.
