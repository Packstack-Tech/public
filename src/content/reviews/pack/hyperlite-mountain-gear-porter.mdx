## Overview

The Porter is Hyperlite Mountain Gear's do-it-all Dyneema Composite Fabric pack: a roll-top tube with a real suspension, four daisy chains, and almost nothing else. It's built for backpackers, skiers and peak-baggers who want a pack that survives scraping against rock and snow and stays dry in bad weather. The trade-off is the usual one for a stripped-down design. There are no side pockets and no top lid, so organization is up to you.

## Performance

**Carrying comfort.** The Porter's suspension is simple but real. Two aluminum stays can be bent to the curve of the wearer's back or pulled out entirely. The back panel is a 1/4" foam pad, and the Dyneema shoulder straps sit over 3/8" closed-cell foam and spacer mesh. The hip belt combines 1/8" rigid foam, 1/4" closed-cell foam and spacer mesh. Owners report that the stiff fabric holds its shape as the pack is loaded, and that it hugs the back closely on skis and scrambles. A Trailspace reviewer who needed a pack long enough for a 21.5-inch torso carried loads up to 40 lb in it comfortably. Hyperlite's own rating tops out at 40 lb, and the pack is happiest below that. Backpacking Light forum members with heavier week-long loads have argued that this class of pack wants more suspension than the Porter offers.

**Capacity.** Hyperlite rates volume with the pack full and the roll-top folded once and clipped. Roll it further and the pack shrinks. A reviewer on Trailspace described tightening the compression straps and rolling the top down for day trips on backcountry skis. The same reviewer reported a winter load of more than 40 lb, with a mid-style tarp tent, a -20°F bag and five days of food and fuel, disappearing into the larger 4400 Porter.

**Weather and durability.** Dyneema Composite Fabric doesn't absorb water, and seam tape covers all vertical side seams and the areas behind sewn-on features. Owners say the fabric is far tougher than silnylon and stays waterproof as long as it isn't punctured. One reviewer noted that punctures are probably inevitable over time. The Porter's reinforced bottom and sides were a selling point for a Trailspace reviewer who skis and climbs with it. Another owner suggested that waterproof stuff sacks inside make a pack liner or rain cover unnecessary.

**Organization.** This is where the Porter asks for compromises. Four vertical daisy chains on the exterior can carry two ice axes. There are also daisy chains on the shoulder straps, six horizontal compression straps, and a Y-strap over the top. The hip belt has zippered Dyneema pockets with #5 YKK zippers. Outdoor reviewer Max Neale called the lack of side pockets the Porter's main disadvantage for backpacking, while ranking it his favorite Hyperlite pack overall. The roll-top has a second cost: reaching something at the bottom means unloading everything above it. Hyperlite's Porter-specific accessories help. The company offers a stuff pocket, a water bottle holder and stackable pods (9 L and 13 L sizes fit the 40 L and 55 L packs) to add external storage. Each one adds weight and money.

**Visibility.** The white fabric is a small but real advantage. Owners say the pale interior lets you see your gear instead of staring into a black hole.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Waterproof Dyneema body with seam taping, and a reinforced bottom and sides
- Removable, bendable stays let the frame be tuned or dropped to save weight
- Daisy chains and compression straps handle skis, axes, pads and snowshoes
- Dual-density hip belt with zippered pockets
- Made in the USA
- Modular: pods and pockets can be added per trip

**Cons**

- No side pockets; water and snacks need accessories
- Roll-top means bottom access requires unpacking
- 40 lb load ceiling, and the suspension is minimal for week-long carries
- Fabric can be punctured, and a puncture ends the pack's waterproof claim
- Accessories add to the price and weight

## Who Should Buy This

The Porter suits three-season and winter backpackers, skiers and scramblers whose loads stay under about 40 lb. It's also for people who value a pack that wears well and don't mind packing in order. It rewards those who already organize gear in stuff sacks and want a body-hugging pack that can carry axes or skis. Thru-hikers who live out of side pockets, and anyone carrying heavy loads for a week or more, should look at packs with more padding and more external storage.

## Verdict

The Porter is a tough, waterproof, well-built 55-liter pack that does most things well up to 40 lb. Buy it if you can live without side pockets and want a pack that handles snow, rock and rain. Skip it if you need quick-access pockets or a load-hauling frame.
