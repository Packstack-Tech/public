## Overview

The Ayacucho Kids Rain Jacket is a no-frills waterproof shell from the house outdoor brand of the UK's Cotswold Outdoor group. It weighs 83 g, packs down for a daypack, and costs £45 at full price. The trade-off is a mid-tier waterproof and breathability rating. That is plenty for family hikes and the school run, but it is not built for sustained alpine storms.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The jacket uses a 10,000 mm waterproof fabric with taped seams. That combination is the usual baseline for a kids' rain shell. It sheds steady rain and the kind of drizzle that sets in for an afternoon in the hills. Seam tape matters more than the headline number on a child's jacket. Kids sit on wet logs, crouch in puddles and lean into packs, and stitched-through seams are where water gets in first. Ayacucho also lists the shell as windproof, which makes it a useful layer on exposed ridges even when the sky stays dry.

**Breathability.** The 10,000 g/m²/24 hr rating is respectable for the price. Children generate a lot of heat on a climb and then cool off fast when they stop. A shell at this level will vent some moisture on a moderate effort, but it will feel clammy when a child is running hard in warm rain. Dressing kids in a light base layer and nothing heavier underneath gives it the best chance of working.

**Weight and packability.** At 83 g, the jacket weighs about as much as a smartphone. It is light enough to live permanently in the lid of a child's pack or the bottom of a parent's, and it is listed as packable. For families counting grams across several small bodies, this is the main reason to buy it. Ayacucho does not publish a stuff-sack size or specify which size the 83 g figure refers to. Expect larger sizes to weigh somewhat more.

**Fit and features.** The fixed hood stays attached and cannot be lost on the trail. Elasticated cuffs keep the sleeves simple to put on and take off, which matters with younger children who resist fiddly closures. Two zipped side pockets keep snacks, a glove or a found treasure from vanishing. Reflective details add visibility on dark winter school runs and late-afternoon descents. The cut is regular, so children in between sizes should go up rather than down, particularly if a fleece goes underneath.

**Durability and value.** The shell is 100% recycled polyester, which adds a modest sustainability point. Snow+Rock backs its Ayacucho range with a three-year product warranty. At £45 RRP, and frequently sold well below that, the jacket costs less than most name-brand kids' shells.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 83 g and packable, so it disappears into a daypack
- Taped seams and a 10,000 mm rating cover the basics
- Reflective details for low-light visibility
- Two zipped pockets and a fixed hood that can't be lost
- Inexpensive, and covered by a three-year retailer warranty
- Recycled polyester fabric

**Cons**

- 10,000 mm and 10,000 g/m² ratings sit below premium shells, so heavy sustained rain will test it
- Regular cut with elasticated (not adjustable) cuffs, so it offers less room to grow into
- Not insulated, so children need a layer underneath on cold days
- Limited availability outside UK retailers

## Who Should Buy This

Parents of children who hike, camp or walk to school in the rain will get the most from this jacket. It suits those who want a cheap, light shell that spends most of its life stuffed in a bag. Day hikers and family campers in temperate, wet climates are the target. Anyone planning multi-day trips in sustained heavy rain, or who wants adjustable cuffs and a hood cinch to stretch one jacket across two seasons, should look at pricier shells with more adjustment.

## Verdict

The Ayacucho Kids Rain Jacket does the basic job at a low weight and a low price: it keeps rain and wind out, folds small and adds reflective trim. It is not a storm shell, but few children need one. Buy it as a dependable pack-stuffer for family outings.
