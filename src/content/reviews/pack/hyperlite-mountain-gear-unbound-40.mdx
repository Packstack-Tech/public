## Overview

The Unbound 40 is Hyperlite Mountain Gear's redesigned flagship pack, built for multi-week and thru-hiking use rather than fast-and-light weekend missions. It swaps HMG's older cylindrical Dyneema silhouette for a paneled, boxier build with a new single-stay suspension, a replaceable hip belt, and dramatically bigger pockets — making it the pick for hikers who want Dyneema's durability and waterproofing but need more organization and load-carrying comfort than HMG's older Southwest or Junction packs offered.

## Performance

**Carrying comfort.** The big story with the Unbound line is that HMG finally built a suspension that can handle real weight without feeling like a stuff sack with straps. <cite index="8-2,8-3">The Unbound 40 Backpack weighs 30.1 oz (853g) and represents a pretty radical departure from Hyperlite's past backpacks with large side pockets, a redesigned and replaceable hip belt, and the use of a single frame stay.</cite> On the trail, that translates to a pack that <cite index="9-13">comfortably carried loads of up to 40 pounds during testing in the Rocky Mountains</cite>, according to CleverHiker's field testing. OutdoorGearLab's testers came away similarly impressed, noting <cite index="1-4">comfort comes from just enough foam padding embedded in the back of the pack and the single-shaped metal stay, adding welcome rigidity and helping transfer pack load onto the well-designed hip belt</cite>. That's a notable shift for a brand that's historically prioritized gram-counting over cush.

That said, it's not a plush carry. CleverHiker flagged that <cite index="9-15">the thin shoulder strap padding and slick back panel may not be ideal for those who prioritize plush cushioning and maximum airflow</cite> — so if you run hot or tend to overload packs beyond the rated 40 lbs, expect some sweat and shoulder awareness on long climbs.

**Weatherproofing and durability.** This is where Dyneema Composite Hybrid earns its reputation. <cite index="9-12">The inherently weather-resistant Dyneema Composite Hybrid fabric and taped seams kept gear bone dry even after extensive water exposure tests</cite> in CleverHiker's testing. The heavier-denier bottom panel is a smart touch: <cite index="4-8">the bottom of the pack is made out of a slightly more durable version of the material, DCH150, since this area of the pack tends to see faster wear.</cite> Sectionhiker's long-term tester, who's run HMG packs since 2013, put roughly 150 miles on the Unbound 40 through a New Hampshire winter and into spring without issue, which speaks to the fabric's resilience in genuinely rough conditions.

**Organization.** This is the single biggest upgrade over older HMG packs. Between the side pockets, hip belt pockets, front mesh, and a bottom stash pocket, you get real modularity: <cite index="1-6">center, side, hip belt, and a unique bottom stash pocket equate to 15 additional liters of storage to complement the 34 L inside</cite> (note: HMG's own current spec lists 9L external / 40L internal, so pocket capacity may vary slightly by test unit or measurement method). Reviewers consistently single out the side pockets as a standout: <cite index="2-30">the XL side pockets proved to be the favorite new feature within the HMG pack family.</cite> The bottom pocket doubles as trash/wet-gear storage, and CleverHiker called out <cite index="9-14">the unique organization features, such as the split front mesh pocket, roomy side pockets, hefty hipbelt pockets, and an innovative bottom pocket with trash port access</cite> as a highlight.

The one recurring gripe: when the main compartment is packed to capacity, the rear pockets lose their give. AdventureAlan noted <cite index="2-17">no slack in rear exterior pockets when pack is fully loaded</cite> — worth knowing if you plan to cram a tent or rain jacket back there on a full food-carry day.

**Fit and adjustability.** Sizing runs S/M/L/Tall, torso 15"–21"+, and the hip belt is removable if you want to shave weight on shorter, lighter-load trips. Sectionhiker's tester did note running into one snag with the new frame/hip belt combination during testing, though didn't elaborate on severity — something to keep in mind if you're buying without trying it on first, and worth double-checking fit at a dealer or during HMG's return window.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely comfortable up to its 40 lb rated load — a real step up from older HMG packs
- Waterproof DCH construction with a tougher DCH150 bottom panel for abrasion resistance
- Best-in-class pocket layout: oversized side pockets, dual hip belt pockets, front mesh, and bottom stash/trash pocket
- Removable hip belt for weight savings on lighter trips
- Available in four sizes plus a Tall option — better fit dialing than most UL packs

**Cons**

- Expensive, even by Dyneema pack standards
- Thin shoulder strap padding and a non-breathable back panel — expect some back sweat
- Rear pockets lose slack when the main bag is stuffed full
- At least one long-term tester flagged a frame/hip belt fit issue worth checking before you commit
- Not for extreme gram-counters chasing sub-2-lb base pack weights

## Who Should Buy This

The Unbound 40 is built for thru-hikers and long-trip backpackers who want Dyneema's waterproofing and durability but also want to actually organize their gear without digging through one giant tube every time they need a snack. If you're hauling multi-day food carries, hiking in wet climates, or just tired of packs that treat external storage as an afterthought, this is worth the price tag. If you're chasing the absolute lightest possible setup and don't mind sacrificing pockets and back padding, HMG's own Southwest 55 or a minimalist frameless pack will save you more grams for less money.

## Verdict

The Unbound 40 is HMG's most thoughtfully designed pack to date — it trades a little bit of the brand's traditional weight-obsession for real comfort and organization gains, and based on the consensus across independent testers, that trade is worth it for most long-distance hikers. It's not cheap and it's not the lightest 40L Dyneema pack on the market, but for hikers who want one pack to handle everything from a weekend shakedown to a full thru-hike, it's an easy 8.5/10.
