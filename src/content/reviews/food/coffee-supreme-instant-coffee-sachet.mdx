## Overview

Coffee Supreme, the Wellington specialty roaster, took a single-origin Colombian coffee, brewed it, and freeze-dried the result into pre-dosed sachets. The pitch is simple: a camp-stove cup that tastes like coffee rather than like a cautionary tale. The trade-off is just as simple. It is among the best-tasting instants a backpacker can carry, and by a wide margin it is also among the most expensive.

## Performance

**Taste.** Most of the case for this product rests on the cup. When Metro magazine put New Zealand's instant coffees through a ranking, Coffee Supreme finished first, with judges describing a dark, bright cup with citrus notes. One said it tasted like filter coffee, and another remarked that, handed this and told it was pour-over, they would have believed it. The Spinoff's reviewer, comparing it with Moccona, found the Supreme clearly better black and noted it still doesn't match a proper Supreme espresso or filter. Hikers on the CoffeeSnobs forum describe a Colombian cup sweet enough to drink without sugar, with a body one called a little like moka pot coffee.

Not every panel agreed. In a blind test run by Consumer NZ, it placed sixth with 42 out of 100, and only 20 percent of panellists said they would buy it for home. Testers there found hints of coffee in the aroma that never arrived in the flavour. The split is telling. The product wins with people who drink black, filter-style coffee and loses with people raised on dark, bitter, supermarket-style instant.

**Milk.** This is a black-coffee product. The Spinoff's tester found that adding milk overwhelmed the delicate profile, leaving something closer to flavoured milk. Hikers who want a milky morning cup report better results using a little powdered milk, in small amounts, and accepting a mellower result.

**Strength.** One CoffeeSnobs owner said a sachet reads like a single shot, and that a double would suit them better. Anyone who needs a big, punchy mug can use a smaller volume of water or tear two sachets.

**On the trail.** The format is the real advantage. The sachet is pre-measured, so there is no spoon, no zip bag of loose powder spilling in a lid pocket, and no guesswork at 6 a.m. in a cold vestibule. At 4 g each, a full week of mornings (a seven-sachet box) adds up to 28 g, which is less than most people spend on a spare stuff sack. It dissolves in hot water, and the maker says it works in cold water or milk too, which suits cold-soak hikers who want caffeine without lighting a stove. The foil sachets are single-use trash that has to be packed out, a small but real consideration against decanting a bulk jar.

**Cost.** Pricing is the persistent complaint. Consumer NZ calculated NZ$76.14 per 100 g, compared with NZ$3.19 per 100 g for Pams Café Roast, and concluded that decanting a cheap jar into a Ziploc was the better value. At launch, Metro put the seven-pack at NZ$13.99, about NZ$2 a cup, and called it an instant for coffee people rather than for instant people. That is a fair summary of who it is for.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Bright, clean, filter-like cup that coffee drinkers rate well above supermarket instant, especially black
- Pre-dosed 4 g sachets: no measuring, no mess, easy to ration by the day
- 100% coffee with no additives or fillers, and a traceable single origin
- Works hot or cold, so it suits stove and no-stove trips alike
- Seven sachets weigh 28 g

**Cons**

- Very expensive per cup compared with bulk instant
- Mild flavour is lost under milk
- One sachet gives a modest, single-shot-style cup
- Foil sachets generate packaging waste
- Mixed reception in blind testing with a mainstream panel

## Who Should Buy This

Black-coffee drinkers who already care about what is in the mug, and who want a no-fuss option for weekend trips, multi-day treks, or an emergency stash in the pack lid. It also suits hikers who go stoveless and want a cold-water caffeine option that does not taste like medicine.

Thru-hikers who go through a cup or two every day for months will find the cost hard to justify, and cheaper bulk instant decanted into a bag does the job for a fraction of the price. Milky-latte drinkers and anyone who likes their coffee dark and heavy should also look elsewhere.

## Verdict

Coffee Supreme Instant is a premium product that mostly earns the premium, provided the drinker takes it black and sees it as a treat rather than a staple. Buy it as a box of seven for a long weekend or a special trip, not as the foundation of a thru-hike resupply.
