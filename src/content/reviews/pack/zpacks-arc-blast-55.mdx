## Overview

The Zpacks Arc Blast 55 is a DCF roll-top pack built around a patented carbon fiber arc frame — a design aimed squarely at thru-hikers and ultralight enthusiasts who want real load transfer without paying for it in ounces. At 565 g (19.9 oz) for 55 liters of capacity, it achieved a weight-to-volume ratio that almost nothing with a frame could match at the time of its production. It has since been discontinued and replaced by the Arc Haul Ultra, but used examples still circulate frequently, and the Arc Blast remains a benchmark worth understanding.

## Performance

### Weight-to-Volume Ratio

This is where the Arc Blast made its name. At roughly 11 g/L, it posted the best weight-to-volume ratio of any framed pack in independent testing — a full half-pound lighter than the next closest competitors at the time. For a pack that actually transfers load to your hips, that's a meaningful gap, not a marginal one.

### Load Carrying & Comfort

The arc frame earns its keep for loads in the 15–25 lb range. The carbon stays flex into a curve that creates a gap between your back and the pack body — good for ventilation, and genuinely effective at directing weight onto the hip belt at sensible loads. Once you get past roughly 27–30 lbs, though, comfort degrades noticeably. One long-distance hiker noted that at 30 lbs-plus it became "painful on the hips," which aligns with what most framed ultralight packs experience at their upper ceiling. If your loaded weight regularly sits in the high 20s or above, the Arc Blast is asking to be pushed beyond its design intent.

### Fit and Adjustment

Getting this pack dialed in takes patience. The arc frame adjustment — tensioning the mesh back panel to achieve the recommended ~2.5" (6 cm) of curve — is not intuitive on first contact. Multiple reviewers flagged it as fiddly, and at least one user noted it works best to load the pack _before_ cranking down the arc rather than after. Once you find the sweet spot, though, the fit is genuinely good. The torso is available in multiple fixed lengths or adjustable sizing, and the hip belt is removable and swappable — a practical feature if your waist size changes mid-trail or you need to share the pack.

### Weather Resistance

The 3.1 oz/sqyd DCF Hybrid body fabric is inherently waterproof, and Zpacks tapes all seams and attachment points. In extended rain use, one AT thru-hiker reported no wet gear issues across 2,000+ miles and eventually sent his rain cover home — though he acknowledged that's an informal test, not a lab claim. A liner bag for electronics and sleeping gear in a sustained downpour is still reasonable insurance; the roll-top closure is only as good as its seal when you're moving.

### Storage Layout

The 42 L main body handles a week of ultralight food comfortably. The two side pockets fit 1 L bottles on the go, and the 8 L front mesh pocket handles rain gear, snacks, and maps — though it's worth knowing the mesh doesn't stretch much, so rigid items like a cook pot can be a tight fit. All standard bear canisters, including the Bearikade Expedition and BearVault 500, fit standing upright in the upper half of the main compartment.

### Durability

This is the one honest asterisk on the Arc Blast's reputation. DCF holds up remarkably well against abrasion and puncture for its weight, but it is a laminate — and extended heavy use eventually shows. One hiker reported four holes appearing in the pack bottom and internal fiber delamination after three years of moderate use. That's not unusual for DCF under hard mileage, but it's worth setting expectations: this is a precision tool that rewards careful use, not a bomb-proof workhorse.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Best-in-class weight-to-volume ratio for a framed pack — 565 g for 55 L
- Carbon arc frame provides genuine back ventilation, uncommon at this weight
- DCF body fabric is waterproof and highly rip-resistant
- Seam-taped construction; roll-top closure locks out rain
- Highly customizable: torso length, hip belt size, modular pockets, bear canister straps
- Hip belt is removable and interchangeable — practical for sizing changes
- Bear canister compatible without external lashing

**Cons**

- Load comfort drops off noticeably above ~25–27 lbs
- Frame adjustment curve is fussy and has a real learning curve
- Front mesh pocket doesn't stretch — rigid items are awkward
- DCF laminate shows wear under sustained heavy use; not indestructible
- Discontinued — no warranty support or direct purchase from Zpacks; used market only
- Zpacks' sizing system requires careful measurement; wrong torso size is genuinely uncomfortable

## Who Should Buy This

The Arc Blast 55 is the right tool for experienced ultralight backpackers — thru-hikers, section hikers, or weekend warriors — who have already trimmed their base weight below 12 lbs and rarely carry more than 20–25 lbs total. If you're willing to invest the time to fit it properly, it rewards you with ventilation and load transfer you wouldn't expect from a sub-20 oz framed pack. It's not for beginners still sorting out their kit, and it's not for anyone who regularly carries heavy food carries or resupplies that push total weight into the 30s. Given its discontinued status, prospective buyers should scrutinize used examples carefully for seam integrity and bottom wear before committing.

## Verdict

The Arc Blast 55 defined what a lightweight framed pack could be for most of a decade — and the fact that it's still sought out on the used market years after being discontinued says something real about its design. Its ceiling is genuinely low at heavy loads, and fitting it takes work, but for the right hiker carrying the right load, it's an exceptional pack. **8.5/10.**
