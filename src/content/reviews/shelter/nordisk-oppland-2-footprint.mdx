## Overview

The Oppland 2 Footprint is a tailored groundsheet cut to the floor of Nordisk's Oppland 2 tunnel tent, in 70D PU-coated nylon rated to 10,000 mm. It is aimed at owners who pitch on rough ground, snow or mud and want to keep the tent floor intact. The trade-off is plain: it adds 600 g, roughly a third of what the Oppland 2 LW weighs on its own.

## Performance

**Protection.** The job is simple. A footprint takes the abuse that would otherwise go to the tent floor: stones, sticks, grit, and the slow abrasion of being pitched night after night. Nordisk says its footprints are tailored to each tent so they guard against dirt, rocks and sharp objects. UK retailers who stock it say the same, calling a footprint worthwhile for anyone camping on rough ground. The 70D nylon is far tougher than the floor fabrics on ultralight tents. Testers at Live For The Outdoors recommend the footprint as an optional add-on to the Oppland 2 LW for extra abrasion resistance, insulation and weatherproofing.

**Waterproofing.** The 10,000 mm rating is well above the figures quoted for the tent itself. One retailer review of the current Oppland 2 LW (2.0) lists the fly at 2,000 mm and the groundsheet at 8,000 mm. A footprint with that rating adds a second waterproof layer under the tent. That helps on saturated ground and on snow. It also gives a clean, dry surface under the porch. Nordisk's product copy goes further and says a footprint redirects ground moisture around the flysheet and cuts condensation inside the sleeping cabin by 90%. Treat that figure as manufacturer marketing. Independent measurements haven't confirmed it.

**Fit.** The cut covers the whole floor of the Oppland 2, vestibule included. It has dedicated attachment points so it stays in position in wind. Nordisk's own advice is that it works best pegged out. On a tunnel tent with a porch over 4 m long, that means a few more pegs and a little more time at pitch.

**Weight and bulk.** This is where the footprint loses ground against the rest of the Oppland 2 LW's pitch. Live For The Outdoors weighed the tent at 1,737 g. Adding 600 g brings the total to about 2.3 kg. Packed, it measures 41 × 22 × 4 cm, a flat slab that slides down a pack's back panel without eating much volume. One UK retailer puts it bluntly: carry the extra weight to prolong the life of your tent. That sells the idea without addressing a thru-hiker's obvious objection, which is that 600 g is a lot to carry for a layer that may never be needed.

**Alternative use.** Some Nordisk footprints can be pitched with the flysheet alone as a stripped-down shelter. Retailers describe that option for the Oppland line. It would cut weight but give up the inner tent and its bug protection, so it suits dry, bug-free conditions.

**Price.** UK testers cite £78 for the footprint. European retailers list it at around €90. That is a lot for a sheet of coated nylon, and the tailored fit is the reason to pay it. A generic polycryo or Tyvek groundsheet costs a fraction as much and weighs far less, though it won't match the fit or the durability.

**Which tent version.** Retailers list the footprint as fitting the Oppland 2 SI, the Oppland 2 LW and the older Oppland 2 PU. The newer Oppland 2 (2.0) tents have their own footprint, listed at 535 g with a similar packed size, so owners of those tents should buy that one. The 2.0 LW footprint is listed at 595 g.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tailored cut covers the full floor, porch included
- 70D nylon is a major step up in abrasion resistance from typical tent floors
- 10,000 mm rating adds a real second waterproof layer
- Flat, compact pack at 41 × 22 × 4 cm
- Gives a clean surface for gear in the porch

**Cons**

- 600 g is about a third of the Oppland 2 LW's weight
- Pricey for what it is, at around £78 or €90
- Works best pegged, which adds pitch time
- Fits only the Oppland 2 tent family
- The condensation claim has no independent testing behind it

## Who Should Buy This

Owners of the Oppland 2 SI or older Oppland 2 PU who camp on stony or wet ground, or who use the tent for basecamp stays and winter trips, will get the most from it. Weight is a smaller issue on a 3 kg tent. Bikepackers and trekkers who stay several nights in one place also fit. Fast-and-light backpackers who bought the LW to save grams should skip it. They are better off with a cheap polycryo sheet, or a careful choice of pitch.

## Verdict

This is a well-made, properly fitted groundsheet that does what it says. It is also a heavy accessory for a tent bought for its low weight. Buy it for the SI or for rough-ground camping. Leave it at home if the LW's weight is the reason you own the tent.
