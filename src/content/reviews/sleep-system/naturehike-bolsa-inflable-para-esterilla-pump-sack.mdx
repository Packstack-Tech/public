## Overview

The Naturehike Pump Sack is a waterproof, roll-top nylon/TPU bag that captures air and squeezes it into a sleeping pad's valve, so nobody has to hyperventilate at 6 p.m. in a damp vestibule. It is sold separately from Naturehike's pads, and it can double as a dry bag or a stuff-sack pillow. The trade-off is simple: it costs very little and does one job well, but it is slower than a powered pump and not a serious dry bag.

## Performance

**Inflation.** The method is the one every pump sack uses. Set the bag's base on a flat surface, open the mouth wide to scoop in air, close it, then tilt and press the air down into the pad's valve. Naturehike's own pad listings are blunt about it: the pad does not come with the bag, and buyers who want one are told to add it separately. Reviewers of Naturehike's ultralight pads treat it as the preferred way to fill them. One review notes it makes both inflating and packing away easier. Another says it avoids pushing moisture into the pad.

**Speed.** Expect a short routine, not an instant one. A review of a Naturehike insulated pad puts a full inflation at roughly 60 to 90 seconds with the sack, against several minutes of blowing by mouth. Reviewers who have tried both say a small electric pump is still the fastest and most convenient option. The sack's advantage is that it has no battery, no charging cable and nothing to fail on day nine of a long section.

**Moisture and insulation.** Breath carries humidity into a pad. That matters most for insulated pads in cold weather, where mouth-inflation is widely discouraged. A pump sack keeps that moisture out, which is the strongest practical argument for carrying one over lung power. It also saves a lightheaded evening at altitude.

**As a dry bag.** The roll-top closure and nylon/TPU fabric make it waterproof in the sense that matters for a quick job: protecting a sleeping bag, a spare insulation layer or a wet-weather clothing bundle. Naturehike markets it for camping, hiking and swimming. Treat it as a spare liner for a few items, not as a primary pack-liner for river crossings. A roll-top bag used as a pump will also pick up condensation and dust from the air it scoops, so it is not the cleanest home for a down quilt.

**As a pillow.** Stuffed with a puffy jacket or spare clothes, it makes a serviceable pillow. Its pump function does not compete with this use, since the bag is empty during the day.

**Durability.** Naturehike's Spanish store lists 390 customer reviews for the bag, and the listing sits among its best-discounted items. The TPU-laminated construction matches the material of Naturehike's pads. Pinholes and seam wear at the roll-top edge are the usual failure points on bags of this type, though at this price a replacement costs less than a freeze-dried dinner.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- No battery, charger or moving parts
- Keeps breath moisture out of the pad
- Doubles as a dry bag, compression bag or pillow
- Waterproof nylon/TPU roll-top construction
- Priced at €6.57 on sale, a small cost on top of any pad

**Cons**

- Slower than an electric pump; a fill takes a minute or more
- Sold separately from Naturehike's pads, so the pad's price understates the full cost
- Roll-top bag is a light-duty dry bag, not a dedicated one
- Stock at Naturehike's Spanish store is limited, and the sack is only listed in one color there

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone with a Naturehike pad who wants to stop inflating by mouth. That includes budget thru-hikers, weekend backpackers and cold-weather campers who care about pad insulation. It also suits gram-counters who want one item to serve as pump, stuff sack and pillow. Hikers who already carry a USB-rechargeable pump, or who want a bomber waterproof liner for wet routes, will find this redundant or underbuilt respectively.

## Verdict

For a few euros, the Naturehike Pump Sack removes the most annoying chore of using an air pad and protects the pad's interior from breath moisture. It is no match for a powered pump on speed or for a dedicated dry bag on waterproofing. Buy it as a backup or a primary inflator for a Naturehike pad, and don't expect it to replace gear built for either of its side jobs.
