## Overview

The Parsec 20 is Therm-a-Rest's three-season down mummy: 900-fill hydrophobic duck down, a 20-denier shell and a footbox built for cold feet. It is aimed at backpackers who want ultralight-adjacent weight without the fragility, or the price, of the true gram-counter bags. The headline trade-off is plain. It gives up a few ounces to the thinnest-shelled competitors and gets a tougher, roomier bag in return.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The numbers say 29°F comfort and 18°F limit, and testers' experience tracks them closely. That makes the "20" in the name the survival number, not the sleeping number. One REI owner who sleeps cold found the bag comfortable down to the mid-to-high 20s, and said a 20°F night would call for a down jacket and warm base layers. GearJunkie's tester used the bag on a week-long Oregon hike and described it as soft, cozy and warm, with a 1-pound-class weight. UKC Gear, which reviewed an earlier 800-fill iteration, concluded the bag works for three seasons and edges into a fourth. For most thru-hikers, that means shoulder-season mountain nights, not deep winter.

**Footbox and zoned insulation.** The Toe-asis footbox is the feature owners mention most. It is a pocket of down that wraps the toes, and the same REI owner reported that it kept notoriously cold feet warmer than other bags at this rating. Reviewers also credit the zoned baffle design, which puts more insulation on top, where it can loft, and less underneath, where body weight crushes it anyway. Box baffles hold the down in place and limit cold spots.

**Weight and packed size.** At 820 g, the Parsec sits in ultralight territory for a bag with this much room and this many features. SectionHiker's sample ran 1.4 oz over the claimed weight, and GearJunkie weighed its regular at 1 lb 14 oz, so expect real-world numbers a little north of the spec sheet. In the included compression sack it packs to 7 by 10.5 inches, about 7 liters. Digital Trends likened the 2018 version to a Nalgene bottle in height and two in width. That works in a small pack, though it will not match the very smallest down quilts and bags.

**Fit.** This is a mummy that behaves more like a semi-rectangular. With a 62-inch shoulder girth and 57-inch hips, side sleepers and restless sleepers get room to move. The flip side: SectionHiker and Better Trail both note that smaller sleepers, women included, may run colder because there is more air volume to heat. A regular fits people up to 6 feet. Tall or broad hikers who find ultralight mummies claustrophobic will like the cut, and narrow-framed sleepers should consider a closer-fitting bag.

**Durability and moisture.** The 20D nylon ripstop shell is heavier than the 7D to 10D fabrics on the lightest bags, and reviewers see that as a feature. Better Trail calls it a clear step up in toughness from delicate ultralight bags, and Man Makes Fire found it surprisingly tough for 20D. The DWR finish and hydrophobic down handle tent condensation and damp nights better than untreated fill. The Outdoor Prolink reviewer's one complaint was that the zipper could be smoother, though the current bag carries an anti-snag YKK design.

**Pad integration.** The Synergy Link Connector straps hold the bag to a compatible sleeping pad, which stops the bag from rotating when a sleeper rolls over. That matters most to side sleepers on slippery pads.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 820 g with 900-fill down is a strong warmth-to-weight ratio at the price
- Toe-asis footbox suits cold-footed sleepers
- 20D nylon shell tolerates rough handling better than 10D ultralight bags
- Hydrophobic down and DWR add margin in damp conditions
- Roomy cut with room to change positions
- Pad-attachment straps, anti-snag zipper, external zip pocket and internal stash pocket
- Compression sack packs it to about 7 L

**Cons**

- Not class-leading in warmth-to-weight against premium ultralight bags
- Roomy cut can feel drafty on smaller sleepers
- Comfort rating of 29°F means the 20°F label is optimistic for many sleepers
- Real-world weight can run above spec
- At $470, it costs real money, though it undercuts much of the competition
- Internal pocket placement and some feature execution could be more refined

## Who Should Buy This

The Parsec 20 suits three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who sleep cold, move around at night, or want a bag that survives years of stuffing and snagging. It is a good fit for Appalachian Trail or Pacific Crest Trail shoulder seasons, and for anyone building a lighter kit who does not want to babysit a delicate shell. Gram-counters who want the lowest possible weight and packed volume should look at thinner-shelled bags or quilts. Narrow, smaller-framed sleepers should also try a snugger bag, and winter campers need a warmer one.

## Verdict

The Parsec 20 is a well-judged compromise: light enough to be called ultralight, tough enough to be used hard, and roomy enough to sleep in. Plan on it as a 25–30°F bag and it delivers.
