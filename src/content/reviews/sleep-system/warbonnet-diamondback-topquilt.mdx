## Overview

The Diamondback is a made-to-order down top quilt from Warbonnet Outdoors, a Colorado cottage company better known for hammocks. The 30°F version is the three-season option. It's built for hammock campers who want a quilt that stays put through the night, and it works equally well on a ground pad. The trade-off is weight. The 15d shell and generous cut make it a bit heavier than the sparest quilts in its class.

## Performance

**Baffle design.** The Diamondback's main selling point is its construction. Warbonnet uses a vertical, constriction-point baffle layout instead of the horizontal baffles on many cottage quilts. Hammock campers say this is why they bought it. One Backpacking Light reviewer had dropped horizontal-baffle quilts after finding that down migrated off the top during the night. That reviewer picked the Diamondback for its vertical baffles and differential cut. Owners on Warbonnet's own product pages say much the same: the down stays where it was put, with no nightly fluffing.

**Differential cut and footbox.** The inner and outer layers are cut at different sizes so the outer shell doesn't squash the down. The same Backpacking Light reviewer wanted this because pushing a foot against the footbox had left thin spots and cold toes. That reviewer found the effect subtle on the hanger, but the quilt wraps itself into a loose cylinder. The 42" footbox circumference is generous. It gives side sleepers and restless feet room, and it matches the hammock-friendly shape Warbonnet is known for.

**Warmth.** Warbonnet's ratings have a good reputation among owners. SectionHiker's Philip Werner tested the colder 10°F Diamondback, which carries 19.5 oz of 900-fill goose down. He reported it was comfortable down to about 15°F. A 30°F quilt carries much less fill, so expect it to be a mild-weather piece: shoulder-season nights, summer at elevation and warm hammock trips. Pair it with an underquilt or pad rated for the same conditions, since a top quilt only covers half the problem in a hammock.

**Fabric and durability.** Fifteen-denier ripstop is heavier than the 7d and 10d fabrics on the lightest quilts. It also feels sturdier. One owner noted it feels much beefier than 10d, though the same reviewer pointed out that Warbonnet doesn't offer 10d with the differential cut. A Warbonnet customer who compared it with a pair of mass-market 15°F bags said he could see through those bags' fabric in spots. He couldn't with the Diamondback. Camo options use 20d polyester, so the lightest, best-looking builds are the solid colors.

**Features and fit.** The wider sizes are roomy. SectionHiker's tester, who is 5'10", found the regular-length 60" model fit nearly perfectly and noted it is 60" wide from about the elbows to just past the hips. Draft collars, adjustable side elastics and pad attachment kits are available. One owner of a zip-footbox version said the collar and elastics sealed in warmth well. The sewn footbox in this version saves the weight of a zipper and eliminates one source of drafts.

**Weight.** At 700.2 g the 30°F version is heavier than the quilts that dominate ultralight gear lists. It's still reasonable for a wide, hammock-ready quilt with a differential cut. In the colder 10°F version, SectionHiker's tester measured about 3 oz more than Warbonnet's claimed weight. Anyone counting grams should check the final weight against the spec sheet when it arrives.

## Choosing a Version

The Diamondback comes in short, regular and long lengths, and in 50", 55" and 60" widths. All of them share the 42" footbox. Because it's made to order, the choice is yours.

- **50":** The narrowest and lightest build. Best for ground sleepers on narrow pads and for hammock sleepers who don't toss.
- **55":** The middle ground for most side sleepers.
- **60":** The roomiest. Best for large frames, restless sleepers and anyone who wants to tuck the quilt around a hammock. It uses the most down and fabric, so it's the heaviest and bulkiest.

For temperature, the 30°F rating suits summer and shoulder seasons. Cold sleepers and winter hikers should order one of the colder ratings Warbonnet offers.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Vertical baffles keep the down from shifting during the night
- Differential cut and 42" footbox leave room for feet
- Fully customizable in width, length, down type and colors
- Hydrophobic-treated, RDS-certified down
- Made in the USA
- Works as a hammock or ground quilt

**Cons**

- 15d shell and wide cut are heavier than minimalist quilts
- Camo versions use 20d polyester instead of nylon
- Made to order, so there's a wait
- 30°F is a narrow-range rating for cold sleepers

## Who Should Buy This

Hammock campers who want a quilt that stays lofted and never needs adjusting should buy this one. So should ground sleepers who like vertical baffles and a roomy footbox. It also suits anyone who wants to choose the exact width, length and fill. Gram-counters chasing a sub-500 g quilt and sleepers who expect to dip well below freezing should look at a lighter fabric or a colder rating.

## Verdict

The Diamondback is a well-built, thoughtfully designed quilt that solves a problem hammock sleepers know well: shifting down and cold spots. It isn't the lightest option at this rating, but it's comfortable and customizable, and owners praise its quality. Hammock campers and comfort-focused backpackers should buy it.
