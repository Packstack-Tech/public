## Overview

The Pack Pocket Padded is a one-liter, zippered pouch with a compression-molded body that hook-and-loops onto a shoulder strap or hipbelt. It does one job: it keeps a phone, a small camera or a set of keys within reach and cushioned, on packs that offer little or nothing in the way of front-mounted storage. The trade-off is plain. At 81 g it is not a gram-counter's accessory, and the padding that protects the contents is also what adds the weight.

## Performance

**Protection.** The compression-molded body is the reason to choose this pocket over a bare stuff pouch. The shell holds its shape, so a phone, earbuds case or compact camera sits in a padded cell rather than getting pressed against a pole tip or a loose stove. On a scramble or a bushwhack, where a strap-mounted pocket tends to take knocks, that cushioning matters more than it does on a groomed trail.

**Access.** Shoulder-strap and hipbelt mounting is the right idea for anyone who is tired of stopping to dig a phone out of a lid pocket. Osprey designed the pocket to work in two orientations: vertical on a shoulder strap, horizontal on a hipbelt. The hipbelt position suits a belt that has no pockets of its own, a common situation on frameless and minimalist packs. The strap position keeps a phone or a small snack within reach of either hand without taking the pack off.

**Organization.** A stretch divider splits the single liter into two zones, which helps keep a phone from grinding against keys or a lip balm. The integrated key clip means a key or a small tool can be tethered inside instead of dropped into the bottom of the pocket. This is a pocket for a handful of small items, not for a day's worth of snacks. At 1 L, that handful is the limit.

**Weather.** The DWR treatment will shed light rain and spray, but it is a surface finish on stretch-mesh nylon rather than a waterproof build. Osprey sells a separate Pack Pocket Waterproof with a water-resistant zipper for those who need that, and the padded model is the better fit for dry or desert use, or for hikers who bag electronics inside it anyway.

**Weight and value.** At 81 g, the pocket weighs roughly as much as a small stuff sack's worth of cuben-fiber gear many ultralight hikers carry in their packs. Hikers committed to a sub-10-pound base weight can get lighter strap-mounted storage from a simple zippered pouch or a hipbelt pocket from a cottage maker, and for most of them the padding is the part they're paying for but not using. The $25–30 price is also not trivial for a pouch. Retail listings at Backcountry show a rating of 4.2 out of 5, which points to a product that does what it claims with few complaints.

**Materials.** The body fabrics are bluesign-approved, and the DWR is made without PFAS. Retailer listings also describe the fabric as GRS-certified recycled. Osprey backs its gear with a lifetime-style warranty, though listings describe the terms differently, so check the current terms with the retailer.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Compression-molded shell protects phones, cameras and other fragile items
- Works vertically on a shoulder strap or horizontally on a hipbelt
- Internal stretch divider keeps items from rubbing together
- Integrated key clip keeps small, easy-to-lose items tethered
- PFAS-free DWR and bluesign-approved fabrics
- Compact: 18 x 11 cm, about 1 L

**Cons**

- 81 g is a lot for a pocket in the ultralight world
- DWR only; not a waterproof pocket
- Only 1 L, with no room for food
- $25–30 is steep for a small pouch
- Padding adds weight and bulk that a stuff pouch avoids

## Who Should Buy This

This pocket suits hikers who carry a phone or compact camera they use constantly, and who hike in rough terrain where a bare pouch would take a beating. It also suits owners of Osprey packs and others with bare hipbelts and strap webbing who want a quick-access pocket without changing packs. It is a poor fit for gram-counters, who can mount a feather-light pouch for a fraction of the weight, and for hikers in persistent rain, who should look at Osprey's waterproof version or bag their electronics.

## Verdict

The Pack Pocket Padded is a well-built, protective strap pocket that does exactly what it says. For fragile gear it is a sensible buy. For a thru-hiker watching every ounce it is a luxury, and the 81 g is easy to cut.
