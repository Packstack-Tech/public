## Overview

The Wildo Shaker is a small, three-chambered plastic container designed to hold salt, pepper, and one more seasoning of your choice on the trail. It's not a novel idea — camp cooking has always needed a way to carry spices without hauling glass jars or fiddly ziplock bags — but Wildo's version is one of the more thought-out takes on it, with individual flip-top lids per compartment and a transparent shell so you can see what's inside at a glance. It's aimed at anyone who's tired of eating unsalted freeze-dried mush and doesn't mind a couple hundred milligrams of gear weight to fix that.

## Performance

There's not a lot of engineering to evaluate in a spice shaker, but the details Wildo did put thought into mostly hold up. <cite index="6-6,6-7">The Shaker has three separate compartments with flip top lids, and it's transparent, which lets you see the contents.</cite> That transparency is more useful than it sounds — after a few weeks on trail, unlabeled spice containers turn into a guessing game, and being able to eyeball whether you're looking at salt or garlic powder saves you from ruining dinner.

The compartment split is asymmetric rather than three equal thirds. <cite index="8-1">It's built with one large compartment and two small ones.</cite> In practice that means you can dedicate the big chamber to whatever you use constantly (salt is the obvious choice for most people) and reserve the two smaller ones for accent spices you use more sparingly, like chili flakes or a bouillon powder. <cite index="3-5,3-6">Each chamber has a simple flip top with holes for shaking, and the larger compartment is meant for the item you regularly add to your food.</cite>

Refilling is straightforward — you're not dealing with a screw-top lid that you'll inevitably lose in the dark at a campsite. The flip tops stay tethered to the body, which is a small thing but matters when you're fumbling with gear at 6am in the cold. One functional detail worth knowing: <cite index="8-2,8-3">one side's lid is for refilling your spices, while the other side lets you dispense them while cooking</cite> — so orientation matters when you're packing it, unless you enjoy dumping a full day's ration of pepper into your pot by accident.

On durability, polypropylene is a sensible material choice — it's flexible enough to survive getting crushed in a pack without cracking the way a more rigid plastic might, and Wildo backs that up with a temperature range that covers real cold-weather use. <cite index="6-11">It's rated for longtime storage in +40°C to -20°C</cite>, so freezing temps on a shoulder-season trip shouldn't make the plastic brittle.

I couldn't find much in the way of dedicated long-term trail reports or forum threads (r/Ultralight, BPL, etc.) specifically discussing the Shaker — it's a low-drama accessory that doesn't generate much discussion, which is itself a decent sign that it just works and people don't think about it much after buying it. Retailer copy consistently frames it as a set-and-forget item: <cite index="3-7,3-8">once you've used it to improve your camp cooking, you'll wonder how you did without it, and the weight is minimal but the rewards at meal time are worth it</cite>. Take that with the grain of salt (pun intended) that it's marketing language, but it lines up with my own experience — this isn't a product with failure modes so much as a product you either remember to pack or don't.

One legitimate limitation: it's not airtight. Nothing in the spec sheet claims moisture-proofing, and the flip-top shake holes aren't the kind of gasketed seal you'd want if you're kayak touring or expect the shaker to get fully submerged. For normal backpacking use — living in the top pocket of your pack, maybe getting rained on — it's fine. For anything wetter than that, dry your spices before switching them into new containers.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely negligible weight at 17g — you'll never notice it in your pack
- Three separate compartments mean you can carry a real seasoning kit instead of just salt
- Transparent body makes contents easy to identify without opening lids
- BPA-free polypropylene handles cold and heat well, and it's dishwasher/microwave safe for home use between trips
- Flip-top lids are simpler and less loss-prone than screw caps
- Small footprint (5.9 x 4.5 cm) fits in a cook kit's dead space

**Cons:**

- Not airtight or fully waterproof — fine for normal use, not for submersion
- Asymmetric compartments (1 large, 2 small) won't suit everyone's spice ratios
- Shake holes are fixed in size, so finer powders (like bouillon dust) can clump or pour unevenly
- Limited third-party long-term reviews available, so durability claims beyond a season or two are largely untested by the community

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for anyone doing multi-day backcountry trips who's tired of bland dehydrated meals and wants a low-effort way to carry real seasoning without committing to heavier, bulkier spice containers. It's especially useful for people who cook from scratch on trail rather than just rehydrating pre-made meals, since you'll actually use the variety. If you're the type who's fine eating whatever a freeze-dried packet gives you, this is an easy skip — the weight savings from leaving it home are trivial either way, so it comes down to whether you actually care about flavor enough to manage refilling it between trips.

## Verdict

The Wildo Shaker does exactly one small job and does it without fuss: it gets three spices into your kitchen kit for 17 grams. It won't transform your cooking, and it's not designed for wet or rugged abuse, but as a cheap, low-risk quality-of-life upgrade for camp meals, it's hard to argue against. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — solid, unglamorous, and one of those items that quietly earns a permanent spot in the food bag.
