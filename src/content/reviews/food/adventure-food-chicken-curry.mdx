## Overview

Adventure Food's Chicken Curry is a Dutch-made, eat-from-the-pouch rice dinner: chicken pieces, green beans, bell pepper and pineapple chunks in a creamy curry sauce. It weighs 148 g and delivers about 600 kcal, a respectable 4 calories per gram. The trade-off is familiar for budget expedition food: the calories and price are good, but the curry is mild and the chicken is mostly a garnish on a bed of rice.

## Performance

**Prep.** The routine is the standard one for this style of meal. Tear the pouch at the notch, pull out the oxygen absorber, pour in boiling water, stir, zip it shut and wait eight minutes. There is no pot to scrub afterward, which is the main reason pouch meals appeal on cold or wet evenings. Testers at Outdoors Magic, who ran the meal through a camping trip in the Lake District, described the process as quick and simple. A tester at grough found the meal held its heat reasonably well over the wait, though it was only just warm enough by the end of eating at camp. A cozy or a jacket wrapped around the pouch is worth the grams in cold weather.

**Taste and texture.** Reviewers broadly agree on the profile: mild, creamy and slightly sweet, closer to a biryani-style rice dish than to a pub curry. Outdoors Magic called it well-spiced and relatively filling, with small squares of chicken. Others were less impressed. Basecamp Gear's staff taste test landed at 5/10, with a verdict of happy to wolf it down but short on heat and spice. Ultralight Outdoor Gear scored it the same and noted that after a day on the hill, the bar for satisfaction drops. The pineapple gives the sauce a sweet edge that suits some palates and annoys others. Anyone who wants a kick should carry a sachet of chili flakes or hot sauce.

**The chicken.** Chicken makes up just 6% of the recipe, and the grough tester flagged the pieces as small and a little chewy even when rehydrated by the book. This is a rice dish with some chicken in it, not the other way around.

**Calories and fill.** At 600 kcal and roughly 68 g of carbohydrate per 100 g, the pouch refuels well after a long day, and the roughly 500 g rehydrated volume reads as a full serving. The protein is modest at about 12–13 g per 100 g, so hikers who track protein will want to add a packet of tuna or a handful of nuts. Outdoors Magic rated the calorie-to-weight ratio as good, though not the best in its test group.

**Price and storage.** UK retail prices run from about £5.50 to £7.50, and a few European shops list it higher. That undercuts most of the big-name freeze-dried brands. The four-year shelf life makes it equally useful as a resupply box staple or an emergency ration in a car or a bivy kit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 4 kcal per gram at 148 g, with about 600 kcal in the pouch
- Eight-minute rehydration and no dishes
- Low price compared with most freeze-dried dinners
- Four-year shelf life suits stockpiling and emergency kits
- Mild enough for people who dislike spicy food

**Cons**

- Little heat or spice; reviewers repeatedly call it unexciting
- Chicken is only 6% of the recipe, in small, slightly chewy pieces
- Contains milk powder, so it is no use for dairy-free eaters
- Sweet pineapple divides opinion
- Modest protein for the calories

## Who Should Buy This

This meal suits hikers and campers who want a cheap, filling, no-fuss dinner and don't mind a gentle curry: weekend backpackers, bikepackers, and anyone building a resupply box on a budget. It also works as a long-term emergency ration. Thru-hikers who burn through food and care about protein should plan to add some. Spice lovers, dairy-free eaters and anyone who wants a meal with a real chicken presence should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

Adventure Food's Chicken Curry is a dependable, inexpensive rice dinner that fills a pouch with 600 kcal for 148 g and asks for eight minutes of patience. It is competent rather than memorable: mild flavor, thin chicken, and a sweet streak from the pineapple. Worth buying as a budget workhorse with a hot sauce sachet on the side.
