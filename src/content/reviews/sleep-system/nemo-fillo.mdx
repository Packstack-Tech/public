## Overview

The Fillo Elite is NEMO's stripped-down answer to the question of whether a pillow belongs in an ultralight kit. It is the ultralight sibling of the original Fillo, which uses a thick foam topper and weighs 9.3 oz (265 g). The Elite swaps most of that foam for a thin layer of recycled insulation, drops to under 3 oz, and packs down to roughly the size of a lime. The trade-off is loft: it is a comfortable pillow for people who sleep on their backs, and a marginal one for people who don't.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** At 2.8 oz on NEMO's spec sheet, the Elite sits at the light end of the inflatable-pillow category. One reviewer who put it on a scale at home recorded 2.9 oz, so the claimed figure holds up. For context, the standard Fillo is more than three times heavier, and CleverHiker's testers flag that pillow's foam layer as a packability penalty, since foam can't collapse the way air can. The Elite has no such problem. It disappears into the integrated stuff sack, and owners describe the bundle as somewhere between a lime and a tennis ball. Several users simply toss it in with the sleeping bag.

**Comfort.** The I-beam baffles are the key to the design. They keep the pillow from ballooning into the round, bouncy shape that makes cheap air pillows miserable, and they hold a rectangular platform under the head. Halfway Anywhere's reviewer, who carried it on routes including the Hayduke Trail and the Pyrenees High Route, called it the most comfortable backpacking pillow they had used. The thin insulation layer adds a little give and a little warmth against the head. The jersey cover feels closer to a pillowcase than to slick pad fabric, which helps on cold nights when bare nylon against the cheek is unwelcome.

The enthusiasm has limits. Trailgroove's reviewer found the comfort sufficient for a good night's sleep but not luxurious, and cautioned that anyone who wants extra height should look elsewhere or pile clothing on top. A verified buyer on eBay reached the same conclusion more bluntly: as a side sleeper, the loft ran out almost immediately and a stuff sack of clothes took its place. That fits the physics. Three inches of air is plenty for a back sleeper and not much for a person with broad shoulders lying on a thin pad.

**Adjustability and durability.** The micro-adjustable valve lets sleepers let out air a puff at a time until the firmness is right, which matters more than it sounds: a quarter-inch of height is often the difference between a good neck and a bad one. The same buyer who found the loft lacking reported that the valve popped open twice when a hand brushed across it during sleep. That is a single report, but it is worth knowing for restless sleepers. Cover care is easy, since it comes off and goes through the washer, a real advantage on a long trip where a pillow collects sunscreen and hair oil.

**Price.** At $59.95 MSRP, the Elite costs more than some air-only pillows that weigh about the same. Backpackers.com's older review noted it as a high price for a highly engineered pillow, and retailers have since moved the list price up rather than down. Street prices on secondhand and closeout listings have run noticeably lower.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About 80 g, under a third of the weight of the standard Fillo
- Packs to roughly the size of a lime
- Baffled air cell holds a stable shape with no balloon effect
- Micro-adjustable valve makes fine tuning of firmness easy
- Removable, machine-washable jersey cover
- Insulated top feels softer and warmer than bare air pillows

**Cons**

- Limited loft for side sleepers
- Thin cushioning compared with the foam-topped Fillo
- Pricey at $59.95
- One owner reported the valve opening accidentally during sleep
- 15 in length is compact; sleepers who roll around will notice it

## Who Should Buy This

The Fillo Elite suits thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who sleep mostly on their backs, or on their sides with a thick pad and a stuffed jacket underneath for extra height. It is also a sensible first pillow for gram-counters who have been using a clothes bag and want something better for roughly 2.8 oz. Dedicated side sleepers, and anyone who likes a deep, plush pillow, should look at the 9.3 oz standard Fillo or at NEMO's Elite Wide, which weighs about 4 oz and gives restless sleepers more surface area, though it too offers limited loft. Anyone who is happy with a stuffed fleece should skip it.

## Verdict

The Fillo Elite is among the best answers to the ultralight pillow problem: light, tiny, and shaped well enough that it feels like a pillow rather than a compromise. It is not the answer for side sleepers who need height, and the price stings. Back sleepers who count grams should buy it.
