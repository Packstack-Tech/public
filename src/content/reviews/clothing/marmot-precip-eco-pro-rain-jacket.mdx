## Overview

The PreCip Eco Pro is Marmot's step-up version of its long-running budget rain jacket. It swaps the standard PreCip's 2.5-layer build for a 3-layer NanoPro laminate made from recycled polyester, then adds mechanical stretch and pit zips. It suits three-season hikers who want a sturdier, more breathable shell without paying GORE-TEX money. The trade-off is weight: at 407 g (14.4 oz), it is a workhorse, not an ultralight piece.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The headline number is 20,000 mm of hydrostatic head, which puts the Pro well above the standard PreCip Eco. That jacket is rated at 10,000 mm and uses a 2.5-layer laminate. Owners who have worn both generally agree the Pro is the more weatherproof of the two. One reviewer on Marmot's site noted it is stretchier and more waterproof than the regular Eco. Another wore it for an hour in a downpour with the hood up and reported that the only water to get in came through pit zips they had forgotten to close. Fully taped seams and an adjustable hood that holds its position in wind help in sideways rain.

**Breathability.** Here the 3-layer build earns its weight. Reviewers describe the fabric as quieter and less clammy against the skin than the standard PreCip. One tester who hiked uphill in damp air expected the usual sticky interior and didn't get it. The pit zips handle the rest on climbs. The 3-layer face and liner combination also avoids the sweat-trapping laminated interior that CleverHiker's testers criticized on the 2.5-layer Eco.

**Fit and features.** The cut is generous. Several buyers say it runs slightly large to fit layers underneath, and one suggests sizing down if the length bothers you. At 30 inches center back, it covers the hips. Large hand pockets earn repeated praise for carrying gloves and a phone, and the jacket works as a shell over a fleece or light insulation on cold mornings. The mechanical stretch keeps it from binding when reaching or scrambling.

**Durability.** This is the Pro's weak spot. REI reviewers who have owned several PreCips over the years describe the outer fabric holding up while the interior liner eventually breaks down. Two separate owners reported the lining failing, and one called it unexpected at this price. Another said the jacket soaked through after light rain on a trip to Portugal, though most buyers report staying dry in light to moderate rain. Marmot's own listing carries a 4.7 average across 33 reviews, with the occasional lower score, and one 3-star buyer complained that the Pro doesn't stuff into its own pocket the way the standard Eco does.

**Weight.** Ultralight hikers will notice the figure. At 407 g, it is a good deal heavier than the shells in the 6-to-10 oz range that most thru-hikers carry. Even Marmot's own PreCip Eco is lighter. What the extra mass buys is fabric thickness, stretch, and a more comfortable feel against the skin. It is not a trade that suits a gram-counting base weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 3-layer NanoPro fabric with a 20,000 mm waterproof rating
- Pit zips and a breathable laminate that reviewers find less clammy than the standard PreCip
- Fully taped seams and an adjustable hood that stays put in wind
- Mechanical stretch and large, usable hand pockets
- PFC-free C0 DWR and recycled face fabric
- Often found well below list price

**Cons**

- 407 g is heavy by ultralight standards
- Does not pack into its own pocket
- Some owners report the interior liner breaking down over time
- Generous cut that can read long and boxy
- Occasional reports of wetting through in light rain

## Who Should Buy This

The Pro suits weekend backpackers, day hikers, and cold-weather layerers who want a stretchy shell that breathes better than a budget 2.5-layer jacket. It also works for anyone who wears a rain jacket on commutes and trail runs and values pockets and a roomy cut. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look at lighter shells, since this jacket's weight and non-stuffable design work against long-distance packing. Anyone planning to rely on one jacket for a decade should also expect the liner to be the first thing to go.

## Verdict

The PreCip Eco Pro is a comfortable, well-featured shell that delivers more breathability and protection than the standard PreCip. It costs less than most premium alternatives, and owners report it works well in light to moderate rain. It loses points for its weight and for the liner failures some long-term owners describe, so it is a good value rain jacket rather than a good ultralight one.
