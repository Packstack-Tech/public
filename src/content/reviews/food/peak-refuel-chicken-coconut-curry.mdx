## Overview

Peak Refuel's Chicken Coconut Curry is a freeze-dried pouch meal of white-meat chicken, instant rice, carrots, onion and spinach in a red-curry coconut sauce. It's built for backpackers who want real meat and a lot of protein at the end of a long day. The trade-offs are price and richness. At $14.99, it's a luxury item, and the coconut-heavy sauce carries a lot of saturated fat.

## Performance

**Calorie density.** At 850 calories in 152 g, the pouch works out to roughly 158 calories per ounce. That is a respectable figure for a cooked dinner and comes mostly from coconut milk and canola oil. Protein is the headline. At 44 g per pouch, it sits well above what most freeze-dried dinners deliver. Hikers who track protein on multi-day trips tend to cite this as the reason Peak Refuel earns pack space. One hiker writing about a Georgia loop trip noted that the brand generally runs higher in protein than its competitors.

**Prep.** Instructions are standard. Tear the pouch, pull the oxygen absorber, add 1⅓ cups of boiling water, stir, zip and wait 10 minutes. A Trailspace reviewer who followed the directions found the rice, chicken and carrot chunks rehydrated fully and came out tender. Because you eat from the pouch, there is no pot to scrub. That's a real convenience on a cold, wet evening.

**Flavor and texture.** Owner feedback is consistently positive. The same Trailspace reviewer rated it 4.5 out of 5 and said the quality was close to restaurant and homemade curries. That reviewer also found it a "dry" curry rather than a soupy one and suggested a couple of extra tablespoons of water for a saucier result. Hunters and backpackers in a long-range forum thread described it as filling without sitting heavy in the stomach. One said it was spicy enough to wake you up. Another warned that the coconut flavor is strong, so anyone who tolerates coconut poorly should skip it.

**Nutrition trade-offs.** The fat comes to 44 g per pouch, and the Trailspace reviewer put saturated fat at around 30 g. For a hiker burning 4,000-plus calories a day, that fat is useful fuel. For anyone watching saturated fat, it is a lot in one sitting. Sodium is moderate by freeze-dried standards, at roughly 480 mg per serving.

**Portion reality.** The pouch is rated for two servings. A hungry solo hiker will usually treat it as one dinner, and 850 calories is a sensible target for that. Someone sharing it between two people on a short trip will want sides or a snack to round it out.

**Cost.** This is the main complaint. Even reviewers who loved it called it a little pricey. Retail is $14.99, with occasional discounts at some retailers. A hiker on a long trail with a food budget will likely pack a handful of these as treat dinners and fill the rest of the week with cheaper staples. Hikers who've reported on the brand say much the same: it's a luxury, and cost and bulk usually limit how many go in the food bag.

**Shelf life.** Peak Refuel lists a 10-year shelf life. That is plenty for a bounce box or an emergency stash.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 44 g of protein and 850 calories in a 152 g pouch
- Real white-meat chicken, with rice and vegetables that rehydrate fully in 10 minutes
- Eat-from-the-pouch prep with no pot to clean
- Strong flavor, with owners ranking it among the better freeze-dried curries
- Sits comfortably in the stomach, according to forum users
- 10-year shelf life and made in the USA

**Cons**

- $14.99 per pouch is steep for a thru-hiker's daily budget
- About 30 g of saturated fat per pouch, mostly from coconut
- Strong coconut flavor will put off some eaters
- Leans dry rather than saucy unless you add a little extra water
- Contains coconut and soy
- Two servings on the label, but one hungry hiker will finish it alone

## Who Should Buy This

This meal suits section hikers, weekend backpackers and thru-hikers who want one reliably good hot dinner a few times a week. It is especially useful for anyone who needs protein for recovery after big-mileage days. It's also a good pick for a cold camp where a hot, fatty meal does real work. Anyone who dislikes coconut should skip it. So should hikers on a tight food budget and anyone limiting saturated fat. Those buyers are better served by cheaper rice-and-bean dinners or by one of the brand's non-coconut entrées.

## Verdict

Peak Refuel's Chicken Coconut Curry delivers real chicken, a flavorful sauce and an unusually high 44 g of protein in a 152 g pouch that prepares in 10 minutes. Its cost and saturated-fat load keep it from being an everyday staple. It earns a spot in the food bag as a dependable treat dinner for anyone who likes coconut curry.
