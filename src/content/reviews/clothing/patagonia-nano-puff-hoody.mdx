## Overview

The Nano Puff Hoody started life as a climber's belay jacket and has since become the default synthetic puffy for anyone who wants one layer to cover camp, travel and the walk to the pub. It pairs 60-g PrimaLoft Gold Insulation Eco with a thin recycled ripstop shell, and it keeps its loft when damp in a way down can't. The trade-off is weight and bulk: lighter, warmer and more compressible options exist, and the Nano Puff is not a jacket to wear while hiking uphill.

## Performance

### Warmth

Owners place the Nano Puff's comfort zone in the mid-40s to mid-50s Fahrenheit for stop-and-go activity. With a decent base layer it stretches into the low 40s, roughly where synthetics like the Arc'teryx Atom sit. The 60-g fill is a moderate load, and the PrimaLoft Gold Eco is a good down-mimicking synthetic, but it is not efficient by ultralight standards. Testers at Better Trail note that the Patagonia Micro Puff and the Enlightened Equipment Torrid deliver more warmth for less weight, thanks to more advanced fills and thinner shells. Gram-counters wanting maximum warmth per ounce are better served by a down jacket.

### Wet weather

This is the jacket's strongest argument. The shell's DWR sheds mist and light snow, and when the face fabric does wet out, the insulation keeps working instead of collapsing into a clammy mat. Reviewers who have used it across seasons rank this above everything else it does. It is still a puffy, not a rain jacket. In a sustained downpour, the Nano Puff belongs under a shell, where its flat quilting and thin fabrics keep it from bunching.

### Breathability and active use

The Nano Puff overheats quickly once the trail steepens. It works as a belay jacket, a camp layer, an early-morning warmup or a midlayer for skiing and other low-output days. Hikers who want something to wear while moving are better off with a sheet-insulated or hybrid piece like the Patagonia Nano-Air or Arc'teryx Proton.

### Fit, mobility and features

The latest revision brought a roomier shoulder cut, articulated elbows and a larger hood. Better Trail's tester found noticeably less restriction, especially in reach-heavy movements like climbing, though the fabric is not stretchy. The hood is the other notable change: the old small, elastic-rimmed opening gave way to a wider one with a stiffened brim, which improves side-to-side visibility. Outdoor Gear Lab testers found the hood well fitted enough to go without a beanie. It has no adjustment, so it won't cinch down against wind on its own, and it fits best under a helmet or over a thin hat. The handwarmer pockets hold gloves, keys and a phone, and an internal storm flap and a chin-height zipper garage add small comforts on longer walks.

### Packability

Here the Nano Puff disappoints. Patagonia designed the chest pocket as a stuff sack, but testers at Outdoor Gear Lab found it challenging to pack into, and Better Trail's reviewer couldn't fit it in the pocket at all. Stuffed into its own hood, it ends up about the size of a football. That is fine for a daypack or carry-on, but it takes more space than the best ultralight synthetic pieces.

### Durability and sustainability

Long-term owners report few problems. One reviewer with four years of regular wear saw no tearing, fraying or trouble in the wash. Occasional complaints involve loose stitching and zipper wear after heavy use. The shell, lining and insulation are all recycled, the DWR is PFAS-free, and the jacket is sewn in a Fair Trade Certified factory.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same fabrics, insulation and features. What differs is weight and cut.

**Women's (306 g / 10.8 oz).** This is 46 g lighter than the men's and cut closer to the body. Reviewers describe the fit as flattering rather than puffy, with enough length to cover the hips and stay put under a pack hipbelt. Better Trail's tester, in a women's small, got full wrist coverage and a hem below the waist. The cut is trim, though. Outdoor Gear Lab found the hood very fitted, and a recurring complaint from REI reviewers is a tight chest and hips on fuller builds. Those wanting to layer a thick sweater underneath should try a size up.

**Men's (352 g / 12.4 oz).** This version has a boxier, casual-athletic cut, with room for a base layer plus a light fleece or vest. Owners on Trailspace say it fits true to size, though narrow-shouldered or lean wearers may want to size up when layering. Across both versions, long-time owners say the current generation runs tighter than older Nano Puffs, so anyone upgrading should try it on first.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Insulation keeps working when damp, which suits shoulder-season mountain weather
- Revised shoulders and articulated elbows reduce restriction
- Low-bulk quilting sits flat under a shell
- Hood fits well without a beanie
- Recycled shell, lining and fill; PFAS-free DWR; Fair Trade Certified
- Durable over years of regular use, with a polished look that works in town

**Cons**

- Overheats on sustained aerobic effort
- Hood and cuffs can't be adjusted
- Awkward to stuff into its own pocket; football-sized when packed
- Weight-to-warmth trails the Micro Puff and similar jackets
- $299 for the men's is a lot for a jacket that Outdoor Gear Lab rated as only okay for its price
- Sizing has shifted between generations, and the women's cut is snug for some

## Who Should Buy This

The Nano Puff suits backpackers and travelers who want one durable jacket for shoulder-season camping, wet-climate hiking, climbing or ski belays and everyday wear. It is especially useful where a down jacket's moisture vulnerability matters. Thru-hikers and ultralighters chasing the lowest base weight should look at the Micro Puff or a down jacket, and anyone needing an insulator to wear while climbing hard should consider an air-permeable piece like the Nano-Air.

## Verdict

The Nano Puff Hoody is a tough, versatile and moisture-tolerant synthetic midlayer that earns its place as a camp and travel jacket. It is not the warmest or most packable option for its weight, and $299 buys more efficiency elsewhere. Buy it for durability and all-round use, not for ultralight mileage.
