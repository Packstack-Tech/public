## Overview

Nobite Skin Spray is a 100 ml pump-spray repellent built around 50% DEET, sold in European pharmacies and marketed for tropical travel and heavy mosquito country. It is the strongest concentration in Nobite's skin lineup, and the headline trade-off is that the percentage on the label doesn't tell the whole story: an independent German test found that the manufacturer's dosing advice left mosquito protection well short of the advertised seven hours.

## Performance

**Mosquitoes.** The label promises up to seven hours. Stiftung Warentest, which tested ten mosquito-and-tick sprays for its May 2025 issue, saw something different: Nobite held off mosquitoes for only about 2.5 to 3 hours. The testers traced the gap to the manufacturer's dosing recommendation at the time, which was roughly a quarter of what most competing sprays call for. In other words, the chemistry is strong and the instructions were stingy. Mosquito protection was rated only sufficient, and the spray finished with an overall "satisfactory" (3.4).

Owners tend to report better outcomes, which fits with applying more than the label suggests. One UK buyer described a week in Africa with a handful of bites at most, and a German traveler who used it on three tours reported no bites at all. Applying generously and evenly, and reapplying every three hours when temperatures top 30°C as the retailer guidance advises, is the sensible approach.

**Ticks.** This is where the spray earns its keep. Stiftung Warentest scored its tick protection "very good," ahead of its mosquito result, which makes it a credible option for brushy European trails in tick season. Nobite's separate clothing spray is designed to pair with it for treated layers; the skin spray itself shouldn't go on fabric.

**Feel and smell.** DEET at this concentration is not subtle. Owners describe the odor as tolerable for a repellent, and the pump delivers an even mist. DEET also dissolves plastics, so the manufacturer warns against contact with nail polish, glasses and watch crystals. That matters to hikers whose kit is mostly synthetic: watch bezels, sunglasses, trekking-pole grips and phone cases are all in range. The liquid is also flammable and irritating to eyes, so it stays off the face except by hand application, and away from the stove.

**Health.** All ten sprays in the German test received no better than "satisfactory" on health, citing allergenic potential and eye irritation from actives and fragrances. For pregnancy and young children, the manufacturer lists the product as suitable from age two, though pharmacy and retailer guidance is more conservative, limiting use for children up to 12 and for pregnant women to areas with disease risk, and for the latter generally after the first trimester. Anyone in that group should check with a pharmacist before packing it.

**Weight and packing.** At 132 g for 100 ml, it's heavier than decanting a smaller amount into a sub-30 ml pump, but a full bottle clears carry-on limits and typically lasts a week or two of daily use. The 100 ml size is a practical match for a long-weekend section hike or a one-bag tropical trip; a thru-hiker covering weeks of mosquito country will go through several. Street price in Germany runs around €11, with pharmacy and marketplace prices higher.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 50% DEET, the highest concentration among the ten sprays in Stiftung Warentest's 2025 comparison
- "Very good" tick protection in independent testing
- Broad coverage: mosquitoes, ticks, sand flies and biting flies
- Carry-on-legal 100 ml bottle; three-year shelf life
- Labeled for children from age two
- Widely stocked in pharmacies and online across Europe

**Cons**

- Independent testing found only 2.5–3 hours of mosquito protection at the recommended dose
- Seven-hour claim doesn't hold up under the original dosing guidance
- Dissolves plastics, nail polish, lenses and watch crystals
- Strong smell, flammable, and can irritate eyes
- 132 g per 100 ml is heavy for the volume

## Who Should Buy This

Travelers heading to tropical or high-pressure mosquito regions, and European hikers who mainly worry about ticks, will get the most from it, provided they apply it generously and reapply on a schedule. It suits a pharmacy-run before a trip when a DEET product is wanted and buying locally is easy. Hikers who want a gentler formula should look at Nobite's own 30% icaridine Sensitive spray, which is listed as suitable from two years and for pregnancy. Anyone who counts grams or who wants a spray to carry through weeks of backcountry mosquito season will be better served by a larger, lighter-per-ml bottle or a lotion in a squeeze tube.

## Verdict

Nobite Skin Spray has the concentration and the tick performance to justify a place in a tropical or tick-country kit, but its mosquito protection is shorter than the label suggests unless the user doses it far more generously than the instructions imply. Treat it as a roughly three-hour repellent, protect the plastics, and it does the job.
