## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is a 100% silk, mummy-shaped sleeping bag liner that weighs 110 g and packs to half a liter. It's built for backpackers who want a cleaner bag and a small warmth bump without paying boutique silk prices. The trade-off is fit: the cut is narrow, and the silk is more workmanlike than luxurious.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 110 g, the MT500 undercuts Cocoon's silk mummy liners, which a Canadian reviewer put at 160 g. That's 50 g saved for a comparable fabric. The liner stuffs into a small attached pouch, and the same reviewer said it ends up so compact that it travels in his bag by default. For thru-hikers counting every ounce, the 3.9 oz figure is the headline. It is a lot less than a fleece liner and not far from a bare-minimum sheet.

**Warmth.** Decathlon says the liner adds roughly 1.5 to 2°C to a sleeping bag's rating. That's a modest number, and it's the right one to plan around. The liner takes the chill off a bag that is just short of the night's temperature. It won't turn a 10°C bag into a 0°C bag. Silk's real contribution to warmth is cutting the clammy, sticky contact between skin and nylon liner fabric, which makes a cool night feel less miserable. Treat it as a comfort margin, not a replacement for a warmer quilt.

**Hygiene.** This is where silk earns its place. The liner takes the sweat, sunscreen and trail grime so the bag doesn't. Decathlon points out that washing a bag less often preserves its loft and durability. The MT500 is machine washable, which makes it far easier to clean after a week of hostel or hut nights than a down bag. Owners also use it alone in huts and cottages, where a clean sheet layer is the point.

**Comfort and fit.** Reviewers describe the MT500's silk as slightly coarse by silk standards, nicer than the slippery, noisy nylon of a typical bag, but not the glassy hand of a silk shirt. The mummy cut is the bigger issue. One 6'3" (190 cm) user found the seam high and the footbox narrow enough to make turning over a chore. He eventually cut the seam open and used it as a flat sheet with a small footbox, which he found much more comfortable. Side sleepers and restless sleepers should expect the same tightness. Those who sleep still in a mummy bag will find the shape matches their bag and adds little bulk.

**Durability.** Silk has a reputation for fragility, but the MT500 has held up better than expected. After close to a year and an estimated 50-plus nights, the fabric looked nearly new, though the stitching started to unravel. That's a fixable seam problem, not a fabric failure. The same reviewer ranked a Cocoon ripstop silk liner above it and kept the Forclaz as his beater. Pack it away from rough zippers and sharp pad valves and it should last.

**Value.** Retail pricing varies by market. In the US it has been listed at $40, down from a $59.99 list price. A Canadian reviewer originally paid about $55. Customer ratings are consistently high: 4.5 across nearly 600 reviews on Decathlon's UK page, and 4.6 to 4.7 across roughly 670 reviews on its Indian and US pages.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 110 g, 50 g lighter than a Cocoon silk mummy liner
- Packs to 0.5 L with an included pouch
- Machine washable, which keeps a bag's insulation cleaner for longer
- Silk feels better than bag nylon against bare skin
- Fabric has proven durable over about 50+ nights
- Inexpensive for real silk

**Cons**

- Narrow mummy cut is restrictive for taller or restless sleepers
- Silk is on the coarse side
- Stitching can unravel with extended use
- Only about 1.5–2°C of added warmth
- No ripstop weave; ranked below Cocoon's version by one long-term user

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who use a mummy bag, sleep fairly still, and want an inexpensive way to keep the bag clean and gain a couple degrees of comfort. It also suits hut-to-hut and hostel travelers who need a sheet that stuffs into a pocket. Tall side sleepers and anyone who dislikes a snug footbox should look elsewhere, ideally at a rectangular or roomier liner. Cold sleepers needing a real temperature bump should buy a warmer bag or a fleece or thermal liner instead.

## Verdict

The MT500 delivers what silk liners are for: a cleaner bag, a smoother feel and a small warmth cushion, all in 110 g. The tight cut and unfinished seams keep it from being flawless. At its price, though, it's an easy recommendation for mummy-bag users.
