## Overview

The Selva 120 is the lightest underquilt in Cumulus's Polish-made hammock line: a ¾-length, 850-fill goose down quilt that weighs 250 g and packs into a 1.5-liter stuff sack. It is designed for summer nights and shoulder-season trips where a hammock's cold-bottom problem needs solving without adding a pound to the pack. The trade-off is built into the name. With 120 g of down, it is a warm-weather piece, and it makes no pretense of stretching into frost.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Cumulus quotes a comfort rating of roughly 9°C and a limit near 4°C, and the company is explicit that those figures were measured together with its Taiga 150 topquilt. They describe a hammock system, not an underquilt in isolation. The brand's own guidance is that the Selva 120 suits nights that stay clearly above freezing and is not meant for cold sleepers. Hammock campers weighing up to the Selva 250 have made the same practical point: it is easier to vent a warm underquilt on a mild night than to coax extra warmth from a thin one on a cold night. The 120 rewards people who know their summer and shoulder-season nights well.

**Weight and packability.** At 250 g, with 120 g of that being down, the quilt is mostly air and a thin shell. Toray Airtastic at 19 g/m² is among the lighter fabrics used in down gear, and the 18 × 11 cm stuff sack is smaller than a water bottle. Next to the Selva 250 at 480 g and the Selva 300 at 575 g, the 120 is roughly half the weight of its nearest sibling, which is the main reason to buy it.

**Fit and coverage.** The ¾ cut covers shoulders to knees, a common approach in ultralight hammock insulation. The quilt is 150 cm long, so the lower legs and feet rely on the pad, a pack or a pair of insulated socks. Cumulus lists a maximum user height of 205 cm for the Standard. The horizontal chamber layout is meant to keep down from migrating to the sides as the hammock sways, and the DWR finish helps shed light condensation, though down remains down in sustained wet.

**Suspension.** The Selva line ships with an elastic cord and line-lock system, and owners on Hammock Forums have found the provided shock cord generous to a fault, with sparse setup guidance. Their consensus for the Selva 120 is that a thinner, lighter cord is plenty and that a single run of cord on each end, tied off near the hammock's own suspension, does the job. One owner reports that running secondary suspension around the sides keeps the quilt tighter against a Hennessy hammock. Another says a friend ditched that approach on a different hammock. Expect a bit of tuning on the first night out. Once the quilt is snug against the hammock without crushing the loft, Cumulus says it should stay put without constant readjustment.

**Build.** Cumulus makes the quilt in Poland and backs it with a lifetime warranty. Standard builds ship in a matter of days, while custom configurations are made to order.

## Choosing a Version

Two versions are offered. The **Standard** (250 g) is the one most hammock campers should buy: the 150 cm length, 110/95 cm width and 205 cm height limit suit average to tall users in a typical single hammock, and it is built and shipped quickly. The **xWide (Custom)** (330 g) is a made-to-order cut, with Cumulus listing a manufacturing time of three to five weeks against a few business days for the Standard. It adds 80 g and is meant for broader sleepers and wider hammocks, where a standard-width quilt would leave the sides of the shoulders exposed. If a Standard hangs snugly around you in your hammock, the extra 80 g buys nothing.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 250 g with 850 FP down, about half the weight of the Selva 250
- Packs to 1.5 L
- Horizontal chambers keep the down spread across the quilt
- Toray Airtastic shell with DWR, Polish-made, lifetime warranty
- Suspension included, adjustable from inside the hammock
- Custom xWide option for wider sleepers

**Cons**

- Comfort rating of ~9°C applies only when paired with a topquilt, and the limit of ~4°C is the practical floor
- ¾ length leaves the feet to a pad or pack
- The stock shock cord is heavier than this quilt needs, so ultralight owners often swap it
- Premium price for a summer-only piece
- xWide takes weeks to build

## Who Should Buy This

The Selva 120 suits summer hikers, bikepackers and shoulder-season hammock campers who sleep warm and don't expect nights below roughly 9°C. It pairs naturally with a light topquilt such as Cumulus's own Taiga 150 and the Mugga hammock, and it fits ultralighters who want to keep a hammock kit under a kilo. Anyone who camps into early spring or late fall, sleeps cold, or wants a single underquilt for three seasons should look at the Selva 250 or 300 instead and accept the extra 230 to 325 g.

## Verdict

The Selva 120 does one job at a very low weight, and it does it in a package that stuffs into a space smaller than a liter-and-a-half. It is easy to recommend for warm-weather hammock trips and just as easy to pass over for anything cooler. Buy it knowing that the rating assumes a topquilt on top.
