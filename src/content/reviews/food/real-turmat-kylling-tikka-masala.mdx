## Overview

The Real Turmat Kylling Tikka Masala (Chicken Tikka Masala) is a <cite index="4-3">vacuum-packed, gluten-free freeze-dried meal</cite> from the Norwegian brand Real Turmat, <cite index="22-2,22-4">made in Tromsø</cite>. At 133g dry, it rehydrates to a <cite index="4-4">generous 500g</cite> serving — enough to call it a full dinner on most trips. It sits in Real Turmat's standard lineup as a go-to option for hikers, paddlers, or anyone who wants an actual meal after a long day out, not just warm paste in a bag.

## Performance

### Taste and Texture

This is where Real Turmat earns its reputation. <cite index="27-34,27-35">Real Turmat's meals are freeze-dried, which means ingredients retain their shape and structure better than other forms of dehydration — the end result is meals that taste more like real meals, and often have better texture and mouthfeel.</cite> The Tikka Masala specifically is built on a solid ingredient list: <cite index="1-3">a combination of rice, chicken, lentils, coconut milk, and spices.</cite> <cite index="2-2,2-3">The gently cooked chicken combines well with the spicy and creamy tikka masala sauce, and the dish is rounded off with a touch of lime juice for subtle freshness.</cite> Ginger shows up at 0.3% and garlic is in there too — this isn't a timid spice profile. It's not blow-your-hat-off hot, but it has actual depth.

User feedback across retail sites backs this up. <cite index="8-2">One reviewer noted it was "very tasty and much quicker to prepare than other leading brands."</cite> Independent outlet Outdoors Magic ranked <cite index="21-2">Real Turmat among the four best-tasting brands in the freeze-dried meal category.</cite>

### Prep and Packaging

<cite index="2-10,2-11">
  Tear off the top of the bag, add hot water while stirring, close the bag, and
  wait 8–10 minutes.
</cite>
That's it. The bag has an external water fill line, so you don't need to measure
anything out — a small but genuinely useful detail on cold nights.
<cite index="27-40,27-41,27-42">
  Real Turmat packages are vacuum-packed, which has two benefits: pack size is
  smaller, and no oxygen absorber is needed.
</cite>
Practically, this means the pouch takes up less room in your food bag than
comparable meals. Worth noting:
<cite index="26-3">rehydration times can be optimistic in practice</cite> — in
cold weather, I'd let it sit a few minutes longer than the stated time and keep
the bag insulated (a fleece works fine).

Cold-water prep is also possible: <cite index="2-12">if you use cold water, extend the preparation time to more than 30 minutes.</cite> Doable in a pinch, but this is a meal that deserves boiling water.

### Calorie Density

At 579 kcal from 133g dry, this lands at roughly 4.35 kcal/g — comfortably above the common ultralight food benchmark of 3.5 kcal/g (100 kcal/oz), but not in the territory of high-fat options that push past 5 kcal/g. For a chicken-and-rice format, that's a fair result. Hungrier hikers doing high-mileage days will probably want to supplement with a side of something calorie-dense — a handful of nuts, an extra bar. As a standalone dinner on a moderate day, 579 kcal is workable.

The 19g of protein is a solid number, and <cite index="2-17">the rice and lentils provide a pleasant texture and make the meal extra filling</cite> — lentils do some of the satiety work that pure rice alone can't match.

## Who Should Buy This

This is the meal for the hiker who's already sorted their shelter and sleep system and now wants to stop treating dinner like a fuel-loading exercise. If taste and texture matter to you — especially on longer trips where morale is real — Real Turmat's Tikka Masala is worth the price premium. It's also a solid pick for expedition preppers given the 5-year shelf life and compact vacuum-packed footprint. If you're purely optimizing for calories-per-dollar or need a dairy-free option, look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Real Turmat Kylling Tikka Masala is one of the most convincingly meal-like freeze-dried pouches I've come across — the lime, ginger, and coconut milk hold up after rehydration in a way that cheaper meals don't. The calorie density is honest and the 8-minute prep is genuinely fast. The price is a real conversation, and at 579 kcal it's not a meal for a 30-mile day without backup snacks — but for what it is, it delivers. **Rating: 7.5/10.**
