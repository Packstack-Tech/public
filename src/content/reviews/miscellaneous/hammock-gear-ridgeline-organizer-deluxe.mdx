## Overview

The Ridgeline Organizer Deluxe is Hammock Gear's updated version of a category staple: a mesh-and-fabric pocket panel that slides onto your hammock's structural ridgeline and hangs overhead, keeping your phone, headlamp, glasses, and other fidgety items within arm's reach instead of sliding down into your quilt at 2 a.m. The "Deluxe" update adds a second size option and a party trick — the whole thing unzips (or rather, unfolds) into a double-ended stuff sack roomy enough to pack your hammock body. It's aimed at hammock campers who are tired of digging around the bottom of their hammock for a headlamp in the dark.

## Performance

The core function here is dead simple — it's a pocket panel that rides your ridgeline — but Hammock Gear's execution is what separates it from the $11 no-name organizers you'll find on Walmart or eBay. The Velcro attachment <cite index="2-16">easily installs on a variety of commercially available hammocks while maintaining the organizer's shape</cite>, which matters more than it sounds like: a lot of cheaper organizers sag or bunch once the ridgeline is threaded through, especially once you load them up. Competing designs that use a threaded channel (like Dutchware's version) address sag a similar way, and reviewers of comparable channel-style organizers note they're <cite index="4-22">rigid enough to not sag when a phone, a charging brick, a headlamp, and glasses are placed in it</cite> — that's the bar this style of organizer needs to clear, and the Ridgeline Organizer Deluxe's Velcro-and-shape-retention approach seems built to do the same job.

Capacity-wise, the split between mesh and fabric pockets is a nice touch. Mesh pockets let you glance in and find things by sight (good for a headlamp or earbuds you need to grab fast), while the fabric pockets keep things like a wallet, keys, or a pocketknife from falling through or getting scuffed. Ten total pockets is genuinely a lot for something under 2 ounces — Hammock Gear's older single-size version was already praised as a "must-have" accessory that's <cite index="1-15">intended to store knick-knacks like your phone, headlamp, headphones, glasses, book, charger, or even a small water bottle</cite>, and the Deluxe redesign just adds more compartments and a size choice on top of that.

The two-size option is the most useful update. Model A (17" x 11") is the one you want if you're mostly stashing small, dense items and want deep pockets that won't spill when the hammock rocks. Model B (29.5" x 9") trades depth for length, spreading pockets out along more of the ridgeline — better if you want to separate categories of gear (electronics on one end, snacks and odds-and-ends on the other) rather than piling everything into one cluster over your chest.

The stuff-sack conversion is a clever bit of dual-use design, and on paper it's a nice way to make an accessory "free" from a base-weight standpoint since it replaces a stuff sack you'd be carrying anyway. In practice, dual-purpose gear like this always asks you to trade a little bit of optimization in both directions — a dedicated stuff sack is usually cut more efficiently for compression, and a dedicated organizer doesn't need to survive being cinched down around a hammock body repeatedly. I haven't run this through enough seasons to know how the Velcro and seams hold up to that double duty over time, so treat that as a question mark rather than a knock against it.

One general critique that comes up across this whole product category, including from reviewers comparing Hammock Gear's version against cheaper alternatives: the price. At $34.95, it's <cite index="5-18">more than double the price of</cite> some generic competitors that offer similar pocket counts. You're paying for US-made construction, the color customization, and a company that's specifically in the hammock business rather than a generic outdoor-gear importer — whether that premium is worth it depends on how much you value buying from a specialist brand versus optimizing purely for pockets-per-dollar.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely light at 1.6 oz for a 10-pocket organizer
- Two size options let you match the organizer to your hammock and gear load
- Mix of mesh and fabric pockets covers both quick-glance items and things you want tucked away
- Doubles as a stuff sack, potentially offsetting its own weight
- Velcro/ridgeline-channel attachment is designed to resist the sagging that plagues cheaper organizers
- 12 color options for the fabric pockets if you care about matching your setup

**Cons**

- Noticeably pricier than generic competitors with similar pocket counts
- Long-term durability of the stuff-sack conversion feature is untested by me and hard to verify from available reviews
- Ten pockets means more surface area to snag on branches or your hammock's bug net when packing up in the dark
- Only one confirmed independent review source (Hammock Gear's own site) — outside long-term user data is thin

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for hammock campers who already have a fixed or adjustable structural ridgeline and are tired of losing their phone or headlamp in their quilt overnight. If you carry more than two or three "keep it close" items — phone, headlamp, glasses, earplugs, snacks — the 10-pocket layout will feel like a real upgrade over the single mesh pouch that ships with a lot of stock hammocks. If you're a minimalist who only needs a place for a headlamp and nothing else, a cheaper 4-pocket organizer or a DIY option will get you there for less weight and less money.

## Verdict

The Ridgeline Organizer Deluxe does exactly what a ridgeline organizer should: keeps small gear accessible and off your chest, without adding meaningful weight. The two-size option and stuff-sack conversion are thoughtful upgrades over the standard single-size design, though you're paying a premium over generic alternatives for that refinement. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — a smart, well-made accessory for hammock campers willing to pay a bit more for a specialist brand's take on a simple problem.
