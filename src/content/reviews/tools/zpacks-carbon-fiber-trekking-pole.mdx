## Overview

<cite index="4-5">
  Zpacks' Carbon Fiber trekking poles are a telescoping trekking pole
  tailor-made for long backpacking trips and supporting the brand's trekking
  pole shelters.
</cite>
They sit squarely in the middle of Zpacks' pole lineup — heavier than the
stripped-down Minimalist but more comfortable for all-day use. At $60 per pole
(or $100 for a pair),
<cite index="24-9,24-10">
  these poles are well-rounded and sturdy, with two flip locks for easy
  adjustability — not the lightest on the market, but they strike a reasonable
  balance between weight, strength, and price.
</cite>

## Performance

### Weight & Packability

<cite index="24-14,24-15,24-16">
  These poles are impressively light — the foam grip version weighs in at just
  7.2 oz (205 g) per pole, including the wrist strap and basket. Strip the
  basket and rubber tip, and they drop to 6.3 oz (180 g).
</cite>
In isolation that sounds lean, but context matters.
<cite index="24-23,24-24,24-25">
  Zpacks sells these poles in pairs for $100, but they're heavier at 410 g /
  14.4 oz for two poles than several alternatives — for example, Black Diamond's
  Distance Carbon Z poles weigh 281 g / 9.9 oz for two poles, and Leki's
  Ultratrail FX.One comes in at 354 g / 12.5 oz.
</cite>
If raw weight is your top priority, you'll want to look elsewhere.

On packability: <cite index="17-25,17-26,17-27,17-28">the packed size is nothing special — they compress down to just shy of 25 inches, more than two feet. That's relatively large compared to folding poles like the Black Diamond Distance series, which can save you 10 or more inches of length. Still, it's on par with most telescoping designs.</cite> For backpacking, they'll fit most side pockets without issue. For trail running or flying, look at a folding pole.

### Adjustability & Locking

<cite index="24-20,24-21,24-22">
  The poles extend up to 54 inches (137 cm) to the "stop" line, and you can push
  them to 56 inches (142 cm) for pitching tents. When collapsed, they measure
  24.5 inches (62 cm). The sturdy clasp locks allow for easy hand-tightening and
  height adjustments.
</cite>
The additional 5 cm of tent-pitch range above the hiking max is a thoughtful
detail for Zpacks shelter users — it means one pole length genuinely serves both
your stride and your campsite.

The clasp locks work well in practice. <cite index="23-29,23-30,23-31,23-32,23-33,23-34">One long-distance user reported hiking about 200 miles with them — no complaints, no problems — and noted the locking mechanisms work well.</cite> That said, <cite index="21-1,21-2">with a thin carbon shaft, the Zpacks poles are more prone to snapping than aluminum poles, and the plastic lever locks are less durable than metal designs.</cite> It's not a deal-breaker for trail use, but it's worth knowing going in.

### Grip & Comfort

<cite index="1-15,1-16">
  There is the option of either EVA foam or cork handles: the EVA foam handles
  are slightly lighter, and the cork handles are slightly more comfortable —
  both are reasonable options.
</cite>
<cite index="17-34,17-35,17-36">
  You can choose either grip style at the same price. Cork custom molds to your
  hands over time and tends to be a bit more comfortable, though it adds roughly
  half an ounce per pole.
</cite>

<cite index="4-17,4-18,4-19">
  The poles feature padded wrist straps, and, best of all, cork grips —
  relatively unique at this price point. The natural material wicks moisture
  well and naturally molds to your hands over time.
</cite>
The foam-extended lower grip section on the shaft also lets you choke down on
steeper climbs without stopping to readjust height.

### Durability & Terrain Suitability

This is where the honest conversation lives. <cite index="4-8">Better Trail put these poles through hundreds of miles of use and abuse, including on the Wind River High Route — while theirs held strong, their relatively fragile nature requires extra care, making them best suited for on-trail hiking and backpacking.</cite> <cite index="17-12,17-13">Carbon fiber doesn't bend as aluminum does — it can snap if you wedge a pole between rocks or take a hard fall. One reviewer witnessed a hiking partner snap these very poles on a trip.</cite>

<cite index="21-10">
  Zpacks does offer replacement parts for most pole components, including tips,
  lower sections, and joints, which helps extend their lifespan.
</cite>
That's genuinely useful — being able to replace only a snapped lower section
instead of the whole pole is a practical edge over competitors with glued-in or
proprietary parts.

### Tent Compatibility

<cite index="18-6">
  In addition to being trekking poles, they can be used to set up all of Zpacks'
  Plex tents, excluding the Altaplex.
</cite>
The extra tent-pitch height ceiling of 142 cm is a key spec to cross-reference
against whatever shelter you're pairing these with — it lines up well with most
of Zpacks' own offerings.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 100% carbon fiber shaft keeps weight reasonable without a premium price tag
- Cork grip option is uncommon at this price point and genuinely gets more comfortable over time
- Clasp locks are reliable and hand-adjustable without tools
- Height adjustment marks for both hiking grip and tent pitch height — convenient in the dark
- Sold individually or as a pair; replacement lower sections available separately
- <cite index="18-14">
    Two-year limited warranty against defects in materials or workmanship
  </cite>
- <cite index="21-21">
    Manufactured in Florida, subject to U.S. labor laws and environmental
    regulations
  </cite>

**Cons**

- <cite index="4-7">
    Not as lightweight and packable as you might expect from ultralight-focused
    Zpacks
  </cite>
- Carbon fiber can snap rather than bend; not ideal for scrambling or rocky off-trail terrain
- <cite index="17-4">Lever locks have a cheap feel</cite> and are plastic rather
  than metal
- Telescoping design packs down to ~25 inches — not ideal if internal pack storage or airline carry-on matters
- Heavier than Zpacks' own Minimalist Pole (167 g vs. 205 g per pole) and several competing carbon options

## Who Should Buy This

This pole is a solid pick for the on-trail backpacker who lives in the Zpacks ecosystem — particularly anyone running a Plex shelter that needs a reliable, dual-purpose stick that can serve as both a hiking aid and a tent upright. <cite index="2-13,2-14">They're well-rounded and sturdy with two flip locks for easy adjustability — not the lightest on the market, but they strike a good balance between weight, strength, and price.</cite> If your trips stay on maintained trail and you want to avoid carrying dedicated shelter poles, these make a sensible pairing. Scramblers, bushwhackers, and off-trail route enthusiasts should look at something more rugged — or at least budget for a spare lower section.

## Verdict

The Zpacks Carbon Fiber Trekking Pole is a competent, well-priced telescoping pole that punches at a comfortable weight for the money — but don't mistake it for an ultralight category leader. <cite index="28-3,28-4,28-5">It's tailor-made for long backpacking trips and Zpacks' trekking pole shelters, and it's a great value at $100 for a pair considering the cork grip option and carbon build — but it's not as lightweight and packable as you'd expect from ultralight-focused Zpacks.</cite> Treat it kindly on trail, know its limits off it, and it'll serve you well for many hundreds of miles. **Rating: 7/10.**
