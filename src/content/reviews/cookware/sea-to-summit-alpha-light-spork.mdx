## Overview

Sea to Summit's Alpha Light is a one-piece spork stamped from hard-anodized 7075-T6 aluminum, with a spoon bowl at one end, fork tines at the other and a mini carabiner threaded through the handle. It comes in a 9 g standard length and a 12 g Long, and it is aimed at hikers who want a metal utensil that weighs about what a titanium one does, at a lower price. The trade-off is a flat-edged handle that does the job without winning any awards for feel.

## Performance

Weight is the headline. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers put the Long on the scale at 11.3 g, a shade under its claimed 12 g, against 17 g for the titanium sporks it was tested alongside. The standard 9 g version sits in the same weight neighborhood as the lightest titanium options, and the carabiner is already counted in the figure. Owners who have compared the Long with a 6.5-inch titanium spork note that it is two inches longer and still lighter.

Durability is the usual worry with aluminum this thin, and the reports are mostly reassuring. Long-term owners describe years of regular use with the finish still close to new. Outdoor Gear Lab did flag that the long shaft and thin metal make the Long slightly more prone to bending under heavy torque. Ordinary cooking and eating, the testers concluded, is well within its limits; prying lids and chipping ice is not. The hard anodizing forms a shell around the alloy that keeps aluminum out of the food, and it is the reason Sea to Summit says to keep the spork out of the dishwasher, where detergent can attack the finish. A rinse and a wipe at camp takes seconds, and the smooth surface cleans easily.

Comfort is where reviewers split. Because the utensil is stamped from sheet metal, the handle has a ridge down its length and flat side edges. Several reviewers flagged that those edges are not pleasant to grip through a long dinner, and Outdoor Gear Lab described the Long as somewhat unnatural in the hand while still scoring it well for strength and ease of cleaning. Nobody calls it a dealbreaker. It is a utensil for efficient eating rather than lingering.

The working end performs. The tines are sharp enough to spear noodles and potatoes, and the bowl is deep enough to function as a real spoon, though one owner of the Long wished it were a bit larger for soup. Aluminum also will not soften against a hot pot wall the way a plastic spork can. The mini carabiner is more useful than it looks: clipped to a pack strap, pot handle or bear canister, the spork stays out of the bottom of the food bag and is harder to leave behind at a campsite.

## Choosing a Version

The versions share the same alloy, finish, carabiner and basic shape. Handle length is what changes, and with it the meals each one suits.

**Alpha Light (9 g, 16.5 cm).** The standard spork suits the typical solo kit, where dinner is eaten from a bowl, mug or wide pot. It stows easily in a small pot or a pocket and is the lightest of the lineup. Hikers who cold-soak in a wide-mouth jar will also find it long enough.

**Alpha Light Long (12 g, 21.5 cm).** The extra 5 cm is there for freeze-dried pouches and tall, narrow pots. With a standard spork in a deep pouch, knuckles end up in the sauce; owners say the Long reaches the bottom of a Mountain House-style bag with a clean hand. The cost is 3 g and some packability: one owner found it a hair too long to sit neatly inside a shorter round bear canister, and it will not fit compact cook kits.

**Long (12 g, 21.5 cm).** Retailers also list this same 21.5 cm, 12 g utensil under the shorter name "Long", and it is the same spork as the Alpha Light Long. Anyone shopping for pouch meals can treat the two listings as interchangeable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 9 g and 12 g weights are in line with, or below, comparable titanium sporks
- Hard-anodized finish holds up to years of regular use, according to long-term owners
- Anodized shell keeps the alloy out of food, and the spork conforms to FDA food-safety standards
- Sharp tines handle noodles and potatoes
- Included mini carabiner makes it easy to clip on and hard to lose
- Will not soften against a hot pot, unlike plastic sporks
- Two lengths cover both bowl eaters and pouch eaters

**Cons**

- Flat, ridged handle from stamped construction gets uncomfortable over a long meal
- Not dishwasher safe, since detergent can damage the anodized finish
- The Long can bend under heavy torque and may not fit shorter bear canisters or compact cook kits
- The standard length will not reach the bottom of a tall freeze-dried pouch

## Who Should Buy This

This is the utensil for hikers building a lightweight kit who want a durable metal spork without titanium pricing. Choose the standard length if dinner happens in a bowl or wide pot, and the Long if most meals come in a pouch or a tall stove pot. Hikers who care most about hand feel through slow meals, or who want a rounded, ergonomic spoon handle, should look at a molded or machined spoon instead.

## Verdict

The Alpha Light delivers what a 9 or 12 g utensil should: it is light, it lasts, and the carabiner keeps it from wandering. The handle keeps it from being a pleasure to hold, but at this weight and price that is a small complaint. Take the standard for bowls and pots, and the Long for pouches.
