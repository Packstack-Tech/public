## Overview

The XOWOOL is XoSkin's cold-weather answer to its XOTOES line: a seamless, five-toe sock that swaps part of the brand's PTFE-and-copper yarn for merino-style wool. It is aimed at runners, hikers and thru-hikers who like toe socks for blister control and want something that handles shoulder-season temperatures. The trade-off is familiar to anyone who has worn the category: a minutes-long ritual getting each toe into its pocket, and a thin sock with limited cushioning.

## Performance

**Blister control.** The sock's reputation rests on its PTFE content, a low-friction fiber that XoSkin says reduces the coefficient of friction against skin, paired with the toe separation that keeps adjacent toes from rubbing. The evidence comes mostly from the original XOTOES, which shares the same base yarn. Ultrarunners in Ultra Running Magazine's comment threads describe wearing a single pair through wet 100-milers and finishing with intact feet. A Garage Grown Gear reviewer reported no blisters or hot spots through a Texas summer. Buyers of the wool version on XoSkin's own site report the same absence of hot spots.

**Temperature and moisture.** The wool version is where the sock separates itself from the standard XOTOES. One buyer reported warm feet down to the low 20s °F, with stream-crossing dampness that didn't chill. He also noted that the socks never got soggy on sweaty climbs. A CDT-bound hiker called the sock warm in the cold and cool in the heat. These are retailer-site reviews, which skew enthusiastic, but the pattern fits the blend: the PTFE and copper yarn moves moisture, and the wool holds warmth when damp. By comparison, a trail runner testing the non-wool XOTOES found them inadequate once temperatures dropped into the low 30s °F. That is the gap the wool version was built to fill.

**Odor.** Copper's antimicrobial properties are a selling point, and testers back it up. Ultra Running Magazine's reviewer noted almost no odor after several consecutive wears. For multi-day trips without laundry, that is a practical advantage.

**Durability.** The most useful data point comes from a hiker who put roughly 1,200 miles on a single pair while preparing for a CDT thru-hike. The heels had only started thinning, and one had a small hole. He rinsed and line-dried them every other day and skipped the liquid fabric softener XoSkin recommends. That is respectable for a thin sock of this weight, though it shows the heel is the wear point. The softener advice is also a nuisance for anyone who doesn't want to carry a bottle on the trail.

**Cushioning and fit.** The sock is thin. One buyer said the wool version has a bit more padding than the standard XOTOES, which improved comfort in his Altras. Even so, reviewers of the line describe it as unpadded, and thin enough to wear under a heavier sock. Fit is anatomically left and right, with a built-in arch band and heel pocket meant to keep the sock from migrating. Don't expect plush underfoot cushioning on rocky descents.

**Donning time.** Toe socks take longer to put on than regular socks. Reviewers say so even when they like the product, and this one is no exception.

## Choosing a Version

The three heights differ in cuff length, not in technology: all use the same yarn blend and toe design.

- **Quarter Crew:** The lightest option and the one that suits trail shoes and low-cut running shoes. It leaves the ankle exposed to debris, so gaiters or trail-runner use make sense.
- **Mid Crew:** More coverage for ankle protection from brush and some added warmth, without the bulk of a full crew.
- **Crew:** Best for boots, cold-weather hiking and shoulder-season trips, where calf coverage helps retain heat.

Buyers have asked for a no-show height, which doesn't exist, so everyday low-cut wear isn't an option.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Seamless toe-separated design with consistent reports of no blisters or hot spots
- Wool content extends the range into cold weather, with reports of comfort in the low 20s °F
- Strong odor resistance from copper yarn
- Made in the USA
- One pair reportedly lasted about 1,200 trail miles before the heels thinned
- Left/right-specific fit with arch and heel structure

**Cons**

- Thin cushioning; not the sock for heavily padded comfort
- Slow to put on, like all toe socks
- Care instructions call for liquid fabric softener, which is awkward on a long trail
- Heels are the wear point
- No no-show height

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and ultrarunners who already like toe socks, or who fight blisters between toes, will find the XOWOOL a strong cool-weather option. It suits those who want one sock that handles wet feet, sweat and cold without a long drying wait. Hikers who want a thick, cushioned boot sock should look elsewhere. So should anyone unwilling to tolerate the extra donning time, and anyone who wants a no-show cut.

## Verdict

The XOWOOL pairs a proven blister-fighting yarn with enough wool to make it useful on cold mornings, and it comes in at a weight that earns a place in a pack. Thin cushioning and heel wear keep it from being flawless, but for toe-sock converts it is one of the better three-season options. Recommended.
