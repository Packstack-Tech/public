## Overview

The Frontier UL Long Handle Spork is Sea to Summit's answer to a simple problem: the bottom of a freeze-dried meal pouch is deeper than a standard spoon is long. At 11 g and 21 cm, it is built for thru-hikers who eat dinner out of the bag or a rehydrating pot and want clean knuckles. The trade-off is a hard-anodized aluminum finish that some people love for its weight and others can't stand in their mouths.

## Performance

**Reach.** This is the point of the product. Retailers and reviewers pitch it at the same job: scraping the last bites out of a freeze-dried pouch and stirring a hot meal without a hand in the steam. Bikepacking and thru-hiking reviewers who live on dehydrated dinners name it as their pick among long-handle utensils, and one tester who used its predecessor, the AlphaLight long spork, for hundreds of meals calls the Frontier the one to carry on any freezer-bag trip. Standard sporks tend to leave mash on the fingers. This one doesn't.

**Weight.** At 11 g, it is a rounding error in a pack, and it matches the Vargo Titanium ULV on the scale while giving up several centimeters of the ULV's compactness. Against the 16 g Snow Peak Titanium Spork, it saves 5 g. Aluminum is why. Titanium is stronger, and aluminum will eventually bend under sustained abuse. For a single thru-hike of normal use, that rarely matters. A spork that gets pried at frozen ground or used as a tent-stake extractor is a different story.

**Durability and finish.** Hard anodizing forms a protective shell over the aluminum, which keeps the alloy from leaching into food. When it holds, it holds well: owners describe years of heavy use. The complaints cluster around the coating itself. A reviewer on REI's site, after four years of rinse-and-dry care and no dishwasher, reported that the anodizing appeared to fail while in storage. The surface turned chalky and left a residue, and the reviewer stopped using it. Several other buyers dislike the feel on the teeth, likening it to fingernails on a chalkboard. That sensory quirk is common to anodized aluminum cutlery, and it is a coin flip for first-time buyers. Anyone who has bitten down on an aluminum spoon and winced should stay away.

**Ergonomics.** Opinions split here too. Some buyers find the handle comfortable and the length right. One critic found the tight ribbing on the back of the handle hard to clean and the edges sharp, and said the design is a step down from the older AlphaLight line. The handle profile does let it stack with other Frontier cutlery pieces, which helps if a full set is already in the kit.

**Fit.** At 21 cm, it will not fit inside most backpacking pots with the lid on. It rides alongside the stove or in a side pocket instead. That is true of every long-handle utensil, but it is worth remembering for anyone building a tight cook kit.

**Ratings and price.** Sea to Summit's own page shows 4.3 out of 5 across 19 reviews, and REI's customer score is 4.1 across 34. Street price runs from $10.95 to $12.95. That is more than a plastic spork, though a lifetime guarantee softens it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 11 g, lighter than most titanium long-handle options
- 21 cm handle reaches the bottom of freeze-dried pouches without messy knuckles
- Lifetime guarantee from Sea to Summit
- Stacks with other Frontier cutlery
- Inexpensive for a metal utensil

**Cons**

- Anodized aluminum has a chalky, squeaky feel on teeth that some people can't tolerate
- A few long-term owners report the coating wearing down or turning powdery
- Softer than titanium, so it can bend under hard use
- Too long to fit inside most pots
- Hand wash only
- Handle ribbing and edges draw complaints from some users

## Who Should Buy This

The ideal buyer eats freeze-dried meals out of the bag, counts grams, and wants a cheap metal spork backed by a lifetime guarantee. It suits thru-hikers on resupply-box diets, alpine starts with in-the-bag breakfasts, and anyone replacing the discontinued AlphaLight. People who cook and eat from a pot, or who need a utensil that fits inside a nesting kit, should buy a short titanium spork. Anyone sensitive to the texture of anodized aluminum on their teeth should choose titanium or a long-handle plastic spoon.

## Verdict

The Frontier UL Long Handle Spork does the one job that matters, reaching the bottom of a meal pouch, at 11 g and about $11. The anodized finish is the gamble: it may feel fine for years or it may feel wrong on the first bite. For freezer-bag cooks, it is an easy, inexpensive buy.
