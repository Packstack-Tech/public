## Overview

Sea to Summit's Wilderness Wipes are thick, pre-moistened body wipes made from plant-based VEOCEL viscose. They're built for the stretch of trail where the nearest shower is days away and the nearest lake is a long way downhill. The trade-off is that a pack of 12 wipes is a luxury item with a real weight cost, and it's less useful on a long thru-hike than on a weekend trip.

## Performance

**Cleaning.** The wipes' main selling point is thickness. Compared with ordinary wet wipes, they're substantially thicker and stronger. An Outside writer who used them throughout an August packraft trip on Alaska's North Slope found they didn't shred or fall apart. Owners describe a soft, non-woven fabric that is nothing like the plasticky feel of cheap baby wipes. On REI, the wipes average 4.7 out of 5 across more than 250 reviews. One Grand Canyon rim-to-rim hiker said the wipes cut through the sweat on arms and legs. The same hiker said they left skin fresh enough for sleep, with little residual smell and no sticky film. A hiker who used them in 90-degree Utah heat, with four or five days between showers, reported the same result: dust and salt came off without a tacky feel.

**How far one wipe goes.** At 6 x 8 inches, one wipe covers a lot of skin. One Grand Canyon hiker got a full-body cleanup from a single wipe, though it was close to dry by the end. A Paddling Light reviewer who tested the XL version found that one wipe can cover face, underarms, groin and feet. He added that two work much better. Plan on two wipes for a proper wipe-down. A 12-pack is then six sessions, or a few days of use for a hiker who cleans up every night.

**Skin feel.** The pH-balanced, alcohol-free formula, with aloe vera and vitamin E, is the reason reviewers keep calling these gentle. Buyers who are prone to skin reactions to conventional wipes say these have worked for them. A reviewer who rated the XL version described them as moist without dripping.

**Weight and packaging.** The 12-pack is listed at 70.6 g, which works out to about 6 g per wipe. Some retailer listings give the weight as 93 g (3.3 oz). Either way, that is the weight of a luxury item. One REI reviewer wanted smaller, lighter packs. Another said the extra weight was worth it for the comfort. The resealable pouch is sturdy enough to survive a pack, but it isn't airtight. The maker says some evaporation happens once the pack is open. It suggests moving a small number of wipes into a zip bag or OPSAK to keep them moist. One buyer found that dirt sticks to the adhesive strip and weakens the seal. Wipes at the bottom of an open pack will dry out on a long trip.

**Disposal.** The VEOCEL fabric is plant-based and compostable, which sets these apart from the polyester-based wipes common elsewhere. That doesn't make them a trail-side disposal option. Sea to Summit asks users to follow Leave No Trace principles and pack the used wipes out. Hikers who treat them as pit-toilet or home-compost material are using them as the maker intends. Burying them in a cathole is a poor idea. Wipes used on the face and body are light, but the used ones still go in the trash bag. A critical REI reviewer claimed the product is made of synthetic fibers. The manufacturer says it is VEOCEL viscose.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Thick and durable; resists tearing in use
- 6 x 8 in size covers the whole body when two are used
- Alcohol-free, pH-balanced formula with aloe and vitamin E is gentle on skin
- Plant-based, compostable fabric
- Leaves little residue or stickiness, according to hikers in hot, dusty conditions
- Low price for a trail luxury

**Cons**

- About 6 g per wipe adds up quickly on multi-day trips
- Wipes dry out once the pack is opened, and the seal can get dirty
- Not individually wrapped, so no single-use grab-and-go option
- Used wipes must be packed out
- Replaces a real wash only in part

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend backpackers, section hikers and packrafters who want to get clean before getting into a sleeping bag will get the most from these. They suit desert and high-sweat trips, where salt and grit are the problem. They also work as a cleanup tool for travel and for hands and face. Hikers who want a larger wipe can look at Sea to Summit's XL version, which has eight 8 x 12 in wipes in a pack that weighs 122 g. Gram-counters on a long thru-hike, where resupply towns offer showers every few days, can skip them or bring a few wipes in a zip bag.

## Verdict

Sea to Summit's Wilderness Wipes do their job: they're thick, gentle and effective, and their plant-based fabric makes them one of the better options in the category. They aren't a base-weight item, and they need to be packed out. For the typical weekend or section-hike, they earn a place in the kit.
