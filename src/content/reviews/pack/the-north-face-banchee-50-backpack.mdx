## Overview

The Banchee 50 is The North Face's do-it-all backpacking pack, sized for weekend trips up to a week or so on trail. It splits the difference between the ultralight crowd and traditional load-hauling packs — a T6 aluminum frame and a wall of organizational pockets, at a weight that lands just a hair over the unofficial 3-lb "ultralight" line. It's a good fit for hikers who want more structure and pocket organization than a frameless UL pack offers, without stepping up to a full-on expedition hauler.

## Performance

**Weight and frame.** At 1,520 g, the Banchee 50 isn't going to win any spreadsheet contests against packs like the Osprey Exos or Gossamer Gear Mariposa, but that extra weight buys you a genuinely rigid carry. <cite index="8-6">The Banchee 50's wire perimeter frame with a cross stay, made of strong but lightweight T6 aluminum, flexes very little, lending the pack solid rigidity and impressive weight capacity for a three-pound sack.</cite> One reviewer who took it on a five-day, 56-mile trip through Yellowstone's Bechler Canyon with over 40 pounds of gear (including extra camera equipment) found <cite index="8-1,8-7">its carrying capacity seemed to be around 40 pounds—with more than that, the shoulder straps started tugging uncomfortably against his shoulders, but with slightly less than 40 pounds, the weight sat more comfortably on his hips.</cite> That's a solid ceiling for a pack this light, and it lines up with a Trailspace user who called it <cite index="7-5,7-7">"excellent weight-to-stability-and-durability ratio," noting the main rival, the Osprey Exos, "is not as stable as Banchee."</cite>

**Ventilation and comfort.** The back panel uses a trampoline-style mesh setup that a few reviewers specifically praised. <cite index="8-9">The trampoline-style back panel, consisting of breathable, quick-drying FlashDry fabric, let air circulate through the gap between the pack and the back, while moisture never built up under the breathable, perforated-foam shoulder straps and hipbelt.</cite> On the women's-specific version, the fit gets even more attention — <cite index="1-2">it's an adjustable-torso length backpack specifically designed to fit a female torso with breast-friendly shoulder straps and hip belt padding that adjusts to the curvature of your hips</cite>, and one reviewer said <cite index="1-6">the lumbar pad keeps the hip belt from slipping down her waist, keeping it comfortable all day.</cite>

**Organization.** This is where the Banchee separates itself from most UL packs — it's stuffed with pockets. One count put it at <cite index="10-3">8+ pockets (10 pockets counted), which will keep you organized on your next adventure</cite>, leading the same reviewer to joke that <cite index="10-13,10-14">the Banchee 50 liter backpack is truly a backpack for the highly organized — there are pockets on pockets then there are pockets within pockets.</cite> In practice that means dual hip-belt pockets, side water-bottle sleeves, vertical "beaver tail" pockets on the front, and a top lid — genuinely useful if you like grab-and-go access to snacks, a phone, or a filter without breaking stride. The trade-off, per a longtime Trailspace user, is that some of those pockets are small: side pockets are described as <cite index="7-27">"very small and suitable only for quick snacks,"</cite> and bottle pockets <cite index="7-28">aren't secured especially well and are less functional than they look.</cite>

**Durability.** This is a strong point across reviews. Carryology, which put both the 50 and 65L versions through repeated airline checked-baggage abuse, reported that <cite index="11-5,11-6">even after checking the bags multiple times, while they came out scuffed at the baggage carousel, everything on them held up perfectly.</cite> The 210D ripstop nylon is a sensible middle-ground fabric — not as delicate as UL Dyneema or X-Pac, but plenty tough for off-trail bushwhacking or getting tossed around in a vehicle trunk.

**Sizing quirks.** Fit is more finicky than it should be. A Trailspace reviewer noted <cite index="7-10,7-11">it was very difficult to find correct sizing instructions online, and while the straps are adjustable, you still have to find your correct size between the two offerings</cite>. If you're between sizes, budget extra time (or a return) to dial it in — this isn't a one-size-fits-most pack like some fully adjustable UL frames.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rigid, stable carry for a sub-3.5-lb pack — comfortably handles loads in the 30–40 lb range
- Excellent back ventilation via trampoline mesh panel
- Tons of pocket organization, including movable hip-belt pockets on women's models
- Durable 210D ripstop construction that shrugs off airline baggage handling and trail abuse
- Women's-specific version genuinely addresses torso and hip fit, not just a re-colored men's pack

**Cons**

- At ~1,520 g, it's heavier than true ultralight packs (Exos, Mariposa) without matching their load-hauling ceiling
- No dedicated sleeping bag compartment or divider
- Side and bottle pockets run small and aren't especially secure
- Sizing between S/M and L/XL isn't well documented — expect some trial and error
- Shoulder straps start to dig in once you push much past 40 lbs

## Who Should Buy This

The Banchee 50 makes sense for weekend-to-week-long backpackers who want more structure, ventilation, and pocket organization than a frameless ultralight pack provides, and who typically carry loads in the 25–40 lb range. It's also a solid pick for hikers who double their pack as travel luggage, given its documented durability through checked baggage. If you're chasing base weights under 10 lbs and want every gram accounted for, look at true UL options instead — but if you want a pack that can take a beating and still keep your gear sorted into ten different pockets, the Banchee earns its keep.

## Verdict

The Banchee 50 isn't ultralight, but it's close enough while delivering a noticeably more stable, organized carry than most packs in that weight class. It's a dependable, well-ventilated workhorse for moderate loads — I'd rate it a 7/10, docked mainly for so-so sizing guidance and pockets that promise more security than they deliver.
