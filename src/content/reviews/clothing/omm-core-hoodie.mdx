## Overview

The OMM Core+ Hoodie is a pullover midlayer built entirely from 125 g/m² PrimaLoft ACTIVE, a mesh knit with synthetic plume woven through it. The result looks like an unusual piece of fleece and behaves more like a breathable insulated jacket. It suits fast-and-light walkers, mountain-marathon runners and winter campers who sweat through conventional puffies. The trade-offs are bulk in the pack, no wind resistance on its own and a price north of £100.

## Performance

**Warmth and breathability.** The Core+ sits in an odd spot on the insulation spectrum. PrimaLoft ACTIVE is an open, lofty structure, so air moves through it freely. UKC Gear's reviewers described it as extremely air permeable, with sweat wicked away quickly. A tester at Ultra Runner Magazine noted that when working hard in the hoodie alone, the airflow was noticeable and kept body temperature comfortable. Stop moving and the trapped loft takes over. UKC's Penny Orr called it the warmest, snuggliest material she could think of.

**Paired with a shell.** This is where the Core+ earns its place. Under a windproof or waterproof layer, UKC found it turns into something closer to a PrimaLoft jacket. Ultra Runner Magazine's tester found that under a wind shell it kept him warm on chilly starts and while waiting for trains. Owners on the UKC forum use it as a winter climbing midlayer under a hardshell, and they also like it on wet days, since it doesn't hold moisture. Others sleep in it, citing the breathability and low weight.

**Weight versus the Core.** The standard Core Hoodie uses 75 g/m² fabric and weighs around 115 g in a men's medium. The Core+ adds roughly 70 g (185 g vs. 115 g) and is described by UKC as considerably warmer. Live For The Outdoors found the extra insulation made it the most reached-for running layer in its testing, though it still treats the hoodie as a midlayer for running rather than a standalone jacket.

**Wind and weather.** On its own the Core+ offers little wind protection. Reviewers consistently note the air passing through at walking pace. OMM itself suggests pairing it with a lightweight shell in extreme conditions. Its fast-drying character helps in damp weather, but this is not a garment to wear alone on an exposed ridge.

**Packability and durability.** Packability is the main complaint. UKC found that even compressed to its smallest, the Core+ stays quite bulky, a notable drawback for runners and ultralight packers hoping for down-like compression. Durability reports are mixed in tone. Live For The Outdoors expressed confidence in its longevity after several weeks of testing. Reviewers of the lighter 75 g/m² Core Hoodie flagged snagging on rock and scrub and a flimsy feel, and the Core+ shares the same open-knit construction, so it warrants care around brush and abrasive pack straps.

**Features and fit.** The hoodie has almost no features: no zip, no pockets. UKC pointed buyers wanting those to the Core fleece instead. Fit is body-hugging and described by one UKC reviewer as peculiar, though the stretch accommodates a baselayer underneath. The two-tone colour scheme, with contrasting underarm panels, divided at least one forum commenter.

## Choosing a Version

The Core+ comes in five sizes, XS to XL, and the listed 185 g is for a medium. There is no cut or insulation change between them. The decision is purely about fit, and the fit runs snug. One UKC forum owner found OMM's sizing small and wished they had gone up a size. A buyer of the lighter Core Hoodie, by contrast, found a medium fit well despite the size guide pointing to a large, thanks to a slim waist relative to chest. Plan to wear it over a baselayer or light T-shirt, check the size guide against chest measurements, and consider sizing up if between sizes or layering over bulkier items. Runners who want a close, next-to-skin feel can stick with their normal size.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very warm for 185 g, noticeably more so than the 75 g/m² Core
- Exceptional breathability and fast drying, so layers stay dry during stop-start effort
- Works as a midlayer under a shell, as a camp layer or for sleeping
- 64% post-consumer recycled content, and Bluesign approved according to Live For The Outdoors
- Hood adds warmth without a separate hat

**Cons**

- Bulky when stuffed; does not compress like down or a PrimaLoft puffy
- Little wind resistance without a shell
- No zip or pockets
- Snug, idiosyncratic fit and a polarising look
- Open-knit fabric may snag on rough terrain
- Priced around £110 at UKC's review

## Who Should Buy This

The Core+ suits people who sweat in a regular insulated jacket: mountain-marathon competitors, fastpackers, winter hillwalkers and climbers who layer under a shell. It also works for anyone who wants a breathable sleeping or camp layer. Thru-hikers who prioritise packed volume above all, or who want a single wind-resistant jacket they can wear on its own, should look at a conventional synthetic puffy or a windshirt plus the lighter Core Hoodie instead.

## Verdict

The Core+ Hoodie is a specialised, effective layer that does one job better than most: it keeps warmth in during effort without turning clammy. Its bulk, the price and the need for a shell keep it from being a universal choice, but for active use in cold, damp conditions it earns its pack space.
