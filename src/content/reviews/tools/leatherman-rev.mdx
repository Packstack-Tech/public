## Overview

The Leatherman Rev sits at the entry-level end of Leatherman's lineup, sharing a frame and price point with the Wingman and Sidekick. It swaps out the Wingman's spring-loaded scissors for a package opener and a combined file/ruler, and pairs that with a lockable 420HC blade you can open from the outside without unfolding the whole tool. It's aimed at everyday carry, car repairs, and campsite chores rather than dedicated backpacking — and that distinction matters for this audience.

## Performance

The tool set here is straightforward: <cite index="10-1">needlenose and regular pliers, wire cutters, a wire stripper, knife, package opener, wood/metal file, can and bottle openers, ruler, and Phillips screwdrivers</cite>. Leatherman rounds that out to 14 by counting both flathead sizes and the Phillips head separately, plus <cite index="17-1">a 5mm screwdriver, 3mm screwdriver, 2mm screwdriver, and Phillips screwdriver</cite> alongside the pliers and file.

The pliers are the heart of any Leatherman, and the Rev's are a known quantity — the multitool wiki community notes <cite index="16-1,16-9">it has a decent plier head that is like the one of the Leatherman Leap</cite>, which is a reasonable pedigree for a budget tool. One BladeForums user who runs a full-size Wave alongside a borrowed Rev summed it up plainly: <cite index="20-15">"i use a wave and the only difference is less tools otherwise pliers seem as strong."</cite> That's the best-case scenario for a tool at this price — you're not getting a lesser plier mechanism, just fewer implements around it.

The outside-accessible blade is the Rev's headline feature, and it's the one Leatherman's own review summary leans on hardest: <cite index="13-18,13-19">customers appreciate its sharp, easily accessible blade, handy package opener, and lightweight design</cite>. In practice this means you can flick the blade open one-handed for quick cuts without unfolding the pliers first — genuinely useful for breaking down boxes or trimming cordage on the trail without digging through every tool to get there. One BladeForums user did flag a quirk on their Rev's knife, noting <cite index="20-17">"the thumb hole moved to the front of the tip so its a tad ackward for one hand opening"</cite> — worth checking on your unit if one-hand deployment matters to you.

Fit and finish get generally good marks. Leatherman's aggregated review data (310+ reviews) puts it at a <cite index="13-17,13-18">4.6 out of 5 from 310 reviews, praised for its perfect size, high-quality craftsmanship, and ease of use</cite>. That said, no product review sample is unanimous — one critical eBay buyer complained the tool they received felt <cite index="5-7,5-8,5-9">"really heavy," "not spring loaded,"</cite> and reported the <cite index="5-9">"tools don't lock good"</cite>. That's an outlier compared to the bulk of feedback, but it's a reminder that QC hiccups happen even with a 25-year warranty behind the product.

For backpacking specifically, the honest take is that the Rev isn't built with grams in mind. At 168 g it's heavier than dedicated UL knife options and even heavier than Leatherman's own Skeletool CX (around 142 g), which drops redundant tools in favor of a more open frame. The wood/metal file and ruler are genuinely useful around a house or job site but are dead weight on a thru-hike. The pocket clip is a nice touch for quick access, but it also adds bulk you won't find on a simple fixed-blade UL knife.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Outside-accessible, lockable 420HC blade — no need to unfold the whole tool for a quick cut
- Solid, Leap-caliber plier mechanism despite the budget price point
- 25-year warranty and USA manufacturing back up the build quality
- Replaceable pocket clip makes it easy to carry without a separate sheath
- Package opener and file are genuinely handy for everyday tasks and car repairs

**Cons**

- At 168 g, it's heavy for anyone counting grams on a long trail
- No scissors (that's the Wingman) — you get a file/ruler combo instead, which is less universally useful in the backcountry
- Sheath sold separately, adding to the real-world cost if you want one
- Some reports of inconsistent locking feel and finish on individual units
- Screwdriver set skews toward household/mechanical tasks rather than backpacking repairs

## Who Should Buy This

The Rev makes the most sense as a glovebox, toolbox, or everyday-carry multitool for someone who wants Leatherman reliability without paying Wave or Skeletool prices. If your "backpacking" is really car camping — where a few extra ounces don't matter and having a real file, can opener, and full screwdriver set is a plus — it's a solid, warrantied option. If you're chasing base weight for a multi-day thru-hike, though, this isn't the tool; look at something in the 2–3 oz range instead, or a simple fixed-blade knife plus a dedicated small pliers/repair kit.

## Verdict

The Leatherman Rev delivers real Leatherman build quality and a genuinely handy outside-access blade at a budget price, backed by a 25-year warranty. It's a good value pick for everyday carry and car-based trips, but its 5.9 oz weight puts it firmly outside ultralight backpacking territory. I'd rate it a 7/10 as an EDC/car tool and a 4/10 if you're weighing it specifically for a thru-hike pack list.
