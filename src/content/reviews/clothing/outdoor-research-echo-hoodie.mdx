## Overview

The Outdoor Research Echo Hoodie is a 115 g (4.0 oz) open-weave polyester sun hoodie built for hot, high-effort days when most long-sleeve shirts feel like a mistake. It covers the arms, neck and ears without the sauna effect of a UPF 50+ fabric. The trade-off is a modest UPF rating and a fabric that is thin by design, which means less sun blocking and less durability than heavier competitors.

## Performance

**Breathability.** This is the Echo's reason for existing. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers gave it a near-perfect breathability score and named it their favorite option for high-output activity. They found it one of the few sun hoodies pleasant to wear under a hot sun without shoving the sleeves up. CleverHiker's reviewers reached the same conclusion, calling it one of the most cooling long-sleeve options available, whether on a desert ultra or in humid southeastern hiking. The fine open mesh pulls sweat away from the skin and spreads it thin so it evaporates quickly.

**Sun protection.** The numbers are honest but modest. Light colors carry UPF 15 and dark colors UPF 20. Outdoor Gear Lab notes that this is the lowest rating of any sun shirt it tested, though it still blocks roughly 95 percent of UV. The same testers flagged that lighter colorways can turn sheer. SectionHiker's reviewer recommends pairing the hood with a brimmed hat and sunglasses, since the hood handles the neck and ears while the brim shades the face. For anyone with sunburn-prone skin in high-altitude or desert exposure, the dark colorways are the better pick.

**Fit and details.** The cut is a relaxed, straight, athletic fit that layers easily over a base layer. SectionHiker describes the hood as very stretchy and roomy enough to fit over a brimmed hat. The flat seams stay out of the way under a pack's shoulder straps and hipbelt. Raglan sleeves keep the underarm seam clear of pack-strap contact points. The thumb holes cover the backs of the hands and wrists, and they are positioned so the sleeve doesn't ride up. Outdoor Gear Lab singled out the thumb hole design as a good one on the women's version. The hood on the women's model also has a ponytail slot.

**Odor and drying.** Thru-hikers wearing one shirt for days at a time will notice the ActiveFresh treatment. SectionHiker found it worked surprisingly well once the shirt had dried. The fabric dries fast enough on a wet creek crossing or after a rinse that CleverHiker's testers describe wearing it as a camp and sleep layer after the day's miles.

**Warmth and wind.** There isn't much. The same open weave that vents heat lets wind through, and SectionHiker is blunt that it is not a thermal layer. CleverHiker credits the hood with a small warmth bonus over a typical base layer and calls the Echo a comfortable three-season piece, but suggests looking elsewhere for cold-weather insulation. It works well under a fleece or puffy jacket because it adds almost no bulk.

**Durability.** This is the second compromise. CleverHiker lists durability as a con relative to heavier shirts. GearJunkie's reviewer, who wore it for a full year of paddling, ski touring and travel, found it more durable than expected for its weight. A reasonable reading: it will hold up to normal pack wear, but brush, rock and abrasive pack-strap friction will take a toll faster than they would on a sturdier shirt.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 115 g is hard to beat for a hooded, long-sleeve sun layer
- Outstanding breathability and fast drying, praised by multiple testers
- Hood, thumb holes and flat seams suit pack carrying
- Odor control holds up over multi-day wear
- Packs down small and layers cleanly under insulation
- Frequently discounted well below list price

**Cons**

- UPF 15–20 is the lowest of the sun hoodies Outdoor Gear Lab tested
- Light colors can be sheer
- Thin fabric is less durable than heavier sun shirts
- Offers essentially no wind resistance or warmth

## Who Should Buy This

The Echo suits backpackers and thru-hikers who run hot and are willing to trade a higher UPF number for airflow. That includes hikers in humid or desert conditions, trail runners, paddlers and warm-weather travelers. It is best paired with a wide-brimmed hat, and a dark colorway is the smarter call for people who burn easily. Hikers who prioritize maximum UV blocking, rough off-trail travel through brush, or cold-weather versatility should look at a UPF 50+ sun hoodie or a sturdier synthetic base layer instead.

## Verdict

The Echo Hoodie does one job better than most of the field: it covers the skin and stays out of the way when the temperature climbs. Its limits are plain — modest UPF, a delicate fabric, no wind protection — but at 4 oz and a frequently discounted price, it earns a spot in a warm-weather pack list.
