## Overview

The prAna Stretch Zion is a longtime favorite for cool-weather hiking,
standing out for its ultra-soft fabric, just-right stretch, and touch of
warmth.

Made in prAna's best-selling Stretch Zion™ twill fabric from recycled
materials with 2-way stretch, it wicks moisture, repels water, and carries UPF
50+ for trusted outdoor durability.

The Stretch Zion doesn't stop at hiking — it's a popular choice among climbers
for its unrestricted mobility, and fits the part for around-town use, too.

## Performance

**Comfort & Mobility**

This is where the Stretch Zion earns its reputation. Its signature nylon and spandex blend is buttery soft — none of that swishy, hard-faced feel you get with more technical fabrics. Whether scrambling, crouching to set up camp, or high-stepping over trail obstacles, the Stretch Zion moves effortlessly without binding or restriction — aided by a gusseted crotch for an unrestricted range of motion and a looser fit through the legs that helps avoid pinch points.

The pants run true to size, but if you need to adjust the waist, the
integrated belt is convenient, simple to use, and comfortable even underneath
a backpack hip belt.

The waistband is lined with a soft material that feels great next to skin and
has never caused rubbing, even on multi-day backpacking trips.

One note on fit:

the relaxed cut accommodates a range of body types but may feel baggy on
slender legs.

**Breathability**

This is the Stretch Zion's biggest trade-off for backpackers. In practice, these pants are quite warm. The ventilation eyelets do not provide enough airflow to air them out fully, and the slightly baggy cut traps more heat and has excess material around the legs. The Stretch Zion feels hot compared to lighter pants — coming in at roughly 14 oz, it's one of the heavier pairs among modern hiking pants, with a thicker fabric that retains more body heat. That said, the front pockets are partially mesh-lined and the crotch has vent holes on each inner thigh to help counteract this, and the roll-up leg snaps can push some extra air to your lower legs. Ultimately, they shine most as spring and early fall pants rather than a pair for the heat of summer.

**Water Resistance**

The DWR is real, but keep your expectations calibrated. At best, the pant provides enough weather resistance to get by if you're caught in a brief rain shower. Although labeled as water repellent, field testing reveals this to be only superficial — the pants can repel the lightest of rains but quickly wet through and hold moisture. The DWR coating requires extra care to maintain longevity, and even with proper care, it fades over time — after three years of testing, one reviewer noted the water-resistance was noticeably less effective. These are not rain pants; treat them accordingly.

**Durability**

Here's where the Stretch Zion holds its ground. The fabric is soft and stretchy yet has held up well to regular trail use, and despite repeated wear, the pants maintain their shape impressively well — the back half of the cuffs are reinforced to prevent fraying. Pack Hacker tested the Stretch Zion for three years with consistent heavy use — climbing, hiking, backpacking, more climbing — and found it still going strong durability-wise. The main caveat: some reviewers have noted stitching that has come undone, and the stitching in general can seem a bit shoddy compared to other pants at the price point. It's worth a quality check on receipt.

**Features**

The one thigh cargo pocket has two zippers for access — one vertically along
the side, the other horizontally across the top — while two hand and two seat
pockets handle smaller essentials.

The integrated belt is sewn into the waistband and never crosses the fly, so
you can take the pants on and off without having to open or close a belt
buckle.

Rolling up the legs shaves about eight inches off the length, and two pairs of
snaps on each seam at different heights hold them in place securely.

These aren't gimmick features — they all earn their keep on trail.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Comfortable, durable fabric that doesn't stretch out too much between
  washings
- Buttery-soft feel that outperforms most technical fabrics in sheer comfort
- Unique dual-zippered cargo pocket, low-profile waist tightener, and snap
  buttons on the lower legs for rolling up and securing cuffs
- Available in a massive range of sizes and styles, including multiple inseam
  lengths
- UPF 50+ sun protection is solid for desert and high-alpine use
- Trail-to-town versatility — not aggressively "techy" looking

**Cons**

- The Stretch Zion fabric is on the thicker side, making these pants heavier
  and warmer than expected

  — a real liability in summer

- In the balance between performance and casual style, the Stretch Zion
  Standard lacks performance in wet weather and is less suited for backcountry
  adventures in inclement conditions
- The relaxed fit accommodates many body types but may feel baggy on slender
  legs
- At ~14 oz, heavier than purpose-built ultralight hiking pants like the Outdoor Research Ferrosi
- Some reports of stitching quality inconsistency between production runs

## Who Should Buy This

These are pants for the outdoor adventurer who is into cool-weather hiking,
climbing, or long-haul travel.

If you spend time in the shoulder seasons — spring scrambles, fall backpacking,
mixed-weather thru-hiking — the Stretch Zion's combination of comfort, mobility,
and moderate durability is hard to beat at the price.

They're perfect for going from the trailhead to town without changing in
between.

Skip them if your trips run hot or wet, or if every gram counts — there are
lighter, more breathable options for summer use or ultralight-focused kits.

## Verdict

The prAna Stretch Zion Pant has earned its cult status honestly. Its soft, stretchy fabric and roomy fit make it one of the most comfortable hiking pants available, and plenty of climbers swear by the Stretch Zion for unrestricted mobility — though its looser fit and midweight build mean it's not the most breathable option for hot weather. For a three-season workhorse that moves from boulder problems to camp kitchen without complaint, it's a smart buy. Just don't expect it to replace a dedicated rain pant or keep you cool on a sweaty July ridge. **Rating: 7.5/10.**
