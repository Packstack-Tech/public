## Overview

The Chameleon is Dutchware's flagship gathered-end hammock, and it's less a single product than a platform. You pick a body fabric weight, a bug net style, a top cover (or none at all), and a suspension, and Dutchware builds it to order. It's aimed at hammock campers who want to dial in weight, warmth, and durability independently rather than buying a fixed setup — and who don't mind spending time in a configurator to get there.

## Performance

**Comfort and lay.** This is where the Chameleon earns its reputation. The body is generously cut, and reviewers consistently point to the diagonal lay as a highlight — <cite index="15-12">the generous dimensions are among the largest tested, enabling a nice, diagonal sleeping position</cite>. CleverHiker's testers were blunt about it: <cite index="15-10,15-11">one 5'9" tester's first reaction was that it was more comfortable than their bed, and every tester rated it excellent for comfort</cite>. I don't have independent long-term data to add on top of that, but it lines up with what I've felt in similar single-layer Hexon setups — the fabric has just enough give without turning into a taco around your shoulders.

**Fabric feel.** Dutchware's proprietary Hexon fabric shows up in nearly every review as a standout. One long-term user summed it up simply: <cite index="20-7,20-8">the supple Hexon 1.6 oz body gives a great night's sleep every night, breathes well, and feels great on the skin</cite>. That same reviewer had logged real mileage on it — <cite index="20-10">after 30+ nights in their first Chameleon, delivered early in the original Kickstarter campaign, their user experience remained excellent</cite>, which is a decent proxy for durability given how thin these fabrics run.

**Modularity, in practice.** The reversible, toothed zipper is the Chameleon's signature trick — <cite index="16-12">it lets the bug net and winter cover be completely removed and flipped, changing the lay direction from left/right to right/left without a special order</cite>. That solves a real problem: most gathered-end hammocks lock you into one lay direction based on which side the zipper's on. Here, you buy one hammock and can switch as needed, which is genuinely useful if you share a hammock or just haven't decided which way you like to lay yet.

**Weight scaling.** Because the fabric, net, and top cover are all separate line items, the finished weight swings wildly depending on build. On the stripped-down end, CleverHiker got <cite index="15-1">a lightweight backpacking setup with an asymmetrical bug net using single-layer Hexon 1.0 fabric down to a scant 15.7 ounces</cite>. Stack on winter features and it goes the other way — <cite index="15-7">a double layer of Hexon, solid top cover, two large side pockets, and a peak shelf still only came to 33.3 ounces</cite>. That's a wide spread for what's nominally "one hammock," and it's the clearest evidence that this is a system, not a fixed product.

**Build quality and customer service.** Fit and finish get repeated praise. One reviewer called it out directly: <cite index="20-11">every feature on the Chameleon performed as advertised, with fit and finish superb to the last stitch</cite>. Customer service also comes up unprompted in owner reviews — when one buyer found a stitching issue, <cite index="18-22,18-23">they noticed some stitching wasn't properly backstitched, emailed Dutchware, and were left the decision on how to resolve it</cite>, which isn't a glowing "they fixed it instantly" story but does suggest a company that engages rather than ghosts you.

**Sizing for bigger hikers.** If you're tall or broad-shouldered, the standard 58 in width can feel tight. Dutchware's own guidance is that <cite index="12-6,12-7,12-8">Hexon 1.6 carries a 350-pound rating and the Wide variant offers the same rating in a wider cut, recommended for anyone over 6'1"</cite>. The Wide adds about 83 g (505 g vs. the standard's typical weight) — a fair trade for more shoulder room if you're on the taller end.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional comfort and diagonal lay, backed by multiple independent testers
- Hexon fabric feels noticeably softer against skin than typical ripstop nylon
- Reversible zipper lets you flip lay direction without buying a second hammock
- Fabric and capacity options (200–350 lb) let you tune weight vs. durability precisely
- Strong build quality and responsive customer service reputation
- Wide variant available for taller/heavier hikers

**Cons**

- Suspension isn't included with the base hammock — budget for straps/buckles separately
- The sheer number of configuration choices can be overwhelming for first-time buyers
- Finished weight varies enormously by build, so "weight" isn't a single number you can shop against
- As a made-to-order product from a smaller cottage brand, lead times can run longer than big-box competitors (I didn't find hard current data on wait times, so ask before ordering if timing matters)

## Who Should Buy This

The Chameleon makes the most sense for hikers who already know they like hammock camping and want to move past a one-size-fits-all setup — someone building a three-season kit who wants a light Hexon 1.0 body for summer and the option to bolt on a solid top cover for shoulder-season trips, all on the same hammock body. It's less ideal for a first-time hammock camper who just wants to order one thing and go; the configurator, while thorough, assumes you know roughly what you want.

## Verdict

The Chameleon backs up its modular pitch with genuinely comfortable execution — soft Hexon fabric, a roomy diagonal lay, and a reversible zipper that solves a real annoyance in gathered-end hammock design. It's not a plug-and-play purchase, and you'll want to budget separately for suspension, but for hikers willing to spend a few minutes configuring, it's one of the more thoughtfully engineered hammocks on the market. I'd put it at an 8.5/10 — docked slightly for the à la carte pricing and setup complexity, not for anything it does wrong once it's hung.
