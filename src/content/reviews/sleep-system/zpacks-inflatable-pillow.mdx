## Overview

The Zpacks Inflatable Pillow is a crescent-shaped air pillow that weighs 1.4 oz (40 g) and costs $34.95. It is built for backpackers who want a real head platform without spending more than a candy bar's worth of weight. The trade-off is the usual one for bare air: it is light and tall, but not plush, and it wants a little help to feel like a pillow.

## Performance

**Weight and height.** The headline number is 40 g, and it holds up against the field. Adventure Alan's 2026 ultralight pillow roundup lists the Zpacks at 1.4 oz and measured its inflated height at 4.0 to 4.25 inches, slightly taller than the 3.5-inch spec on paper. That earned it the roundup's "best height-to-weight ratio" call. Zenbivy's inflatable matches it on weight and dimensions (1.4 oz, 14 x 10 in) but measured a little lower at 3.5 to 4.0 inches, and it costs about $10 less. Sea to Summit's Aeros UL Regular took the "best overall" label in the same roundup, so the Zpacks isn't the default winner on every axis.

**Shape and stability.** The crescent profile does most of the work here. Zpacks designed it so the pillow stays centered under the head whether the sleeper is on their back or their side, and the recessed baffles across the top form a shallow cradle instead of a flat balloon. In practice, that means less of the midnight pillow-chase that plagues rectangular air pillows, particularly when the sleeper rolls from side to back.

**Inflation and adjustment.** The flat valve on the bottom edge is low-profile and quick to open, so firmness can be tuned by releasing air a puff at a time. A couple of breaths gets the pillow usable, with a final top-off lying down. Dumping air to dial in neck angle is the main way to fit it to a particular sleeper, and owners such as one five-star reviewer on the Zpacks site describe the height as just right for them.

**Comfort.** This is where the pillow's limits show. Polyester air chambers feel cool and a bit slick against the face, and they transmit more noise than foam or down. Sleepers who want a softer surface tend to pair air pillows with a stuff sack of clothes or a puffy jacket. Zpacks sells a padded pillowcase for exactly that purpose, and the Adventure Alan roundup measured the Zpacks pillowcase and inflatable combination at 3.8 oz with a height range of 4.5 to 7.0 inches. Adding the Comfy Camp Pillow instead brings the system to 4.2 oz and a 4.0 to 6.0 inch range, a good fit for side sleepers who need more loft. The bare inflatable is the lightest building block in those setups.

**Staying put.** An inflatable pillow on an inflatable pad is a recipe for sliding. The optional attachment cord ties the pillow to the pad, and Zpacks also suggests stick-on hooks on the pad to lock it in place. It's a small add-on, but sleepers who toss around on slick pad fabric will want it.

**Durability.** Welded seams and a durable ultralight polyester construction are what Zpacks cites for longevity. There are no stitched holes to leak, which is the main failure point for lightweight air pillows. As with any 40 g inflatable, it needs to be kept away from thorns and sharp tent-site debris.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 40 g (1.4 oz) for roughly 4 inches of measured loft, among the best height-to-weight figures in its class
- Crescent shape keeps the head centered for both back and side sleeping
- Recessed top baffles cradle the head instead of letting it roll off
- Flat valve allows quick inflation and fine-tuning of firmness
- Welded seams, with no needle holes to leak
- Works as the base layer in a hybrid pillow system with Zpacks' padded case or Comfy Camp Pillow

**Cons**

- At $34.95 it costs more than Zenbivy's equivalent inflatable
- Bare air feels cool and slick, and many sleepers will want to add a layer of clothing
- The attachment cord is an extra purchase
- A single air chamber can feel tall and unforgiving for stomach sleepers or those who like a squishy pillow
- Too thin for side sleepers with broad shoulders unless it is boosted by a jacket or padded case

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and gram-counters who sleep on their backs or sides, already stuff a jacket or spare clothes under their head, and want a pillow that disappears into a pack are the target buyer. It is especially good for anyone planning a hybrid setup with Zpacks' pillowcase, since the inflatable provides the structure while clothing provides the softness. Stomach sleepers, plush-pillow loyalists and anyone who dislikes the feel of polyester against their face should look at a stuffed or fleece-covered pillow instead. Budget shoppers can get nearly the same weight and size from Zenbivy for less.

## Verdict

The Zpacks Inflatable Pillow does what an ultralight pillow should: it adds a few inches of adjustable height for 1.4 oz, and its crescent shape actually keeps a sleeper's head in place. It isn't the cheapest or the softest option, but it's one of the best-balanced weight-to-height choices for back and side sleepers who are willing to add a layer of clothing.
