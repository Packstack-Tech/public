## Overview

Trek'n Eat's Chana Masala is a freeze-dried, just-add-water version of the classic Indian chickpea curry, built around rice and chickpeas with a curry-forward spice blend. It's one of the few genuinely vegan, gluten-free, and lactose-free entrees in the freeze-dried meal world, which makes it a go-to for hikers with dietary restrictions who are tired of scanning ingredient lists for hidden dairy or meat stock.

## Performance

**Prep and texture.** The instructions are dead simple: pour 360 ml of boiling water directly into the pouch to the fill line, stir, reseal, and wait 10 minutes. <cite index="6-5,6-6">You pour boiling water up to the scale mark directly into the pouch, stir well, seal it, and leave for 10 minutes.</cite> That's a fairly generous water ratio, and it produces a soft, almost porridge-like texture rather than distinct curry-and-rice — expect the chickpeas to be tender but the overall consistency to lean mushy, which is typical of rehydrated legume-and-rice combos in this format.

**Flavor.** This is the part where opinions genuinely diverge depending on your spice tolerance. The manufacturer describes it as having <cite index="4-6">a delicate mix of spices that pays perfect homage to India</cite> — and "delicate" is the operative word. This isn't a fiery vindaloo; it's a mild, approachable curry flavor built on paprika, coriander, and a modest spice blend rather than heavy heat. Buyer reviews on retailer sites back this up as a genuine positive rather than a knock — one verified purchaser called it <cite index="3-8,3-9">"really good," adding "I definitely recommend it."</cite> Another repeat buyer noted <cite index="3-15">the dish is delicious every time after eating it several times over a few months</cite>, which is a decent proxy for consistency across batches. If you're the type who dumps a bottle of hot sauce on everything anyway, you won't be disappointed you brought extra chili flakes — but out of the bag, this reads as a comfort-food curry rather than an aggressive one.

**Calories and nutrition.** At 610 kcal for a 180g pouch (about 344 kcal/100g dry), this sits mid-pack for freeze-dried entrees — not as calorie-dense as some of the cheese- or oil-heavy meat dishes in the category, but perfectly reasonable for a legume-and-rice base. The chickpea content pulls real nutritional weight here: <cite index="4-3,4-4,4-5">chickpeas are a legume high in protein with a high percentage of carbohydrates too, and they bring a range of minerals including iron, magnesium and zinc</cite>. That combination of complex carbs and plant protein makes it a solid choice for an end-of-day recovery meal rather than just empty calories.

**Portion size.** The label lists it as one serving at 540g reconstituted, though at least one retailer lists it as "1.5 servings," and at 610 kcal it's genuinely filling for a single sitting rather than needing to be split. For a big-mile day you may still want a side snack, but as a standalone dinner it holds up.

**Packaging and practicality.** The pouch is a standard sealed freeze-dried bag with a fill-line, works fine as its own bowl, and the shelf life is generous — <cite index="6-7">the product is rated for consumption within 5 years from the production date</cite>. That long shelf life makes it a reasonable pick for stashing in a resupply box or bear can for weeks without worrying about spoilage.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely vegan, gluten-free, and lactose-free — rare to hit all three in one freeze-dried entree
- Mild, approachable curry flavor that multiple verified buyers rate as tasty rather than bland
- Decent protein and mineral content thanks to the 22% chickpea base
- Simple 10-minute, boil-and-pour prep with no cook pot cleanup
- Long shelf life (~5 years) makes it solid for emergency or long-term stash use

**Cons**

- "Delicate" spicing means heat-seekers will want to pack a hot sauce or chili flake backup
- Texture skims toward soft/mushy given the water ratio — not a "distinct grains and curry sauce" experience
- Allergen note: contains celery and mustard, so check labels if you're sensitive
- At 610 kcal, it's not the most calorie-dense option per gram if you're chasing max efficiency on a thru-hike

## Who Should Buy This

This is a strong pick for vegan, vegetarian, or lactose-intolerant hikers who are frustrated by how few freeze-dried entrees actually avoid dairy and meat-based flavor bases. It's also a solid option for anyone who wants a comfort-food curry without aggressive heat — if you like your spice mild and your dinner filling, this checks the boxes. Spice enthusiasts and anyone chasing max calorie density per ounce should look elsewhere or plan to doctor it up with their own seasoning stash.

## Verdict

Trek'n Eat's Chana Masala delivers on its core promise: an easy, genuinely allergen-friendly curry that real buyers consistently describe as tasty rather than an afterthought vegan option. It won't wow spice fans, but as a mild, protein-forward dinner with a long shelf life, it's an easy 7/10 — reliable, honest, and worth having in rotation, especially for dietary-restricted hikers.
