## Overview

Sea to Summit's Trek & Travel Pocket Soap is a snap-shut plastic case of 50 paper-thin soap leaves, roughly the size of a pack of breath strips. Each leaf dissolves in a splash of water, which makes it a leak-proof stand-in for the dropper bottle of camp soap that ends up smearing the bottom of a pack. The trade-off is that it is a hand-washing and bird-bath tool, not a shower replacement, and it hates moisture.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 14 g, the whole case weighs less than most people's spare lighter, and it slips into a hip-belt pocket or a toiletry bag without registering. Sea to Summit's own guidance puts a sink wash at one to two leaves, so a single case covers a long section hike of hand-washing with leaves to spare. Owners also like that a leaf can be torn in half when a full dose is too much. That is a level of dosing control a squeeze bottle doesn't offer.

**Leak-proofing and travel.** This is the product's clearest win. There is no liquid to spill, and Sea to Summit says the leaves are accepted in carry-on bags, which suits hikers flying to a trailhead. Reviewers on REI mention carrying the case in a pocket for festivals, trail towns and places where public restrooms run out of soap. One user even shared leaves with a stranger facing an empty dispenser in Athens.

**Lather and cleaning.** Results depend on expectations. Many owners report that a leaf and a little water give a real lather for hands and face. Others say the label's two-leaf guidance is optimistic for anything bigger. One body-wash user reported needing close to half the pack to get a lather going. A separate reviewer found the thin leaves left soap residue that was hard to rinse, and went back to liquid. For trail hygiene (hands after the cathole, face and neck at day's end, a quick wipe-down with a damp bandana), one or two leaves do the job. For laundry, dishes or a full wash, a small bottle of concentrated camp soap is more efficient.

**Moisture is the weak point.** The leaves dissolve on contact with water, and that includes damp fingers. Buyers repeatedly warn that the leaves have to be pulled out with a dry finger. One hiker reported that a single drop on the case ruined several sheets, and a roundup of biodegradable soaps noted that the case is not waterproof. Leaves stick together, and sodden clumps are awkward to clean out of the case. The practical fix is simple: keep the case in a dry-bagged toiletry kit, not in the pocket you reach into with wet hands, or use the trick some backpackers favor of loading a leaf onto a damp towelette for a quick sudsy wipe-down. Separating the leaves can also be fiddly for people with short nails, and one buyer reported a case hinge breaking on first opening.

**Scent and skin.** The green tea fragrance is light, and reviewers describe it as a clean smell rather than a perfume. A buyer with sensitivities to artificial fragrances and colors reported carrying the leaves without problems. Hikers who want a completely unscented soap should look elsewhere in the lineup or at another brand.

**Environmental footprint.** The leaves are biodegradable and phosphate-free. As with any soap, the standard practice applies: wash at least 200 feet from water sources, and don't rinse directly into a stream or lake. The amount of soap per leaf is tiny, which is the argument one thru-hiker gives for keeping them in the pack.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 14 g for 50 uses
- No leaks, no spills, and carry-on friendly
- Individual leaves can be torn in half for light jobs
- Dissolves quickly, even in cold water
- Light scent; paraben- and phosphate-free
- Inexpensive and widely stocked, in five formulations

**Cons**

- Leaves dissolve on contact with damp fingers, and the case is not waterproof
- Leaves can stick together, and separating them takes dry, patient fingers
- Thin lather for body washing; heavier uses can burn through many leaves
- Some users find the residue hard to rinse
- Not a substitute for camp soap for dishes or laundry

## Who Should Buy This

Ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers who want a hand-washing option that can never leak, as well as travelers who fly carry-on to the trailhead. It also suits section hikers who mostly want clean hands before meals and after bathroom stops, and a place to put a leaf in a first-aid kit. Hikers who plan to wash clothes, pots or their whole body on the trail, and anyone whose pack pockets tend to get wet, should carry a few ounces of concentrated liquid camp soap instead.

## Verdict

Sea to Summit's Pocket Soap does one job, trail hand-washing, at nearly no weight and with no risk of leaks. It takes a bit of care to keep dry and it is no shower in a box, but for 14 g it earns a place in a kit built around hygiene basics.
