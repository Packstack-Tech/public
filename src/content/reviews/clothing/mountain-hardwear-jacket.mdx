## Overview

The Ghost Whisperer (formerly the Ghost Whisperer/2) is Mountain Hardwear's featherweight down sweater, and it has been a regular in thru-hiker packs for more than a decade. At roughly 226 g in a men's medium, it stuffs into its own hand pocket and carries 2.6 oz of 800-fill down, enough for a cold three-season camp. The price of that weight is a 10-denier shell that demands some respect around branches, Velcro and granite.

## Performance

**Warmth.** A jacket this light invites skepticism, but testers keep finding it warmer than the scale suggests. Outdoor Gear Lab's reviewer called it surprisingly warm for the weight. The narrow stitched baffles keep the down from migrating. Thru-hikers on the Pacific Crest Trail report wearing it over a base layer alone on sub-freezing mornings, and one retailer review puts comfort at around 0°C (32°F) with a decent base layer. It is a sweater-weight puffy, not a winter jacket. Adventure Alan rates it as less warm than the average jacket in its class, and other reviewers say it falls short once temperatures drop well below freezing. What it does well is sit in the useful middle: enough for camp and the sleeping bag's cold side, not so much that it overheats on a brisk climb.

**Weight and packability.** This is why the jacket sells. Adventure Alan's testing found it lighter, with a better warmth-to-weight ratio, than the vast majority of down sweaters from mainstream outdoor brands. Outdoor Gear Lab's tester called it the lightest jacket in its test group and credited it with some of the best compressibility of the lot. Only specialists such as Montbell's Plasma 1000, and Mountain Hardwear's own thinner-shelled UL version, undercut it on grams.

**Wet weather.** The recycled shell carries a DWR finish that sheds light drizzle and condensation. That is the extent of it. CleverHiker's testers note it is not suited to heavy precipitation, and as with any down jacket, a dry bag liner or a trash compactor bag is cheap insurance. A synthetic final baffle at the cuffs and hem helps the areas that get wet first.

**Durability.** Here the jacket loses points. Switchback Travel's testers note that a sub-9-ounce jacket inevitably gives up some toughness and needs extra care. Owners say a 10D shell shows wear faster than a heavier face fabric, and one PCT and Himalayan reviewer scored durability a middling 3 out of 5. The lightweight zipper draws repeated complaints, and elastic cuffs loosen with use. Still, a decade-old original has been reported to keep most of its loft, especially when washed as rarely as possible. A roll of repair tape is part of the deal.

**Fit and features.** Outdoor Gear Lab's tester describes an athletic fit, while Switchback Travel found the torso boxy with some bagginess. The consistent thread is room for a fleece underneath, with sleeves that run long enough to keep cuffs over the wrists on reach. The two hand pockets are functional but awkward to use, there is no chest pocket, and the drawcord hem is the only fit adjustment.

## Choosing a Version

All three listings are the same men's jacket in the same construction: 800-fill down, 10D shell, no hood. The difference is a few grams of fabric. Mountain Hardwear's 226 g figure is based on a medium, and independent scale readings run higher. Outdoor Gear Lab recorded 235 g on the one it weighed, and CleverHiker found its large hoody ran about half an ounce over the claimed weight. Expect the XL to land at the heavier end of the range, a matter of roughly 9 g, or a third of an ounce. That gap is smaller than the weight of a lighter and a few feet of cord.

Size is mostly a fit decision. Reviewers describe the cut as true to size to slightly loose, so anyone planning to layer a fleece or a second puffy underneath should stay with their normal size, and those wearing it over a single base layer can size down without losing sleeve length. Hikers who care about shaving grams should pick the size that fits properly and ignore the difference between versions.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 226 g in a medium, among the lightest full-zip down jackets from a major brand
- Packs into its own pocket, with a carabiner loop for clipping to a harness or pack
- 800-fill RDS-certified down with narrow baffles that keep loft even
- Recycled shell with a DWR finish for light moisture
- Synthetic final baffles at the cuff and hem
- Long sleeves, with room to layer underneath

**Cons**

- The 10D shell snags and abrades more easily than thicker fabrics
- Lightweight zipper and elastic cuffs draw wear complaints
- No chest pocket, and the hand pockets are awkward
- Not warm enough alone for deep-cold shoulder season
- Retail price is steep, so owners watch for sales
- No hood on this version

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and fast-and-light backpackers who want a camp and sleep-system layer at the lowest weight from a major brand are the target, provided they treat the shell with some care. Travelers and runners who want a puffy that vanishes into a bag also fit. Anyone using one jacket for winter, bushwhacking or scrambling should look at a thicker-faced, heavier jacket with more insulation. Cold sleepers who want a hood should step up to the hooded version.

## Verdict

The Ghost Whisperer remains one of the easiest down jackets to recommend for three-season ultralight use, with real warmth for under 235 g. Buy it for the weight and compressibility, carry a repair patch, and accept that the shell won't forgive rough treatment.
