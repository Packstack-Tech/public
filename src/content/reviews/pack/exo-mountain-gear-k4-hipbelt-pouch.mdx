## Overview

The K4 Hipbelt Pouch is a small, zippered pocket that clips onto the MOLLE webbing of Exo Mountain Gear's K4 hip belt. It exists to solve one problem: getting a phone, rangefinder or handful of snacks without shrugging off the pack. The trade-off is that it fits the K4 system and little else, and it holds about as much as a large sandwich bag.

## Performance

**Capacity.** The pouch measures 7" x 5" x 2", which works out to roughly 70 cubic inches. That is enough for the things hikers want within reach. One reviewer at Outside Bozeman carried a phone, keys and a few snacks in it. The same reviewer called it a good home for electrolyte tablets. Backwoods Pursuit's long-term tester ran two pouches, one per side, and counted about 70 cubic inches apiece toward the pack's total carrying volume. A forum veteran who runs Exo pouches on his belt liked the design but wished it were a little larger. That is a fair summary: the pouch is sized for essentials, not for a full snack bag plus a headlamp.

**Attachment and access.** The K4 hip belt carries laser-cut MOLLE webbing, and the pouch rides on it. Backwoods Pursuit's reviewer described the K4-era pouch as much more secure and easier to operate with one hand than its predecessors. He called the change small but a great improvement. That matters because complaints about earlier Exo belt pouches centered on a loud zipper that took two hands to work. The MOLLE system also means the pouch can be moved or paired with other MOLLE accessories. The K4 belt itself can take two pouches plus a Nalgene holder.

**Carry feel.** Hip belt pouches have a reputation for getting in the way of swinging arms. Carryology's reviewer avoids bulky belt pouches for that reason, and singled out the K4 pouch as slim enough to be welcome. At 2" deep, it stays tight to the belt rather than bulging outward. A 96 g weight is noticeable on a spec sheet, but it sits on the hips, where the K4's stiff, wide belt can carry it without complaint.

**Durability.** No reports of failures surfaced. Exo builds the K4 system in the USA, and testers consistently describe the pack's construction as durable. The pouch's main wear point is likely the zipper. Earlier-generation owners reported that dust could make Exo zippers stiff, so grit-heavy desert use warrants an occasional rinse.

**What changed from the K3.** Backwoods Pursuit noted the K4 pouches are nearly identical to past versions, except that the ammo cartridge insert is no longer offered. Hunters who relied on it have improvised with Velcro tabs to transplant the old insert.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Holds a phone, rangefinder or a few snacks at hip level
- Slim 2" profile stays out of the way of arm swing
- MOLLE mounting is secure and workable with one hand
- Moves or doubles up (two pouches) to suit the load
- Modest price at around $28

**Cons**

- Fits K4 systems only
- About 70 cubic inches is tight for anything beyond essentials
- At 96 g, it is heavy for what is essentially a small zipper pocket by ultralight standards
- Zippers on earlier Exo pouches drew complaints about dust and noise
- Ammo insert from the K3 era is gone

## Who Should Buy This

K4 owners who carry a rangefinder, phone or trail food and want it reachable on the move should buy it. That covers most hunters and anyone using the K4 as a day-to-day load hauler. Buyers who want a bigger pocket should consider running two. Gram-counting thru-hikers with non-Exo packs should look elsewhere. The pouch is made for the K4 belt, and ultralight packs with integrated belt pockets or stretch-mesh pockets do the same job for less weight.

## Verdict

For K4 owners, the Hipbelt Pouch is a simple, well-fitted accessory that does what it claims. It is not a standout for capacity or weight, and it has no use outside the K4 ecosystem. Buy it if the pack is already in the garage.
