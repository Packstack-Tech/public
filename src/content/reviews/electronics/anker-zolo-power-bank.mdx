## Overview

The Anker Zolo 20K (model A1681) is a 20,000 mAh power bank with a short braided USB-C cable and, on the Lightning version, a second built-in cable. It delivers up to 30 W, enough for a phone, tablet or small laptop. On paper it is a sensible resupply-town battery. In practice, it is covered by a global recall for fire and burn hazards, and that outweighs everything else on the spec sheet.

## Performance

**Recall status.** This is the headline. In June 2025, Anker announced a voluntary global recall of several power banks, citing a manufacturing issue with lithium-ion cells from a single vendor. The A1681 was on the list, along with its USB-C-only sibling, the A1689. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission followed in September 2025 with a formal notice covering about 481,000 units across five models. It cited 33 reports of fire and explosion incidents, including four minor burn injuries. The remedy is a refund or an Anker gift card, not a repair. Anker has described the likelihood of malfunction as minimal. Even so, owners are told to stop using affected units and check the serial number on Anker's recall page. A battery that spends its life inside a pack, a quilt-side stuff sack or a tent vestibule is the wrong place for a cell with an open overheating question.

**Power delivery.** Set the recall aside, and the Zolo's output is respectable. Anker's listing claims an iPhone 15 reaches about 58% in 30 minutes over the 30 W USB-C connection, and the 20,000 mAh cell is rated for roughly four iPhone 15 charges. The catch is port sharing. Plug in more than one device and the total drops to 5V/3A, or 15 W. On a trail, that works for a phone and earbuds overnight, but it is slow for anything bigger. A trickle-charge mode, activated with a double press of the power button, routes a low current to the USB-A port for earbuds, a headlamp or a watch.

**Recharging.** Input tops out at 20 W, so a full refill takes about 5.2 hours. That is fine for a hostel bed or a motel outlet, but slow for a lunch stop at a cafe.

**Build and ergonomics.** TechRadar's reviewer found the plastic chassis sturdy but lighter than its size suggests, called it thick, and noted that a bank this large has only two ports (one USB-C, one USB-A). The same review called the small battery readout a missed opportunity. The roughly 6-inch hardwired cable is braided and rated for more than 10,000 bends. Notebookcheck points out that integrated cables like these can't be replaced once they fail, which matters on a trip where cable abrasion is routine.

**Weight.** At 353–391 g for 74 Wh, the Zolo works out to about 4.8–5.3 g per watt-hour. That is a fair ratio for a mainstream bank, but it is not an ultralight one. Most thru-hikers carrying a modern USB-C phone will not need the Lightning cable, which adds mass and bulk. At 74 Wh, the bank is under the 100 Wh airline carry-on limit.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ by 38 g. The base 20K weighs 353 g. The 20K, 30 W version with built-in USB-C and Lightning cables weighs 391 g. Hikers carrying a USB-C phone, which includes the iPhone 15 and later, gain nothing from Lightning. Anyone still on an older iPhone and sharing the battery with a partner is the only person the dual-cable version suits. Both versions fall under the same model-number recall, so the version matters less than the serial number.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 74 Wh of capacity covers about four phone charges
- 30 W USB-C output can also feed tablets and some laptops
- Braided built-in cable means one less thing to pack
- Trickle-charge mode suits earbuds and other low-draw gear
- Under the 100 Wh airline limit

**Cons**

- Subject to a global fire-hazard recall; withdrawn from sale
- Heavy for an ultralight kit at 353–391 g, and thick at 31.4 mm
- Combined output drops to 15 W when several ports are used
- 20 W input means a slow recharge of about 5.2 hours
- Hardwired cables can't be replaced if they fail
- Only two physical ports plus the built-in cables, and a minimal display

## Who Should Buy This

Nobody should be buying this one new. It was a decent value battery for commuters and travelers, but it is a bad fit for backpackers. A unit that sits in a pack for days, gets compressed under a lid, and charges unattended in a tent should not carry an active overheating recall. Current owners should check the serial number against Anker's recall page and claim the refund if it matches. Hikers who want a 20,000 mAh bank should look for a current model with no safety notice, and preferably one with a faster input and a lower weight per watt-hour.

## Verdict

The Zolo 20K's features were competitive for its price: a 30 W output, a durable built-in cable and a roomy 74 Wh cell. The recall for fire and burn hazards disqualifies it from a thru-hiker's kit. Check the serial number, take the refund, and buy something else.
