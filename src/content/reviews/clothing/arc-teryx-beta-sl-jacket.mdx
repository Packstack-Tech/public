## Overview

The Beta SL is the lightest jacket in Arc'teryx's Beta hardshell line. It's a three-layer GORE-TEX shell with a PFC-free membrane and finish, built for hikers, climbers and skiers who want serious storm protection without carrying a mountaineering parka. The trade-off is plain: at $500 list, it costs far more than most rain jackets that keep people dry, and at 340 g it's a proper hardshell, not an ultralight emergency layer.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** Outdoor Gear Lab ranked the Beta SL first among 18 rain jackets it tested, giving it a 9.4 for water resistance, the highest-weighted category in its scoring. Testers wore it through weeks of conditions ranging from steady drizzle to monsoonal downpours. Their base layers stayed mostly dry, with a little water getting in at the sleeves and around the neck when movement opened gaps. Other reviewers hiking in heavy thunderstorms report the same result: the fabric itself doesn't leak.

The caveat comes from The Trek, whose testers found the new ePE-based waterproofing holds up but wets out faster than expected. That is a DWR issue rather than a membrane failure. A face fabric that darkens and soaks through loses breathability before it loses waterproofness, and the PFC-free finish will want regular washing and reproofing to keep beading water.

**Breathability and ventilation.** The C-KNIT backer is a knitted, tricot-free inner layer, and it does two jobs. Reviewers say it breathes better than traditional tricot-backed shells. It's also quieter: one climbing reviewer called the jacket a little crinkly in motion but less so than most three-layer shells. Outdoor Gear Lab scored breathability at 8.5. Large pit zips give hikers a way to dump heat on climbs when the rain is warm, something the earlier Beta SL generation lacked. Owners of that older jacket complained about the missing vents and a weak main zipper.

**Weight and packability.** At 340 g, the Beta SL is light for a jacket this durable, but it isn't light by thru-hiker standards. Outdoor Gear Lab gave it just 6.6 for weight, and ultralight shells such as the Outdoor Research Helium or Patagonia Torrentshell-class budget options come in far below it. What the extra grams buy is construction. Reviewers who brushed against rock and trees reported no worry about tearing, and the 40D x 70D face fabric is noticeably more robust than the 7D to 15D fabrics on the lightest shells. It stuffs into its own hood to a package smaller than a Nalgene.

**Fit and mobility.** The cut is slim and the torso fabric doesn't billow. The rear hem runs long enough to stay put under a pack, and the hand pockets sit high enough to clear a hip belt. Arms-overhead mobility earned praise from climbers. The slim cut cuts both ways, though: Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found themselves between sizes, and a size that worked over a base layer got tight over insulation. Anyone planning to wear a puffy underneath should try a size up.

**Hood and details.** The StormHood is large and adjustable, with a rear toggle that cinches it over a cap or helmet. Wrist cuffs use Velcro and fit small wrists well. The jacket comes in seven sizes, XXS to XXL.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Top-tier waterproofing; ranked #1 of 18 by Outdoor Gear Lab
- Pit zips and C-KNIT backer give strong breathability for a three-layer shell
- Durable face fabric for the weight
- Helmet-compatible hood adjusts over a cap or helmet
- PFC-free membrane and DWR
- Packs into its own hood

**Cons**

- $500 list price, well above most competitors
- Heavier than true ultralight rain jackets at 340 g
- Slim cut leaves some wearers between sizes and tight over insulation
- FC0 DWR can wet out sooner than older fluorocarbon finishes
- Some water can creep in at the neck and sleeves in hard rain

## Who Should Buy This

The Beta SL suits backpackers, climbers and skiers who hike in sustained, serious weather and want one durable shell that covers trail, crag and resort. It's the right call for anyone in a wet, windy range such as the Pacific Northwest, the Scottish Highlands or the Alps, where a shell is worn for hours rather than minutes. Weight-focused thru-hikers on dry routes, or anyone who mostly needs a jacket for brief showers, should look at a 6 to 8 oz shell for a fraction of the cost. Shoppers on a budget can also watch for discounts: REI has dropped the price to around $300 to $350 in past sales.

## Verdict

The Beta SL is one of the best-performing rain jackets reviewers have tested, with strong breathability, dependable durability and a feature set that rivals shells weighing more. It's expensive and not especially light, and buyers who need ultralight weight or have a tight budget will get better value elsewhere. For hard-use storm protection at a moderate weight, it earns its place.
