## Overview

The Merino 175 Everyday is Icebreaker's do-everything base layer — a 175gsm 100% merino wool top built for the trail, travel, and everything in between. It sits in the middle of Icebreaker's weight lineup (lighter than the 200-series, heavier than their ultralight 150 tees), and it's aimed at hikers and everyday wearers who want one merino piece that works as a standalone shirt on cool days or a base layer under a shell in winter.

## Performance

**Warmth and temperature regulation.** At 175gsm, this sits in the classic midweight base layer zone — warm enough to wear alone on a cool day, thin enough to layer under a shell without feeling bulky. One long-time wearer of the women's version described wearing it <cite index="12-26">kept them warm layered under other clothes in -30C and kept them cool during a 25C hike in full sun over 25km</cite>. That's a wider range than I'd expect from a single midweight layer, and it lines up with merino's reputation for temperature regulation rather than pure insulation.

**Odor resistance and travel use.** This is where merino — and this piece specifically — earns its keep. Reviewers consistently point to it as a travel staple: it's <cite index="12-1,12-2">easy to hand wash in a sink while traveling, dries quickly whether from rain, sweat, or washing, and essentially never smells, which is why it gets packed on every trip</cite>. If you're doing multi-day trips without laundry access, that no-stink quality is the real selling point over a synthetic base layer.

**Fit.** Icebreaker lists this as a slim fit, and most reviewers confirm that: <cite index="11-6,11-7,11-8">it runs slim, so if you don't have much to hide (or don't mind showing what you've got), order your usual size — for a more relaxed fit, size up</cite>. That said, fit consistency isn't universal — one reviewer who sized down still found it ran loose, noting it <cite index="10-12,10-13">hangs off the body, dangling from the forearms, and isn't stretchy and fitted like other merino tops</cite>. Fit-wise, this feels more consumer/travel-oriented than performance-tight, so don't expect a compression-style hug.

**Durability.** This is the most contested area. On the positive side, one long-term owner reported the shirt <cite index="17-13,17-14">had a great weight useful for a range of temperatures and held up over extended use and several washings without any piling or other problems</cite>. On the flip side, a few detail complaints crop up repeatedly: the sleeve logo's stitching is noticeably <cite index="11-5">stiff, thick for this weight of fabric, and a little scratchy</cite>, and another buyer's only gripe was that <cite index="17-15">the seams are not flat stitched</cite>. More concerning is a note from a reviewer worried about the neckline's construction, saying <cite index="10-14">it feels like it might tear if pulled over the head too vigorously</cite>. It's also worth flagging that Icebreaker's separate 175 Everyday Leggings (same fabric weight, different garment) drew harsher durability criticism in third-party lab testing, where testers found they <cite index="16-1,16-2">weren't durable enough for everyday use and required care pulling them up over the legs for fear of creating another hole</cite> — a useful data point on how this fabric weight behaves under stress, even if the crew top itself hasn't drawn the same complaints. A Rokslide forum poster who's used Icebreaker for years since the brand's early days offered a blunter take: <cite index="15-17,15-18">having worn a lot of their gear hiking years ago, in his opinion their quality has gone down over the years</cite>. That's one anecdote, not a trend line, but it's worth knowing the sentiment exists among longtime users.

**Versatility as a shirt, not just a layer.** Several reviewers use it as a stand-alone top rather than strictly a base layer. One noted it's <cite index="11-16,11-17">finished nicely at the neckline, dressy enough to wear with slacks or a skirt, and still looks good with jewelry if it peeks out under a sweater</cite> — though another cautioned not to expect it to pass as regular clothing, since <cite index="11-9,11-10">it's genuinely thin, and unless you're going for a long-underwear look, it's best used as a base layer in chilly weather</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely versatile temperature range — reviewers report comfort from well below freezing to warm, sunny hikes
- Excellent odor resistance and quick-dry performance for multi-day trips or travel where laundry access is limited
- Neckline and overall look clean enough to double as a casual or semi-dressy top, not just backcountry underwear
- Some owners report no piling or wear issues after extended use and repeated washing

**Cons**

- Fit reports are inconsistent — some find it slim as advertised, others find it loose even sized down
- Sleeve logo stitching is noticeably scratchy for some wearers
- Seams aren't flat-locked, which a few reviewers flagged as a comfort/quality miss at this price point
- Durability concerns around the neckline and (in the related legging version) general hole formation under stress
- Not the cheapest way into merino — you're paying for the Icebreaker name as much as the fabric

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for hikers and travelers who want one merino piece to cover a wide temperature band without carrying multiple layers — especially if you value not smelling like a gym bag three days into a trip and don't have laundry access. It also works well for anyone who wants a base layer that can pull double duty as an everyday shirt around town. If you're chasing bombproof durability for hard technical use or a snug, performance-fit base layer, the mixed fit reports and stitching complaints here suggest it's worth trying on first or buying from a retailer with easy returns.

## Verdict

The Merino 175 Everyday delivers on the core promise of a good midweight merino layer — comfortable, odor-resistant, and flexible across a wide temperature range — but it's not without rough edges, from inconsistent fit reports to some scattered durability complaints on stitching and seams. It's a solid 7.5/10: a dependable everyday base layer rather than a rugged technical one, and worth buying if travel versatility and no-stink performance matter more to you than a snug athletic fit.
