## Overview

Macpac's Men's 180 Merino Boxers are a near-pure merino brief-length boxer from the New Zealand outdoor brand, built for hikers who want underwear that can survive a week on the trail without a wash. The fabric is 95% merino and 5% elastane, a much higher wool content than the nylon-blended boxers from Icebreaker and Smartwool. The trade-off is the usual one for wool: superb odor control, slower drying, and a durability record that owners describe differently.

## Performance

**Odor and multi-day wear.** This is the reason to buy merino, and the Macpac delivers on it. Macpac pitches the high wool content as a way to wear them longer on extended trips, and a reviewer at Bike Hike Safari who used a pair for nearly five years singled out the odor resistance as excellent. For a five-day section without a laundromat, that is the difference between fresh enough and a pair worth burying.

**Fabric weight.** At 180 g/m², the cloth sits above the 150 g/m² class that many ultralight hikers favor for summer underwear. The same reviewer describes it as a touch more substantial than a 150, which adds a little warmth in cooler conditions and likely helps the fabric hold up. In a hot, humid climate, that extra body is a small liability. In shoulder season, or in the cool mornings of the Southern Alps and Tasmanian highlands that Macpac designs around, it is a quiet advantage.

**Comfort and fit.** The construction is straightforward: a wide elastic waistband, a contoured pouch for support, and a touch of elastane for stretch. Seams on the current version are merrow stitched, a change from the flatlock seams on earlier releases; the V2 revision also made minor tweaks to the patterning and elastane. There is no fly, which some hikers will register on the first trail stop. The cut is a snug, next-to-skin boxer rather than a loose pair.

**Drying and moisture.** Merino of this weight dries more slowly than a nylon or polyester pair such as ExOfficio's Give-N-Go. After a creek crossing or a sweaty climb, the Macpac will stay damp longer, though wool remains comfortable against the skin while wet in a way synthetics often do not. Hikers who rinse underwear in camp and expect it dry by morning will do better with a synthetic.

**Durability.** Reports split. The Bike Hike Safari tester called these the most durable merino underwear in their roundup after nearly five years of regular use. A years-old thread on the New Zealand tramping forum Tramper.nz includes an owner who stopped buying them after repeated holes and ladders within two to three months, and who moved to Icebreaker, whose pairs showed no wear 18 months later. That complaint predates the current versions, but it is a reminder that 95% merino with only 5% elastane and no nylon core has little margin against abrasion. Wool-specific care matters here: cold wash, no softener, no tumble dryer.

**Value.** At A$59.99 list, and frequently discounted by 20 to 30 percent through Macpac's member pricing, the boxers cost noticeably less than Icebreaker's Anatomica equivalent. Underwear is final sale, so fit should be checked against the size guide before ordering.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent odor resistance from a 95% merino fabric
- Soft next to skin, with a wide waistband and contoured pouch
- 180 g/m² fabric adds warmth in cool conditions
- Priced well below Icebreaker and Smartwool, with frequent discounts
- Elastane gives freedom of movement without a bulky synthetic blend

**Cons**

- Slower drying than synthetic boxers
- No fly
- Long-term durability reports are mixed, and the high-wool, low-nylon blend is vulnerable to abrasion
- Heavier fabric is less ideal for hot-weather hiking
- Underwear is non-returnable
- Easiest to buy in Australia and New Zealand; harder to source elsewhere

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers in Australia and New Zealand, or anyone doing multi-day trips in cool to mild weather, will get the most from these: a wool boxer that tolerates days of wear at a price that makes a spare pair easy to justify. They suit section hikers and hut-to-hut walkers who want comfort over speed of drying. Hikers in hot, wet or humid conditions, anyone who rinses underwear daily and wants it dry by morning, and those who put a premium on abrasion resistance should look at a nylon-blend merino like Icebreaker's Anatomica or a synthetic pair like the ExOfficio Give-N-Go.

## Verdict

The Macpac 180 Merino Boxers do the one thing merino underwear needs to do, staying fresh over many days, at a friendlier price than the established premium brands. Durability reports are mixed and drying is slow, so they suit cool-weather and mixed-conditions hiking better than summer thru-hikes. Recommended for AUS/NZ hikers on that basis; a second pair on a long trip is cheap insurance.
