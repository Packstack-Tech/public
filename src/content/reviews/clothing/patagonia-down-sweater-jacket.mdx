## Overview

The Down Sweater has been Patagonia's default midlayer for well over a decade, and it still sells on the same premise: a plain, durable down jacket that works on the trail, at the trailhead and in the office. It carries 800-fill-power Responsible Down Standard down inside a NetPlus recycled nylon ripstop shell. The trade-off is weight. At about 13 oz it is a practical jacket, not an ultralight one, and gram-counters will find lighter options.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Reviewers consistently put the Down Sweater in the lightweight-to-midweight class. Altitude Sports rates it for roughly -5°C to 10°C (23°F to 50°F), which matches how it is used: a shoulder-season jacket, a camp layer in three-season backpacking, and a mid-layer under a shell when it gets colder. One long-time Trailspace reviewer wore it under a shell down to single-digit temperatures with decent base layers. A hooded sibling tested by CleverHiker carries 5.3 oz of fill, noticeably more than the roughly 3 oz that older reviews attribute to the hoodless men's jacket. Expect the Down Sweater to be warm for its weight rather than expedition-warm.

**Weight and packability.** This is where the jacket gives ground. Testers weigh the men's version at about 13 oz, and Greenbelly's review marks it down for a modest fill-weight-to-total-weight ratio, since a lot of that mass is shell, zippers, pockets and baffle structure. The lightweight down category runs from roughly 16 oz down to under 5 oz, and the Down Sweater lands in the middle. The stuff sack pocket packs the jacket into a tidy bundle, though Greenbelly found it does not compress the jacket much. It stows easily in a pack but takes up more room than a dedicated ultralight puffy.

**Durability.** The 20-denier ripstop shell is the jacket's strongest argument. Owners describe it as tougher than the 10- and 15-denier shells on ultralight jackets, which snag on brush, pack straps and granite. Greenbelly's reviewer calls it something that will last long enough to become an old friend. For hikers who bushwhack, scramble or toss a jacket on the ground at every rest stop, the extra grams buy durability.

**Weather resistance.** The shell is windproof and the DWR is PFAS-free, which handles mist and brief snow. Treeline Review's comparison table lists the Down Sweater without hydrophobic down, so a sustained soaking will still collapse the loft. On a wet trail, that makes a rain shell mandatory or the synthetic Nano Puff a better call.

**Comfort and fit.** The fit is regular and leans boxy, with layering room, not a trim alpine cut. The chin-level zipper garage keeps the zipper pull off the skin, and an interior storm flap blocks drafts behind the zipper. The hem cinches by drawcord from inside the handwarmer pockets, and the nylon-bound cuffs hold heat at the wrists. Trailspace owners compare it to a favorite fleece in feel, and a Patagonia customer reported sizing down from a medium because the fit was generous.

**Value.** At $279 retail, the Down Sweater is no bargain, but Patagonia discounts it often. It dropped to $139 for some colors in March 2026. The Hoody costs about $70 more, according to CleverHiker, and weighs 14.8 oz in men's.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Durable 20-denier ripstop shell that shrugs off snags
- 800-fill RDS-certified down with a windproof recycled shell
- Easy layering over or under other pieces, and acceptable in town
- Zipper garage, storm flap, hem drawcord and handwarmer pockets
- PFAS-free DWR and Fair Trade Certified construction
- Frequently discounted

**Cons**

- Heavier than ultralight down jackets, at about 13 oz
- Boxy cut, so some wearers will want to size down
- Down is not hydrophobic and loses loft when soaked
- The stuff sack pocket doesn't compress the jacket much
- Full retail price is high for the warmth delivered

## Who Should Buy This

The Down Sweater suits three-season backpackers, weekend hikers and travelers who want one tough down jacket that also works as a daily layer. It also fits climbers and skiers who value a shell that survives rough use. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look at lighter jackets with thinner shells, such as Katabatic's Tarn or Montbell's Plasma 1000, and accept the fragility. Hikers in persistently wet climates should consider synthetic insulation instead.

## Verdict

The Patagonia Down Sweater is a sturdy, comfortable and sensible jacket that wins on durability and versatility, not on ounces. Buy it on sale for hard use and everyday wear. Skip it if the pack weight is the only metric that matters.
