## Overview

The Petzl Altitude is a skeletal harness for glacier travel, ski mountaineering and alpine routes, where the harness is mostly insurance and spends most of the day in the pack. It weighs 150 g in size S/M, packs to roughly the size of a soda can, and goes on with both feet planted, skis or crampons included. The trade-off is that it has almost no padding, so it suits short hangs and crevasse-rescue scenarios better than long days on a rope.

## Performance

**Weight and bulk.** This is the whole argument for the Altitude. Retailers who stock it describe a harness about the size of a small energy-drink can once stuffed in its pouch, and one owner's review calls it light enough to leave in the pack "just in case." Another reviewer put it at less than half the weight of a standard climbing harness. The Altitude's 150 g is also a rounding error next to a conventional sit harness, which is why skimo racers and long-distance glacier travelers carry it.

**Donning.** The leg loops open with plastic buckles, and the waist strap threads through the belay loop before closing at the Doubleback Light buckle. In practice that means the harness goes on over ski boots, skis or crampons without anyone hopping on one foot at a belay stance. Owners report the design is intuitive enough to get right without reading the manual, and that the buckle works with gloves on. One skimo buyer who uses it on long glacier traverses and volunteer mountain-rescue patrols noted it is also easy to pull down for a bathroom stop, which is the sort of detail that matters at 4 a.m. on a glacier.

**Comfort.** WIREFRAME replaces foam padding with widely spaced HMPE strands that spread the load across the waist and legs. Early users were surprised at how comfortable it felt for something so thin. Reviewers also say it doesn't bite while hanging, and that it is nearly unnoticeable while walking. The caveat is physics: a harness this thin will always feel less supportive than a padded one in a long hang or under a heavy rack. Roundups aimed at high-altitude climbers flag the lack of padding and robustness as the main limitation for more technical climbing or heavier loads.

**Gear carry.** Two gear loops are enough for a few carabiners, a prusik or two and some slings, and they sit where a backpack hip belt won't smother them. The silicone-lined retainers on each leg loop hold an ice screw in place. At least one reviewer questioned what problem they solve, since screws hang fine from a loop, but others like the extra security when moving across steep ground.

**Fit.** Waist range runs 64–86 cm in S/M, 74–96 cm in M/L and 84–108 cm in L/XL, with leg loops adjustable on every size. One buyer with a 33-inch waist found the L fit but had little spare adjustment for baggy shell pants over insulation, so anyone who layers heavily should size with the bulk on.

**Durability.** Petzl backs the harness with a 3-year manufacturer warranty and lists a 10-year shelf life. The HMPE-and-monofilament construction is light, not rugged, so it is a poor candidate for being dragged across rock, belaying sport pitches or sitting in a gym. Buyers who have used a summer climbing harness with crampons on go further: they say a purpose-built mountaineering harness saves weight and volume, and spares a regular harness from spiky feet.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 150 g in S/M, with larger sizes at 160 g and 170 g
- Steps on with skis, crampons or big boots still attached
- Packs to roughly the size of a soda can
- Buckle works with gloves
- Silicone ice screw retainers and two accessible gear loops
- Comfortable for its weight, with no pressure points reported while hanging

**Cons**

- Minimal padding, so long hangs and heavy racks are uncomfortable
- Not built for repeated rock climbing or abrasion
- Two gear loops are enough for glacier kit but limiting for anything rack-heavy
- Runs a little snug over bulky winter layers
- Roughly $120 at list price for a harness many will use a few times a season

## Who Should Buy This

The Altitude suits ski mountaineers, glacier travelers, and thru-hikers or alpinists linking snow and ice sections who need a rated harness for rope teams and crevasse rescue but don't want to carry a pound of padding. It also works well as a just-in-case item on long ski traverses. Sport climbers, gym users, multi-pitch trad climbers and anyone who will spend hours hanging in a harness should look at a padded model instead.

## Verdict

The Altitude does one job, and it does it with almost no weight or volume: a rated harness that goes on over skis or crampons and disappears into the pack. For glacier and ski-mountaineering use it is an easy pick. As a do-everything harness, it isn't.
