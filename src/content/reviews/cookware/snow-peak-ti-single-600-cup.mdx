## Overview

The Ti-Single 600 is Snow Peak's large-format titanium cup — really more of a mini pot — sized to boil water for two cups of coffee or rehydrate a full freeze-dried meal. It's aimed at solo backpackers and thru-hikers who want one vessel that can drink, cook, and pack a stove and fuel canister inside it, rather than carrying a mug and a pot separately.

## Performance

At 600ml, this cup sits in an odd but useful middle ground — bigger than a standard mug, smaller than a true group pot. Snow Peak markets it as capable of nesting <cite index="11-1,11-2">a large, single-wall mug that can be used for coffee, or fit a freeze-dried meal or GigaPower Fuel</cite>, and that's borne out by user reports. One Trailspace reviewer found <cite index="1-13">their Brunton Raptor fit perfectly inside the cup</cite>, and multiple owners mention fitting a 100g fuel canister inside without rattling, which is the whole point of buying titanium cookware at this capacity — you're building a nested cook kit, not just a drinking cup.

As a boiling vessel, it does the job you'd expect from single-wall titanium: fast heat-up, low mass, and a surface that <cite index="1-20,1-21">has low heat transfer properties, is essentially a non-stick type surface, cleans easy, and does not melt or freeze — well it does melt, but it's gotta be super hot</cite>. The tradeoff for that fast heat-up is exactly what you'd expect too: the whole thing gets hot to hold. A Trailspace reviewer who paired it with a stove for actual boiling noted <cite index="15-17,15-18,15-19">the biggest issue with using the mug for boiling water or heating soup is that titanium conducts heat very well — the whole cup and the handles get very hot, so they used gloves when taking the cup on and off the stove and waited before drinking directly from it</cite>. If you're planning to cook in this rather than just drink from it, budget for a bandana, glove, or some heat-shrink tubing on the handles.

The lack of a lid is the most consistent complaint across reviews. One CampSaver buyer summed it up simply: <cite index="16-2,16-3">it's good volume, it's reliable, and it can be used as a small tea pot "for one" on the stove, but what I'd like to have is optional lid available</cite>. Snow Peak sells this exact cup separately as the HotLips 600 bundle (MGH-044) with a silicone lip guard, but the base MG-044R ships with nothing to keep heat in or bugs out. Several owners mention improvising a foil lid as a workaround.

On durability, this is where titanium cookware earns its price premium. Owners with years of use report the cup holding up essentially forever — <cite index="10-1,10-2,10-3">reviewers call it the best mug they own, moving it into daily house-drinker duty, with the expectation their Snow Peak mugs will outlast them</cite>. Titanium dents rather than cracks, and one reviewer put it well: <cite index="1-4,1-5">considering this thing is titanium, expect it will last a lifetime — it will get some dents and dings but that only gives it personality</cite>.

Capacity-wise, real users confirm the specs check out. <cite index="1-3">The capacity of the mug is 600 ml, 0.59 liters, 20.28 fl ounces, or 2.5 cups</cite>, and one REI reviewer was pleasantly surprised that <cite index="2-2,2-3">the cup can actually hold a pint's worth of liquid with ease — it's nice and light, plenty stout, and does what a mug ought to</cite>. It's not, however, cavernous enough for everything — one owner found it <cite index="17-4">not big enough to fit a Nalgene inside</cite>, so don't expect it to swallow a full water bottle the way some larger titanium pots can.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- True dual-purpose design: works as a mug and as a legitimate boiling/cooking vessel
- 88g is competitive for a cup this size, especially given it replaces a separate pot for solo use
- Foldable handles pack flat and don't add bulk
- Titanium build means dent resistance and effectively unlimited lifespan
- Nests a canister stove and fuel inside for a self-contained cook kit

**Cons**

- No lid included — a recurring complaint, and one that costs you both heat retention and bug protection
- Handles get uncomfortably hot when used for actual boiling, not just drinking
- Titanium's fast heat transfer means you'll want gloves or a pot cozy for cooking use
- No measurement markings inside, which matters if you're precise about water-to-meal ratios
- At ~$38 for a cup with no lid, it's a premium price point next to aluminum or steel alternatives

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right call for solo backpackers and thru-hikers who want to consolidate their cook kit into one titanium vessel — mug, pot, and canister storage in one 88g package. If you're already running a canister stove setup and want to shed the weight of a separate pot, this earns its keep. If you mainly want a mug for coffee at camp and don't plan to boil in it, a double-wall Snow Peak mug or something with a lid will serve you better with less fuss.

## Verdict

The Ti-Single 600 does exactly what Snow Peak titanium cookware has always done well: it's light, nearly indestructible, and genuinely dual-purpose as a mug-slash-pot for one. The missing lid and hot handles are real, well-documented annoyances, but neither is a dealbreaker once you know to plan around them. I'd rate it an 8.2/10 — a smart minimalist cook kit centerpiece, docked a bit for the accessory gaps that Snow Peak makes you pay extra to fix.
