## Overview

The Black Diamond Vapor is the featherweight flagship of BD's helmet lineup, built for alpinists, trad climbers, and skimo racers who count grams obsessively but still won't skip head protection. At 155g for the S/M size, it's one of the two or three lightest UIAA/CE-certified climbing helmets on the market, and it's aimed squarely at people doing long multi-pitch routes, glacier travel, or ski mountaineering where every ounce on your head matters more than it does in your pack.

## Performance

The headline number is the weight, and it's not a gimmick — the current Vapor really does feel like nothing on your head. One longtime reviewer described the current-generation Vapor as <cite index="6-8,6-9">weighing in at 178 grams on a home scale, two grams lighter than a Petzl Sirocco, calling it "forgot I had my helmet on" light</cite>. That's consistent with what I've seen across multiple hands-on reviews: nobody complains about weight on this helmet, ever.

The construction is unusual for a climbing helmet. Instead of a traditional hardshell over foam with a plastic suspension cradle, the Vapor uses <cite index="3-3,3-4">a two-piece build with a layer of ALUULA composite under the polycarbonate crown for added protection and reduced weight, while molded EPP foam makes up the sides for ventilation</cite>. That EPP/EPS hybrid foam is the same family of material used in high-end bike helmets — think firm-but-flexible, more like a car bumper than rigid packing foam, which is a decent way to think about how it absorbs impact.

Ventilation is where the Vapor separates itself from nearly everything else on the market. OutdoorGearLab's testing team noted the two-piece shell <cite index="2-15">offers better ventilation than any other helmet they've tested</cite>, and that tracks with independent reviews calling out the sheer number of vent openings compared to conventional lids. On hot approaches or long summer multi-pitch days, that airflow is genuinely noticeable — this is not a helmet that turns your scalp into a sauna by pitch three.

Fit is the one area where opinions diverge. Black Diamond's own aggregated customer reviews put the overall rating at <cite index="10-3">4.58 out of 5 from 88 reviews</cite>, with fit as the most-praised category, but not everyone agrees the sizing runs true. One user complained they were <cite index="3-15,3-16">"absolutely swimming" in the S/M, surprised that BD's most popular helmet's smallest size was as big as it was on them</cite>. UKClimbing's reviewer echoed a milder version of that critique, noting the rear cord adjustment <cite index="4-17,4-18,4-19">uses a minimalist cord-and-webbing cradle where the round cord, being thicker than the webbing, feels like it presses into the head more than lying flat</cite>. If you have an oval or narrow head shape, know that Black Diamond helmets in general run better for some skull shapes than others — try one on before committing if you can.

Durability is the recurring caveat across every review I found, old and new. The current model is more robust than earlier Vapors, and UKClimbing's testers noted the latest version has <cite index="4-7,4-8">been brought down to a barely perceptible 155g while also becoming more durable than its predecessor thanks to construction changes</cite> — but they still warned it's <cite index="4-9">not one you want to leave at the bottom of your rucksack, and benefits from a gentle touch</cite>. That matches the manual's explicit warnings against sitting on the helmet, packing heavy items on top of it, or exposing it to heat and solvents. This isn't a helmet you toss in the bottom of a haul bag under your rack — you baby it a little, the way you'd baby a carbon fiber trekking pole.

For skimo and ski mountaineering use, the Vapor also earns praise for being one of the few climbing helmets light enough that you forget it's replacing your ski helmet on approach — skimo.co called it <cite index="12-8">"light, comfortable, and secure-fastening"</cite> with removable headlamp clips that stay out of the way when you don't need them.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Genuinely disappears on your head — among the lightest UIAA/CE-certified helmets available
- Best-in-class ventilation for hot approaches and summer multi-pitch days
- Removable headlamp clips keep the profile clean when you don't need them
- Cord/webbing fit system allows fine-tuning without bulky ratchet hardware
- Comes in two sizes covering a wide head-circumference range

**Cons**
- Fit runs inconsistently for some head shapes — a few users found the S/M surprisingly large
- Foam/shell construction demands gentle handling; not something to stuff under gear in a pack
- Rear cord adjustment can dig into the head for some wearers compared to flat webbing systems
- Premium materials (ALUULA composite, polycarbonate/EPP hybrid) come at a premium price
- Less ideal for high-abrasion sport climbing or gym use where durability matters more than grams

## Who Should Buy This

The Vapor makes the most sense for alpinists, trad climbers on long approaches, and ski mountaineers who spend hours a day with a helmet on and genuinely feel every extra gram and degree of heat. If you're mostly sport climbing at the local crag or top-roping at the gym, a heavier, more durable helmet will serve you better and last longer under the wear-and-tear of regular clipping into anchors and bumping against rock.

## Verdict

The Black Diamond Vapor delivers on its core promise: it's astonishingly light, breathes better than almost anything else on the market, and still clears UIAA/CE certification. The trade-off is a helmet that wants to be treated with some care and a fit that's worth trying on in person before you buy. For big alpine days and long approaches where weight and heat management matter most, it's an easy 8.3/10 — just don't expect it to survive years of being crammed carelessly into an overstuffed pack.
