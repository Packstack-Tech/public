## Overview

The M10 Anorak is Patagonia's stripped-to-the-studs alpine shell: a 300 g, three-layer pullover with one chest pocket, a long half-zip and almost nothing else. It was built for climbers, but its weight and packed size have made it a quiet favorite for fast-and-light backpackers who want real hardshell protection at near-windshirt bulk. The trade-off is a slim cut, a fabric that reviewers call delicate, and a price of $379 at launch.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 300 g, the M10 sits right at the line where a full hardshell starts to feel like a windshirt. Testers note that it stuffs into its own chest pocket, and Active Gear Review describes it as wind- and watertight while stowing very small. The weight is achieved by cutting rather than by compromise in the membrane: the seams are ultrasonically welded and covered with narrow tape to keep bulk down, and the pattern eliminates shoulder and back seams. One earlier-generation M10 Anorak was reported at under 200 g, so the current version is a heavier, slightly sturdier take on the idea. The High Route confirmed the 300 g figure for a men's medium.

**Mobility and fit.** This is where the M10 earns its reputation. Peak Mountaineering found the cut generous through the shoulders, tapered toward the hem and effectively unnoticeable in use. GearJunkie's tester never felt the sleeves bind during overhead movements while climbing and skiing. The same tapered, slim cut is also the most common complaint. Reviewers who normally wear a small in Patagonia found it too tight and moved up to a medium, and two separate testers described the anorak as awkward to pull on, with a snug lower third. GearJunkie's tester wore only a base layer and a light insulating piece underneath before mobility suffered. Anyone planning to layer a puffy beneath it in cold shoulder-season weather should size up.

**Hood and ventilation.** The hood draws consistent praise. It fits over a climbing helmet with room to turn the head, and owners say it adjusts well and keeps weather off the face. Ventilation comes from the long front zip, which also makes the garment easy to get on and off; there are no pit zips. Reviewers describe the 3-layer fabric as breathable enough for steady uphill effort, though an anorak with no full-length zip or underarm vents will run warmer than a full-zip jacket during sustained hiking. A full-zip Gore-Tex alternative, which Backpacking Light's reviewer compared against it, weighs about 0.4 oz more, adds pit vents and costs $350 to the M10's $380.

**Weather protection.** Early testers had no complaints on this front. A Tahoe blizzard weekend with the broader M10 line kept water out, and ice climbers in Norway worked the anorak through several weeks of winter. Owner reviews on Patagonia's own page are more divided, however. Several recent buyers describe it as strongly water resistant but not a shell for extended heavy rain, and one owner who took it to North Cape was disappointed. The membrane and DWR are standard-issue ultralight fare, which means the fabric needs periodic care and sustained downpours will tell on it.

**Durability.** This is the M10's weak point. GearJunkie's tester described the 12-denier fabric as wispy and prone to cuts from the sharp gear involved in ice climbing, and flagged durability as a concern. For a backpacker, that translates into caution around brush, scree and pack straps. One long-term owner calls an older M10 a favorite despite heavy abuse, so the line can last, but the fabric rewards careful treatment.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 300 g for a full three-layer hardshell
- Packs into its own chest pocket
- Helmet-compatible hood that adjusts well
- Excellent mobility through the shoulders and arms
- Long front zip for easy on/off and venting
- Welded seams and seamless shoulder pattern keep bulk down
- Waterproof chest pocket and RECCO reflector, uncommon at this weight

**Cons**

- Slim cut leaves little room for insulation underneath
- Tight to pull over the head in smaller sizes
- Fragile 12-denier fabric prone to snags and cuts
- No pit zips; pullover design limits dumping heat
- Some owners find it unreliable in prolonged heavy rain
- $379 at launch

## Who Should Buy This

The M10 Anorak suits alpine climbers, ski tourers and fast-and-light mountain hikers who want a shell light enough to carry every day and who accept that it is a minimalist tool. It also fits ultralight backpackers on dry-leaning routes who see a rain jacket mainly as a wind and storm layer. Thru-hikers facing days of continuous rain, anyone who wants pit zips or a full-length zip for temperature regulation, and hikers who regularly bushwhack should look elsewhere. A more durable full-zip shell will serve them better.

## Verdict

The M10 Anorak is among the most refined pieces of minimalist hardshell design on the market, with a hood and mobility that most competitors can't match at 300 g. Its slim fit, delicate fabric and mixed reports in sustained rain keep it from being a general-purpose rain jacket. Buy it for alpine objectives and fast days in the mountains; skip it for long, wet, brushy trails.
