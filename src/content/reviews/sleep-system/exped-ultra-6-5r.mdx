## Overview

The Exped Ultra 6.5R is a four-season inflatable pad that gets its warmth from recycled microfiber insulation and a reflective foil layer, a combination Exped calls SynMat Plus. It suits backpackers who camp through shoulder season and winter and don't want to carry a brick to do it. The trade-off is a thin 20D shell that rewards careful campsite selection, and a price that is hard to justify if the pad only sees July.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The 6.9 R-value is the headline, and testers report it holds up. Treeline Review's testers logged more than 50 nights across all four seasons, including sleeping on snow at 15°F, and never felt cold coming through the pad. A GearJunkie tester reached a similar verdict on 0°F and 2°F nights, adding a layer only because the sleeping bag was at its limit, not the pad. Backpacker, which tested the pad at release, noted the gains over Exped's earlier Ultra 5R: lighter, smaller packed, and a better tested R-value. CleverHiker named it a favorite cold-weather pad, citing warm Grand Canyon camps as well as chilly Rocky Mountain nights.

The flip side shows up in summer. One Reddit owner points out that a 6.5R pad can feel like a lot of insulation in real heat. That is the cost of a pad built for winter.

**Comfort.** At 3.5 inches thick, the Ultra sits well clear of the bony-hip threshold. The vertical baffles produce a softer, more cradling surface than the stiffer horizontal tubes on many competitors, and the slightly raised outer rails help keep a sleeper centered. CleverHiker rated it 4.7 out of 5 for comfort and singled it out for side sleepers. Better Trail's tester slept comfortably on the side, on the stomach and in a figure-four. Not everyone likes vertical baffles, though, and Live for the Outdoors flagged that as a matter of taste.

**Noise.** Backpacker ranked the Ultra 6.5R among the quietest pads it has tested, and the best in its one-pound class on decibel level. Foil-lined pads have a reputation for crinkling every time a sleeper rolls over. This one largely avoids it, which light sleepers will notice at 2 a.m.

**Weight and packability.** At 544 g, the rectangular version is a mid-weight winter pad, not an ounce-counter's piece. The mummy is where the warmth-to-weight reputation comes from. Backpacker weighed the regular mummy at 12.7 ounces, calling it the lightest three-season pad it had tested and a hair under the NeoAir XLite at 13 ounces. Treeline Review measured 12 ounces. CleverHiker's view was that warm-weather hikers can find lighter options, but that the weight is impressively reasonable for what the pad delivers in the cold. It rolls easily and packs to about 8.3 x 4.3 inches.

**Inflation.** A one-way inflate valve and a separate deflate valve keep setup simple, and a lightweight pump bag comes in the box. Testers generally found inflation easy. Better Trail's reviewer found the pump bag less intuitive than some competitors', while Trek & Mountain counted it among the best.

**Durability.** This is the pad's soft spot. The recycled 20D fabric is Bluesign-approved and DWR-free. GearJunkie's tester found it durable enough for snow camping, where puncture hazards are minimal, but not the most puncture-resistant. A Reddit commenter comparing it with the 30D Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite mentioned the thinner denier and occasional baffle-weld failures. One REI buyer reported cold spots from gaps in the reflective layer, though that review sits among seven averaging 4.0 stars. In practice, that means a groundsheet and a quick sweep of the tent floor for pine needles.

## Choosing a Version

The insulation, thickness and R-value are identical across the lineup. What changes is how much pad sits under the sleeper's shoulders and feet, and how many grams that costs.

**Mummy Medium (340 g).** The weight-saver. It is 183 cm long and tapers from a 52 cm shoulder width to a 35 cm foot. This is the one that earned the warmth-to-weight praise, and it suits back sleepers, narrow-shouldered hikers and anyone whose pad lives in the pack for weeks at a time. Side sleepers with broad shoulders will find the edges close.

**Mummy Long / Wide (425 g).** The compromise cut. Shoulder width grows to 65 cm and the foot to 42 cm, while the taper still trims fabric from the legs. Exped also stretches the mummy to 197 cm for taller sleepers. It costs 85 g more than the Medium. It is the best fit for side sleepers and anyone over about 6 feet who still wants a mummy's weight.

**Rectangular Medium / Wide (544 g).** Full 65 cm width from head to toe, 183 cm long. It is 119 g heavier than the wide mummy and 204 g heavier than the Medium, and that buys room to sprawl, shift and sleep with knees apart. Restless sleepers and winter campers who value a stable, roomy platform over pack weight should choose this one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 6.9 R-value handles snow camping and sub-freezing nights
- Mummy Medium weighs 340 g, among the lightest pads at this warmth rating
- 3.5-inch thickness with vertical baffles and raised rails suits side sleepers
- Quiet, without the crinkle common to foil-insulated pads
- Pump bag included; simple dual-valve system
- Priced below many winter-rated competitors at about $200

**Cons**

- Thin 20D fabric needs care around sharp ground
- Too warm for summer-only use
- At 544 g, the rectangular version is not ultralight
- A few owners report weld failures or gaps in the reflective layer
- Vertical baffles are not to everyone's taste

## Who Should Buy This

Shoulder-season and winter backpackers, cold sleepers, and side sleepers who find narrow, stiff pads unforgiving will get the most from the Ultra 6.5R. It also works as a one-pad-for-the-year choice for anyone willing to accept that it runs warm in July. Weight-focused hikers should take the Mummy Medium or the wide mummy. Hot-weather thru-hikers should look at a lower R-value pad, and anyone who regularly sleeps on rough ground without a groundsheet should consider a thicker-fabric alternative.

## Verdict

The Exped Ultra 6.5R is one of the best-reviewed cold-weather backpacking pads, with a winter-capable R-value, real comfort and a competitive price. Buy it for shoulder season and winter, pick the mummy unless the extra room is worth the grams, and protect the shell. Skip it if the plan is mostly warm-weather trips.
