## Overview

The Give-N-Go Sport 2.0 is ExOfficio's thinned-out mesh take on its long-running travel boxer brief, built for people who sweat through their clothes and rinse them in a sink. The 6-inch inseam covers the upper thigh, which is where chafe tends to start on a hot, hilly day. The trade-off is money: owners love how it performs, but few call it cheap.

## Performance

**Chafe and comfort.** The design pairs a nylon-dominant mesh with flatlock seams that keep stitching flat against the skin. Owners who log serious miles in them tend to start with the same observation: nothing rubs. One walker who spent two weeks trekking through Italy reported zero chafing in hot, all-day conditions and rated the pair far above cotton. The wide waistband gets its own praise for staying put without pinching or digging in, even on scrambles.

**Drying and odor.** This is why people buy them. Owners describe rinsing a pair in a shower or sink at night and pulling it on dry in the morning, and many treat that as the entire point of the garment. ExOfficio says the 2.0 mesh is up to four times more breathable, 50% more moisture-wicking and 25% quicker drying than the original Give-N-Go. Those are the company's figures, but the owner reports line up with them. The anti-odor treatment and nylon blend keep the pair inoffensive over multiple days. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers rated the standard Give-N-Go 2.0 well on odor control and noted that nylon resists odor buildup better than cotton between washes.

**Breathability.** The Sport version is the airier of ExOfficio's two lines. Owners who have worn both say the thinner mesh and flatter seams make it the better pick for summer hiking and travel. The same fabric makes it feel close to weightless on the body.

**Durability.** Reports split. Some owners say pairs bought for backpacking trips years ago are still in rotation, and others report five pairs lasting around five years. Others find the thin mesh has limits. One buyer had two of five pairs unravel in a delicate wash cycle, and testers of the standard Give-N-Go noted the nylon tends to pill. Some longtime customers also say recent batches feel different from older ones, citing changes to the seams and front pouch and a fabric hand that reads more like polyester. Treat it as a thin performance garment and wash it gently.

**Fit.** The cut is snug and low-rise. Some buyers say it runs tighter than their usual boxer brief. One reviewer who owned pairs from 2020 says current sizing is noticeably smaller, with less room in the pouch. Others say the same cut gives better support than competing brands. Thinner-legged hikers report the leg hems creeping up under shorts over a long day. One owner with a short rise dropped from a large to a medium and found the support improved considerably, so anyone at the bottom of a size range should consider the smaller size.

## Choosing a Version

The two listed versions, Standard Small and Men's Small, are the same garment: the men's small in the 6-inch boxer brief, at 115.1 g, with the same fabric, cut and seams. There is no weight or construction difference to weigh between them.

The choice that matters is size. ExOfficio's chart puts the small at a 30-inch waist, with the medium covering 32 to 34 inches. Because the cut runs snug and low, hikers between sizes should drop down rather than up for support and to keep the waistband from rolling. Those with a hip measurement at the top of a range should size up to keep the leg hems from digging in.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Mesh dries overnight after a sink wash
- Flatlock seams and a wide waistband keep chafe and pinching down on long days
- Anti-odor treatment helps stretch wear between washes
- At 115.1 g in a small, light enough to forget on the body
- Functional fly and 4-way stretch
- Long-term owners report years of use from well-kept pairs

**Cons**

- Roughly $32–$35 a pair is steep for underwear, and some owners say comparable cheaper options exist
- Thin mesh can pill, and some owners report seams unraveling
- Snug, low-rise cut doesn't suit everyone, and legs can ride up
- Some recent buyers say sizing and fabric have changed from earlier batches

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers, section hikers and travelers who carry two or three pairs and wash them daily should buy these. Hot-weather walkers who've been burned by chafe in cotton or heavy synthetics will get the most from the mesh. Buyers who want a looser, roomier leg should look elsewhere. So should anyone who plans to wash on a hot machine cycle and expects indestructible fabric. Shoppers on a tight budget who only need underwear for a weekend trip can skip them too.

## Verdict

The Give-N-Go Sport 2.0 does what trail underwear should: it stays comfortable, dries fast and mostly goes unnoticed. The price, the snug sizing and a few durability complaints keep it from a higher score. For hot-weather hiking and travel, though, it remains one of the safer buys in the category.
