## Overview

The Arc UL is Nunatak's flagship backpacking quilt: 900-fill goose down, a sewn-in footbox, a draft collar, and a build sheet the buyer largely writes. It is aimed at thru-hikers and three-season ultralighters who want a quilt cut to their body rather than one pulled from a shelf. The trade-off is time and money. Each one is made to order, and owners and reviewers agree that getting one is the hard part.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The Arc UL's reputation rests on its generosity with down. In the 20°F configuration it carries roughly 15 oz of 900-fill, and Greenbelly's testers found it ran warmer than most 20°F quilts they tried. SectionHiker's reviewer, who tested a 20°F quilt weighing 23.2 oz with 14.5 oz of down, called it overkill for shoulder-season trips "in a good way." For comparison, the same review notes that Enlightened Equipment's Magma 30 holds 9.5 oz of down. The draft collar earns particular praise: around freezing, it keeps heat in and cold air out, and the reviewer said he wouldn't buy a quilt without one.

Nunatak itself is candid about limits. The company describes 15°F as near or past the threshold of effective quilt use, and it says many women find a 20°F quilt fails before the thermometer reaches 20°F. Sleepers who run cold should order a step warmer than their target.

**Weight and packability.** At 635 g for the 20°F build, the Arc UL isn't the lightest 20°F quilt on the market, because the fill is not skimpy. Greenbelly's testers said it compacts well and lofts quickly. Fabric choices shift the number: waterproof-breathable panels on the footbox and collar add weight, and a 7D shell and 10D liner shave it back. The 30°F version, at about 540 g, is a more conventional ultralight proposition.

**Fit and sleeping comfort.** The differential cut, with the outer shell cut larger than the liner, keeps the down lofted rather than compressed. Nunatak sells four width options. Buyers should size carefully. One owner who sold a 70-inch quilt reported that the nominal 56-inch shoulder width measured closer to 58 inches in practice. At 5'11" and 160 lb, he found a 54-inch width and 38-inch footbox fit better. Nunatak's own guidance is that larger builds, restless sleepers and side sleepers should go wider. The footbox is generous enough that Greenbelly's testers singled it out.

**Attachment.** ETC is now standard on every Arc UL. Cordlocks on each side adjust the edge tension to close gaps around the sleeper. The pad attachment system is similar to the two-flat-clips-per-side setup used by Enlightened Equipment and Zpacks. Ultimate Gear Lists found it works well in all but the coldest temperatures, when small drafts begin to matter. On a winter-adjacent night in the teens, that means the quilt is better paired with a well-fitted pad than with a loose one.

**Customization and lead times.** Shell, liner, fill type, overfill, elastication and dimensions are all on the order form. Nunatak's Dry Down is offered at no extra charge, according to SectionHiker. That flexibility is the main reason owners buy. The cost is a long wait: recent roundups flag months-long lead times as the quilt's one real downside, and Greenbelly marks it down for price. The used market on Backpacking Light is active, which tells its own story about demand and about the occasional mis-sized first order.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Generous 900-fill down load (15.3 oz in the 20°F build) with a warm-for-its-rating reputation
- Draft collar and differential cut hold heat well near freezing
- Nearly every spec is adjustable: width, length, footbox, fabrics, fill and overfill
- ETC and pad straps standard; quilt can be secured under the body or to the pad
- Quality of materials and craftsmanship draws consistent praise from owners

**Cons**

- Made to order with lead times measured in months
- Premium price, among the most expensive quilts in comparison roundups
- Heavier than some 20°F competitors that use less down
- Sizing takes thought, and the nominal 56-inch width runs larger in practice
- Edge drafts can creep in at the coldest end of its range

## Who Should Buy This

The Arc UL suits the hiker who knows their measurements, plans ahead and wants a 20°F quilt that sleeps closer to its number. Thru-hikers starting a PCT or AT season and cold sleepers who have been burned by optimistic ratings are the natural buyers. It's a poor fit for anyone who needs a quilt in a few weeks, shops primarily on price, or wants the lowest possible weight at 20°F. Those hikers will be better served by an off-the-shelf quilt with less fill.

## Verdict

The Arc UL is a carefully built, customizable quilt that keeps its warmth promise and gives sleepers real control over fit and fabric. The price and the wait are the costs of admission. Hikers willing to pay both should order it early and get the width right the first time.
