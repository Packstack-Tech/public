## Overview

The Mountain Series Backpacker is Adventure Medical Kits' answer to a question every multi-day hiker eventually faces: buy a pile of loose supplies and sort them yourself, or buy a kit that's already organized. It's built for one or two people on trips of up to four days. The trade-off is weight. It carries 386 g, roughly two to three times what a minimalist ziplock kit weighs, and the bag itself isn't weatherproof.

## Performance

The kit's main selling point is layout. Supplies sit in clear pockets grouped by injury rather than by item type, and a printed map on the outside of the bag shows what's where. On a bad day, that means a hiker can reach for the "bleeding" or "sprains" pocket without unpacking everything onto a wet groundsheet. The zippers keep contents from spilling when the bag is opened. Reviewers at CleverHiker, who named it their top pick for multi-day backpacking after logging thousands of trail miles, singled out the labeled compartments and the compact footprint for a kit meant to cover several people. The bright blue bag is also easy to spot at the bottom of a full pack, a small virtue when someone is bleeding and patience is short.

The contents lean toward trauma and wound care more than toward a drugstore aisle. A large trauma pad handles serious bleeds from a fall. Butterfly bandages, tape, tincture of benzoin and an irrigation syringe cover wound closure and cleaning. An elastic bandage supports sprains and strains, and the 14 pre-cut moleskin pieces are shaped for common hot spots. EMT shears and forceps are included, and the medication set covers fever, stomach upset, pain and allergies, with multiple doses of each so a four-day trip doesn't drain it on day two. The included Wilderness First Aid Guide is useful for the hiker who has the kit but hasn't taken a course. It also accounts for the difference between the 386 g and 309 g weights.

Owner feedback tracks with that design. One REI buyer reported using the kit after a partner broke an ankle on trail, and credited it with making the situation manageable. Another described it as the main kit for four-night trips with groups of two to six people, and praised the quality of the contents and how easy the layout makes restocking. The same owners also make the obvious caveat: no pre-built kit covers everything a particular hiker wants, and this one is no exception. Personal prescriptions, a preferred blister system, extra tape and any specialty items still have to be added. The darted bag leaves room for that.

Durability is average. CleverHiker's testers noted the bag isn't water-resistant and kept it inside a dry bag or a ziplock in wet weather. That's an easy fix but a real consideration for anyone heading into rain-prone terrain. Several retailer listings describe the bag as water-resistant, but a hiker in a Pacific Northwest downpour shouldn't count on it.

Against its siblings, the Backpacker is the larger, more complete option. The Ultralight/Watertight .7, which CleverHiker lists at 5.8 oz and $45, is smaller, waterproof and nearly as well stocked, so it suits day hikes and short trips. The Mountain Series Hiker kit weighs about 7.2 oz and costs around $35. The Backpacker's extra bulk buys more trauma capacity, more medication and a better organized interior for two people, not just one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Injury-labeled, see-through pockets and an external map make supplies quick to find under stress
- Large trauma pad, irrigation syringe, shears and forceps give it real wound-care capability
- Multiple doses of common medications keep it viable for the full four days
- Includes a wilderness first aid guide
- Bright bag is easy to find; extra internal room for personal additions
- Easy to restock after use

**Cons**

- 386 g is heavy for gram-counters, and well over what a solo ultralight kit needs
- The bag isn't waterproof, so wet-weather trips call for a dry bag or ziplock
- More kit than a solo weekend hiker needs
- At around $57, it costs more than building a lean kit from drugstore items
- Personal medications and preferred blister supplies still have to be added

## Who Should Buy This

Two hikers sharing a pack on trips of two to four days will get the most from it, as will small groups that want a single, well-organized group kit. Newer backpackers who want a kit that tells them where everything is, and includes a guide, are also a natural fit. Solo thru-hikers and ultralight purists should look elsewhere. A smaller waterproof kit like the Ultralight/Watertight .7, or a custom kit built around personal needs, will save considerable weight. Anyone who already carries a well-built personal kit and a first aid course's worth of knowledge gains little from the extra bulk.

## Verdict

The Backpacker is the easy choice for two people on a multi-day trip. It's easy to find things in, stocked beyond the basics, and sensibly priced. It isn't the lightest option, and the bag needs a dry bag in wet weather, but few pre-built kits organize this much into a pack this size.
