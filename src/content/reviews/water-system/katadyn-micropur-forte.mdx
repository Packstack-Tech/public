## Overview

Katadyn's Micropur Forte MF is a 100 ml bottle of liquid disinfectant that pairs chlorine with silver ions. The chlorine kills bacteria and viruses, and the silver keeps the treated water from re-fouling for up to six months. One bottle covers 1,000 liters, so the chemistry costs almost nothing in weight per liter. The trade-off is that it only works on clear water, and the protozoa wait is two hours.

## Performance

**Chemistry.** Micropur Forte is not a chlorine dioxide product like Katadyn's MP1 tablets. It is a conventional chlorine treatment, and retailer listings give the concentrations as 19.5 mg/g of sodium hypochlorite and 0.82 mg/g of silver. The chlorine does the fast work against bacteria and viruses. The silver does the slow work of preservation. Chlorine dioxide has a broader kill spectrum, and this formula doesn't match it. Katadyn's claims cover bacteria, viruses, amoebas and Giardia. Cryptosporidium isn't on the list.

**Contact times.** In clear water, bacteria and viruses are done in 30 minutes. Amoebas and Giardia need 120 minutes. That is a long wait for a hiker who stops at a spring, drinks, and walks on. The usual fix is to treat a liter or two at camp and let it sit overnight. Cold water slows everything. Near-freezing sources can push protozoa contact time toward four hours, so alpine hikers should plan for it.

**Clear water only.** Katadyn is blunt that suspended matter undermines both the chlorine and the silver. After rain, a silty creek needs a pre-filter before any drops go in. A bandana works for coarse debris, and a squeeze filter such as the BeFree works better. Hikers who expect to dip and dose from whatever is flowing will find this limit frustrating.

**Preservation.** Silver is the real differentiator. Few hiker-oriented chemical treatments claim to hold water clean for months, and this one is marketed for exactly that. It prevents algae and bacterial growth in stored, stagnant water. Boat and RV owners are the core buyers. One marina user reported using it for several seasons on water with high iron content and having no problems. Desert hikers who cache water in clear containers have a similar use.

**Trail weight.** The bottle is not the trail weight. A full 100 ml bottle weighs about 147 g and measures roughly 4.2 x 4.2 x 12.2 cm. The 3 g figure is a decanted portion. At 1 ml per 10 L, a hiker drinking 3 L a day for a week needs only 2 to 3 ml. Decant that into a small dropper bottle and the purification system weighs next to nothing. The big bottle stays home as the refill. One buyer noted the packaging doesn't say how many drops go in a liter, so it is worth working that out before leaving.

**Taste.** Chlorine is noticeable. Hikers describe it as less harsh than iodine and more pronounced than chlorine dioxide drops. The taste fades with time, and many people stop noticing it after an hour or so. Katadyn sells Micropur Antichlor, which neutralizes the chlorine taste in about three minutes. A flavored electrolyte mix also covers it.

**Handling.** The product is classified as toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects, and it can irritate skin and eyes. A leaking dropper bottle can bleach clothing and contaminate other gear, so it belongs in a zip-top bag or a hard container. A liquid is also exposed to air every time it is opened, which is a reason to cap it tightly and use it steadily. Dosing is simple, with no mixing and no activation step. If the water goes into a full tank, it needs stirring to spread the active ingredients.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 1,000 L from 100 ml, with a trail portion that weighs about 3 g
- Silver ions keep treated water fresh for up to 6 months
- One-step dosing with no mixing or activation
- 30-minute contact time for bacteria and viruses
- Low cost per liter next to tablets

**Cons**

- Clear water only, so silty sources need a pre-filter
- 120-minute protozoa contact time, longer in cold water
- Chlorine taste unless Antichlor is added
- Spill and bleaching risk from a liquid, plus aquatic toxicity
- No claim against Cryptosporidium
- The bottle is exposed to air with each opening

## Who Should Buy This

Buy it if you travel where tap water is questionable, manage a boat or RV tank, or carry large clear-water caches on dry desert stretches. It also suits hikers who want a nearly weightless chemical backup behind a squeeze filter to cover viruses. Skip it as a primary system if you pull from silty alpine streams, or if you want water drinkable in minutes. A filter or a chlorine dioxide tablet suits that style better.

## Verdict

Micropur Forte MF is a focused tool. It treats very large volumes of clear water cheaply and keeps it clean for months, which almost nothing else in the category does. The turbidity limit and the two-hour protozoa wait keep it from being a fast-moving hiker's main purifier. It earns a place as a travel treatment, a tank preservative, or a featherweight backup.
