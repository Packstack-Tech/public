## Overview

The NeoAir XTherm is Therm-a-Rest's four-season air mattress for people who refuse to carry a pound-and-a-half of insulation under their hips. Reflective ThermaCapture film and Triangular Core Matrix baffles deliver an R-value of 6.9 at 425 g in the Standard size. The trade-offs are a crinkly shell that some sleepers (and their tent mates) notice, and a price that sits well above three-season pads.

## Performance

**Warmth.** This is the XTherm's reason for existing. Reviewers at CleverHiker call it one of the warmest ultralight pads on the market and its go-to for four-season trips. They have tested it in snow, and they say the 6.9 R-value holds up into sub-zero Fahrenheit conditions when it is paired with a suitable bag. Long-distance hikers report a practical benefit: the closed-cell foam pad that many carried under an XLite for shoulder-season nights is no longer needed. Section Hiker, which recommends a minimum R-value of 5 for winter camping and snow, would put the XTherm well above that line. Some warm sleepers on summer trips find it more pad than they need, and report kicking a foot off the edge to balance temperature.

**Weight and packability.** At 425 g, the Standard weighs about what many three-season inflatables do. It rolls to 4 x 9 in, about the size of a Nalgene bottle. Owners comparing it to the XLite note that it adds only a few ounces for a very large jump in insulation. That makes the XTherm a rare case where the warm option doesn't punish the pack scale.

**Comfort.** At 2.5 inches, the pad stays off the ground for back and stomach sleepers, and a 215-pound tosser-and-turner on Trailspace reported sleeping comfortably. The horizontal baffles are a different story for side sleepers. Pads with this layout can feel like they nudge a sleeper toward the edge, and one longtime owner called it a poor choice for side sleeping and said it was easy to roll off. The mummy shape is narrow in the Standard size, which makes the Large's extra 5 inches of width worth considering for restless sleepers.

**Noise.** The XTherm's most common criticism is crinkle. CleverHiker found the current version noticeably quieter than earlier XTherms and XLites, though it is still not silent. Owner opinions on r/Ultralight-style forums split sharply. Some have used the pad for years without noticing noise, and one bought it, took it on a single trip, and sold it. Underinflation tends to make the rustling worse. Light sleepers and anyone sharing a small tent should treat the noise as a real factor.

**Durability.** The 70D bottom fabric is thick for the category. Section Hiker-style testers and thru-hikers praise it. One hiker on Trailspace logged more than 500 miles on the PCT without a failure, and another reviewer says the pad has held up for long-distance hiking as long as camp is chosen with some care. The usual caveat applies: any air pad needs a clear site and a repair kit.

**Inflation.** The WingLock valve is a one-way design that stops air from escaping between breaths or pump-sack squeezes, and it opens fast for deflation. Owners say inflation takes a while on the thicker pad, and the bundled pump sack is a necessity. Fully inflating the full-size pad takes roughly 20 breaths for one Rokslide owner, and CleverHiker lists slower deflation than some competitors among its cons.

## Choosing a Version

**Standard (425 g).** It is 72 x 20 in and packs to 4 x 9 in. This is the version for solo hikers up to about average height who want the lowest weight. It is the size the weight and warmth numbers are usually quoted for.

**Large (540 g).** It is 77 x 25 in, giving taller sleepers five more inches of length and five more inches of width. The extra 115 g buys real comfort for broad-shouldered sleepers and people who shift in the night, which partly offsets the narrow, roll-off feel of the horizontal baffles. It packs to roughly 4.5 x 11 in. Tall users and side sleepers should start here.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- R-value of 6.9 at 425 g, among the best warmth-to-weight ratios in an air pad
- Packs to a Nalgene-sized roll
- 70D bottom fabric survives rough ground better than most ultralight pads
- WingLock valve allows fast deflation and micro-adjustments while lying on the pad
- Pump sack included
- Proven on long trails and in winter conditions

**Cons**

- Crinkly shell, quieter than past generations but not silent
- Horizontal baffles can feel unstable for side sleepers
- Slow to inflate and, per some testers, slower to deflate than rivals
- Expensive
- Overkill for summer-only use

## Who Should Buy This

The XTherm suits cold sleepers, shoulder-season and winter backpackers, and ultralighters who want one pad for all four seasons. It also suits anyone who wants to stop carrying a foam pad as a backup layer. It is a poor fit for summer-only hikers, who can save weight and money with an XLite, and for noise-sensitive light sleepers sharing a tent. Buyers should also know that Therm-a-Rest has since released the NXT version, which is 3 inches thick and rated R-7.3. The original remains a very warm pad, but it is the older design.

## Verdict

The NeoAir XTherm remains one of the best answers to the question of how to stay warm on frozen ground without a heavy pack. It gives up some quiet and some side-sleeping stability for its warmth and weight, a bargain most winter hikers will take. It is a clear buy for four-season use and an unnecessary expense for anyone who camps only in summer.
