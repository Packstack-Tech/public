## Overview

The Give-N-Go 1.0 Boxer Brief is the pair that taught a generation of travelers and thru-hikers to wash their underwear in a sink and wear it dry the next morning. It is a stretchy nylon mesh boxer brief with a 5.5-inch inseam, built for people who want to carry two pairs instead of seven. The trade-off is familiar to anyone who has chosen synthetics over merino: speed and durability on one side, a less forgiving nose on the other.

## Performance

**Drying.** This is the whole premise, and it holds up. The open diamond mesh sheds water quickly, and PackHacker's reviewers noted that drying time is the one place these beat merino outright. A Backpacking Light member once reported putting a pair on wet after a wash in New Mexico and finding them dry in under an hour, though he cautioned that humid climates would be a different story. Wring, roll in a towel, hang, and they are generally ready by morning.

**Chafing.** Owners keep returning to this point. The thin, smooth nylon clings to the skin without gripping, and the flat seams stay out of the way. Reviewers on Trailspace describe 15-mile days of heavy sweating with no chafing, and one Appalachian Trail thru-hiker carried two pairs for the whole route in 2012, reporting trouble only on a day of driving rain and very high mileage. The 5.5-inch leg covers enough thigh to keep skin from rubbing on skin. Some owners also like that the waistband sits slightly high, so it rides above the waistband of shorts or pants instead of under it.

**Odor.** The Aegis treatment helps, and several long-term users say they go days between washes without complaint. It is not merino, though. Reviewers who put synthetics next to wool consistently give wool the edge on smell over multi-day wear, and at least one Backpacking Light poster abandoned the Give-N-Go for Icebreaker for that reason. Hikers who rinse their underwear every day or two will not notice. Those who wear one pair for a week will.

**Durability.** The strongest argument for the 1.0. Buyers on ExOfficio's own site describe pairs four or more years old that still look new, and Outdoor Gear Lab's testers scored the line 9 out of 10 for construction quality when they evaluated the 2.0 update. Synthetics of this type are generally reported to outlast merino by a wide margin. One long-term owner of about a dozen pairs said the waistbands eventually started to go after five or six years of use, which is hardly a damning timeline. Line drying prolongs life further; ExOfficio-style nylon does not love a hot dryer.

**Weight and packing.** ExOfficio's spec sheet lists 99.8 g, while PackHacker weighed a size Large at 86.5 g. Either way, a pair rolls to roughly the size of a fist and weighs less than a typical energy bar.

**Fit.** Standard and true to size, per PackHacker. The leg is looser than athletic compression-style briefs, which suits larger thighs but may feel baggy on lean builds. Some reviewers of the Sport Mesh variants advise sizing down for more support; that advice is aimed at a different, snugger product and should not be assumed here.

**Versus the 2.0.** ExOfficio now sells a 2.0 with claims of up to four times the breathability and 25% faster drying. Treeline Review judges the two nearly identical in practice. One REI reviewer who had worn 1.0s for a decade reported that a 2.0 pilled badly within days, an anecdote that favors tracking down the original if it turns up on sale.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Dries overnight, usually faster, so two pairs cover any trip
- Smooth fabric and 5.5-inch leg limit thigh chafing
- Durable enough to last years of regular washing
- About 90 g and packs small
- True to size, with a waistband that stays flat

**Cons**

- Odor control is weaker than merino on multi-day wear
- Priced at $26 per pair, a lot for underwear
- Should be line dried rather than tumbled hot
- The looser leg can feel baggy on slim builds
- Waistband does not suit everyone, and long-term owners report eventual wear there

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers, section hikers and long-haul travelers who wash often and want a two-pair kit that will not need replacing for several seasons. It also suits hot, humid trips where cotton and wool become liabilities. Hikers who wear the same pair for five days straight, or who want the smell-proof reputation of wool, should look at merino boxer briefs instead. Budget-minded hikers can try a cheap micro-mesh brief, though it will not match the build quality.

## Verdict

The Give-N-Go 1.0 remains a benchmark for synthetic hiking underwear: fast-drying, comfortable under a pack and remarkably long-lived. Buy it if you wash regularly and value durability over odor resistance. It earns an 8.5 out of 10.
