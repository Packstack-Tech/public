## Overview

The Cloud Up 2 Pro is Naturehike's upgraded take on its best-known freestanding backpacking tent. It adds a higher-headroom door design and a 20D nylon rainfly to a pitch-anywhere double-wall format that costs a fraction of what Big Agnes or MSR charge. The trade-off is the one the whole Cloud Up family carries: the "2" is generous, and the tent is happiest as a roomy solo shelter.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** At 1,800 g, the Pro sits well above the featherweight end of Naturehike's own line. Reddit's r/Ultralight regulars point to the Cloud Up 2 UL, a roughly 2.6 lb version, as the pick for anyone who wants the lowest weight in the family. What the Pro offers in exchange is heavier 20D fabric. The 40 x 13 cm packed bundle is slim enough that reviewers comparing it against pricier rivals describe it as putting several of them to shame. Naturehike's published weights have also held up in owner checks of the Cloud Up line.

**Weather.** Owners of the Cloud Up series report few leaks. One long-term Trailspace reviewer cited YKK zippers and a dry interior through heavy downpours. Another owner described pitching in a storm with hard rain and wind and coming through dry. The aluminum poles are widely praised as strong for the price.

Wind is a matter of orientation. The tent is a wedge, and testers say it handles gusts well when the narrow end faces the wind. A broadside hit flattens the walls, though the poles spring back. Plan on guying it out and swapping the stock stakes for sturdier ones if exposed camps are on the itinerary.

**Space.** This is where the name overpromises. Sloping walls eat into usable room, and the single most common owner verdict across the Cloud Up line is "one person plus gear, a dog or a small child." Two adults on full-width pads will find the fit tight, and one buyer wished for more room for two sleeping mats. A Trailspace reviewer concluded that sloping walls and a single door make it a poor match for two independent adults. The Pro's higher-headroom door design is aimed at easing entry and exit, but nothing in owner reports suggests it changes the floor-space math.

**Setup.** The pole architecture is conventional, and owners generally call pitching quick. One buyer set it up in the dark in about 20 minutes on a first attempt. A UK review team that tested the standard Cloud Up found the included instructions thin and the diagrams confusing at points, though the structure is no harder than comparable tents once understood.

**Ventilation.** An Australian owner on r/Ultralight called the Cloud Up 2 UL a hot tent in summer. Double-wall nylon with limited mesh area is the likely reason. It makes the tent better suited to cool and mild conditions than to humid heat.

**Durability and small things.** Long-term owners over a year or more, including one with three years of use across national parks, report no major failures. The 20D nylon rewards gentle handling at pitch and pack-down. The stuff sack is thin, and one owner noted that stuffing a wet tent into it will dampen the rest of a pack. Some buyers found the reflective guylines hard to see at night.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 1,800 g for a freestanding double-wall tent at roughly $140–180
- Slim 40 x 13 x 13 cm packed size
- 20D nylon fly and aluminum poles that owners rate as tough and weatherproof
- Pitches fast, and stands without stakes
- Higher-headroom door design improves entry over earlier Cloud Up versions

**Cons**

- Heavier than the Cloud Up 2 UL
- Tight for two adults, with sloping walls
- Wind performance depends on pitching the narrow end into the breeze
- Runs warm in summer heat
- Thin stuff sack, stock stakes and guylines that owners tend to upgrade

## Who Should Buy This

The Pro suits solo backpackers who want a freestanding double-wall tent with room for gear, or couples who are small, close and willing to trade space for weight. It also fits as a first lightweight tent or a backup for experienced hikers. Two adults who each need a door, tall sleepers, and anyone chasing the lowest possible base weight should look at a two-door design such as Naturehike's Cloud Peak 2 or Star River 2, or at the lighter Cloud Up 2 UL.

## Verdict

The Cloud Up 2 Pro delivers dry, stable, easily pitched shelter at a price that undercuts name-brand freestanding tents by half or more. Treat it as a roomy one-person tent that tolerates a second occupant in a pinch, and it earns its place in the pack.
