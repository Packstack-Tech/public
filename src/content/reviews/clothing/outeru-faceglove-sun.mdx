## Overview

The faceGlove SUN is a sliver of Polartec® Power Shield that covers the nose and cheekbones, the two patches of skin most likely to blister on an exposed ridge. It weighs 9.8 g, and it clips straight onto sunglasses instead of looping behind the ears. The trade-off is simple: it is minimal, fast and easy to forget about, but it protects only what it covers and only while your sunglasses are on your face.

## Performance

**Sun protection.** The SUN is the warm-weather sibling in OuterU's faceGlove line. OuterU calls it the lightest and most breathable faceGlove it makes, with UPF 50+ fabric. The idea is to replace the sunscreen you forget to reapply. Backpacker's senior editor made that point about the winter FLEX model: she has a hard time reapplying sunscreen on long days, and a fixed layer of fabric does the job without her. On a sun-baked ridge, desert wash or open lake, the same logic applies. The nose-tip pocket is meant to shade the tip of the nose, which burns first. Reviewers of the line also credit it with cutting some reflected glare and wind.

**Attachment.** This is what separates the SUN from the rest of the line. The FLEX and earlier faceGloves hold on with soft ear loops. Reviewers generally like those loops, but they are still one more thing to fiddle with under a hood or hat. The SUN drops them. Greg at White Mountain Ski, who tested it in Baxter State Park and on Mt. Washington, described the appeal plainly. It goes on and off with the sunglasses, and he no longer has to wrestle ear loops while wearing a hood in the wind at Tuckerman Ravine. He said it earned a permanent spot in his pack. Hat wearers get the same benefit: there is no strap tangled up with a brim or a cap's back adjuster.

**Fit and fogging.** A dual adjustable wire system lets the wearer bend the mask around the bridge and cheeks. OuterU says that is what keeps it in place through wind, sweat and movement. The faceGlove concept also leaves the mouth and nostrils uncovered, so breath doesn't get trapped behind fabric and fog lenses. Owners and testers of the line keep returning to this point. Backpacker's tester said the ear-loop FLEX was comfortable enough that she forgot she was wearing it. Moldable-wire fit takes some setup, and the first fitting is the step where users of earlier faceGlove models have struggled. A mask that clips to sunglasses should be adjusted at home, not at the trailhead.

**Packability.** At 9.8 g it has no real weight penalty. A flat, soft, shell-fabric piece of this size disappears into a hip-belt pocket or a hat. Reviewers of the family describe stuffing the mask into a pocket when conditions warm up and pulling it out again when the wind returns. That is the right habit here too. It is also assembled by hand in New Hampshire, which is unusual for a 10 g accessory.

**Limits.** The SUN is not a full-face solution. Chin, jaw, lips, ears and neck are all uncovered, so a hiker in true high-exposure terrain still needs a hat, a buff or sunscreen for those areas. Because it is carried by the sunglasses, it only works while the glasses are on. Anyone who takes them off to look at a map or eat lunch loses coverage and has to re-clip. Early forum owners of the original faceGlove also found the fabric thin and cheaply finished for its role as a cold-weather nose warmer. That complaint is about winter warmth, not UV, but it signals that this is a minimalist piece of kit and should be treated as one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 9.8 g: effectively no pack weight
- UPF 50+ Polartec® Power Shield fabric covers the nose and cheeks
- Clips to sunglasses, with no ear loops or head straps to manage
- Nose-tip pocket and dual wire allow a shaped fit
- Mouth stays open, so breathing is unobstructed and lenses stay clearer
- Handmade in the USA

**Cons**

- Covers only the nose and cheeks; chin, ears and neck need other protection
- Depends on sunglasses being worn
- Wire fit takes setup and some adjustment
- Distinctive look that even fans of the brand call a little goofy

## Who Should Buy This

The SUN suits hikers, paddlers, anglers and sailors who already wear sunglasses all day and burn on the nose and cheekbones in spite of sunscreen. It is especially good for exposed alpine and desert routes, where reflected light adds to the dose. Hikers who pull a hood and hat on and off through changing weather will appreciate the strapless design. Hikers who want one piece of kit to cover the whole face, neck and ears should look at a sun hoodie or a hooded balaclava instead. People who wear prescription glasses with unusual frames, or who rarely wear glasses at all, should consider the ear-loop faceGlove FLEX or GO.

## Verdict

The faceGlove SUN does one narrow job at a weight that is almost unmeasurable on a pack scale, and it removes the ear-loop step that makes other face masks annoying. It earns a spot in the kit of anyone who burns on the face while wearing sunglasses. It is less useful for anyone who wants broader coverage from a single item.
