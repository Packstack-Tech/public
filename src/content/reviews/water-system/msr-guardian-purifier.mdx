## Overview

The MSR Guardian is a hand-pump water purifier built to a U.S. military standard. Its 0.02-micron hollow-fiber membrane physically removes viruses along with bacteria, protozoa, and sediment. It suits international travelers, expedition parties, and anyone drinking from sketchy sources. The cost of that protection is weight, bulk, and a price tag that makes a Sawyer Squeeze look like a rounding error.

## Performance

**Water quality.** The Guardian's draw is that it purifies rather than merely filters. A float-mounted pre-filter keeps big debris out of the hose, and the hollow-fiber element handles the rest. Ultimate Gear Lists reports that even silty, sediment-heavy sources come out looking clear. Outdoor Gear Lab scored it 9 out of 10 for water quality, one of the few hand pumps that can stop viruses. One limit: there is no activated carbon, so industrial chemicals and heavy metals pass through. For most backcountry use that doesn't matter. For farmland runoff or mining country, it does.

**Speed and effort.** MSR rates the flow at 2.5 L/min, and testers at Better Trail put the stroke count at about 35 per liter. One Trailspace reviewer clocked roughly 2.1 L/min at a comfortable pace and said hitting 2.5 takes aggressive pumping. That's still about 30 seconds a liter. Reviewers also describe a smooth, rigid handle that doesn't flex, and say it feels less fatiguing than older pump filters after several liters. Outdoor Gear Lab scored ease of filtration lower, at 6 out of 10, so a pump remains a pump. It also can't be inline-mounted on a hydration hose for hands-free drinking.

**Maintenance.** This is where the Guardian separates itself from the field. About 10 percent of the water drawn in is sent back across the membrane as it pumps, flushing the fibers continuously. Owners report no flow decay and no need to backflush, which is the usual chore with hollow-fiber pumps. The one upkeep item is rinsing the pressure relief valve with clean water now and then. Better Trail's testers saw consistent performance after weeks of daily use, and Outdoor Gear Lab named the build "insanely durable". It works in freezing, hot, and silty conditions alike, which is where lighter squeeze filters tend to struggle.

**Weight and packability.** On paper the Guardian is 490 g, about five times a Sawyer Squeeze. Testers who weigh the complete kit with hoses and carry bag report figures closer to 1 lb 9 oz or more. Outdoor Gear Lab gave it 2 out of 10 for weight and packability, and CleverHiker describes the weight as its biggest drawback. At 8.2 x 4.7 in, it takes real room in a pack. Anyone carrying a sub-10-lb base weight will feel it.

**Cost.** Better Trail lists it at $400, and replacement filters are also expensive. At 10,000 L of rated life, though, a solo hiker will never wear one out. The cost is front-loaded, but the unit is built to outlast most gear in a pack.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Removes viruses, with a 0.02-micron membrane meeting NSF P248 and U.S. military standards
- Self-cleaning design avoids flow-rate decay and routine backflushing
- Roughly 30 seconds per liter at a comfortable pumping pace
- Rigid, well-built handle and a design that works in cold, heat, and silt
- 10,000 L rated filter life
- Can pull water from shallow puddles through the float intake
- Made in the USA

**Cons**

- Heavy and bulky next to squeeze and gravity filters
- Expensive up front, with pricey replacement filters
- No carbon stage, so no chemical or heavy-metal removal
- Pump-only; no hands-free drinking
- Dropping the output hose can cross-contaminate it, so the user has to handle it carefully
- Overkill for typical North American backcountry water

## Who Should Buy This

The Guardian fits international trekkers, expedition and basecamp groups, guides, and anyone filtering liters at a time from silty or questionable sources where viruses are a real risk. It also suits people who value a pump that doesn't need cleaning in the field. Ultralight hikers on domestic trails, where bacteria and protozoa are the main concern, should look elsewhere: a Sawyer Squeeze or Katadyn BeFree weighs a fraction as much and costs a fraction as much. Solo weekenders will never use its capacity.

## Verdict

The MSR Guardian is the best hand-pump purifier available when the water is bad and the stakes are high. It's close to bulletproof, fast, and nearly maintenance-free. For most ultralight use it's the wrong tool, and its rating reflects that: excellent at what it was built for, a poor fit for gram-counting.
