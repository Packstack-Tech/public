## Overview

The Stealth looks like a low-poly 3D render of a climbing helmet, and that angular polycarbonate shell is there for a reason. Co-molded onto expanded polystyrene foam, it protects the soft inner layer from the dings that wreck bare-foam helmets in a pack or on a rack. The trade-off is a few grams over the lightest foam-only lids, plus a headlamp attachment system that owners find fiddly.

## Performance

**Weight and comfort.** Grivel quotes 190 g, or 6.7 oz. GearJunkie's testers noted that other scales put it between 6.9 and 7.4 oz, so expect a little drift from the claim. The weight is rarely the story on the head. UKC Gear's reviewer said the helmet is barely noticeable in use, and Trek & Mountain made the same point. Customers at European retailers repeatedly list weight and comfort as the top reasons to buy. A Bergfreunde review page showed a 4.8 average across 17 ratings, with 95 percent recommending it.

**Durability.** This is where the shell earns its keep. The Climbing Zine's reviewer has found that very light helmets tend to get smashed up, and pointed to the Stealth as a lightweight one that tolerates bumps. UKC Gear reported several months of rugged use without damage. Trek & Mountain's reviewer was confident the helmet would survive being stuffed in a rucksack. One owner reported ice strikes that he expected to crack the shell, and the helmet was unscathed on inspection. The shell does cosmetic penance: buyers on Bergfreunde mention dents and scratches. Some reviewers say the helmet is a soft-shell design by classification, despite feeling like a hard shell.

**Ventilation.** Sixteen vents make this one of the airier helmets in its class. Trek & Mountain's reviewer rejected Grivel's "best airflow ever made" line but still rated the mix of airflow, durability and protection as possibly the best around. UKC Gear called the venting second to none. In practice that suits summer alpine routes and sweaty cragging, and it matters less on a cold belay, where the same holes let air in.

**Harness and fit.** The adjustment system is a simple webbing strap at the back of the head with ladder-lock buckles, pulled from either side. Reviewers at GearJunkie liked that it adjusts with gloves on and with the helmet on or off. They also preferred it to ratchets and dials that fail under abuse. The whole harness tucks inside the shell for packing. The Climbing Zine's reviewer found the rear strap awkward on first use but got used to it after a few tries. The low-profile shell fits under a hood without fuss.

Fit is the main caveat. GearJunkie recommends the helmet for medium to large heads and says it doesn't suit small ones well. An Alpinist reviewer with a 56 cm head, who tested the heavier Stealth HS version of the same design, found the one-size harness frustrating to dial in. Trek & Mountain's reviewer noted that the sticker in the test helmet read 53–61 cm rather than the claimed 54–62 cm. That reviewer's head just fit, with no room for a hat in cold conditions. Other owners wear it over a thin beanie or fleece hat without problems. Additional foam pads are available to fine-tune the fit.

**Coverage.** The Climbing Zine's reviewer said the Stealth has more neck coverage at the back than most helmets, which counts in an upside-down fall. Climbing's reviewer said the rear drops low, below the occipital bun.

**Headlamp clips.** This is the most common complaint. The four low-profile clips sit flush with the shell and double as harness anchor points. Grivel's reasoning, relayed by GearJunkie, is that you take the helmet off and flip it over to attach the lamp, rather than stretching a band over the dome. In practice, reviewers at GearJunkie, UKC Gear and Climbing Gear Reviews UK all found the system a hassle compared with the elastic-and-hook clips on other helmets. The clips do hold a lamp securely once it is on.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 190 g claimed, light enough to forget on long approaches and multi-pitch days
- Polycarbonate shell shrugs off pack abuse better than bare-foam helmets
- 16 vents keep air moving on hot climbs
- Webbing harness is simple, glove-friendly and packs flat inside the shell
- Deep rear coverage and a low profile that fits under hoods
- Retail price around €95 undercuts many premium ultralight helmets

**Cons**

- One-size harness is best suited to medium and large heads
- Headlamp clips are slow to use and take the helmet off the head
- Shell scratches and dents cosmetically
- Little room for a hat in cold weather on the smaller end of the range
- The angular styling divides opinion

## Who Should Buy This

Alpinists, ice climbers and multi-pitch rock climbers who want a helmet light enough to carry all day, and tough enough to be stuffed in a pack without a stuff-sack hug. It's also a fit for thru-hikers and fastpackers who need a certified helmet for the occasional scramble or glacier crossing. Climbers with small heads should try one on first. Anyone who changes headlamps constantly in the dark should look at helmets with conventional clips.

## Verdict

The Stealth balances weight, protection, venting and durability better than most helmets at its price. The headlamp system and one-size fit are the real drawbacks, and both are easy to check before buying. For anyone with an average-to-large head who climbs across rock, ice and alpine terrain, it is a strong all-round pick.
