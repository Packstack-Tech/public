## Overview

The Coleman 100-Quart Xtreme Wheeled Cooler is a big, plain, blow-molded ice chest built for car camps, boat days and trailhead resupply tables, not for anything that touches a shoulder strap. At 9,525 g (about 21 lb) empty, it makes no claim to being light. What it offers is 160-can capacity, a tow handle and a price that sits far below rotomolded competitors, in exchange for ice retention that depends heavily on how it's packed and handling that rewards wheels over carrying.

## Performance

**Insulation.** Coleman rates the Xtreme for five days of ice at 90°F, and owners largely back that up under favorable conditions. One buyer who packed it with the recommended amount of ice and pre-chilled drinks reported things staying cold for at least eight days; another with three Xtreme coolers says all of them hold ice for five days through hot summers. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers were less dazzled, scoring its insulation 6 out of 10 and noting the Igloo Glide PRO delivers the same insulating performance. Skeptics on deal forums are blunter: a cooler this size loaded with a couple of bags of store-bought ice and room-temperature contents turns into an ice-water bath in a long weekend. The pattern is consistent. Block ice, a pre-chilled interior and a shaded spot get close to the claim. Casual packing gets two to three days.

**Rolling and carrying.** The tow handle is the selling point, and owners say it feels sturdy for plastic, with one reporting 90 to 100 lb inside while pulling it a good walking distance. Outdoor Gear Lab was less impressed, finding it rolls less easily than the best wheeled models and describing the cooler as large for its handles on a one-person carry. Marine reviewers at Motor Boat & Yachting flagged tight ground clearance under the box on rough ground and loose sand. Two people with the rigid side handles is the intended carry once it's loaded.

**Build.** The HDPE shell and foam-injected walls keep it lighter than most 100-quart boat coolers, per Motor Boat & Yachting, which also found the lid supports up to 110 kg. Four recessed cup holders hold drink cans and mugs, and the lid doubles as a bench. Owners praise the drain: it empties without tilting and the plug seals well. The durability complaints are specific. At least one owner on his third Xtreme reports hinge failures on the first two, and Outdoor Gear Lab's durability scores were modest. This is a cooler priced to be replaced eventually, not inherited.

**Inside space.** The 31 x 13 x 11.5 in interior is long and shallow. Case-style loads of cans fit; tall bottles and big jugs are awkward, and the 160-can rating assumes no ice. Add the ice that this cooler's performance depends on and real capacity drops substantially.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Large 100-quart capacity at a budget price; Outdoor Gear Lab called it a good value at its $100 list price
- Five-day retention is achievable with block ice and pre-chilling
- Tow handle holds up under 90 to 100 lb loads, per owners
- Lid works as a seat and has four cup holders
- Channel drain empties without tilting

**Cons**

- 21 lb empty, and far heavier once packed with ice
- Wheels roll less smoothly than the best wheeled coolers, and clearance is low on sand or rough ground
- Real-world ice retention often falls short of the five-day claim
- Hinge failures reported by repeat buyers
- Interior is shallow for tall items

## Who Should Buy This

Car campers, tailgaters, pool-party hosts and boaters who need to move a lot of cold food and drinks across a parking lot or campground for a weekend, and who would rather spend $100 than several hundred on a rotomolded chest. It also suits anyone staging a section-hike resupply from a vehicle. Backpackers, bikepackers and anyone who needs ice retention they can count on past day three in direct sun should look elsewhere, whether to a rotomolded cooler or a smaller, better-packed one.

## Verdict

The Coleman 100-Quart Xtreme is a competent, inexpensive big-volume cooler that works best when the user packs it like a pro and treats it as a seasonal appliance. It doesn't match the ice retention or ruggedness of premium coolers, but it doesn't cost what they do either.
