## Overview

Naturehike's foldable camping chair (sold as the PeakLite Ultralight Foldable Camping Chair) is a shock-corded, tent-pole-style seat built around a 7075 aluminum frame and 600D Oxford fabric. It is aimed at campers, festival-goers and hikers who want a real backrest for around a kilogram without paying Helinox money. The trade-off is weight: at 1,080 g it is a car-camping and base-camp chair first, and a thru-hiking luxury a distant second.

## Performance

**Setup and stability.** The frame goes together like a tent: pole sections snap along internal bungee cord, the seat fabric slips over the ends, and the chair is ready in about a minute. Owners on Naturehike's own page like the two-bar design, which keeps the chair from spinning while it is being assembled. Independent testers of Naturehike's closely related YL08, which shares the 7075 two-pole layout, measured poles about 0.59 in thick. That is chunkier than most budget chairs, and it shows in how little the frame flexes under a seated adult.

**Soft ground.** The anti-sink feet are spherical rubber-style caps that spread the load over a wider contact patch. On sand, damp grass and the soft margins of a lakeshore campsite, that is the difference between sitting level and slowly tilting backward as one leg digs in. One owner of the related YL01 reported the feet have to come off before the chair will pack down completely, which is a minor chore. They stay in the bag.

**Comfort.** The 55 cm seat is wider than most packable chairs, and the contoured back wraps the shoulders rather than perching a sling under them. Mesh side panels vent heat in summer. Testers of the sibling YL08 gave comfort its highest marks, though they also noted the 600D fabric is thick and holds warmth, a point in its favor on cold evenings and a mild annoyance on a humid beach. A field test of a similar Naturehike ultralight chair flagged the low seat height, the snug fit for larger sitters and the lack of armrests or a cup holder. Anyone who dislikes folding themselves up out of a ground-level seat should take note.

**Durability.** The 7075 alloy is the same grade used in many premium trekking poles and tent poles, and the 150 kg rating gives an ample margin for most users. The usual weak point on any chair of this style is where the poles meet the hubs. Careless folding or sitting down hard is where cheap versions fail, and owners generally report that Naturehike's QC holds up better than that of anonymous clones.

**Packability.** At 50 cm long and 13 × 14 cm in cross-section, the bag is a baguette rather than a coin. It slides down the side of a duffel or a 40-liter pack, but it will not vanish into a running vest, and some 1,000 g chairs like the Helinox Chair Zero pack smaller.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 55 cm seat is roomy for a packable chair
- 7075 alloy frame with a 150 kg load rating
- Anti-sink feet make it usable on sand and soft turf
- Assembles in about a minute with no loose parts to sort
- Mesh side panels add ventilation
- Priced well below premium ultralight chairs

**Cons**

- At 1,080 g, it is heavy for a backpacking-specific item
- Low seat height makes getting up harder
- No armrests or storage pockets
- Foot covers must be removed to pack the chair completely
- Thick 600D fabric runs warm in hot weather

## Who Should Buy This

Car campers, kayakers, festival-goers and anglers who want a comfortable seat that stays out of the way in the trunk will get the most out of it. Section hikers and weekend backpackers who accept a gear-weight penalty for a real backrest at camp are a decent second group, especially if their routes include sand or soft ground. Thru-hikers and gram-counters watching base weight should skip it for a sit pad or a lighter chair, and anyone who wants armrests or a high seat will be happier with a conventional camp chair.

## Verdict

Naturehike's foldable camping chair is a well-built, wide-seated, stable chair that costs less than the premium names and is a good fit for casual camping. It is too heavy to be an ultralight chair, but for car camping and short trips it is a sound buy.
