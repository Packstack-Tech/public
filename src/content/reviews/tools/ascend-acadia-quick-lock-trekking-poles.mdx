## Overview

The Ascend Acadia Quick Lock Trekking Poles are Bass Pro Shops' house-brand take on a classic 3-section aluminum trekking pole. They're aimed at day hikers, hunters, and casual backpackers who want a dependable, adjustable pole without spending carbon-fiber money — think of them as a gateway pole rather than a thru-hiker's dedicated tool.

## Performance

**Build and materials.** The 7075-T6 aluminum shafts are a genuine step up from the cheap 7001 or unspecified aluminum you find on many sub-$30 poles — <cite index="4-15">these are made of strong yet lightweight 7075-T6 anodized aluminum with quick-lock adjustment</cite>. That alloy is stiff and dent-resistant, so the poles feel solid in hand and don't flex noticeably under load on rocky descents.

**Adjustment mechanism.** The quick-lock lever system is genuinely quick — flip both levers, slide to your height, snap shut. It's faster than twist-locks, especially with cold or gloved hands. That said, this is also the poles' weak point. In one multi-day field report I found, a hiker using the Acadia poles as improvised tent poles noted the plastic quick lock started breaking right below where the spike section stopped, after just three nights of that kind of stress. To be fair, that's an off-label use case (poles doing shelter duty, not just walking), but it does suggest the lever housing is plastic rather than metal, and plastic parts under repeated cinching stress are the first thing to watch for degradation over a multi-month trip.

**Grip and strap.** The EVA/cork blend grip is comfortable for a budget pole — cork wicks a bit of sweat and molds slightly to your hand over time, which is a nice touch at this price point. The wrist straps are padded and adjustable, though they're not the quick-release style you'll find on pricier poles like the Black Diamond Distance or Leki Micro Vario.

**Weight.** At 9 oz per pole (18 oz / 510 g for the pair), these sit solidly in the "affordable aluminum" weight class — noticeably heavier than ultralight carbon poles like the Gossamer Gear LT5 (around 5 oz/pole) but competitive with other big-box aluminum poles like Cascade Mountain Tech, which run closer to 10.4 oz per pole. If you're chasing an ultralight base weight, these aren't the pick; if you just want a durable pole for day hikes and moderate trips, the weight is a non-issue.

**Versatility.** The interchangeable carbide and rubber tips are a genuinely useful feature — carbide bites into dirt, roots, and rock, while the rubber tip cover protects floors and pavement on the drive to the trailhead. Bundled trekking baskets (and snow baskets on some listings) extend usability into shoulder-season and light winter conditions.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 7075-T6 aluminum construction feels sturdy and resists denting better than cheaper alloys
- Quick-lock levers are fast to operate, even with gloves on
- Comfortable EVA/cork grip for the price point
- Comes with interchangeable carbide/rubber tips and baskets — no separate purchase needed
- Widely available and affordably priced through Bass Pro and Cabela's

**Cons**

- Plastic lock lever housing is a potential failure point under sustained or heavy stress
- Heavier than dedicated ultralight poles — not built for gram-counters
- Wrist straps lack the quick-release feature found on premium poles
- Limited independent long-term durability data — most available feedback is from manufacturer listings rather than extensive trail testing

## Who Should Buy This

These poles make sense for casual to moderate hikers, hunters, and anglers (wading use is explicitly called out by Ascend) who want a reliable, adjustable pole without spending $150+ on carbon fiber. If you're a thru-hiker chasing every ounce or someone who regularly uses trekking poles as tent supports, I'd look at a purpose-built ultralight pole with a metal lever mechanism instead.

## Verdict

The Ascend Acadia Quick Lock poles deliver solid aluminum construction and a comfortable grip at a price that undercuts most name-brand competitors, making them a reasonable choice for weekend hikers and multi-use outdoor pursuits. Just keep an eye on that plastic lock lever if you're putting them through repeated heavy use — I'd call this a dependable 6/10: good value, but not a pole I'd bet a thru-hike on.
