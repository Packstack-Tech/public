## Overview

The Delta Insulated Mug is a 473 ml (16 fl oz) polypropylene cup with a removable polyurethane sleeve and a sip-through lid. It is built for backpackers who want a hot drink that stays hot and a cup they can grip right after the boil. The trade-off is the lid: owners praise the thermal performance and are split on whether the lid seals.

## Performance

**Heat retention.** This is the mug's strongest suit. A tester on Trailspace poured boiling water into an earlier nylon-bodied Delta Insul-Mug and could hold it straight away with hands that felt only nicely warm. Coffee in that mug stayed near-scalding through a 15°F morning of breaking camp in the Virginia high country. A more recent UK comparison of four camping mugs found the same thing: after 15 minutes the Delta held its heat best, and the drink was still too hot to sip at once. Owners credit the sleeve and lid working together. One buyer who had watched coffee go cold in under five minutes in an open-top mug bought it for that reason.

**Handling.** There is no handle, and none is needed. The sleeve acts as the grip, so a cup of boiling water can be picked up and carried to the tent door. Reviewers also like that a handleless mug packs more neatly than a handled one. The wide, low shape makes it hard to knock over when pouring. The base pattern adds grip on slick surfaces. Several owners note it is not stackable, which makes storage in a pot awkward.

**The lid.** The lid is where opinions split. Some owners like that nothing protrudes to bump against the face and report no leaks. Others report the opposite: one Sea to Summit customer said the lid did not seal and dripped each time they drank from the spout, and a European buyer said half the drink ended up down their front. In practice it is a steam-venting sip lid, not a travel-mug seal. Treat it as a spill guard in a tent vestibule and don't toss it into a pack with liquid in it.

**Staining and flavor.** Light-colored plastic and coffee don't mix. One owner reported coffee stains they couldn't remove. An older Trailspace review of the nylon version also said the material seemed to change the flavor of coffee. Tea and cocoa drinkers are less likely to notice.

**Durability.** Reviewers describe the mug body as sturdy and abuse-tolerant. The weak point is the sleeve, which one tester thought could tear if packed beside anything sharp. The tester saw no damage in use. Because the sleeve comes off, the cup can also be used bare, and the plastic body insulates a little by itself.

**Weight.** At 125 g (about 4.4 oz), it is not the lightest way to drink coffee. A titanium cup weighs a fraction of that. But a bare titanium mug conducts heat to the lips, and users who have switched say they would rather carry the extra grams than burn their mouths. The Delta also holds 473 ml, enough for a full instant-oatmeal or soup serving, with measurement markings inside.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent heat retention, confirmed by trail users and a side-by-side test
- Sleeve makes it comfortable to hold right off the boil
- 473 ml capacity covers soup, cocoa or a big coffee
- Dishwasher and microwave safe
- Stable, wide base with a non-slip pattern
- Inexpensive, at around £10.75 at one UK retailer

**Cons**

- The lid drips for some owners
- Not stackable
- Plastic can stain with coffee
- Heavier than titanium
- Sleeve may tear against sharp objects
- No handle, which some drinkers dislike

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who start cold mornings with coffee, tea or cocoa and want it hot for the first 15 minutes of the day will get the most from it. It also suits anyone who has burned their lips on a titanium mug. It works for car campers and weekend hikers too, because the price is low and the cup also goes in a dishwasher.

Gram-counters who cook and drink from one titanium pot should skip it. So should people who want a leakproof lid for travel, or who drink black coffee from a mug they intend to keep spotless.

## Verdict

The Delta is a good cold-weather mug with a lid that does not always live up to its design. If the main job is to keep a drink hot and spare the hands, it does that well for about 125 g and a small outlay. Buyers who need a sealed lid or a minimal base weight should look elsewhere.
