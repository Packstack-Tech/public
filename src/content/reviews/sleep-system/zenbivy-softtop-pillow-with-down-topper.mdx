## Overview

The Zenbivy SoftTop is a three-piece camp pillow: an ultralight air bladder for height, a 650+ fill power duck down topper for softness, and a machine-washable pillowcase that holds both together. At 231 g in the large size, it is built for side sleepers who want a pillow that feels like one rather than a stuff sack of dirty socks. The trade-off is plain: it weighs more than most inflatable pillows, and the down topper accounts for well over half of that.

## Performance

**Comfort and support.** The pitch is a division of labor. An air-only pillow is bouncy and firm; a down-only pillow collapses under a head. The SoftTop stacks the two, and the layering is where reviewers land most enthusiastically. The Pacific Northwest hiking site I Heart Pacific Northwest calls it the most comfortable backpacking pillow its reviewer has used, citing noticeably more height and support than most competitors, particularly for side sleeping. The same site names it a top pick in its backpacking pillow roundup. Owner ratings on the topper run 4.9 out of 5 across nearly a hundred reviews, with buyers describing it as a pillow they now bring everywhere, including planes and cars.

**Adjustability.** Because the bladder is inflated by mouth, loft is a dial rather than a fixed number. A few puffs gives a low, squishy pillow for back sleepers; a full inflation gives a side sleeper enough rise to keep the neck level with the spine. The down layer takes the edge off the plastic feel that makes many air pillows feel like sleeping on a balloon.

**Weight and packability.** This is where the criticism sits. The Radavist's reviewer, who tested the lighter 167 g regular size on a Kazakhstan trip, put it firmly in the luxury-item category, questioned the down topper most of all, and called the system bulky and overkill for most hikers. Packed, one early reviewer compared it to a Nalgene bottle in volume. That is small for a pillow with real loft, but it is still more pack space than a basic inflatable that rolls up to the size of a fist.

**The ultralight workaround.** Zenbivy's own answer to the weight complaint is to leave the topper at home. Bladder and pillowcase together weigh 90 g in this size, and the case can be stuffed with a down jacket or fleece instead. That drops 141 g from the carry weight and still delivers a layered feel, though it depends on a puffy that is clean and not already doing duty as insulation.

**Bed integration.** Small clips on the pillowcase attach inside the hood of a Zenbivy bed, so the pillow stays put on a slick pad during a night of rolling over. Reviewers who sleep in Zenbivy systems like this. The Radavist's tester noted that the hood alone probably keeps most pillows in place, so the clips are a nicety rather than a reason to buy. Without a Zenbivy bed, the clips are dead weight, and an ordinary pillowcase would do the same job.

**Care.** The separate case is the underrated part. Hair, sweat and sunscreen collect on a pillow quickly on a week-long trip, and a case that can be thrown in a washing machine at the end of the season is better than the wipe-down most air pillows get. The 2026 version has a contoured case with a wider opening, which makes it easier to stuff the bladder and topper inside, historically a fiddly step.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Layered air-and-down design gives more loft and a softer feel than air-only pillows
- Adjustable firmness through the inflatable bladder
- Modular: the topper can be dropped, or replaced by a jacket, to save 141 g
- Washable pillowcase
- Clips integrate with Zenbivy hoods
- Pillow-sized dimensions suit side sleepers

**Cons**

- 231 g total is heavy by ultralight pillow standards
- The down topper alone weighs 141 g
- Bulkier packed than minimalist inflatable pillows
- Three pieces mean more to assemble, and to lose
- Clips are of no use to anyone not sleeping in a Zenbivy bed
- Topper is sold separately from the bladder and case if bought piecemeal

## Who Should Buy This

Side sleepers who have tried inflatable pillows and found them either too hard or too noisy will get the most from the SoftTop, especially if they already sleep in a Zenbivy bed and can use the clips. It also suits section hikers and bikepackers who accept a pound-ish of comfort items in exchange for better sleep. Gram counters on long thru-hikes should look elsewhere: a basic inflatable pillow weighs a fraction of this, and a jacket stuffed in a sack weighs nothing extra. Back sleepers who are comfortable on a low pillow will not need the loft.

## Verdict

The SoftTop is the most comfortable pillow of its type, and the one most clearly aimed at the hiker who values sleep over base weight. At 231 g it is a luxury, but the modular design lets owners cut it to 90 g when the miles get long. Recommended for side sleepers and Zenbivy users; skip it if your pillow budget is measured in grams.
