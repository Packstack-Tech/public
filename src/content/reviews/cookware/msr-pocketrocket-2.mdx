## Overview

The MSR PocketRocket 2 is a 73 g canister stove with three folding pot supports, an adjustable flame and a WindClip to take some of the edge off a breeze. It has been a benchmark in the category for years, and it suits three-season solo backpackers who want a fast, simple burner that vanishes inside a mug. The trade-off is the usual one for exposed-burner canister stoves: it is excellent in calm air and humbled by gusts and cold.

## Performance

**Boil speed and fuel use.** In still air, the PocketRocket 2 brings a liter of water to a boil in about 3.5 minutes, and testers measured roughly 0.4 oz of fuel spent doing it. MSR's efficiency claim held up in controlled testing, with about 1 oz of fuel needed to boil 2 liters. Lab testing placed it near the middle of the pack for fuel efficiency, burning about 0.25 oz of isobutane to bring 0.5 L to a boil, which is typical for small canister stoves of this type. It is quick and sensible with fuel rather than frugal. Integrated systems with heat-exchanger pots do better on both counts.

**Flame control.** This is where the PocketRocket 2 separates itself from its peers. The valve is easy to grip, and it extends far enough past the pot supports that fingers stay clear of the heat. It is responsive without being twitchy, and it holds a genuine low flame. A regulated system like the Jetboil MiniMo offers finer control, but few stoves at 73 g can simmer rice or reheat a sauce without scorching it. The burner head spreads heat reasonably evenly, which helps when cooking real meals instead of just rehydrating them.

**Wind.** The burner head is split into three sections, so that if a gust knocks out one or two, the rest can relight the flame. In light breezes the design works. In gusty conditions on Mount Rainier's Paradise Glacier, testers watched the wind put the flame out repeatedly. The WindClip improves efficiency in a breeze, but it is not a true windscreen, and the burner is not recessed the way an integrated stove's is. Hikers who cook above treeline or on open ridges generally add a separate windscreen or shelter the stove with a pack and a rock. In strong wind it also boils noticeably slower than rivals.

**Cold and altitude.** Like every unregulated canister stove, it loses output as temperatures fall below freezing or the canister runs low, because the propane and butane in the blend vaporize at different rates. Winter campers and high-altitude users typically move to a liquid-fuel stove such as the MSR DragonFly. Staying with canisters in the shoulder seasons means stepping up to the PocketRocket Deluxe, which adds a pressure regulator along with extra weight and cost.

**Packability.** Folded, the stove fits in the palm of a hand and nests inside most cook pots. It fits in a 550 ml pot alongside an 8 oz canister and a mini Bic. The hard-shell case protects it from dirt and knocks, but it takes up room, and unless the pot is large, case, canister and stove won't all fit together. Many hikers leave the case at home and carry the stove in a small stuff sack.

**Durability.** The design has a long record on the trail. Two issues come up in owner reports. A screw at the base of the stem can work loose and make the stove wobble, which a pocket knife fixes in seconds. After several years of heavy use, the pot support arms can also begin to swing out of position. Neither is a dealbreaker, but both are worth checking before a big trip.

**Noise.** The PocketRocket 2 is loud. It will not make a quiet morning coffee beside a sleeping tent mate.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- At 73 g, it matches almost anything in the canister category
- Lights and runs with no priming, pumping or preheating
- Simmer control is better than most stoves of similar weight and price
- Fits any isobutane-propane canister with a Lindal valve, which makes it easy to refuel around the world
- Handles pots from 450 ml mugs up to 1.5 L
- Years of use across thousands of trips back up its reliability

**Cons**

- No built-in igniter, so a lighter or matches must come along
- Gusts can blow out the flame; a separate windscreen is close to essential on exposed terrain
- No regulator, so output drops in the cold and as the canister empties
- Stem screw and pot supports can loosen with age
- The hard case eats pack space, so most hikers drop it
- Runs loud

## Who Should Buy This

The PocketRocket 2 is the stove for three-season solo hikers and thru-hikers who want a proven, no-fuss canister burner at a weight no one needs to apologize for. CleverHiker named it the best overall backpacking stove out of 40 tested over a decade, after about 500 miles on the Colorado Trail. It also makes a sensible backup for climbers whose main system is liquid fuel. Winter campers, ridge-line cooks who face sustained wind, and groups who would save fuel and time with an integrated system should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The PocketRocket 2 is a durable, compact stove that boils quickly and simmers well, and it has earned its status as a default recommendation. The wind and cold limits come with the canister format rather than this particular design. For solo three-season use, it remains one of the easiest stoves to recommend.
