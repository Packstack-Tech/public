## Overview

The Quechua Comfort 10°C is a rectangular polyester camping bag that Decathlon sells for €24.99 in France. It is built for summer campsites, vans and sleepovers, not for carrying over a mountain pass. The headline trade-off is plain: a roomy, zip-anywhere duvet at close to a throwaway price, in exchange for 1.6 kg and a packed size that eats a pack's lower compartment.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The 10°C comfort rating and 6°C limit put this bag firmly in summer territory. Decathlon says the ratings across its sleeping bag range are tested by an independent lab, AITEX, to the EN 13537 standard. The 300 g/m² polyester fill is a moderate loft, and the rectangular cut leaves air to heat around the legs and shoulders, which a mummy shape would trim away. Reviewers of Quechua's related 10°C bags, including the double version, describe them as adequate for UK summer camping and road-trip nights, and too cold for variable spring conditions in Scotland. The same logic applies here: treat 10°C as a floor, not a target. Cold sleepers should add a liner or a layer, or move up to a 0°C bag.

**Weight and bulk.** Here the bag shows its origins. At 1,600 g it weighs as much as a good three-season down quilt with a pad strapped to it, and it compresses to 10.5 L, about the volume of a large water bladder and a half. Quechua's own 0°C Arpenaz owners complain that its heavier siblings are a chore to carry, and this lighter bag is a smaller version of the same problem. For a short walk-in to a lakeside pitch it is fine. For a multi-day load, every other piece of kit would have to be ultralight to compensate.

**Comfort and versatility.** The soft polyester lining and wide 80 cm width give it the feel of a duvet. The full-length zip opens the bag flat as a blanket, which is the right move on a muggy night when the limit rating is academic. It also zips to a matching bag for a double. The polyester shell and lining are quick-drying and machine-friendly, a real advantage over cotton-lined car-camping bags like Quechua's Ultimcomfort, which weighs 2.3 kg and packs to 28 L.

**Value and durability.** Nearly 2,000 buyers on Decathlon's site give it an average of 4.8 out of 5, and the company backs it with a five-year warranty. Owners of older Quechua bags describe them as well made and durable for the money. At this price, a dedicated bag for the car, the shed or the kids' friends is an easy call.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- €24.99 price, with a 4.8/5 average across roughly 2,000 Decathlon reviews
- Full-length zip opens into a blanket or couples with a matching bag
- Roomy 190 x 80 cm cut that doesn't pin sleepers in
- Polyester shell, lining and fill dry fast and are easy to look after
- Five-year warranty

**Cons**

- 1,600 g and 10.5 L packed are far too heavy and bulky for backpacking
- 10°C comfort rating limits it to summer and mild shoulder-season nights
- No hood or draft collar, so heat escapes at the neck and head
- Rectangular cut is thermally inefficient compared with a mummy
- Two-season rating means no margin for cold snaps

## Who Should Buy This

Car campers, festival-goers, van travelers and families who want a cheap, comfortable summer bag, plus anyone who needs a spare for guests or a twinnable pair for two. It also suits a hut-to-hut hiker who only needs something warm in a heated or sheltered room, with weight a secondary concern.

Thru-hikers, ultralight backpackers and anyone who sleeps cold should look elsewhere. A purpose-built synthetic or down mummy bag or quilt will save around a kilogram and a lot of pack volume. Buyers wanting more warmth at campsites should consider Quechua's 0°C Arpenaz.

## Verdict

The Comfort 10°C does what a €25 campsite bag should: it's warm enough for mild nights, easy to open up, and sturdy enough to survive years of car-boot duty. It isn't designed for the trail, and as a backpacking bag it falls short on weight and bulk. Buy it for the campsite, not the carry.
