## Overview

Most backpackers' toiletries live in a zip-top bag and get dumped on a sink edge, a log or a damp bench. Sea to Summit's Travelling Light Hanging Toiletry Bag replaces that routine with a 79 g Cordura nylon pouch that hangs from its own hook and shows its contents at a glance. The trade-off is price and structure: it costs far more than a freezer bag and has almost none of the rigidity of a conventional dopp kit.

## Performance

**Organization.** The layout is the selling point. Open the bag and hang it, and the toothbrush, floss, razor, sunscreen stick and travel-size bottles sit in view rather than buried at the bottom of a stuff sack. Two zippered mesh pockets handle small loose items, a divided main compartment takes bottles, and an exterior zip pocket holds whatever should stay separate. Matador's writer, who tested both sizes, singled out the hook as the standout: it tucks into its own compartment when the bag is zipped and holds the bag flat on a shower rod, towel rack or door hook without twisting. Reviewers on retail sites consistently praise the pocket count for a bag this size.

**Fabric and weather.** The 30D Cordura-yarn Ultra-Sil nylon is slick, water-resistant and easy to wipe down. Pack Hacker's testers noted that it cleans easily and works as advertised in damp bathrooms. The all-mesh interior drains rather than holds water, which matters after a campground shower, and a bag that is packed slightly damp contains moisture instead of passing it to a sleeping bag. A leaking sunscreen or shampoo bottle stays inside the coated fabric. Cleaning the mesh interior is a different story: one verified buyer called it the bag's only real annoyance.

**Durability.** Owners are mostly positive. Long-term users report the bag lasting for years with little wear, and one traveler who used it for roughly two years had no trouble with the small zippers, which look flimsy but have held up. Not everyone agrees: a critical REI reviewer found it decent but short on longevity. Sea to Summit backs the line with a lifetime warranty, according to Backcountry's listing. Overall ratings are strong, around 4.8 out of 5 at both REI and Backcountry.

**Where it falls short.** Light fabric means no structure. Pack Hacker's reviewers found that the bag tips over when set on a flat surface, so it works as a hanging bag or not at all. They also found the main compartment zipper finicky and the mirror prone to dangling at odd angles. Buyers describe the mirror as small but usable in a pinch. The hook is sized for typical hotel rails and curtain rods, and some owners say it is too small for thicker towel racks. A loop of cord solves that, and on trail the same hook works on a tent loop, a bear-hang cord or a convenient branch.

**Capacity.** One liter suits a weekend or a section-hike kit: toothbrush, small paste, soap, deodorant, razor, sunscreen, lip balm and a travel-size lotion. Hikers who carry a menstrual cup, contact lens kit and multiple first-aid items will crowd it. Thru-hikers on a two-ounce toiletry budget will find the 79 g bag a luxury, though it may replace a few separate bags.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 79 g, light for a bag with a hook, a mirror and multiple pockets
- Hook stows inside the bag and holds it flat when hanging
- Mesh pockets show contents and drain quickly
- Wipe-clean, water-resistant Cordura nylon
- Strong owner ratings and a lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- Costs roughly $47–50, several times the price of a plain stuff sack
- No structure; the bag won't stand up on a counter
- Mirror is small and sits at awkward angles
- Main zipper can be fiddly
- Mesh interior is harder to clean than a smooth liner
- Hook is too small for some thicker rails

## Who Should Buy This

This bag suits weekend backpackers, section hikers and mixed-trip travelers who pass through hostels, huts, campground shower blocks and motel bathrooms, and who value an organized kit without a heavy dopp bag. It is also a good fit for anyone hiking a trail with frequent town stops. Gram-counters who carry five items and brush their teeth beside the trail should save the money and the 79 g and stay with a small zip-top bag. Anyone who needs more room should look at Sea to Summit's larger 6 L version, which weighs 112 g.

## Verdict

The Travelling Light Hanging Toiletry Bag does one job well: it keeps a small kit organized and off dirty surfaces for 79 g. The price, floppy shape and fussy mirror keep it from being a must-buy, but for travelers who shower in shared bathrooms, it is the better way to carry toiletries.
