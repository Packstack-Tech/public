## Overview

The Cocoon MummyLiner is a mummy-shaped sleeping bag liner designed to do two things: add a few degrees of warmth to whatever bag you're already carrying, and keep that bag's interior clean between washes. It comes in several fabric options — CoolMax, silk, cotton, microfiber, and flannel — so you can dial in the trade-off between warmth, weight, and price. This review focuses mainly on the CoolMax version (the 255g variant most commonly stocked for backpacking), with notes on how the other fabrics compare.

## Performance

**Warmth boost.** Cocoon's own numbers put the CoolMax liner at <cite index="16-8">a temperature rating boost of up to +4.7 °C for the Coolmax version, weighing 255 g</cite>, while the retailer copy for the same liner cites it as <cite index="11-3">adding additional warmth (8.4 degrees Fahrenheit) and increasing the longevity of your sleeping bag</cite>. That's roughly in line with what other liner brands claim, and matches what I've seen in practice — it's enough to take the edge off a bag that's a bit under-rated for the night, not enough to turn a 40°F bag into a 20°F bag. REI reviewers echo this: one noted they <cite index="13-17">recently used it with my 20 degree bag when the temperature got down to around 30 degrees at night, and I could feel the difference compared to my sleeping bag alone</cite>.

**Packed size and weight.** At 255g and packing down to roughly 15 x 9.5cm, the CoolMax liner is genuinely stuff-sack-small — smaller than a full water bottle. If you want to go lighter, the silk version drops to 135g according to Cocoon's spec chart, though you give up some of CoolMax's moisture-wicking behavior in humid climates.

**Comfort and feel.** This is where the liner earns its keep for a lot of people — several reviewers mention the tactile experience being a bigger selling point than the raw warmth numbers. One REI reviewer wrote that <cite index="13-8,13-9">it does provide more warmth, but it's the comfort that I didn't expect. Slipping into this is like getting between really soft sheets</cite>, and went on to say they now <cite index="13-10">use this whenever I camp - winter, spring, summer, or fall</cite>. That's a common thread — people buy it for warmth and end up using it every trip because it makes an aging or slightly clammy bag feel fresh again.

**Sizing quirks.** The liner runs long and wide by ultralight standards — full dimensions are 220 x 80cm tapering to 60cm at the feet — and at least one reviewer of the silk version found it oversized for their frame, noting <cite index="12-9,12-10">it is HUGE! It is a one-size fits all, and they mean fits a giant</cite>. The same reviewer flagged the lack of a side zip as an annoyance, since <cite index="12-14,12-15">it also doesn't open on the side at all, so you have to crawl into the top of it. Not very convenient</cite>. If you're used to liners with a side-entry zipper, expect an adjustment period — you're getting in and out through the top opening and drawstring hood.

**Durability.** Reviews are mostly positive on longevity, but not universally so. One REI reviewer flagged an early stitching issue, saying it was <cite index="13-13">a nice weight and comfortable fabric but points off because the stitching on one seam is coming undone in different spots and it's only been two nights</cite>. That seems to be an outlier rather than a pattern — most long-term owners report years of use, with one noting <cite index="14-24,14-25">I've owned 3 or 4 of these through the years. They are very light and very comfortable</cite> — but it's worth knowing quality control isn't flawless.

**Care.** A real practical upside: you can machine wash the liner instead of your expensive down bag. Cocoon's own care instructions note it can be <cite index="17-7,17-8">machine wash (86°F/30°C). Tumble dry low</cite>, and one owner specifically appreciated being able to keep <cite index="14-19,14-20">washed it in the washing machine with no ill effects... one of my most valuable travel accessories</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely small packed size (15 x 9.5cm) and reasonable weight for the warmth-per-gram trade-off
- Multiple fabric options let you match the liner to your trip — CoolMax for humid climates, silk for minimum weight, cotton/microfiber for more warmth
- Keeps your primary bag clean and extends its wash intervals, which matters a lot for down bags
- Comfortable against the skin — several long-term owners specifically cite this as the reason they keep using it
- Machine washable, unlike most sleeping bags

**Cons**

- No side-entry zipper on most versions — you enter through the top drawstring hood, which some find awkward
- Runs large; the tapered mummy shape is roomy enough that some reviewers found it "one-size-fits-giant"
- Warmth boost is modest (roughly 3–5°C depending on fabric) — it's a supplement, not a bag replacement
- At least one report of seam stitching failing after minimal use, though this appears to be the exception rather than the rule

## Who Should Buy This

The MummyLiner makes the most sense for thru-hikers and traveling backpackers who want to stretch one sleeping bag across a wider temperature range without carrying a second bag, or who want to keep a down bag cleaner over a long trip without constant washing. It's also a solid pick for hostel-heavy trips where you want your own clean sleep surface. If you're chasing grams above all else, the 135g silk version is worth a look; if you're hiking somewhere humid, the CoolMax's wicking properties are the better call.

## Verdict

The Cocoon MummyLiner does what it says: a few extra degrees of warmth, a smaller stuff sack than most people expect, and a genuine quality-of-life upgrade for keeping your main bag clean. It's not a dramatic warmth solution and the sizing/entry design won't suit everyone, but for the weight it carries a lot of practical value. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — a smart, low-cost addition to a sleep system rather than a game-changing piece of gear.
