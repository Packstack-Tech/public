## Overview

The Quechua MH500 22L is Decathlon's answer to the question of what a roll-top daypack looks like when someone actually has to find their lunch. It pairs a roll-top main compartment with a side zipper, a pocket count more typical of a commuter bag, and a rain cover stowed in its own pocket at the base. The trade-off is weight: at 780 g, it is organized and durable, but it is a long way from the cuben-and-dyneema end of the daypack market.

## Performance

**Access and organization.** Roll-tops are good at saving weight and bad at retrieving anything. Anyone who has dumped a pack on a trailside rock to reach a rain shell knows the routine. SectionHiker's Philip Werner, who reviewed the pack in late 2025, singles out the side zipper as the fix: it opens into the bottom of the main compartment so a hand can reach what's buried without unrolling the top. The rest of the layout reinforces the point. A hidden zippered front pocket takes maps, snacks or documents. Two shoulder-strap pockets hold a phone or sunglasses in a case. A zippered key pocket sits behind the straps, and a front elastic holds a shell. Pole attachments are included for trekking-pole stowage on steep sections.

**Carry and ventilation.** The back panel is semi-rigid, which Werner describes as a middle path: enough structure to keep the pack from slumping and making packing awkward, without the stiffness and weight penalty of a framed design. The foam-and-mesh back panel keeps the pack from plastering itself to a shirt on hot days. The shoulder straps are padded and adjustable. Werner, at 5'11" with an 18.5-inch torso, found the one-size pack fit well, though comfort on longer days depends on load and torso length. At 22 liters, a day's worth of layers, food and water is the intended load, not a heavy kit.

**Durability.** Testers praise the build. The fabric is a dense ripstop polyester that resists abrasion from rock and bark. Zippers stay aligned when the pack is stuffed, and stress points such as strap joins, the top grab handle and side-pocket edges are bar-tacked. Decathlon backs Quechua packs with a 10-year warranty, an unusual commitment at this price tier.

**Weather and water.** The fabric is not waterproof, and the rain cover is the only real protection in sustained rain. Covers are fiddly in wind and don't protect against a roll-top that's been left open, so anyone expecting waterproof dry-bag performance should look elsewhere. The cover's dedicated pocket at the base means it is always on hand.

**Bottle pockets and small flaws.** The two side pockets are cut for tall, slim bottles and will not accept a 1 L Nalgene. Hydration-bladder users get an external pocket behind the back panel, which makes refilling easier than digging into the main compartment. A Decathlon customer reviewer also reported that the chest strap kept slipping off, a minor but irritating flaw on an otherwise well-regarded pack.

**Weight reality check.** Decathlon lists 780 g. SectionHiker's listing puts the pack at 1 lb 14 oz (roughly 850 g) without the rain cover. Either figure is light for a pack this feature-rich, but it is double or more what a purpose-built ultralight daypack weighs. That weight buys the zippers, pockets, padding and ripstop that make the pack easy to live with.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Side zipper solves the central roll-top problem of reaching gear without unpacking
- Six pockets plus an external bladder compartment keep small items sorted
- Semi-rigid, ventilated back panel carries a day load without sagging or sweat-soaking
- Dense ripstop polyester and reinforced stitching hold up to abrasion
- Rain cover included, in its own pocket
- 10-year warranty

**Cons**

- At 780 g, heavy by ultralight standards for a 22 L pack
- Not waterproof; relies on the rain cover
- Side pockets are too narrow for 1 L wide-mouth bottles
- Chest strap reported to slip off
- $114.99 is not cheap for a daypack

## Who Should Buy This

The MH500 22L suits day hikers, new hikers moving up from a generic school-style bag, and anyone who wants a single pack that also works for travel and the office. It rewards people who like to stash small items where they can reach them mid-stride. Gram counters assembling a sub-10-pound base weight, or hikers who want a pack to double as a fastpacking kit, will find lighter, simpler options. Anyone needing true waterproofing should consider a dry-bag-style roll-top instead.

## Verdict

The MH500 22L is a smart, tough, well-organized daypack that fixes the roll-top's biggest annoyance. It is a better value as a practical everyday hiking pack than as an ultralight one. Buy it for the access and durability, not for the scale weight.
