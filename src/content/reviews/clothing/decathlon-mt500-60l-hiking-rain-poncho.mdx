## Overview

The MT500 60L is a long-sleeved rain cape from Decathlon, cut generously enough to drape over a pack of up to 60 liters. It targets hikers who would rather keep the whole rig dry than fuss with a pack cover and a jacket. The trade-off is that it is a coated polyester poncho, not a membrane shell, so it asks for an unhurried pace and gives back a lot of coverage for very little money.

## Performance

**Rain protection.** The 2,000 mm coating is modest by hardshell standards, but Decathlon says the garment passed a shower test of 60 liters per square meter per hour for two hours, which is a fair simulation of a sustained mountain downpour. The poncho's real advantage is geometry. Water has few places to enter: the seams are taped, the hood cinches down, and the cuffs are elasticated. Because the cape hangs over the pack instead of sitting under the shoulder straps, the usual leak points of a jacket-plus-pack-cover system, namely the shoulders and the back panel, are covered by one piece of fabric.

Walkers on the Camino de Santiago have made this argument most forcefully. One couple who swapped quality rain jackets and pack covers for Decathlon ponchos during a month of near-daily heavy rain on the Vía de la Plata found the ponchos kept them noticeably drier in the upper body. Another Camino walker reported staying dry without overheating and liked the full arms and the pack-sized hump at the back. Decathlon's own page rates the product 4.6 out of 5 across roughly 2,000 reviews.

**Breathability.** Decathlon is upfront that its ponchos have no waterproof/breathable membrane, and it recommends calm, gentle use over fast hiking. The RET 11 hydrophilic coating limits condensation, and the poncho's loose, open-bottomed cut does most of the ventilating, since air circulates freely around the torso. Wearers on moderate walking days report not overheating. Anyone who runs hot on climbs, or hikes at a trail-runner's pace, will still feel the coated fabric working against them.

**Fit and handling.** The L/XL covers hikers from 174 to 190 cm and ends at the knees, so shins and calves are left exposed. Owners say it pulls on and off easily over a loaded pack, and the front zip is short, so it slips over the head rather than opening like a jacket. Reports vary on how much the cut flaps in wind. One owner describes it as closer to a long coat than a cape, and keeps it from flogging in gusts by running a belt across the front through the pocket openings. In a hard crosswind, a poncho of this size will always act partly as a sail.

**Weight and packability.** At 380 g, this is about 4 oz heavier than a 9 oz Frogg Toggs Ultra-Lite2, the standard budget-ultralight poncho, and it is a real garment where the Frogg Toggs is a sheet with a hood. It is also about 150 g lighter than Decathlon's own 530 g Forclaz MT500 hardshell. Because it replaces both a jacket and a pack cover, the effective weight penalty shrinks, and in some kits disappears.

**Durability.** The polyester face has good tear resistance by Decathlon's account, and the Camino reports suggest the design holds up better than a lightweight membrane jacket in repeated wet use. The water-repellent treatment is the maintenance item: Decathlon says it needs renewing about every three washes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Covers hiker and a pack up to 60 L with a single garment
- Fully taped seams, elastic cuffs and an adjustable hood
- Long sleeves and pocket access make it feel like a jacket, not a tarp with a hole
- 380 g (L/XL) is reasonable for what it replaces
- Owners put the price at roughly 30 Canadian dollars, and Decathlon's own page shows consistently high ratings

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than minimalist ultralight ponchos
- No membrane; it suits slow-to-moderate hiking, not hard efforts
- Knee-length cut leaves legs exposed and catches wind
- 2,000 mm coating needs periodic re-treatment of the water-repellent finish
- A pack over 60 L will push the cape past its design limit

## Who Should Buy This

The MT500 suits three-season hikers in wet climates who carry a pack around 60 L or smaller and prefer sleeves to a flapping sheet. That description covers Camino walkers, hut-to-hut trekkers in the Alps, and anyone tired of wet shoulders under a pack cover. Gram-counting thru-hikers who want one rain layer under 10 oz, fast hikers who generate a lot of heat, and anyone planning exposed ridge scrambling in high wind should look at a membrane shell or a lighter poncho-tarp.

## Verdict

The MT500 60L is a practical, cheap and properly finished poncho that handles sustained rain better than many jacket-and-cover combinations. It will not impress the sub-10-oz crowd, but for steady-paced hikers with a mid-size pack, it is one of the better-value ways to stay dry.
