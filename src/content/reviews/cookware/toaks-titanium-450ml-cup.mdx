## Overview

The TOAKS 450ml Titanium Cup is the classic ultralight backpacking mug — a simple titanium vessel with foldable wire handles, graduated markings, and a mesh sack, sized to double as a solo cook pot or a big coffee mug. It's aimed at thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who want one vessel that boils water, holds a freeze-dried meal, and packs down small enough to nest inside a larger pot.

## Performance

**Boiling and heat transfer.** Titanium's calling card is how fast it moves heat, and this cup is no exception. Reviewers of TOAKS titanium cookware consistently note that <cite index="12-1">the thin, quick-to-heat titanium boils water in no time, so you can quickly get dinner or coffee started and move on to other camp chores</cite>. The flip side of that thin-wall efficiency is heat retention — or the lack of it. One Trailspace reviewer summed it up simply: <cite index="13-10">"Cools quickly, I really need to make a cozy."</cite> If you're sipping coffee slowly at camp rather than gulping it down, budget for a foam or reflective cozy, or expect lukewarm coffee by the time you finish your oatmeal.

**Actual capacity vs. stated capacity.** This is the biggest recurring gripe I found. A long-term Trailspace user measured their cup and found <cite index="13-1,13-15">450ml really means around 420ml, but the rolled lip makes it very hard to move it around full so somewhere around 375-400ml is about what it holds</cite>. In practice that's the difference between "just enough" and "not quite enough" for a two-serving freeze-dried meal plus a hot drink. If you're planning meals around the rated 450ml, plan on running it closer to the rim than you'd like, or bump up to the 550ml/650ml if you regularly cook bigger meals.

**Durability.** Titanium's strength-to-weight ratio is excellent, but the material is thin by design, and that has consequences over time. Long-term testing of TOAKS titanium cookware (in this case the 750ml pot, which shares the same construction philosophy) found <cite index="12-12,12-13,12-14">despite titanium's impressive strength-to-weight ratio, the thinness of the material sacrifices some durability — after hundreds of meals cooked and pots boiled, testers noticed some warping on both the bottom of the pot and its overall round shape, with the lid no longer fitting quite as perfectly</cite>. On the 450ml cup specifically, a daily user of several months reported <cite index="13-4,13-18">wishing it had markings to measure water more accurately and that the handles have started to go a little wonky, sometimes not lining up perfectly</cite> after heavy daily use (5–15 cups of coffee, by their account). None of this is catastrophic — titanium doesn't rust or crack easily — but don't expect it to look pristine after a season of hard use.

**Cooking versatility.** Like all thin titanium, this cup rewards boiling and punishes actual cooking. Reviewers of TOAKS cookware broadly agree that <cite index="11-5,11-6">titanium transfers heat more rapidly than other materials, so "real cooking" can lead to scorched food if you don't watch it, and it's best if you boil water most of the time and are prepared to use a lower temperature when you do cook</cite>. Treat it as a boil-and-rehydrate vessel, not a saucepan, and it'll serve you well.

**Sizing and nesting.** At 79mm internal diameter, the 450 is TOAKS' smallest mug-sized cup, and it's specifically built to pair with larger TOAKS pots. It's designed to nest with the larger 750ml pot in TOAKS' lineup, and can carry a small stove or fuel canister when not nested itself. If you're running a solo ultralight setup, that stacking compatibility is one of the more useful features — you get a cup, pot liner, and packing cell all in one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- At 76g, it's genuinely ultralight and one of the lighter true titanium mugs in this size class
- Boils water fast thanks to thin titanium walls
- Graduated oz/ml markings help with measuring water for meals
- Nests neatly with larger TOAKS pots for a compact solo cookset
- Doubles as a cook pot in a pinch, saving you from carrying a separate mug

**Cons**

- Real-world capacity runs closer to 375-400ml usable than the advertised 450ml
- Loses heat quickly — plan on a cozy if you like to linger over coffee
- Thin walls mean it's easy to scorch food if used for actual cooking, not just boiling
- Some owners report handles loosening or misaligning after months of regular use
- No insulation on the handles, so grip carefully when contents are hot

## Who Should Buy This

This cup makes the most sense for solo ultralight backpackers who want a single vessel to double as their cook pot and coffee mug, especially if you're already running other TOAKS gear and want it to nest cleanly. It's less ideal if you regularly cook two-serving meals that need the full 450ml of water, or if you like your coffee to stay hot for more than a few minutes — in either case, size up to the 550ml or 650ml, or add a cozy.

## Verdict

The TOAKS 450ml Titanium Cup delivers on the basics — it's light, it boils fast, and it packs small — but the real-world capacity runs a bit short of the marketing number and the bare titanium walls mean your drink cools quickly. It's a solid 7.5/10: a dependable, no-frills piece of the ultralight puzzle rather than a standout, and worth buying if you know its limits going in.
