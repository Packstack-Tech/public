## Overview

The Sea to Summit Delta Bowl is a rigid, 800ml Nylon 66 camp bowl aimed at backpackers and campers who want a durable, purpose-built eating vessel — not a flimsy silicone pancake or a repurposed cottage-cheese container. <cite index="23-20,23-21">It offers a combination of functionality and convenience for outdoor dining, using lightweight BPA-free material that's genuinely durable in the field.</cite> At 79g (2.8 oz) for the bowl alone, it's not going to win any ultralight awards, but the design details are thoughtful enough to make the weight feel earned — most of the time.

## Performance

### Heat Management

The headline feature is the Protex hex pattern on the base. <cite index="5-5">The patented Protex hex pattern cuts down weight and also helps disperse surface temperature.</cite> In practice, this means you can pour boiling ramen into the bowl and hold it by the base without burning your palm — a genuine quality-of-life improvement over bare metal options. <cite index="9-15">The hex design reduces surface temperature as stated, however when food's fresh off a cast iron griddle, you may still need a cloth between the plate and your lap.</cite> So don't treat it as a magic insulator; it just buys you a few extra seconds before you need to set the bowl down.

The flip side is heat _retention_. Nylon doesn't hold heat well. <cite index="3-37">Food does not stay warm in cold conditions, which is a problem when you're force-feeding yourself at altitude.</cite> If you're camping in cold or shoulder-season temps and your dinner takes more than a few minutes to eat, expect it to cool off faster than you'd like. No insulation here — it's a bowl, not a thermos.

### Ergonomics

The thumb grip is one of those features you don't think you need until you use it. <cite index="5-1">The thumb grip on the side allows you to easily hold the bowl in one hand and eat with the other.</cite> <cite index="25-5">It also doubles as a carabiner hang loop</cite>, so you can clip it outside your pack when it's wet — or just because you feel like it. Multiple users across different forums have called this their favorite detail on the bowl, and I'd agree it's more ergonomically useful than it sounds on paper.

<cite index="6-15">
  The specifically designed steep side walls mean you won't slop your dinner or
  soup over the top
</cite>
— a thoughtful touch if you're eating while standing, perched on a log, or
generally being inelegant about it.
<cite index="28-3">
  Measurement increments on the inside help with cooking and portioning food

, which is especially handy when rehydrating freeze-dried meals that require a

### Cleanability

Generally clean-up is simple. <cite index="3-5">The round contours and rigidity make it easier to clean quickly without wasting much water.</cite> The smooth interior surface doesn't cling to food the way silicone can. That said, a couple of caveats from real-world use: <cite index="9-17">turmeric will stain the plastic, but will not change the durability of the material.</cite> And <cite index="9-16">egg yolks really like to stick once they cool, so cleaning promptly after eating — or soaking briefly — prevents the need to scrub later.</cite>

The hex pattern on the bottom base also has a downside: <cite index="14-10">the honeycomb pattern on the outside of the bottom could possibly trap bits of food or grime if you don't take care to keep it clean.</cite> A quick scrub with your camp sponge usually handles it, but it's worth knowing.

### The Lid Question

The Delta Bowl is sold both with and without an optional lid. If you use it as a standalone bowl, it's fine on its own. But the lid (sold separately or as a set) opens up some genuinely useful functionality. <cite index="3-3,3-4">For stoveless hiking, you can add cold water to food a couple of hours ahead of meal time, pack the covered bowl near the top of your backpack, and keep hiking. The triple o-ring makes a pretty reliable water-tight seal, though there's no way to clamp the lid shut.</cite> That's a decent solution for no-cook rehydrating on the move.

<cite index="11-3">
  The o-rings are a little harder to clean if they get dipped in food, but a wet
  toothbrush handles it without wasting much water.
</cite>
Worth knowing before you use the lidded version as a soup carrier.

### Durability & Stackability

<cite index="6-22">
  Users report it's more durable than silicone bowls and easier to clean, with
  the bottom waffling working well for heat dissipation on multi-day treks.
</cite>
The Nylon 66 construction holds up to being tossed around in a pack without
cracking or deforming.

One genuine weakness: <cite index="15-22">it's a bit expensive for a plastic bowl, and if you're trying to nest more than one of them, they don't fit in each other very well.</cite> Solo hikers won't care, but anyone outfitting a group should know the bowls don't stack neatly — <cite index="9-18">they don't nestle as well as others.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Protex hex base genuinely keeps the exterior cooler to hold with hot food inside
- Thumb grip is more useful than it looks — one-handed eating, pack hang loop
- Steep side walls prevent spills from soups and stews
- Interior measurement marks are practical for cooking
- Rigid construction cleans faster than silicone
- BPA-free, dishwasher-safe — works at home too
- Lid (sold separately) creates a functional seal for stoveless rehydrating

**Cons**

- Poor heat retention in cold conditions — food cools fast
- Doesn't nest with other Delta Bowls — bad for group trips
- Hex base pattern can trap food debris if you're not thorough cleaning it
- Turmeric and sticky foods (egg yolk, ramen residue) can stain or stick if left to sit
- Price feels steep for a plastic bowl relative to budget alternatives
- At 79g, not the lightest option — Snow Peak titanium bowls and silicone collapsibles weigh less

## Who Should Buy This

The Delta Bowl is the right choice for solo backpackers or campers who want a durable, practical eating bowl and don't need to replicate it for a group. It particularly shines if you eat a lot of one-pot or rehydrated meals — the steep walls, thumb grip, and measurement marks make it well-suited to that use case. <cite index="23-1">It's been recognized as one of the best durable backpacking bowls on the market</cite> for a reason. If you're going pure ultralight and your bowl doesn't need to be rigid or come with a lid option, look at the Snow Peak Titanium Bowl or a collapsible silicone option instead.

## Verdict

The Delta Bowl does most things right for solo backpacking and camping use: the Protex base works as advertised, the thumb grip is a genuine ergonomic win, and the rigid Nylon 66 holds up to multi-trip abuse without fuss. The weak spots — poor cold-weather heat retention and unhelpful nesting — are real but predictable for the format. At around $10–13, it sits in a price range where the design polish justifies the cost for a solo traveler, but only if you're buying one. **7.5/10.**
