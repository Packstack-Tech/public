## Overview

The Aether Plus 85 is Osprey's big-volume men's expedition pack. It replaces the original Aether 85, which has been discontinued. It pairs a stronger LightWire peripheral frame with Custom Fit-on-the-Fly hipbelt and shoulder straps, and its top lid detaches into a daypack. The trade-off is plain from the scale: at roughly 6 pounds, this is a pack for people carrying 40 to 70 pounds, not for people trying to avoid carrying that much.

## Performance

**Load carrying.** This is the pack's reason for existing. The LightWire frame feeds weight into a substantial lumbar pad and hipbelt, and the crossbody ErgoPull hipbelt tugs load toward the lower back as it tightens. A reviewer at Australian Geographic found it carried loads up to 30 kg and beyond well, and judged that it rides lighter than its weight suggests. The pack runs about 300 g heavier than some rivals but carries more comfortably under heavy loads. A Trailspace reviewer took it on a week-long, 62-mile trip in Glacier National Park. It stayed comfortable from the first day, when the load was bulkiest and heaviest, to the last, and the reviewer judged it a clear step up from an older Osprey Terraplane in comfort, weight and features.

**Fit.** Osprey's on-the-fly adjustment is the other selling point. The shoulder straps and the extendable hipbelt both adjust for wrap and length, and the torso length is adjustable within each size. The AirScape foam back panel sits close to the body and keeps some airflow, which makes it more breathable than a conventional foam panel, though not as airy as a trampoline-mesh design.

**Capacity and organization.** Bulky winter kit, a bear canister, or a week-plus of food fit without strain. Owners point to the sleeping bag compartment with its removable divider, dual front panel pockets, a front panel zip into the main bag, and hipbelt and side pockets that are large enough to be useful. The hipbelt zippers are a recurring nitpick. Trailspace's reviewer found them hard to open and close fully with one hand.

**The DayLid.** The floating top lid detaches and converts into a small daypack that weighs about a pound. It has no padded shoulder straps, but the Trailspace reviewer found it carries comfortably enough for summit pushes and side trips from a base camp. An integrated FlapJacket covers the main opening if the lid is left behind.

**Durability and weight.** The 210D nylon is tough, and testers describe the build as made for sustained hard use. Durability costs weight. Most trekking packs in this class aim to come in under 2.5 kg. Retail weight also puts the Aether Plus 85 in the upper tier on price.

## Choosing a Version

The Aether Plus 85 comes in two sizes, and the difference is torso length rather than capacity or features.

- **S/M (83 L):** fits torsos of about 17–20.5 in (43–52 cm). It is the lighter of the two at 2,756 g and about 2 inches shorter overall. Hikers with short or average backs should start here.
- **L/XL (85 L):** fits torsos of about 19.5–23 in (49–59 cm), with 2 extra liters and a taller frame. It weighs about 120 g more. Taller hikers need this size, because a pack that is too short loads the shoulders instead of the hips.

The two sizes overlap between 19.5 and 20.5 inches. Hikers in that range can pick whichever is closer to their hipbelt size and preferred carry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Stable, comfortable carry at 40–70 lb
- Adjustable torso, hipbelt and shoulder straps allow a precise fit
- Detachable lid works as a real daypack
- Holds bulky gear such as a bear canister, winter insulation and a week or more of food
- Tough 210D nylon, with a raincover included
- Good organization: front zip access, sleeping bag compartment, hipbelt pockets

**Cons**

- About 6 lb empty, which is heavy for a pack with this much capacity
- Hipbelt pocket zippers are fiddly one-handed
- Expensive
- The DayLid straps are unpadded
- The 83–85 L volume invites overpacking

## Who Should Buy This

This pack suits backpackers who carry a lot: winter and shoulder-season trips, group or family loads, and long unresupplied stretches. It also suits hunters and photographers hauling dense or awkward gear. The lid-turned-daypack is a real extra for basecamp trips.

Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers with base weights in the single digits should look elsewhere. A pack with this much frame and fabric is a mismatch for loads under about 30 lb, and a lighter 50–60 L frame pack will serve them better.

## Verdict

The Aether Plus 85 is a heavy, expensive, well-built hauler that carries big loads more comfortably than its weight suggests. It is a clear recommendation for anyone who routinely hits 40 lb or more and wants one pack to cover winter trips, long carries and basecamp side trips. Anyone aiming to carry less should buy something lighter.
