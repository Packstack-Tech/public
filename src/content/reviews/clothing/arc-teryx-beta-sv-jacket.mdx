## Overview

The Beta SV is Arc'teryx's heavy-duty all-mountain shell: a 3-layer GORE-TEX PRO hardshell with a helmet-compatible StormHood, pit zips and a four-pocket layout, aimed at people who expect to be out in sustained wind, rain and snow. Its current ePE build moves to a 100-denier face fabric and a PFAS-free DWR, which makes it the toughest jacket in the Beta line. The trade-off is the usual one for this class: $800 and 480 g buys durability and storm confidence, not minimalism.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The Beta SV is built for the days when a lighter shell starts to fail. Reviewers at The Trek describe it as most at home when wind picks up, precipitation settles in and temperatures hover just above freezing. What stood out to that reviewer over time was the shell's consistency: it behaves the same in hour eight as in hour one. Owners echo this, with one calling it the burliest jacket they have worn and another reporting a shell that looks as good as new after heavy use.

**Durability.** This is the Beta SV's defining trait. Treeline Review's testers found the fabric extremely tough for how light the jacket is, and Better Trail called the full 100-denier construction about as sturdily built as a hardshell gets. The Great Outdoors tester judged it tough without being stiff, and well suited to high-impact use such as scrambling and long-term backpacking. For a thru-hiker, that translates into a shell that can take brush, pack straps and rock without the abrasion wear that gradually compromises thinner 2.5-layer jackets.

**Breathability and venting.** The 3-layer GORE-TEX PRO fabric breathes well for a shell this heavy-duty, and owners moving up from older Beta jackets report it wears lighter than the weight suggests. The pit zips are large; The Great Outdoors called the two underarm vents very generous, and an owner who runs in it on rainy days says they make the difference. One buyer noted that opening them is essential, and the jacket is happiest in cold conditions. Owners who have used it mostly in fall and winter say it is too much jacket for summer, with one placing its sweet spot below roughly 15°C with sensible layering.

**Fit and mobility.** The cut is regular but roomy, a touch longer and baggier than the Beta AR, with room for a midlayer and a hem that sits just below the hip. Buyers wearing it with ski bibs say it keeps snow out. Better Trail's tester found medium true to size, with long sleeves and room to reach without a boxy feel. Several owners say the SV runs generous: one dropped from a medium in the AR to a small in the SV, and another warned against sizing up. Not everyone loves it: a buyer found the hips snug. The Arc'teryx articulated patterning draws consistent praise for movement.

**Hood and pockets.** The StormHood is helmet-compatible and has laminated brim support and adjustments designed to work with gloves or mittens. One owner found it generous but wished for more articulation. The pocket layout, with higher hand pockets that stay clear of a pack hip belt or harness, is cited by owners as a reason to pick the Beta SV over the Alpha SV.

## Choosing a Version

Two weights are listed: 480 g for the standard version and 499.5 g for the Medium, a difference of 19.5 g, or about 4 percent. That is small enough that weight should not drive the choice. Fit should. Owners consistently describe the Beta SV as roomy and recommend against sizing up, and those who fall between sizes have found the smaller one works better here than in the thinner Beta AR. A hiker who wants to layer a puffy underneath on cold mornings may prefer the roomier option. Anyone running lean should start with the version that matches their normal Arc'teryx size.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 100D 3L GORE-TEX PRO ePE is among the toughest shell fabrics in its weight class
- Large pit zips and breathable fabric for a severe-weather jacket
- Helmet-compatible hood with good adjustability
- Roomy, slightly longer cut layers well and covers the hips
- PFAS-free DWR
- Owners report years of heavy use with warranty support when problems arise

**Cons**

- $800 is a lot for a rain jacket
- At 480 g, it is not an ultralight shell
- Too warm and stiff-feeling for summer hiking
- Roomy cut leaves some owners with a snug-at-the-hips fit while others find it baggy
- Reviewers at Treeline Review point out that cheaper, lighter shells handle ordinary rain just as well

## Who Should Buy This

The Beta SV suits hikers who spend time in winter, alpine or shoulder-season conditions, such as mountaineers, ski tourers, winter hikers and backpackers in places where bad weather lasts for days. It also suits people who want one shell that lasts a decade of abrasive use. Warm-weather thru-hikers, gram-counters and anyone whose rain jacket spends most of its life at the bottom of a pack should look elsewhere. Treeline Review points to the lighter and cheaper Beta SL as a better fit for those users, and The Trek concludes that most people need less jacket than this.

## Verdict

The Beta SV is one of the most trustworthy hardshells available for sustained severe weather, and its fabric and construction are hard to fault. It is the wrong purchase for fair-weather or weight-focused backpackers, who will pay for protection they rarely use. For cold, wet, rough-use mountain travel, it earns its price.
