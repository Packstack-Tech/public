## Overview

Pi Active's pump spray is a Canadian-made repellent built around 20% icaridin, the same active ingredient sold in the US as picaridin. It's aimed at hikers, paddlers and campers who want DEET-level protection without DEET's greasy feel or its habit of attacking synthetic fabrics. The trade-off for ultralight use is bulk: the bottle is a full-size pump with a novelty cap, and it weighs more than a decanted dropper of the same chemistry.

## Performance

**Protection.** The formulation is the strongest argument for the product. Consumer Reports' repellent testing found that its recommended picaridin products were all pump or aerosol sprays, and its lead tester advised sticking with sprays at 20% concentration. Lotions and wipes didn't match them. A 2018 systematic review concluded that picaridin gives roughly the same mosquito protection as DEET, and the Wilderness Medical Society rates it comparably against ticks. Pi Active sits squarely in that sweet spot.

**The 12-hour claim.** Field reports are more guarded than the label. One Canadian paddler who carries the bottle in a PFD pocket says it works but wouldn't vouch for a full 12 hours, particularly in June, when black flies and mosquitoes are at their worst. Hikers in heavy bug country should treat the number as a best case and plan to reapply after a long, sweaty day.

**Feel and fabric safety.** The spray is unscented and non-greasy, which is the main reason families and long-haul hikers move away from DEET. Pi Active says it won't harm polyester, nylon, spandex, rayon, cotton, fishing line or plastics. Picaridin generally doesn't dissolve synthetics the way DEET does. Still, Consumer Reports' lab tests of other 20% picaridin products have turned up staining on vinyl, leather and some spandex blends. Keep it off the pack's coated fabrics and watch, and spray clothing from a short distance.

**Ticks.** Owners who work in tick-heavy country, including spring trips around Thunder Bay, report good results with the spray, which makes it a reasonable pick for shoulder-season hiking in the Northeast and Canada.

**The cap.** The lid carries a small compass and an SOS whistle, and the bottle has a carabiner hole for hanging it off a pack strap. Reviewers treat it as a fun extra. It's a convenience, not a replacement for the whistle on a sternum strap or a real compass, and the carabiner hole adds nothing for hikers who keep repellent in a hip-belt pocket.

**Weight.** At 100 g for the Standard bottle and 175 g for the Regular, this is a car-camping and base-camp weight, not a thru-hiker's one. Most ultralighters carry a 30 mL decant. The 40 mL size is the better fit for weekend trips, and anyone on a long trail can refill a small bottle from a larger one.

## Choosing a Version

The formula is identical across sizes, so the choice comes down to weight and how long the trip runs.

- **40 mL:** The right size for overnighters and short trips where bugs are a nuisance rather than the main event.
- **Standard (100 g):** Covers a week or two of regular use for one person. It's a sensible compromise for section hikers who don't want to decant.
- **Regular / 175 mL (175 g):** Best for families, base camps, canoe trips and peak black fly season, when the bottle comes out several times a day. Ultralight hikers should leave it home and decant a portion.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 20% icaridin, the concentration Consumer Reports' testers favour for picaridin
- DEET-free, unscented and non-greasy
- Targets mosquitoes, ticks and black flies
- Whistle and compass cap, carabiner hole
- Three sizes, including a 40 mL option

**Cons**

- Heavy for ultralight kits at 100–175 g
- The 12-hour claim is optimistic in peak bug season, according to field reports
- The cap's compass and whistle are gimmicks that add bulk
- A full pump bottle is awkward to pack and can leak if the pump isn't locked
- Can mark some coated or blended materials, as other 20% picaridin sprays have

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers, paddlers and campers in Canada and the northern US who want a DEET-free spray that works against ticks and black flies. It also suits families, since the manufacturer rates it for ages 6 months and up. The Regular size suits groups and base camps. Thru-hikers counting grams should decant a small amount into a lighter bottle or buy the 40 mL.

## Verdict

Pi Active delivers what matters most: a 20% icaridin spray that works on mosquitoes, ticks and black flies without the stink or fabric damage of DEET. The whistle cap is a pleasant extra, but the weight and the optimistic 12-hour claim keep it from the top tier for ultralight use. Buy it for short trips and shared camps, and decant it for long ones.
