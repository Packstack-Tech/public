## Overview

The A90 Half-Zip Hoodie is Senchi Designs' bestseller — a minimalist midlayer built from 85 gsm Polartec Alpha Direct that weighs about as much as a candy bar. It's aimed at backpackers, thru-hikers, and anyone doing high-output movement in variable temps who wants warmth without the sweat-soaked stop-and-shed cycle that heavier fleece causes. If you've spent any time in r/Ultralight, you've probably already seen this hoodie (or one of its many imitators) show up in half the base weight spreadsheets posted there.

## Performance

Alpha Direct is a weird fabric to hold for the first time — it looks and feels like a loosely-knit fuzzy net, almost like you could poke a finger through it. That open structure is the whole point. <cite index="17-1,17-2">The fabric is designed for active thermal management, providing warmth at rest while allowing excess heat and moisture vapor to escape during high-exertion activities, and it's characterized by a high warmth-to-weight ratio, extremely fast drying times, and high breathability.</cite> On climbs or fast hiking, that translates to noticeably less clamminess under a wind shirt than I get from a traditional 100-weight fleece — you stay warm without cooking.

The tradeoff is exactly what you'd expect from something this airy: <cite index="17-3">a notable trade off for its breathability is that it provides minimal inherent wind resistance and is typically paired with a windproof or waterproof shell in windy conditions.</cite> Worn alone in any kind of breeze, the A90 loses a lot of its warmth fast — this is a layering piece, not a standalone outer layer for exposed ridgelines. Pair it with a wind shirt and it comes alive; pair it with nothing on an alpine pass and you'll be reaching for a shell within minutes.

Wet performance is the other asterisk worth knowing about before you buy. One reviewer who put serious time in the Alpha 90 noted that <cite index="11-14,11-15,11-16">they hadn't found Polartec Alpha to be that warm when it gets soaking wet — it dries fast, but that doesn't help much when the air gaps in the fabric are saturated, and a tighter weave like Kuiu's Peloton resists becoming overwhelmed by moisture.</cite> In practice, this means the A90 is at its best as a dry-air midlayer — under a rain shell in a downpour, don't expect it to keep pulling its weight the way a denser fleece would.

On sizing: multiple testers flag that Senchi runs small. Better Trail's reviewer, a 6'2", 175 lb tester who normally wears a Large, ended up sizing up to XL and <cite index="1-12,1-13,1-14">found that in general, Senchi runs a little small, and going up a size gave a regular fit that felt just right, with sleeve and torso lengths spot-on and no restriction across the shoulders.</cite> If you're between sizes, size up — especially if you plan to layer the A90 over a baselayer rather than wear it next-to-skin.

As a midlayer specifically, it does its job well. That same reviewer found <cite index="1-15,1-16">that as a midlayer, the A90 functions exactly as intended — the trim-but-not-tight fit slides easily under a shell without bunching or adding bulk, making it an ideal piece for layering systems.</cite> Durability has also held up better than the fabric's flimsy first impression suggests: Better Trail called out <cite index="10-11">its minimalist build — no pockets, just a hood and quarter zip — as keeping weight down while still offering surprising durability after years of use.</cite> That said, Alpha Direct's open knit does snag on velcro, packs with sharp buckles, and rough rock — treat it gently and it'll last; get careless and you'll pick up small pulls.

Against competitors, the A90 punches above its price. Comparing it to the Patagonia R1 Air Hoody, Better Trail found the Patagonia <cite index="1-4,1-5,1-6">offers greater durability and noticeably more warmth at rest, and it's no slouch on breathability, but it can't match the airflow of the Senchi A90, which stays comfortable in high-output scenarios where the R1 Air starts to feel swampy — at 12.9 ounces, the R1 Air is also more than twice the weight.</cite> Against The North Face Futurefleece, the verdict was similar: <cite index="10-2,10-3,10-4">the Futurefleece is the closest match in terms of fabric — highly breathable and built with the same performance-first mindset — but it lacks the fuzzy loft and warmth of Alpha Direct and is about 2.5 ounces heavier, and the Futurefleece is a snug full-zip while the A90 is a looser pullover that feels more casual.</cite> The overall take: <cite index="10-5">the Senchi Designs A90 is the better value, but the Futurefleece is a worthy alternative if you prefer its zippered design and sleeker style.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional warmth-to-weight ratio at 145 g / 5.1 oz
- Breathes far better than traditional fleece during high-output efforts
- Dries quickly and compresses down to almost nothing in a pack
- Half-zip adds venting control without much added weight
- Holds up surprisingly well for how delicate the open-knit fabric looks
- Strong value relative to similarly-performing competitors like the R1 Air or Futurefleece

**Cons**

- No wind resistance whatsoever — requires a shell or wind shirt in any breeze
- Loses much of its insulating value when fully saturated
- No pockets, and sizing runs small — plan to size up
- Limited color availability and can be back-ordered given its cult following
- Not a standalone piece for cold, exposed, or wet conditions

## Who Should Buy This

The A90 is built for hikers who run warm on the move and need a midlayer that won't turn into a sauna suit on climbs — thru-hikers, fastpackers, and ski tourers layering under a wind shirt or rain shell in a base-mid-shell system. If you mostly camp in cold, wet, or windy conditions and need a layer that performs well when soaked or gusted on, look at a tighter-knit fleece like the Kuiu Peloton 97 instead.

## Verdict

The A90 delivers genuinely excellent warmth-for-weight and breathability for the price, and it's easy to see why it's become a staple in ultralight layering systems. Just go in knowing its limits — it needs a shell in wind, it's not going to save you when wet, and you should probably size up. With that context, it's a smart, well-made buy: 8.5/10.
