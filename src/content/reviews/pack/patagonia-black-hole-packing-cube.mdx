## Overview

The Black Hole Cube 14L is a clamshell packing cube cut from the same coated, weather-resistant fabric as Patagonia's Black Hole duffels. It's meant to turn a large bag into a tidy closet. The trade-off is plain: it's among the sturdiest organizers around and one of the heaviest, so it suits travel and basecamp-style trips better than gram-counting thru-hikes.

## Performance

The fabric is the main selling point. The 300D recycled ripstop carries a matte TPU laminate, and Pack Hacker's reviewers describe it as sturdy enough to be treated like a tech pouch or toiletry bag. They say it could be tossed in the mud at camp without the contents suffering. The laminate sheds rain and splashes. Like any zippered cube, though, it isn't a dry bag, and the zipper line is the weak point in a downpour.

The clamshell layout is the other draw. Opening the cube exposes two equal halves. One is mesh, so a glance shows what's inside. The other is solid taffeta, which keeps dirty socks, laundry or wet kit away from clean gear. Owners on REI's product page use it both ways: as a hygiene kit that stays spill-contained inside a 70L duffel, and as a clothing cube sized for a motorcycle tour in Panama. The 14L volume swallows bulky items such as a pair of shoes or a week of laundry.

This isn't a compression cube. Pack Hacker's testers note that the fabric is stiff and strong enough to take a lot of stuffing without any worry about the zip or seams giving out. The cube keeps its shape and stacks neatly, but it doesn't squeeze a puffy jacket down to nothing. On a trail, that stiffness has a cost. A firm, boxy 14L cube doesn't conform to the curve of a frameless pack the way a floppy nylon stuff sack does, and it eats volume in a pack that's already tight.

The weight is the central issue for this audience. At 291 g, one cube outweighs many complete ultralight shelter stuff-sack-and-pad combinations carried as a unit, and several times what a ripstop nylon or Dyneema organizer of similar volume weighs. The extra grams are the price of the laminate, the dividers and the handle. The daisy chain and grab handle make more sense lashed to a rack or clipped inside a duffel than hanging in a 40L pack.

Construction details get consistent praise. Plastic sliders resist corrosion in salt air, and the webbing and lining are recycled along with the body fabric. The cube also fits inside Patagonia's Black Hole duffels, which is how many owners use it, and several report buying the 3L and 6L siblings after trying the 14L. REI shoppers rate the large size 4.6 out of 5 across 10 reviews.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Burly 300D ripstop with a weather-resistant TPU laminate that handles abuse
- Mesh/solid split makes clean-versus-dirty separation easy
- Holds a pair of shoes or a week of laundry
- Daisy chain and grab handle add carry and lashing options
- Fair Trade Certified sewing and bluesign-approved fabric, with recycled body, lining and webbing

**Cons**

- 291 g is heavy for backpacking
- Stiff, non-compressing design; it organizes but doesn't shrink loads
- Boxy shape wastes space in small frameless packs
- Zippered closure isn't waterproof
- Premium price, around $65

## Who Should Buy This

Buy it if you pack a duffel, a large travel pack or a van, and you want an organizer that survives years of airport floors, wet tailgates and saltwater. It also suits car campers, boaters, and anyone who wants a dedicated dirty-laundry or hygiene compartment inside a big bag.

Look elsewhere if you carry a sub-10-pound base weight on a long trail. A thru-hiker is better served by a lighter nylon or Dyneema sack, or by skipping cubes entirely and using the pack's own pockets.

## Verdict

The Black Hole Cube 14L is a well-made, long-lived organizer that does its job in duffels and travel bags. For ultralight backpacking, its weight and rigid shape count against it. Recommended for travel and basecamp use, but not for the trail.
