## Overview

The INIU P512 is a 20,000mAh power bank that trades the featherweight appeal of a 10,000mAh stick for enough energy to run a phone, headlamp and watch through a week or more between resupplies. It carries two USB-C ports, one USB-A port, a digital battery readout and a built-in flashlight, and it sells for roughly $37 at list price. The headline trade-off is plain: at 313.5 g it is far from the lightest way to carry a charge, but it is among the most efficient and best-priced large-capacity packs that backpackers can buy.

## Performance

**Efficiency.** This is where the P512 earns its reputation. CleverHiker's testers drained the pack through a multimeter and dummy resistor load to measure real output, and it finished third among the power banks in their lineup, behind only the Nitecore NB10000 Gen4 and the FLEXTAIL Zero Power. It delivered about 71 Wh of usable energy against a 74 Wh rating. Plenty of budget packs lose a far larger share of their label capacity to heat and voltage conversion, which makes the P512's number the more meaningful one on a long section.

**Weight and size.** At 313.5 g, the P512 is heavier than every 10,000mAh pack CleverHiker tested, and the site noted it felt bulky next to the smaller banks. The comparison that matters is against other 20,000mAh packs, where it is one of the more portable options. The 2.9 cm thickness is the real consideration: it is a brick, not a slab, and it fills a hip-belt pocket on its own. Buyers on deal forums have also warned that INIU's product photos can make its packs look slimmer than they are.

**Charging speed.** The 22.5W ceiling is enough for modern phones, earbuds, watches and headlamps, and CleverHiker ranked the P512 among the top large-capacity packs for charging speed. INIU quotes a 61 percent phone charge in 30 minutes at full output. It will not run a laptop, and anyone who wants to feed a tablet-and-camera setup at higher wattage should look at INIU's 65W models instead. Refilling the bank itself is slow, as it is with any 20,000mAh unit; owners of INIU's 20K packs note that charging the bank takes longer than the bank takes to charge a phone.

**Features on trail.** The digital readout shows remaining charge as a number rather than four vague LEDs, which helps with rationing on day five. The flashlight works as a camp backup, and the automatic trickle-charge mode keeps low-draw devices such as earbuds and smartwatches from being ignored by the pack's auto-shutoff. CleverHiker's testers singled out the feature set as one of the best they compared, alongside INIU's 10,000mAh P41.

**Durability.** The matte outer layer scratches easily, a trait CleverHiker also noted on INIU's Megapower 10000. The testers initially judged the pack fragile-looking, but concluded it handles the abuse of backcountry use. Expect cosmetic wear from a life inside a pack lid with keys and a spork.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- One of the most efficient packs in CleverHiker's testing, with about 71 Wh usable
- 20,000mAh covers multiple phone charges for a couple or small group
- Digital readout, flashlight and trickle-charge mode
- Two USB-C ports plus USB-A allow charging two or three devices at once
- Low price for the capacity, around $37 list

**Cons**

- 313.5 g is dead weight for solo hikers who need only a few charges
- 2.9 cm thick; bulkier than slim 10,000mAh packs
- 22.5W output rules out laptops and fast tablet charging
- Outer coating scratches easily
- Slow to recharge itself

## Who Should Buy This

The P512 suits couples or groups sharing one battery, hikers on 7-to-10-day stretches without a resupply, and anyone running a phone for navigation, a camera and a headlamp together. It is also a sound pick for travelers who want one pack for a long week of airports and trailheads. Solo weekenders and gram-counters who charge a phone once or twice a trip should skip it: CleverHiker's own verdict is that the average user needing a few charges may not find the extra weight worthwhile, and a 10,000mAh pack will save about half the mass.

## Verdict

The INIU P512 does the unglamorous job of a big power bank well: it wastes little of its capacity, charges at a respectable clip and costs about as much as a trail-town dinner. It is a strong value for long trips and shared loads, and a poor fit for anyone trimming a base weight under the 10-pound mark.
