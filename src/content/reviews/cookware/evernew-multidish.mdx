## Overview

The Evernew mulTiDish (model EBY280) is a shallow titanium disc, made in Japan, that weighs 13 g. It's built to snap onto Evernew's Ti cups as a lid, and it also serves as a small plate, a base for solid fuel tablets, or a preheating dish for an alcohol stove. The trade-off is simple: it adds almost nothing to a pack, but it only fits a narrow range of cups and does a handful of small jobs rather than any one big one.

## Performance

**As a lid.** This is the job the dish was designed for, and the one most reviewers judge it on. UK retailer Ultralight Outdoor Gear reports that it fits the Evernew Ti Cup 400FD and 760FD snugly, which keeps heat in during a morning coffee and keeps pine needles and ash out. The same review flags the catch: the tight fit can make the lid fiddly to remove when the cup is full of hot liquid. Titanium conducts heat poorly, but a 13 g disc that has just been sitting on boiling water is still not something to grab with a bare thumbnail. Hikers who use it every day tend to learn to nudge it off with a spoon handle or a gloved finger.

**As a plate.** At 104 mm across and 5.5 mm deep, the dish is a coaster-sized tray rather than a dinner plate. It suits a handful of nuts, a few squares of cheese, or a tea bag after it has been used. Nobody is eating a freeze-dried meal off it. The wide, shallow shape also makes it easy to wipe clean, and titanium doesn't hold on to flavors.

**As a fuel base.** The dish gives a solid fuel tablet a flat, contained platform, which is useful on bare rock or dry duff where a burning tablet is a fire risk. It also works as a small preheating dish for an alcohol stove, a trick that helps the burner bloom faster in cold weather. That's the use case where the dish stops being a gimmick for the people who run alcohol or Esbit systems. For canister-stove users, it's mostly a lid and a snack tray.

**Build.** Evernew has a long reputation for tidy, durable titanium work, and owners of its cookware routinely describe the quality as a notch above competing kits. A flat disc has no hinges, seams or coatings to fail. The realistic risk is a bent rim from getting crushed in a pack, since the dish is thin by design.

**Fit with other gear.** Because it's sized for Evernew's own cups, it's a poor choice for hikers who carry another brand's mug. A lid cut from a cheap sheet of foil or a flat titanium disc from another brand will do the same job on a different cup, though without the snap fit or the plate and fuel-base versatility.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 13 g, so the weight penalty is effectively zero
- Fits Evernew's 400FD and 760FD Ti cups snugly
- Does four small jobs: lid, plate, solid fuel base, alcohol stove preheater
- Titanium doesn't corrode or hold flavors, and cleans in seconds
- Japanese manufacture from a maker known for consistent quality

**Cons**

- Can be fiddly to remove from a cup full of hot liquid
- Too small to serve as a real plate
- Designed around Evernew cups, which limits its usefulness with other cookware
- A lid is a lid: hikers who drink from a cup without covering it gain little

## Who Should Buy This

The ideal buyer already owns, or is about to buy, an Evernew Ti 400FD or 760FD cup and wants a lid that doubles as a fuel-tablet base or stove preheater. Alcohol and Esbit users get the most from it. Gram counters who like a cup lid for faster boil times and cleaner water will appreciate how little it asks of the pack. Hikers using a different brand's mug, canister-stove cooks who never cover their cup, and anyone looking for a real plate or bowl should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The mulTiDish is a small, well-made accessory that does what it says at a weight most hikers will never notice. It's a smart add-on for Evernew cup owners, especially those who cook with solid fuel or alcohol, and an unnecessary one for everybody else.
