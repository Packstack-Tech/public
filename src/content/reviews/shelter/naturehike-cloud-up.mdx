## Overview

The Naturehike Cloud UP is a double-wall, three-season backpacking tent built from 20D silicone-coated nylon, with a single front door, a vestibule and a matched footprint in the box. It is aimed at budget-minded hikers who want a weatherproof shelter without paying cottage-industry prices. The headline trade-off is space. The 2-person version is a generous solo tent, not a comfortable pair's tent, and the 3-person version is the one that fits two adults.

## Performance

### Setup

The Cloud UP pitches around a single aluminum pole set that forks into a Y at the rear. The pole ends go into grommets, the inner tent clips to the spine, the fly goes over the top and buckles at the corners. Owners report pitching times under five minutes, and Naturehike rates the 3P's setup at the same figure. The structure stands without stakes, but the foot corners need to be pegged out to open up the full interior volume. Naturehike also packs in the footprint, which many competitors sell separately.

### Interior Space

This is where the 2P falls short of its name. Its 210 × 125 cm floor works out to about 28 square feet, and the sloping walls and modest 105 cm peak make it feel smaller. Reviewers report that tall sleepers, anyone over about six feet, end up with head and toes against the walls. Owners on Reddit describe it as a "1.5-person" tent. It suits one adult with a pack, a dog or a child, but not two adults on full-width pads.

One door makes things worse for two people. Whoever sleeps at the back has to climb over the other person to get out, which gets old fast on a wet night with a nervous bladder.

### Weather Resistance

Weatherproofing is the Cloud UP's strongest point. Owners describe years of use through hard thunderstorms without a leak, and credit the silicone-coated fly, taped seams, steep side walls and extra guy-out points. The 3P's fly is rated to 4,000 mm. The fabric is thin, so campsites should be free of sharp sticks and rocks. A Cloud UP is not a tent to drag over granite.

Wind performance depends on orientation. The tent is a wedge, so pitching the narrow foot into the wind lets it shed gusts. A broadside hit deforms the walls, though the aluminum poles spring back. Users report it holding through sustained 40 mph winds with gusts above 60 mph, with a noisy, drumming fly. The included stakes can bend in hard or sandy ground. Owners suggest swapping in sturdier stakes, such as MSR Groundhogs.

### Ventilation and Condensation

A rear vent and side tie-outs pull the fly off the mesh. Even so, single-door designs move less air than two-door ones, and humid nights bring some moisture to the inside of the fly. Owners get the best results by staking out the clips along the inner tent's bottom edge, which widens the interior and tightens the fly. They also say to adjust the fly forward to give the foot of the tent room to breathe. Sleeping bags should stay clear of the walls.

### Durability and Quality Control

Naturehike's published weights are reliable. Owners report that actual weights land within an ounce or two of the spec, which is unusual at this price. Longevity is the weak point. Long-term users say the walls begin to sag noticeably after roughly 100 nights, even with a careful pitch. The 20D nylon is a proven tent fabric, but the tent rewards gentle handling at setup and when packing.

## Choosing a Version

**2-Person (1,380 g).** The lighter, smaller tent. It packs to Φ12 × 45 cm and suits one hiker who wants room for gear inside, or a parent and small child. It is also the better choice for a cyclist or a hiker who values compactness. Two adults will find it tight.

**3-Person (1,850 g).** The 3P adds about 470 g (roughly a pound) and 55 cm of floor width, going from 125 to 180 cm. It also gains 5 cm of length and 5 cm of peak height, reaching 110 cm. It is the version for two adults. One owner review describes it as comfortable for two and cramped for three. Packed, it grows to Φ18 × 51 cm, and the manufacturer lists a total weight of 2.4 kg. It still has a single door and a single vestibule, so the entry problem remains.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low weight for the price, with the 2P starting at 1,380 g
- Pitches in about five minutes
- Footprint included
- Reports of leak-free performance through heavy storms
- The 2P packs small enough to let a hiker choose a smaller pack
- Published weights are reliable

**Cons**

- The 2P is cramped for two adults and short for tall hikers
- One door means climbing over a tentmate to get out
- Broadside winds deform the walls
- Walls sag with use, and wet fabric calls for guy lines
- Thin 20D fabric needs careful site selection
- Condensation takes active management
- The stock stakes bend

## Who Should Buy This

The 2P suits solo backpackers who want a dry, inexpensive shelter with space for gear. It is also a good first ultralight tent, and a sound backup for experienced hikers. The 3P is for couples who want a double-wall shelter with room to spread out, and who will accept a pound of extra weight. Hikers over six feet tall should skip the 2P. Two adults who both need quick night exits, or who hike with a pair of full-width pads, should look at a two-door design like the Naturehike Mongar 2.

## Verdict

The Cloud UP is an honest, weatherproof tent at a modest price, and it delivers on its weight claims. Buy the 2P as a roomy one-person shelter and the 3P for two people. Expect to replace the stakes and to guy out the walls as the tent ages.
