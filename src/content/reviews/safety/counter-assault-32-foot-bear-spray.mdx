## Overview

Counter Assault's 32-Foot Bear Spray is the smaller of the company's two canisters. It's an 8.1 oz can of EPA-registered deterrent that ships with a nylon belt holster. It's built for backpackers who want a proven Montana-made formula without the bulk of the 10.2 oz can. The trade-off is plain: about 60 g less to carry, and roughly 8 feet less reach.

## Performance

**Range and duration.** The headline numbers are 32 feet and 7 seconds. That clears the 25-foot, 7-second benchmark that Counter Assault itself cites as the commonly accepted minimum. It does so with less margin than the company's 10.2 oz can, which is rated to about 40 feet or more. Yellowstone's guidance is to start spraying when a charging bear is 30 to 40 feet out, a window the 32-foot can covers only partly. Some reviewers who favor the larger can make exactly that argument. In practice, 7 seconds is enough for two or three short bursts, which is how bear spray is meant to be used.

**Formula.** The can carries 2% capsaicin and related capsaicinoids, the maximum allowed by law. Counter Assault says it was the first EPA-registered bear spray, and that the formula was developed with the University of Montana by a grizzly-encounter survivor, company president and chemist Pride Johnson. Every can is leak-tested at the factory. Field-test roundups from Field & Stream and others have tended to rank Counter Assault at or near the top for reach and spray time. The wider evidence base for bear spray is also strong: research in the Journal of Wildlife Management found it stopped undesirable bear behavior in 92% of the Alaskan encounters studied.

**Carry.** The can is 8.5 inches tall and 2 inches across. The weight listed above is the can alone. Retailers list the can with holster and packaging at about 11 oz (roughly 312 g). That is still not an ultralight number. Field & Stream's tester flagged the size of Counter Assault's canisters as the line's most common complaint. The 8.1 oz can is the more forgiving of the two on a hip belt or shoulder-strap pouch, and one roundup calls it the best lightweight option in the lineup. The holster attaches to belts and packs with a touch closure, and the same holster fits both can sizes. One reviewer described the holster as tight, which slows the draw until it breaks in. That is a reason to practice the draw before the trip.

**Deployment.** The glow-in-the-dark safety wedge is tied to the can with a string, so it can't be lost on the trail. It can also be found by feel and by glow when someone is fumbling inside a tent at 2 a.m. Counter Assault sells a separate inert training canister that mimics the real can's range and duration. Owners who want to practice the draw and the aim can use it without wasting a live can.

**Shelf life and logistics.** Each can lasts four years and carries an expiration date. After that it needs replacing, and the old can becomes hazardous waste. It also can't fly on commercial airplanes, which matters for anyone starting a hike far from home. Trips to Alaska, Canada, or the Northern Rockies usually mean buying a can on arrival or shipping one ahead.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Maximum-strength 2% capsaicin formula with EPA registration
- Rated effective on all bear species
- 230 g can is about 60 g lighter than Counter Assault's 10.2 oz can
- Holster included, and it fits either can size
- Tethered, glow-in-the-dark safety wedge
- Every can leak-tested; four-year shelf life
- Made in Montana

**Cons**

- 32-foot range is the short end of what's sold for grizzly country
- 7-second spray leaves little room for repeated bursts
- Still bulky and heavy by thru-hiker standards, and roughly 312 g with holster and packaging
- Holster reportedly stiff at first
- Expires after four years and can't be flown
- About $57, a premium over some competing cans

## Who Should Buy This

This can suits backpackers in black bear country, and summer hikers in grizzly territory who want a real deterrent that sits more comfortably on a hip belt or pack strap than the 10.2 oz can does. It also suits couples sharing a trip. The 2-can bundle covers two people at a modest saving over buying twice. Hikers headed deep into grizzly country, particularly in open terrain where a charge can start from a long way off, should spend the extra weight on the 10.2 oz version. Anyone who is sure the grams matter more than the reach should skip it. The ultralight argument for carrying less deterrent than this is thin.

## Verdict

The Counter Assault 32-Foot Bear Spray is a strong, well-documented choice at the compact end of the category, with the legal-maximum formula and a holster in the box. Its weakness is a shorter reach than its sibling, which makes it a good fit for black bear country and a more debatable one for serious grizzly territory. For most backpackers, it's a sound buy.
