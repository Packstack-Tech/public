## Overview

The RainbowRoost is Tarptent's purpose-built gear loft for its popular Rainbow family of shelters — the Rainbow, Rainbow Li, Double Rainbow, Double Rainbow DW, Double Rainbow DW Ultra, Double Rainbow Li, and Triple Rainbow DW. At 20 grams and $17, it's a low-commitment add-on for anyone tired of digging through their pack for a headlamp or phone at 2 a.m. This isn't a universal loft — it's cut and corded specifically to clip into the geometry of the Rainbow line, so it won't fit a random trekking-pole tent from another brand.

## Performance

This is about as simple a piece of gear as you'll find, but "simple" doesn't mean "pointless" — a good gear loft solves a real annoyance in single-wall, trekking-pole-supported tents like the Rainbow series: there's nowhere obvious to stash small items you want within arm's reach overnight.

Tarptent built the RainbowRoost with <cite index="11-1,11-2">a clip-in design for fast installation and removal, with adjustable tension.</cite> In practice, that means you can pitch the tent, clip the loft to the interior attachment points, snug the cording, and be done in under a minute — and just as quickly pull it if you decide you don't need it for a given trip (handy if you're trying to shave every gram for a long resupply stretch).

Storage-wise, you get <cite index="11-5">3 separate pockets with additional platform storage on the upper shelf, and an oversized loop for hanging headlamps or other gear.</cite> That's a sensible layout: the pockets are good for phone, headlamp batteries, earplugs, or a small first-aid kit, while the flat upper shelf can hold something bulkier like a stuff sack of extra layers or your glasses case. The hanging loop is a nice touch for a headlamp you want to grab without fumbling in the dark — small detail, but it's the kind of thing that makes a $17 accessory feel considered rather than an afterthought.

One wrinkle worth flagging before you order: <cite index="11-6,11-7">the Triple Rainbow DW requires one longer piece of cording for a proper fit, and if you're ordering for that tent you need to note it at checkout.</cite> Easy to miss, and if you skip it you'll end up with a loft that doesn't tension properly in the larger tent.

I couldn't find much independent field-testing or forum chatter specifically on the RainbowRoost itself — it's a low-profile accessory that doesn't generate the same review volume as the tents it's designed for. Reviews of the Rainbow and Double Rainbow tents themselves are plentiful and generally positive: CleverHiker's testers noted the Double Rainbow's <cite index="14-2">large side pockets, which can easily hold a headlamp, phone, or even the complete contents of a ditty bag</cite>, and praised the tent's <cite index="14-8,14-9,14-10">overall stitching quality and feel, noting that Tarptent has been making tents for a while, and it shows, giving the Double Rainbow a solidity that inspires confidence</cite> compared to budget competitors. That same build quality reputation extends to Tarptent's accessories generally, though I'd treat the RainbowRoost's real-world durability as an open question rather than a confirmed strength, since concrete long-term reports are thin on the ground.

At 20 grams, the weight penalty is negligible — less than the weight of a couple of AAA batteries — so the calculus here isn't really about pack weight. It's about whether you value the organization enough to spend $17 and give up a small amount of headroom inside the tent. Some ultralight users skip gear lofts entirely in favor of just tossing items in a stuff sack near their head; others find the dedicated pockets genuinely reduce nightly fumbling. It's a preference call, not a performance one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Extremely light at 20g — a rounding error in your base weight
- Clip-in installation with adjustable tension means quick setup and removal
- Three pockets plus a shelf platform gives more organization than a single mesh pouch
- Dedicated headlamp loop is a small but genuinely useful touch
- Affordable at $17, especially relative to the cost of the tents it's paired with
- Sewn in the USA (Seattle), consistent with Tarptent's typical build quality

**Cons:**

- Only fits Tarptent's Rainbow-series tents — not a universal gear loft
- Triple Rainbow DW owners need to remember to request longer cording, an easy detail to overlook
- Adds a bit of clutter/headroom reduction inside an already-compact single-wall tent
- Very little independent long-term testing or user feedback exists specifically on this accessory
- Not sold with the tent by default — it's a $17 add-on you have to know to look for

## Who Should Buy This

This makes sense for existing or prospective owners of any Rainbow-series tent who want a designated, no-fuss spot for a headlamp, phone, and other small items rather than digging through a stuff sack in the dark. It's not going to change your experience dramatically, but for $17 and 20 grams, it's a low-risk way to add a bit of nightly convenience. If you're the type who hangs everything from a single interior loop and calls it good, you can safely skip it.

## Verdict

The RainbowRoost is a small, sensible accessory that does exactly what it says: gives you organized, easy-access interior storage for a negligible weight cost. It won't wow anyone, but it's a reasonable $17 upgrade for Rainbow-series owners who want a bit more order inside the tent — just remember the longer cording note if you're outfitting a Triple Rainbow DW.
