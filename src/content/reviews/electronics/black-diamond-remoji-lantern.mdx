## Overview

The ReMoji is a palm-sized, USB-rechargeable lantern built for hanging from a tent ceiling and lighting a small space for dinner, reading or sorting gear. At 81.2 g it is one of the lighter rechargeable lanterns from a major brand, and it spares backpackers from carrying spare AAAs. The trade-off is weather protection: the IPX4 rating looks fine on paper, but at least one owner has reported water getting in through the charge port.

## Performance

**Light quality and output.** One hundred lumens is not much on a spec sheet. Inside a one- or two-person shelter it is plenty, and the frosted globe is the reason. It spreads light evenly instead of throwing a hot spot, so a tent ceiling glows rather than glares. Owners who have used it for reading and for cooking in the dark describe it as brighter than its size suggests. Reviewers who compare it with older AAA-powered Black Diamond lanterns call it a clear step up in brightness and a step down in bulk.

**Runtime and dimming.** Owners report roughly 10 hours at full brightness, which covers several nights of normal camp use between charges. Black Diamond's claimed runtime on the lowest setting is far longer, around 100 hours. One owner said they hadn't run it long enough on low to confirm that figure. Dimming is the setting most people will actually use. One reviewer who liked the lantern wished the color options could be removed, because stepping the brightness down can land on a color instead of a lower white level.

**Hanging and carrying.** The double-hook hang loop folds away and lets the lantern hang from a tent loop, a tarp ridgeline or a branch. The magnetic top is meant for attaching it to metal surfaces, such as a vehicle or a metal tent pole. Owners consistently say it is easy to attach almost anywhere and that it disappears in a pack.

**Charging.** USB charging is the main reason to choose the ReMoji over Black Diamond's battery-powered Moji. Owners say it charges easily. The cost is that it can't take a handful of AAAs when the battery dies on day five of a long section. Hikers who already carry a battery bank for a phone and headlamp will have no trouble. Hikers who don't will find a dead lantern is simply dead.

**Weather resistance.** This is the weak point. The IPX4 rating covers splashes from any direction, which suits condensation and a light shower. One owner left the lantern out in heavy rain for about 30 minutes. They found the globe full of water and the unit dead. They blamed the seal around the USB port, even though the port cover was closed. A single report isn't a pattern, but it fits the rating. Treat it as a tent light. Don't leave it hanging from a tarp edge in a storm.

**Weight in context.** At 81.2 g the ReMoji is about 24 g lighter than the 105.5 g Moji, which has twice the output but needs replaceable batteries. It is also a clear step above the heavy rechargeable car-camping lanterns. Compared with a headlamp hung from a loop, the ReMoji gives more even light and frees the headlamp for dark-trail duty. It is still an 81 g item that does one job. A strict gram-counter who hangs a headlamp from a trekking pole to light the tent will find it hard to justify.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 81.2 g with a built-in rechargeable battery, so no spare cells to carry
- Frosted globe gives even, tent-friendly light
- Dimming stretches runtime well beyond the roughly 10 hours at full output
- Folding double-hook hang loop and magnetic top give several mounting options
- Compact enough to forget in a pack

**Cons**

- 100 lumens is half the output of the battery-powered Moji
- A sealed lithium-ion cell means no swapping in AAAs when the battery runs out
- One owner reported a flooded globe after 30 minutes of heavy rain, despite the IPX4 rating
- Color modes can get in the way when dimming

## Who Should Buy This

The ReMoji suits backpackers and bikepackers who already charge devices from a power bank. They want a pleasant, diffuse tent light that weighs less than most rechargeable lanterns, mostly for evenings in a shelter. It also works as a spare for car camping, power outages and kids' tents.

Anyone on a multi-week trip without reliable charging should look at the AAA-powered Moji instead. So should anyone who expects a lantern to hang outside in heavy rain. Gram-counters who light the tent with a headlamp should skip lanterns altogether.

## Verdict

The ReMoji is a light, easy-to-use tent lantern with good light quality for a 100-lumen unit, and USB charging fits most weekend and section-hike routines. The reported sealing problem and the lack of swappable batteries keep it from a higher score. It is a good buy for fair-weather and tent-bound use, and a poor one for stormy, remote trips.
