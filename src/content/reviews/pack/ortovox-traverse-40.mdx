## Overview

The Ortovox Traverse 40 is a year-round mountaineering and trekking pack built for multi-day tours where you're carrying a full kit — think hut-to-hut alpine traverses, three-season backpacking, or ski touring approaches. At 1390g it's nowhere near ultralight, but it's aimed at hikers who want a durable, weatherproof workhorse rather than the lightest possible bag. Ortovox pitches it as ideal for exactly the kind of point-to-point alpine trekking the name implies, and reviewers who've guided with it for multiple seasons back that up.

## Performance

**Carry and load distribution.** The Comfort Contact back system uses ribbed, webbed material and perforated foam panels, and Ortovox's stated goal here is straightforward: <cite index="1-13">thanks to the ribbed, webbed material and perforated foam in the back pads, the Comfort Contact back system provides excellent air circulation and improved comfort</cite>. In practice, a guide who used the pack for nearly two seasons found the frame held up well even when overstuffed — he noted he <cite index="9-19">definitely overfilled a few times without any noticeable change in comfort</cite>, which says something about how forgiving the suspension is compared to lighter, less structured packs.

**Storage and access.** This is where the Traverse 40 earns its "trekking pack with all the necessities" reputation. The same guide review calls out <cite index="5-1,5-2">a capable and comfortable 40L trekking pack with all the necessities, side pockets, a kangaroo pocket, a floating lid, load lifters, hip pockets that close with one hand, and a back pad that looks like it's part of a sci-fi outfit</cite>. The full-wraparound front zipper is a genuine convenience feature — instead of digging from the top, <cite index="1-17">the TRAVERSE 40 can be opened up completely using the circumferential front zipper, providing a good overview of the equipment inside</cite>. For hut-to-hut trips where you're re-packing daily, that panel access saves real time and frustration versus a top-loader.

The lid itself is bigger than you'd expect on a 40L pack. One reviewer estimated <cite index="5-19,5-20">this is a rather large brain — there is no official size listed on the brain but I would say it holds at least 3-4 liters</cite>, and noted it includes <cite index="5-21,5-22,5-23">a key holder, and on the underside of the lid a small zippered storage pocket that would hold a map or notebook easily, plus a small clear pocket that holds Ortovox's Emergency card</cite>. Side pockets and a map compartment round things out — <cite index="1-18">your drink bottles will always be close at hand in the elastic side pockets, as will your map in the special map compartment</cite>.

**Versatility across seasons.** Ortovox designed this as a genuine four-season pack, not just a summer hiking bag. It's got <cite index="1-19">an integrated helmet net, hiking pole and ski fastenings, and stowable material loops at the end of the shoulder straps</cite>, which makes it a reasonable choice if you split your year between hiking and ski touring approaches and don't want to buy a separate pack for each. One guide review specifically framed it as suited to multi-day alpine treks like the E5 through the Alps, saying <cite index="9-17">it holds enough gear that you can spend 6 days on the trail hut-to-hut without having to skimp</cite>.

**Durability and materials.** The 210D Robic nylon shell is on the heavier, tougher end of pack fabrics, and Ortovox's sustainability angle — 72% recycled content — doesn't come at the expense of toughness, based on available reports. I didn't find any user complaints about abrasion or fabric failure in the reviews I found, which for a pack that's clearly been dragged over alpine rock is a reasonable vote of confidence, though the sample size of long-term durability reports online is still thin.

**User sentiment.** Customer reviews aggregated on Bergfreunde skew positive, repeatedly flagging the pack as <cite index="2-3">comfortable to carry, well-ventilated, easy to load, with good adjustment options and good storage options</cite>. That said, not every owner is fully sold — one two-year owner on Ortovox's own site said flatly that they weren't sure they'd buy it again, without elaborating on specifics, which is worth flagging even though it's an outlier among otherwise strong reviews.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full circumferential front zip makes packing and finding gear far easier than a top-loading design
- Large lid pocket (roughly 3-4L) with dedicated map/document pocket and emergency card slot
- Ventilated Comfort Contact back panel performs well even loaded heavy
- Genuine four-season versatility — ski and pole attachments, helmet net, hydration compatible
- Durable 210D Robic fabric with 72% recycled content
- Rain cover included, so no add-on purchase needed

**Cons**

- At 1390g, it's a heavy 40L pack compared to ultralight competitors in the same volume class
- Not built for anyone counting grams — this is a durability-first, not weight-first, design
- Independent long-term durability data is limited outside of a couple of dedicated reviews
- At least one long-term owner expressed lukewarm ambivalence without giving specifics, so buyer experiences may vary

## Who Should Buy This

The Traverse 40 makes the most sense for hut-to-hut trekkers, alpine traverse hikers, and anyone who wants one pack that can flex between three-season backpacking and ski touring approaches. If your priority is a bombproof, well-organized pack that can take a beating on rocky alpine trails and you're not chasing base-weight records, this is a strong fit. Gram-counters and thru-hikers optimizing for the lightest possible 40L pack should look elsewhere — there are packs in this volume class that shave off 400-500g by cutting features and using lighter fabrics.

## Verdict

The Ortovox Traverse 40 is a well-built, feature-rich mountaineering pack that prioritizes durability, organization, and all-season versatility over shaving grams. It's earned genuine praise from long-term users for its ventilated back system and clever storage layout, and the front-zip access is a real quality-of-life upgrade for multi-day trips. If you want a rugged four-season 40L that can handle both summer trekking and ski touring, this is worth a look — I'd put it at a **7/10**, docked mainly for its weight relative to more streamlined competitors in the same capacity class.
