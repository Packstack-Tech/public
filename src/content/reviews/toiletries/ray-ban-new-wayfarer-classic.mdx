## Overview

The New Wayfarer Classic (RB2132) is Ray-Ban's slimmed-down, softer-cornered revision of the 1950s original: a nylon frame, mineral glass lenses and full UVA/UVB blocking in a 52 mm eye size. At 33 g it's light enough to forget on your face, and sturdy enough that owners describe sitting on them with no lasting harm. The trade-off for hikers is that it's a lifestyle frame first. Coverage, grip and lens toughness are not what a thru-hiker's sun kit is usually built around.

## Performance

**Fit and comfort.** The New Wayfarer's main selling point over the 2140 original is geometry. Buyers who found the original too large, or who disliked how its lens bottoms tilted inward and pressed into their cheekbones, consistently report that the newer shape sits flatter and feels more natural. The 52 mm size reads as smaller than a classic Wayfarer, and owners with narrower faces say it fits without swallowing them. Fit is not universal: some buyers describe the frame as snug, others as loose, and a few received pairs that arrived slightly bent. Anyone with a wide head should try a pair before committing.

**Weight and carry.** At 33 g, the frame is not an ounce-counter's prize, but it's far from a burden. Nylon is the key to the durability story. Reviewers who treat their sunglasses roughly say the frame flexes rather than snaps, and one long-time owner notes that the pairs in their household have been sat on, stepped on and crushed at the bottom of bags, bending without breaking. That tolerance matters when the case lives in a hipbelt pocket or the lid of a pack.

**Optics and protection.** Every version blocks 100% of UVA and UVB. The glass lenses are precision-cut and scratch-resistant, and buyers praise the clarity and the depth of the tint, with one reviewer noting they stay comfortable even in low winter sun. Glass is the right material for optical quality and scratch resistance. It is also heavier than polycarbonate and, unlike the polycarbonate in dedicated sport frames, it is not designed to absorb impact from flying debris. One reviewer at The Modest Man flags exactly that caveat: the frame suits nearly every outdoor situation except when eyes need protection from dirt or debris.

**Glare.** Polarization is available on the New Wayfarer and is where repeat Ray-Ban buyers say to spend the extra money. Owners report it cuts reflected glare noticeably on water, which matters on lake shores, river crossings and snowfields. Check the lens code when ordering, since standard G-15 lenses are not polarized.

**Trail limitations.** The Wayfarer is a thick, squared-off frame with no wraparound coverage and no side shields. On a bright high-alpine traverse, light leaks in at the temples. The smooth nylon frame also offers no rubberized nose or temple grip, so on a sweaty climb the glasses will migrate downward faster than a sport pair would. They also have no retention strap by default, and a 145 mm temple isn't built to take one gracefully. Stylistically, they pair as well with a town resupply as with a campsite. Few dedicated trail sunglasses can say the same.

**Durability and authenticity.** Reports of long service life are common, with one owner noting these are far more likely to be lost than worn out. Counterfeits, however, are a recurring complaint in third-party marketplaces: buyers have received fakes missing the etched markings that real pairs carry. Buying from Ray-Ban directly or an authorized dealer avoids the problem.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 33 g with a flexible nylon frame that tolerates being sat on and stuffed in pockets
- Optically clear mineral glass lenses with full UVA/UVB protection
- Slimmer, flatter fit than the original Wayfarer, popular with smaller and narrower faces
- Polarized options cut water and snow glare
- Looks fine on the trail, at a diner, or in a hostel

**Cons**

- No side coverage, so light leaks in at high altitude or on snow
- Glass is heavier than polycarbonate and offers weaker impact protection from debris
- No rubberized grip, so the glasses can slide on sweaty climbs
- Fit is inconsistent: reports run from snug to loose
- Pricey for something you may leave behind at a trailhead; counterfeits are common on marketplaces

## Who Should Buy This

The New Wayfarer Classic suits section hikers, weekend backpackers and town-to-trail travelers who want one pair of sunglasses that works at camp, in town and on moderate terrain, and who value durability and style over sport-specific function. It also makes sense for hikers with smaller faces who have never found a Wayfarer that fits.

Thru-hikers logging high-mileage days in desert, alpine or snow terrain, trail runners, and anyone who expects sweat and fast movement should look elsewhere. A wraparound frame with rubberized contact points, or a sport pair with side shields, will cover more ground, grip better and weigh less.

## Verdict

The New Wayfarer Classic is a well-made, durable, optically sound pair of sunglasses with a flattering fit, and it earns its reputation as a pair that lasts years. It isn't a trail-first design, and the lack of side coverage and grip will bother hikers who spend long days in strong sun. Buy the polarized version if town and camp share equal billing with the trail, and skip it if function matters more than looks.
