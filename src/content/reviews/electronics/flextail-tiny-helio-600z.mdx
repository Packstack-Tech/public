## Overview

The Tiny Helio 600Z is a 59.5 g headlamp built around a Luminus SST-20 LED and a replaceable RCR123A cell that charges over USB-C. It targets backpackers who want a headlamp that gets through a long trip on one battery, or who want the option of carrying a spare instead of a charger. The trade-off is the stripped-down bungee headband, which saves grams and costs some comfort and stability.

## Performance

**Brightness and beam.** The 600-lumen figure applies only when the spot and flood LEDs run together in mixed mode. Paddling Light's tester found that mode bright enough to light the trail and much of the surrounding forest, and a little too bright at full power. Spot alone drops to roughly 400 lumens. That tester found it reached about the same distance ahead without washing out the surroundings, which makes it the more useful setting for walking. The flood LED handles camp chores. The light is a cool 6,500K, so it reads blue-white rather than warm.

**Battery life.** Flextail rates the lowest setting, 5 lumens, at 50 hours. Testers describe that setting as plenty for reading in a tent. Owners report getting through full nights without recharging. One European owner covered a 20 km night trek with about 2,100 m of climbing on the 900 mAh cell and still had charge left. Paddling Light's tester also left the lamp in a pocket on sub-0°F (-17.8°C) days and found the battery held up in the cold.

**The replaceable cell.** The headlamp accepts a standard-format cell, so a spare can ride in a pocket, and the lamp can run from a power bank over USB-C. The cell is also interchangeable with Flextail's Zero Pump, so one battery can serve both if a hiker carries the pump. Owners cite this swap-and-go flexibility as the main advantage over sealed-battery lamps like the Nitecore NU25 UL. Adventure Alan's roundup rates the Helio as one of the best value-for-performance blends among ultralight headlamps. It places it behind the Nitecore NU25 UL on weight and behind the Black Diamond Spot on battery.

**Controls.** Two buttons run the lamp, one for each LED. A double-click powers it on, a single click steps through modes, and a long press reaches special modes. Holding both buttons together engages a safety lock against accidental activation in a pack. Two complaints recur. One is that the lamp powers up in its brightest mode rather than the dimmest. The other is that it must be cycled through every mode to turn off. Some owners also find the red mode too bright to protect night vision, and a few say six modes is more than they need and took some learning at first.

**Fit and comfort.** The headband is a pair of reflective elastic cords with a foam pad behind the lamp. The pad makes the lamp comfortable for such a minimal rig, and the cords are lighter and more visible than a conventional band. They also tangle, and one tester found the adjuster stiff. Road Trail Run's tester found the lamp a little top-heavy, with some bounce while running, and attributed this to its depth. At walking pace this matters much less than it does for trail runners.

**Weight and durability.** Road Trail Run measured 63 g on its own scale against the 59.5 g claim, still light for this output. The IP67 rating covers dust and temporary submersion to 1 m, and Flextail also claims a 1 m drop rating. Paddling Light's tester reported no problems during about two months of use that included night skiing and early-morning hikes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 59.5 g claimed (63 g on one tester's scale) with a 600-lumen maximum
- Replaceable RCR123A cell, with a spare easy to carry
- USB-C charging in the lamp or out of it, and power-bank operation
- IP67 sealing, a step above the splash-proof rating common in this class
- 5-lumen low mode with a 50-hour claimed runtime
- Spot and flood LEDs can be used separately or together
- Safety lock for pack transport

**Cons**

- Bungee headband tangles and has a stiff adjuster
- Slightly top-heavy; bounces when running
- Starts in the brightest mode, and turning it off means cycling through modes
- Red mode is brighter than ideal for preserving night vision
- Cool 6,500K light
- 600 lumens is a mixed-mode figure; spot alone is about 400

## Who Should Buy This

This lamp suits backpackers and thru-hikers who want a lightweight headlamp with real output and the ability to swap in a fresh cell on a long stretch between resupplies. It also suits anyone who wants to share a battery with Flextail's Zero Pump. It works for camp use, night hiking and predawn starts. Trail runners who move fast at night should look at a lamp with a wider, more stable headband. Hikers who want the lightest possible light should consider the Nitecore NU25 UL. Anyone sensitive to color temperature or wanting a truly dim red light may be frustrated.

## Verdict

The Tiny Helio 600Z gives ultralight hikers more output, better water protection and more battery flexibility than its weight and price suggest. The headband and the mode logic keep it from a higher score. For three-season backpacking it is an easy recommendation, and the 900 mAh battery option is the better choice for long trips.
