## Overview

The GSI Outdoors Cathole Trowel is a 10.3-inch slab of recycled polycarbonate with a serrated blade, a thick handle and depth markings molded in. It is built for backpackers who want a tool that survives rocky ground and rough handling at a low price. The trade-off is weight: at 91 g it sits well above the aluminum and titanium trowels that thru-hikers usually carry.

## Performance

**Digging.** The serrated edges are the working feature. They bite into compacted soil and fine roots, and Australian Hiker's reviewer noted that the teeth help in tougher ground without being sharp enough to cut a hand. The same reviewer carried it on the Larapinta Trail in Central Australia. Even there, where rocky soil stretched a single cathole to as long as 20 minutes, the trowel never felt close to failing.

**Durability.** This is where the GSI earns its place. In 99Boulders' side-by-side test of six trowels, two other plastic models had durability problems in hard soil, while the GSI felt burly and was the only plastic trowel that held up reasonably in those conditions. One competitor bent back substantially when testers levered soil out of the hole. An Outdoor Life reviewer reports more than ten years of use and hundreds of catholes on the same trowel. Reviewers do note the limits of plastic. A sharp, well-placed rock can chip it, and it can turn brittle in deep-freeze temperatures.

**Handling.** The handle is thick and rounded, and owners with larger hands single it out as more comfortable than the thin grips on ultralight metal trowels. That matters on a long dig in clay, where the narrow handle of a 17 g aluminum trowel can start to bite. It does not fold or collapse. One owner's only real complaint is that it stays a rigid 10 inches, so it rides in a side pocket or strapped outside the pack rather than in a toiletry bag.

**Depth marks and Leave No Trace.** Measurement markings run up to 6 in, so a hiker can check the hole against the Leave No Trace guideline of 6 to 8 in without guessing. Leave No Trace usage guidelines are also molded into the back of the blade. The markings are subtle. One reviewer said they can only just be seen.

**Cleanup.** The serrated edges and molded text collect dirt, and the Outdoor Life reviewer lists hard-to-clean blade edges as the main drawback, along with a tool that looks grubby after years of service. It is still fully functional. A plastic bag or a quick scrub with a stick and a splash of water handles most of it.

**Weight.** At 91 g, the trowel is about 3.2 oz. TheTentLab's Deuce #2, 99Boulders' top pick, weighs 0.6 oz. Owners on the lighter-weight side of the hobby describe the GSI as pretty light but not "ultra hipster lightweight", which sums up the situation. Over a weekend it is irrelevant. For a gram-counting thru-hiker, it is roughly 70 g of base weight spent on convenience.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Holds up in rocky and compacted soil where other plastic trowels flex or bend
- Thick, comfortable handle that suits larger hands
- Serrated edges and a 6 in depth scale make a proper cathole quick
- Made from recycled polycarbonate, with Leave No Trace guidance molded in
- Owner ratings are consistently high, around 4.6 out of 5 across 35 reviews at one retailer
- Lanyard loop for strapping to a pack

**Cons**

- 91 g is heavy next to aluminum and titanium trowels of 17 to 35 g
- 10.3 in rigid length with no folding or collapsing
- Blade edges hold dirt and are tedious to clean
- Can chip on sharp rock and grow brittle in deep cold

## Who Should Buy This

This trowel suits weekend backpackers, scout leaders, first-time buyers, and anyone hiking rocky or clay-heavy terrain who would rather carry a few extra ounces than snap a trowel mid-dig. It also works as a durable camp or car-camping tool and as a cheap backup. Thru-hikers and gram-counters chasing a sub-10-lb base weight should look at an aluminum or titanium trowel instead, as should anyone hiking in hard-freeze conditions where a brittle plastic blade is a concern.

## Verdict

The GSI Cathole Trowel does one job, digging a legal hole in difficult ground, without fuss and at a modest price. It is overbuilt for a weight-focused kit and underpriced for how long it lasts. Buy it for toughness and comfort; skip it if every ounce counts.
