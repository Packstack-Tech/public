## Overview

The BeFree AC is a 1-liter collapsible soft flask with a hollow-fiber filter screwed into its neck and a removable activated-carbon pad behind it. It is built for solo hikers and runners who want to scoop, squeeze and drink without ceremony. The trade-off is that the carbon stage, the feature that earns it the "AC," costs flow speed, and the flask has a history of durability complaints that the latest redesign only partly lays to rest.

## Performance

**Flow.** Katadyn rates the BeFree AC at 2 liters per minute, and Better Trail's testers found that figure holds up in real use. Outdoor Gear Lab places the BeFree family among the fastest soft-bottle filters it has tested. The caveat is the carbon pad: Adventure Alan measured roughly half the flow with both elements new, and a REI reviewer who tried it described the same slowdown, and removed the pod when taste wasn't an issue. Better Trail also found the carbon version noticeably slower than the older non-AC BeFree. A Hiking Daily test emptied the 1.0 L flask of clean water in 12.5 seconds, which is quick enough that the squeeze, not the filter, is the bottleneck on a typical stream stop.

**Flow over time.** Every hollow-fiber filter slows as it loads up with silt and algae. Better Trail reports that the BeFree's decline is gradual rather than sudden, unlike the abrupt drop-offs its testers have seen with the Sawyer Squeeze and LifeStraw Peak Squeeze. Adventure Alan puts the slowdown at about half by mid-life in silty water. The EZ-Clean membrane is cleaned by swishing it in water, with no syringe or tools. The trade-off is that there is no backflush option.

**Taste.** The carbon stage is a real improvement for chlorinated tap water and tannin-heavy sources. Hiking Daily's tester found it improved the taste and odor of Boulder tap water. Most backcountry users run the hollow fiber alone and add the carbon only in town or at questionable sources. At a rated 200 L, the carbon pad wears out five times faster than the membrane, so it works as a consumable.

**Durability.** This is where the BeFree built its reputation. Better Trail notes that earlier versions were notorious for pinhole leaks in the reservoir and for the drinking cap's dust cover coming apart. The AC revision has a thicker, double-layer flask and a redesigned flip cap. Outdoor Gear Lab, which had durability problems in earlier tests, says the updated flask is far tougher. It still files durability under "limited" in its verdict, and the 1.0 L flask feels noticeably sturdier than the thinner 0.5 L. The flask is still TPU, so it is no match for a rigid bottle in a crammed pack.

**Use.** The wide mouth makes scooping from shallow sources easy, and the flask rolls to a thin slab when empty. It runs on both hands, so it doesn't multitask the way a gravity setup does. Better Trail adds that flow feels about like the Platypus QuickDraw and LifeStraw Peak Squeeze in practice, since refilling the 1-liter reservoir between squeezes evens out the rated differences. Drinking directly through the cap works, and the filter also fills a second bottle.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 85 g for a complete filter and 1 L of carrying capacity
- Fast, easy flow, with the carbon pad removed
- Wide mouth fills quickly, and the flask collapses flat
- Cleans in the field by swishing, with no tools
- Removable carbon pad for taste and chlorine reduction
- Sturdier double-layer flask than earlier BeFree versions

**Cons**

- Carbon stage cuts flow substantially when installed
- No backflush option
- Carbon pad is rated for only 200 L
- Flask durability still trails rigid bottles
- Requires two hands while filtering
- Costs more than a bare Sawyer Squeeze, at a $53 list price per Outdoor Gear Lab

## Who Should Buy This

Solo section hikers, ultralight backpackers on water-rich routes, and trail runners who want to filter while they drink. It also suits anyone who sometimes fills from chlorinated taps or tea-colored streams and wants the option of a better-tasting carry. Thru-hikers who put economics ahead of speed may prefer a Sawyer Squeeze with separate flasks, since the Squeeze is cheaper per liter and can be backflushed. Groups, and anyone treating large volumes at camp, are better served by a gravity system.

## Verdict

The BeFree AC is the benchmark for fast, light personal filtering. The sturdier flask fixes the biggest complaint about the original, and the carbon pad is a useful option as long as it can be pulled out. It loses points for the flow penalty with carbon installed and for the lack of backflushing.
