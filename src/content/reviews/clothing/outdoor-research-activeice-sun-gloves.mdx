## Overview

The ActiveIce Sun Gloves are thin, fingerless stretch-knit gloves that cover the back of the hand and a little past the wrist, which is where sunscreen tends to vanish first on anyone who hikes with poles. At 18 g, they cost almost nothing to carry. The trade-off is durability: this is a sun garment, not a work glove, and long-distance users report wear to match.

## Performance

**Sun protection.** This is the reason to buy them. Owners describe them as a replacement for reapplying sunscreen to the backs of their hands, which take the worst of the exposure on exposed ridgelines, snowfields and open water. One reviewer who wore them for a 27-mile Grand Canyon rim-to-rim day, with temperatures near 100°F at the bottom, called the coverage ideal for use with poles. Another noted that the cuff extends past the wrist bone, which closes the gap that fingerless gloves often leave.

**Cooling.** The ActiveIce treatment is hard to measure, and no independent lab figures have been published. Owners are nonetheless consistent that the gloves feel cool rather than stuffy. An REI reviewer who started out skeptical said the gloves keep hands protected without sunscreen and feel cooler on the trail. The more reliable benefit is the thin, breathable knit, which dries in minutes after a dunking, according to a paddler who has worn the gloves for a year.

**Grip and pole use.** The textured palm is built for trekking-pole handles, ice axes and handlebars, and owners say it gives just enough purchase without adding bulk. Reviewers also report that the gloves protect against pole-strap abrasion. They fit like a swimsuit for the hands, snug enough that fabric doesn't bunch under a strap. Leaving the fingertips free keeps dexterity intact for zippers, snacks and map work.

**Durability.** This is the weak point. A hiker who wore an earlier ActiveIce version through roughly two months of the PCT's 700-mile desert section, using trekking poles daily, said the gloves held up but fell apart after that much use. REI and Outdoor Research owners also report that the palm material is the first place to give out. Owners who use them lightly, or only for sunny days, report better longevity. One wildlife field worker described a pair lasting three years with some staining and a single tear. Light colors also get dirty fast. A PCT hiker's Alloy pair turned dark brown within days, so darker colors hide trail grime better.

**Fit.** Sizing is generally true, though stretchy enough that one owner with small hands found a size small fit well. One long-time REI customer complained that a recent update brought a different fabric and a tighter fit than the previous version, so those who want a looser glove should try a pair before a big trip. Ratings remain strong overall: 4.6 out of 5 across 269 reviews at REI, and roughly 4.8 across 117 at Backcountry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 18 g, light enough to leave clipped to a pack strap or tucked in a hip-belt pocket
- UPF 50+ coverage on the back of the hand and past the wrist
- Grippy palm works with trekking poles, ice axes and handlebars
- Dries in minutes and washes easily
- Fingerless design keeps full dexterity
- bluesign® approved fabric

**Cons**

- Thin palm material wears out under daily pole use over weeks
- Not suited to scrambling, rope work or protecting hands from sticks and rock
- Light colors show dirt quickly
- Some owners find the latest version tighter than earlier ones
- Cooling effect is subjective and not independently measured

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who spend hours above treeline, on snow or on the water, and who are tired of sunburned knuckles, will get the most from these. They suit section hikers, day hikers, paddlers and anyone driving long distances in bright sun. Thru-hikers in desert or high-alpine terrain can treat them as a consumable: cheap and light enough that carrying a replacement pair is reasonable. Those who need hand protection from abrasion, brush or cold should look at a more robust glove instead, since these offer sun coverage and little else.

## Verdict

The ActiveIce Sun Gloves do one job well at 18 g: they keep the sun off the backs of the hands while preserving grip and dexterity on poles. Durability falls short of a true work glove, and the palm is the part that gives out. For sun protection per gram, they are an easy add to a summer kit.
