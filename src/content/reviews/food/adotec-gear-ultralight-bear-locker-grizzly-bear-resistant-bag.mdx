## Overview

The Adotec Ultralight Bear Locker is a 14-liter food sack sewn from thick Dyneema Composite Fabric, and it is the lightest soft-sided bag certified as grizzly-resistant by the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee (IGBC). It is aimed at ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers who want legal, bear-country food storage without a plastic canister riding in the pack. The trade-off is money: it costs about $163, and it only works where soft-sided containers are permitted.

## Performance

**Bear resistance.** The IGBC certification is the headline, and it covers the 14-liter bag specifically. Backpacker's testers pointed to the fabric as the reason: a 6 oz/sq yd Dyneema they described as roughly twice as heavy as the cloth in the burliest Dyneema packs. The seams are triple-stitched and sealed, with reinforced panels at the top and bottom. In the grizzly version, the drawstring is pulled tight with two overhand knots, then the cord ends are looped around a tree trunk and tied off. Backpacker rated it as secure as the Ursack, the long-time default in this category.

**Weight and bulk.** On Backpacker's scale the 14-liter bag came in at 6.8 oz, against 9.8 oz for the Ursack AllMitey. The AllMitey is also smaller, so the gap is narrower than it looks. Backpacker measured about 11.5 liters of usable space in the Adotec and about 10.5 in the Ursack. Against a hard-sided canister the difference is much larger, and the bag compresses around whatever else is in the pack instead of claiming a cylinder of dead space.

**Fit in the pack.** The bag is wider and shorter than the Ursack, which Backpacker found fits most packs better. Owners on the manufacturer's site make the same point: it sits beside other gear rather than eating the whole bottom of the pack. The fabric is stiff enough that the bottom corners can be pushed inward, origami-style, to give it a flat base and some structure. That makes packing a resupply easier than wrestling a floppy sack.

**Weather and rodents.** The fabric is waterproof and the seams are sealed, so a wet-weather night under a tarp isn't a problem. Reviewers also report it keeps mice and other rodents out. The roll-down closure gives the bag a dry-bag function, while the cinch closure is what the grizzly certification rests on. Owners also pair it with an odor-proof liner, since the bag itself doesn't block scent.

**Capacity.** Adotec rates the 14-liter bag at 5–6 days of food for one person. Backpacker's testers put it at about five days, and a similar bag volume to a BearVault BV500. Hikers with calorie-dense, bulky diets on longer carries between resupplies should plan on four to five days.

**Limits.** Soft-sided bags are not accepted everywhere. Areas that mandate hard-sided canisters, such as parts of the Sierra Nevada, are off the table, and GearJunkie's review flagged this as the main drawback along with price. Anyone planning a route needs to check each jurisdiction's rules before committing to a bag.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 199 g, the lightest IGBC grizzly-certified soft-sided food bag
- Certified for grizzlies, so it also covers black bear country
- Waterproof, rodent-resistant, and sealed-seam construction
- Wide, short shape packs better than taller bags and canisters
- Push-in corners give a flat base

**Cons**

- About $163, which is steep for a sack
- Not legal where hard-sided canisters are required
- IGBC certification covers only the 14 L size
- Usable volume is closer to 11.5 L than the 14 L rating suggests
- No odor-blocking; a separate liner is a good idea

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and section hikers on routes in grizzly and black bear country that allow soft-sided containers, such as the Colorado Trail and Northern Rockies trips, will get the most from it. It suits anyone who is tired of the weight and awkward shape of a canister. Hikers whose routes require a canister, or who want the cheapest legal option, should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Bear Locker cuts roughly 100 g or more from the Ursack's weight and far more from a canister, and it earns its IGBC certificate with heavy fabric and tidy construction. The price is high and the rules of the route decide whether it's usable at all. For hikers where it is legal, it is the best soft-sided option available.
