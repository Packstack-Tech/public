## Overview

The Summit Evo is Petzl's technical-leaning classic mountaineering axe: a hot-forged steel head, a hydroformed aluminium shaft with a kink near the top, and a rubber-coated lower grip. It is built for people whose snow days run from glacier approaches to steep couloirs and who want one axe to cover all of it. The trade-off is cost, plus a weight that makes sense for the job but won't win any gram-counting contests.

## Performance

**Pick and adze.** The hot-forging process is the Summit Evo's calling card. Because the steel head is forged rather than welded together, the pick can be tapered toward the tip without giving up strength. Testers at Live for the Outdoors found it penetrated firm snow and ice far more readily than wider, welded designs, and reported minimal skating even on polished ice. Outdoor Gear Lab rated it the best-penetrating of the non-modular axes it tested, and called the adze one of its team's favorites. The adze is wide enough for step-cutting and angled more steeply than many rivals, so the lower shaft hangs straight down when the axe is held by the head. That makes plunging the shaft into snow quicker.

**Shaft and handling.** The shaft is hydroformed, meaning it is shaped under high pressure so it can be curved without losing strength. The kink near the top gives clearance for the knuckles on steeper ground while the lower shaft stays straight for plunging. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers singled out the curve as a favorite feature, and a Backcountry reviewer found it comfortable in cane position. One British reviewer at The Great Outdoors called it the most pronounced curve among the axes in her test, which she said made it a pleasure on easy gullies. Balance gets steady praise: enough head weight to make the pick bite, without the top-heavy fatigue of a long swinging day.

**Self-arrest.** Live for the Outdoors rated self-arrest performance as exceptional. REI buyers, who rate the axe 4.9 out of 5 across a small number of reviews, say the same thing in plainer terms: easy to arrest with and pleasant in the hand.

**Range.** The rubberized lower grip makes overhead swinging more secure, which is why owners report using the axe for more than it was strictly designed for. One REI reviewer described climbing some easy ice with it, and Outdoor Gear Lab testers took it up 50-degree neve without complaint. A Mt. Shuksan climber with the 52 cm version recommended it for snow steeper than 45 degrees. The ceiling is real, though. A Backcountry owner wouldn't take it onto serious steep ice, and it is a T-rated mountaineering axe, not a technical ice tool.

**Weak points.** The rubber grip is prone to wear when the axe is used in walking mode, according to David Lintern's test for The Great Outdoors. A buyer with small hands returned an axe because the section where the grip meets the head felt too wide. Some European owners have criticized the leash system.

**Weight.** At 420 g in the 59 cm length, this is light for a steel-headed axe with this much capability. Hikers carrying a glacier axe for a snow-covered pass in an otherwise dry-trail season may find the weight and cost hard to justify. The same weight buys versatility only if the route needs it.

## Choosing a Version

Weight scales with shaft length: 400 g for the 52 cm, 420 g for the 59 cm and 450 g for the 66 cm.

- **52 cm:** The choice for steep snow and routes where the axe is swung or planted more than leaned on. A Mt. Shuksan climber reported it worked well on slopes over 45 degrees. It is also the lightest, and the shortest to stow on a pack.
- **59 cm:** The all-rounder, and the length most testers' impressions are based on. It suits mixed days of low-angle approaches, glacier travel and the occasional steeper pitch.
- **66 cm:** For walking-cane use on low-angle terrain, where a longer shaft supports the hand at a comfortable height. Taller users and those who spend most of their time on moderate snow will get the most from it. It is the heaviest of the three at 450 g.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Hot-forged pick penetrates hard snow and ice well, with a tapered profile
- Large, well-angled adze for step-cutting and digging
- Curved shaft gives clearance on steeper ground while still plunging cleanly
- Rubber lower grip is secure for swinging
- Balanced feel, with a head that bites without tiring the arm
- CE, UIAA and UKCA certified
- Three lengths

**Cons**

- Expensive, and about double the price of several competing axes in Outdoor Gear Lab's lineup
- Grip rubber wears with heavy walking-mode use
- Handle-to-head junction feels broad for small hands
- Leash system draws criticism from some owners
- More axe than a casual snow-slope walker needs

## Who Should Buy This

The Summit Evo suits alpinists, glacier travelers and ambitious hikers who expect to meet steep snow, firm neve or the occasional easy ice and want one axe that doesn't force a compromise. Those whose snow encounters are limited to a few miles of soft slope on a high pass will be paying for capability they never use, and a simpler, cheaper, lighter walking axe is the better fit. Anyone planning sustained steep ice should look to a dedicated technical tool.

## Verdict

The Summit Evo is the axe Outdoor Gear Lab says it would choose if limited to one, and the reasoning holds up: strong pick penetration, a great adze, a shaft shaped for both plunging and steeper moves, and a weight that stays reasonable. Buy it if the route list includes real mountaineering. Skip it if the axe will mostly ride on a pack for insurance.
