## Overview

The Hiker Quarter Cushion (style 1959) is Darn Tough's midweight merino hiking sock cut to sit just above the ankle bone, which makes it the default pairing for trail runners and low-cut hikers. It has terry-loop cushioning under the foot and a padded patch over the Achilles, and it comes with the company's lifetime guarantee. The trade-off is a cuff that some hikers find too short to keep trail debris out, and a price that rewards you only if you actually take advantage of the warranty.

## Performance

**Cushioning and comfort.** Darn Tough calls this cushion level its most popular, and the "midweight" label describes a sock that adds padding without turning into a winter garment. A reviewer who wore them for nine days on the Pacific Crest Trail said they kept feet comfortable, dry and warm. The same reviewer found them a better balance than thinner socks: some padding, still breathable, not overly hot. A smaller group of buyers wanted more padding than the sock delivers. Hikers who want a plush, pillowy feel should look at a heavier Darn Tough model.

**Moisture and odor.** Merino-nylon blends dry faster than most people expect from wool. The PCT hiker above rinsed a pair, wrung it out and left it in the sun, and it was dry in about an hour. The same hiker said the socks smelled better after nine days than most of their other socks did after one. Side vents add airflow over the top of the foot, and owners who hike in shorts or in warm weather say the sock doesn't cook the foot.

**Durability.** This is where Darn Tough earns its reputation. Treeline Review's testers, who have put dozens of sock models through thousands of miles, call Darn Tough the best on fit, comfort and durability, and say long-distance hikers generally regard them as the benchmark for thru-hiking. One PCT hiker who alternated the brand with a toe-sock competitor reported holes in the competitor's socks after about 500 miles and none in the Darn Toughs. The pair did eventually wear out, and Darn Tough swapped them at a trail sock exchange. That warranty is the point: wear them out and you get a replacement.

**Fit and sizing.** The knit has just enough stretch to hug the foot without squeezing. Many owners report no bunching or slipping, and some with wide feet say the sock accommodates them. Sizing reports are mixed, though, and one reviewer who wears a size 12 found the fit more like an XL. There is also a real risk with this height. One Appalachian Trail hiker reported that the cuffs stretched out within hours, let sand and dirt into the shoe, and needed taping several times a day over a 52-mile trip. Another buyer found that the Achilles area pilled after the first wash. These are the minority experience in a pool of 552 men's reviews that average 4.7 out of 5 at REI (the women's version averages 4.8 across 254), but they are consistent enough to mention.

**Weight.** At 91 g a sock, this is a substantial pair rather than an ultralight one. Treeline Review points ultralight backpackers toward this quarter-height version over the micro crew when they hike in trail runners, because it has the same cushion in less fabric. Hikers who count every gram will find lighter options, including Darn Tough's own Light Hiker line, but those trade away padding.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Lifetime guarantee and a track record on multi-month thru-hikes
- Midweight cushion that reviewers describe as comfortable without being hot
- Dries quickly and resists odor over multi-day use
- True Seamless Toe and side vents for blister control and airflow
- Made in Vermont

**Cons**

- At 91 g it is heavier than lightweight alternatives, including Darn Tough's own Light Hiker
- The cuff can stretch out and let debris in, according to some hikers
- Some buyers want more cushion, and some report sizing inconsistencies
- Pilling at the Achilles has been reported after washing
- Needs gentle care to last: cold wash, low heat or air drying

## Who Should Buy This

Trail-runner and low-cut-shoe hikers who want one sock for everything from weekend trips to a full thru-hike will get the most from it. It also suits anyone who has worn through cheaper socks and wants a free replacement when a pair fails. Hikers who want maximum padding for heavy loads or cold weather, or who lose grams on principle, should look at a thicker or thinner Darn Tough model. People with wide ankles or calves, or those prone to sand getting into low-cut socks, should consider the micro crew with the same cushion.

## Verdict

The Hiker Quarter Cushion is a proven thru-hiking sock that backs its durability with a lifetime guarantee and keeps feet dry and comfortable across long days. The cuff height and sizing are the only recurring complaints, and they affect a minority of owners. For most hikers in trail runners, it is the sock to buy first.
