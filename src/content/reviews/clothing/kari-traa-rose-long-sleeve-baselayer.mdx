## Overview

The Rose Long Sleeve is the Norwegian brand's best-selling merino top, a midweight, slim-fit crew in 100% merino with a Nordic floral jacquard pattern. It has been in the line for roughly two decades, and it is built for cold-weather skiing, snow hikes and shoulder-season trekking. The trade-off is plain: it is warmer and better finished than most merino tops at its price, but at 240 gsm it is a cold-weather piece rather than an all-season ultralight one.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Warmth is the Rose's calling card. Reviewers who have worn it on ski trips describe it as the warmest merino in Kari Traa's range, and one owner said it suits people who run cold. A Trailspace reviewer called it "incredibly warm" for its thickness and classed it as a midweight layer. The 240 gsm knit has enough loft to take the edge off a windy ridge before a shell goes on, and it still works as a standalone top around camp when temperatures drop. Owners also report that it stays warm in damp conditions, which is the usual advantage of wool over cotton.

**Odor and breathability.** Merino's natural odor resistance holds up. One owner reported wearing the layers for four or five days of skiing before washing them, with no smell, even after sweating. That makes it a sensible choice for multi-day winter trips where washing isn't an option. Breathability is good for steady-state activity like snowshoeing, skinning or cold-weather hiking. It is less well suited to hard uphill efforts in mild weather, where a 240 gsm fabric will run hot. The thinner side panels help a little, and the button placket at the neck allows some venting.

**Fit and comfort.** At 19.5 microns the wool sits in the middle of the merino range. It is soft against the skin but not the softest on the market. Several reviewers with sensitive skin, including some who normally avoid wool, say the Rose doesn't itch. A Trailspace tester did note a faint wooly scratch, as with any merino. The cut is slim and sporty, and the 4-way stretch lets the top move with the body. Raglan sleeves and flatlock seams keep it from bunching under a pack's shoulder straps, and reviewers praise the body length for staying tucked in when reaching or falling. Sizing reports are mixed: one early Trailspace review warned the top runs small and advised sizing up, while a more recent reviewer found a small true to size. Anyone between sizes should size up for layering room. The size range runs up to XXXL, wider than many merino brands offer.

**Construction and durability.** Reviewers rate the construction above that of similarly priced competitors, citing the flatlock seams, the underarm-seam-free raglan build and the neck placket. Durability is the weak spot. Fine-gauge merino of this weight can stretch or develop holes over time, and one REI buyer complained of holes in under a year of use. Care matters: a cool wash and no dryer will extend its life.

**Style.** The jacquard pattern is the product's other selling point. The top looks fine in a lodge or on a town street, and several reviewers wore it well beyond the trailhead. The designs are bold, though, so those who prefer a plain base layer should look at the neutral colors.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Warm for its weight, with real insulation at 240 gsm
- 100% merino with strong odor resistance on multi-day trips
- Soft enough that sensitive-skinned owners report no itch
- Flatlock seams, raglan sleeves and no underarm seam keep it comfortable under a pack
- Long body length stays tucked in
- Traceable, non-mulesing wool
- Wide size run, up to XXXL

**Cons**

- Too warm for summer or high-output use; not a three-season piece
- Can develop holes or stretch out with heavy use
- Sizing reports are inconsistent; some find it runs small
- $130 list price is high unless it's on sale
- Made in China, which disappointed some reviewers who expected Norwegian production
- Bold patterns won't suit everyone

## Who Should Buy This

This top suits women who hike, ski or camp in genuinely cold conditions and want a warm merino layer that doubles as a nice-looking top off the trail. It works well for winter day hikes, shoulder-season backpacking in the cold and hut trips. Thru-hikers and gram-counters who want a single base layer for warm and cold days should look at lighter-gauge merino tops. Hikers who are hard on gear should also expect to baby it.

## Verdict

The Rose Long Sleeve is a warm, comfortable, well-made midweight merino top with a distinctive look. Its weight is sensible for what it does, though it is not an ultralight piece in the strictest sense. Buy it for cold-weather trips, ideally on sale, and size carefully.
