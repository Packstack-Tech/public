## Overview

The HydraPak Stow 1L is a soft-sided, collapsible water bottle built for people who resent carrying dead weight and dead volume once a bottle is empty. It's TPU construction with a self-sealing silicone nozzle, a 28mm thread that accepts most squeeze filters, and an internal baffle that keeps it from ballooning into a shapeless water balloon. It's aimed at thru-hikers, ski tourers, and anyone who wants a bottle that disappears into a pocket when it's not full.

## Performance

The core pitch of the Stow is simple: it's a soft flask that scales up to a full liter without turning into an unmanageable water bag. HydraPak accomplishes this with an internal baffle in the 1L size — <cite index="16-9">the larger 1 L has an internal baffle to help it retain its flat-ish shape</cite>. In practice, that baffle is the difference between the 1L Stow and a generic collapsible pouch: it stays flattish and grippable when full instead of turning into an awkward sphere, which matters when you're trying to squeeze it one-handed while hiking.

Fit and carry are where this bottle earns its keep. <cite index="18-8,18-9">When full they can be stuffed into pockets or hung from a small carabiner, and the internal baffle in the center of the bladder on the 1L size adds structure and keeps the bladder from ballooning out into a big ball when full.</cite> One backcountry skier put it well: <cite index="11-1,11-2">the Stow 1L actually fits in a pocket, making it easier to hydrate in the skintrack, and as you drink, the bottle shrinks, eventually rolling into a ball the size of a roll of quarters.</cite> That collapsing behavior is the entire reason to buy a bottle like this over a Nalgene — you're not hauling a rigid liter-sized brick around once you're down to your last few sips.

Durability holds up reasonably well across multi-year use reports. One reviewer who's <cite index="18-4,18-6,18-7">used both the 500ml and 1L sizes for over two years snowboarding, splitboarding, hiking, fly fishing, and at outdoor concerts reported they've been leak proof and functioning properly</cite> the whole time. That's a meaningfully longer track record than most gear reviews offer, and it's reassuring given how thin-walled soft flasks can be prone to pinhole leaks after repeated rolling.

Cold-weather performance is a genuine strength — this isn't just a "technically freezable" claim on a spec sheet. A reviewer who used the Stow 1L in Patagonia, the Tetons, and Norway reported he's <cite index="13-12">never had a problem with the water bottle freezing in my pack</cite>, and noted the material <cite index="13-14">has a supple feel that can stretch if frozen, and tough enough to resist punctures from poles</cite> stuffed into a pack. If you're doing winter trips, that flexibility (versus a hard bottle risking a cracked seam when the contents freeze and expand) is a real advantage.

Not everything is glowing, though. The nozzle is spill-resistant, not spill-proof — <cite index="15-10,15-11">the spill-proof nozzle works, but it needs to be solidly closed; if it's partially or fully open, pressure on the unit will push out water.</cite> That's worth remembering if you toss it loose in a pack side pocket where it might get squeezed. There's also a texture complaint that comes up in casual use: one user described the 1L in particular as having <cite index="18-12">a bit of an awkward floppy feel to them, especially the 1L</cite>, though they added that the packability more than compensates. On the more negative end, at least one budget-price buyer reported <cite index="1-8">water gets tainted by "plastic" flavor along with quick leaks from repeated rollups</cite> — worth noting, though it's a single data point and may reflect a knockoff or damaged unit rather than a representative sample.

Weight-conscious buyers should also know this isn't the lightest soft bottle on the market. At 57g, it's heavier than some minimalist soft flasks, and one long-term user acknowledged <cite index="13-15,13-16">the only downside is if you're a weight weenie — there are other soft bottle options that weigh less</cite>, but argued the tradeoff is fewer leaks.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rolls down to a fist-sized ball when empty — genuinely saves pack space versus a hard bottle
- Internal baffle keeps the 1L size manageable and grippable when full, unlike unbaffled soft flasks
- 28mm threads are compatible with common squeeze filters (Sawyer, HydraPak filter kits)
- Freezable and flexible enough to handle winter use without cracking
- Multi-year durability reports with no leaks are common among long-term users

**Cons**

- Nozzle needs to be fully closed or it can weep water under pack pressure
- Feels floppy/awkward in hand compared to a rigid bottle, especially at full 1L capacity
- At 57g it's heavier than some ultralight-focused soft flasks
- Isolated reports of plastic taste and leaks after repeated rolling (may vary by unit/batch)

## Who Should Buy This

The Stow 1L makes the most sense for backpackers, ski tourers, and thru-hikers who want a full liter of capacity without carrying a rigid bottle's worth of dead space when it's empty, and who also want filter compatibility built in. If your priority is shaving every possible gram, look at lighter soft flasks instead — but if you value durability and filter threading over the absolute lightest weight on the scale, this is a sensible pick.

## Verdict

The HydraPak Stow 1L delivers on its core promise: a full liter of water storage that rolls up small and holds up over years of real use, with cold-weather flexibility that hard bottles can't match. It's not the lightest or most rigid-feeling option out there, but for the price and versatility, it's an easy recommendation for anyone tired of hauling an empty bottle-shaped void in their pack. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — reliable and practical, just don't expect it to feel like a "real" bottle in your hand.
