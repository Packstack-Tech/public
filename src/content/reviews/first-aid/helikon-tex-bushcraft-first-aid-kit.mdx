## Overview

The Helikon-Tex Bushcraft First Aid Kit is a Cordura medical pouch with a removable inner pouch, an internal tourniquet pocket, and enough attachment options to ride on a belt, a MOLLE panel, or a pack strap. It's aimed at people who swing axes and handle saws. Those are the injuries it's organized around. The trade-off is weight: it arrives empty at 195 g, which is a lot of mass before a single bandage goes in.

## Performance

**Organization.** The design splits into two parts: a padded outer pouch and an inner pouch with a handle and a quick-access opening. The inner pouch pulls free by tugging, so the outer shell can stay on a belt or pack while the medical kit goes to the injured person. That's the feature owners single out most. One buyer called the removable-insert idea a highlight, even while quibbling about how it sits in the cover. Another reviewer noted the outer pocket is well designed and the snap buckle is excellent.

**Capacity.** At 17 x 10 x 6 cm, this is a small pouch that holds more than it looks like it should. A Polish owner reported fitting two small bandages or one large one, gauze pads, disinfectant wipes, single-use eye drops, a 375 cm roll of compression gauze, personal medication, and disposable gloves. A UK buyer described room for tablets, wipes, plasters, and dressings, enough to handle some "quite nasty cuts". This is a cuts, blisters, and bleeding-control kit. It isn't a kit for a group, and it won't absorb a full trauma loadout without compromises.

**Tourniquet placement.** The internal tourniquet pocket is a new addition to this version, and it's the headline feature for the bushcraft pitch. It also draws the one practical caution in owner feedback: a tourniquet fits inside, but the UK buyer suggested keeping it somewhere it can be grabbed instantly. Burying it inside a zipped pouch works against the point of carrying one.

**Handling.** The pull tabs on both the inside and outside closures are easy to work with gloves on, according to owners. The zippers are YKK and the buckles are Woojin, which is the hardware Helikon-Tex uses across its line. A reflective paramedic badge on the exterior helps in finding the kit in the dark.

**Carry options.** The kit mounts to MOLLE/PALS webbing, rides on any belt up to 50 mm wide through a loop, and can be carried horizontally on Woojin clips, in the style of the brand's Foxhole bag. For a bushcrafter with a belt-carried knife and a haversack, that flexibility is the point. For a thru-hiker with a frameless pack, it is mostly irrelevant, since there is little webbing to attach it to.

**Weight.** Here the ultralight case falls apart. A bare 195 g pouch is heavier than many complete, stocked day-hike kits. Helikon-Tex's pouch is built with reinforced fabric and a structured shape, and that's what costs the grams. The pouch also ships without supplies, so every dressing, glove, and wipe adds to the total.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Removable inner pouch lets the kit be handed over or moved without unmounting the shell
- Dedicated internal tourniquet pocket
- Belt, MOLLE/PALS, and horizontal carry options
- Cordura 500D shell with YKK zippers and Woojin buckles
- Pull tabs usable with gloves
- Fits dressings, gauze, wipes, gloves, and personal meds in a 17 x 10 x 6 cm footprint

**Cons**

- 195 g empty is heavy for a backpacking kit
- Sold without any medical supplies, at $42.99
- Tourniquet pocket is not a fast-access spot
- Fit of the insert inside the cover draws mixed comments from some owners
- MOLLE-first design adds little for packs without webbing

## Who Should Buy This

Bushcrafters, campers, hunters, and anyone who works with edged tools and wants a belt-mounted kit that can hand off its insert. It also suits people already running MOLLE-based packs or haversacks who want a single tidy medical pouch. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere: a stuff-sack-style kit or a zip bag with a handful of well-chosen supplies will carry the same essentials at a fraction of the weight.

## Verdict

The Bushcraft First Aid Kit is a well-built, thoughtfully organized pouch for people whose injury risk comes from blades and saws. It's the wrong tool for ultralight trips, where 195 g of empty container is hard to justify. Buy it for camp and belt carry. Skip it for the long trail.
