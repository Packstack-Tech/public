## Performance

### Packability

This is where the Frontier UL earns its keep. The cup genuinely disappears in a pack — tuck it between a rain jacket and a food bag and you won't think about it again until you want coffee. <cite index="1-37,1-38">These collapse to be almost flat for storage, pop out from a flat compressed size, and become rigid and stable once opened out.</cite> I've carried cups that technically collapse but feel like a sad, crinkled accordion in hand. This one doesn't.

### Structural Rigidity

The defining feature is the dual-ring system. <cite index="12-35,12-36,12-37">It's the two plastic rings — one at the top and one halfway up — that make such a difference compared to other flexible cups. This design supports the soft flexible folding material and creates a rigid cup that does not collapse and is not soft and floppy when unfolded. They are stable when filled with liquid and placed on a flat surface.</cite> That last point matters more than you'd think. A cup that tips over on a log or uneven tent floor at 6 a.m. is a cup you'll resent. <cite index="2-4">Glass-reinforced nylon 66 is used for the base, rim, and center ring, providing more stability and allowing you to grip the cup more easily.</cite> Sea to Summit also stress-tests the design: <cite index="2-18">the bowls are tested on a rig that extends and collapses each item 5,000 times.</cite> That's convincing longevity data.

### Heat Handling

Manage expectations here. <cite index="2-17">The silicone rubber sidewalls reduce heat transfer, but not anything like a double-walled rigid stainless steel mug.</cite> Your morning coffee will cool down faster in this than in a titanium mug, full stop. That said, <cite index="1-23,1-24">one user who switched from the Sea to Summit Cool Grip X-Mug found that handling hot coffee was not an issue when holding the cup at the plastic top rim and/or center ring.</cite> The nylon rings do genuinely insulate your fingertips — it's a real design choice, not marketing fluff. <cite index="16-18,16-19">The heat-resistant silicone used in Frontier dinnerware is tested to both EU and FDA food-grade standards and is heat resistant to 150°C / 300°F, and doesn't crack in cold temperatures.</cite>

### Taste & Off-Gassing

This is the one flag worth raising. <cite index="15-12,15-13,15-14,15-15">At least one reviewer cleaned the cup twice before use, let room-temperature water sit in it for about five minutes, found the result undrinkable, and imagined that boiling water would magnify the taste.</cite> This isn't unique to Sea to Summit — new food-grade silicone often has a detectable scent or taste on first use. A good practice with any new silicone gear: fill it with boiling water, let it soak 15–20 minutes, dump it, and repeat once or twice. The issue typically resolves with a few uses. That said, if you're highly sensitive to silicone taste, that's worth knowing going in.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Collapses to a truly negligible profile — under 2cm, slides in a hip belt pocket
- Dual-ring design delivers legitimate rigidity; stable on uneven surfaces
- Nylon rings provide meaningful heat shielding for fingertips on hot drinks
- Interior volume markings add practical utility for cooking
- <cite index="1-17">Dishwasher safe</cite> — easy to keep clean at home between
  trips
- <cite index="16-21">BPA-free and non-PFAS</cite> across the entire product
  line
- Backed by a lifetime guarantee
- Nests with the broader Frontier system

**Cons**

- Heat retention is genuinely limited; don't expect hot coffee to stay hot
- Some users report a noticeable silicone taste fresh out of the box — requires a few break-in soaks
- 355ml (12 fl oz) is a standard-mug size, not a large camp mug; fine for coffee, tight for ramen
- No lid option — faster heat loss and no spill protection in a pack hip belt pocket
- ~$13 is reasonable, though budget-conscious buyers will find cheaper silicone cups that are heavier and floppier

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right cup for weight-conscious backpackers, thru-hikers, and travelers who want a proper drinking vessel that actually packs flat. <cite index="2-6">Designed to nest and stack, these cups really do fit in anywhere.</cite> It also makes good sense as a dedicated coffee or tea cup for minimalists building a Frontier cook system. If you're a coffee-temperature zealot who can't tolerate a tepid cup, look at an insulated titanium option instead — but accept the weight and bulk penalty that comes with it.

## Verdict

The Sea to Summit Frontier UL Collapsible Cup is a well-thought-out piece of kit — the dual nylon ring structure solves the floppiness problem that plagues cheaper collapsible cups, and the packed footprint is genuinely impressive. <cite index="8-9,8-10">The Frontier Camp Kitchen Collection earned a Gold award at the 2023 Australian Good Design Awards, recognized by the World Design Organization as Australia's peak international design endorsement program.</cite> For ~$13 and 54g, it's hard to argue against adding this to a trail kit. Just soak the silicone a couple of times before trusting it with your morning coffee — that's 10 minutes of effort that will save you a lot of disappointment on day one of a trip. **Rating: 7.5/10.**
