## Overview

The Iniu P50-E1 is a 10,000mAh (36 Wh) power bank that Iniu bills as the smallest of its capacity, with 45W output, three ports, a battery-percentage display and a detachable USB-C lanyard cable. It suits backpackers who want a phone refilled quickly, plus the occasional partial top-up for a tablet or small laptop, without carrying a brick. The trade-off is a short, thick body with a plastic shell that picks up scratches, and capacity that stops at roughly two full phone charges.

## Performance

**Size and carry.** The P50-E1's selling point is volume rather than slimness. Pack Hacker's testers likened it to two AirPods cases side by side, and a Slickdeals owner summed up the shape as "fat and light." It is thick, but it slips into a hip-belt pocket or a shoulder-strap pouch, and a tester carrying it in a two-liter sling said it barely registered. At 160 g, it works out to about 6.4 Wh per ounce, a respectable ratio for a bank with this much output.

**Charging speed.** The 45W ceiling is the reason to choose this over the many 20–22.5W banks in the same size class. Reviewers report that it supports PPS from 5V to 11V at up to 4.8A, which is what lets Samsung's flagship phones, the Galaxy S25 Ultra among them, pull their full fast-charging rate. One Amazon commenter said it held 45W from start to finish without throttling. Iniu claims a phone can go from 0 to 73% in 25 minutes. TechRadar's lab testing found "above-average" results for the size, crediting well-matched cells and an efficient converter. An independent bench test measured about 31.5 Wh delivered from the 36 Wh pack at 5V, an output efficiency of 87.5%.

**Real-world capacity.** Ten thousand milliamp-hours means one to three phone charges depending on the phone. Iniu's own figures are two full iPhone 16 charges or about one and a half Galaxy S24 charges. For a weekend or a five-day stretch between resupplies, that covers a phone, a watch and a headlamp. For a thru-hiker running a phone as a camera, GPS and e-reader, it covers roughly two days of heavy use. The 45W output can revive a MacBook Air or similar ultrabook, but only partially, and it won't run a laptop for long.

**Recharge time.** A 45W charger filled the bank from 51% to 100% in about an hour in one owner's test, and a Yahoo Tech reviewer found a full recharge took around two hours, which matches Iniu's published figure. That makes it practical for a town stop or a hostel outlet. Pass-through charging lets it charge in the same sitting as your devices.

**Heat and multi-device use.** Owners report little warmth when charging a phone. TechRadar's reviewer found it fine tucked inside a bag for phone charging, but advised giving it open air during laptop charging. It can feed three devices at once, but total output is shared, so each one charges more slowly than it would alone.

**Cable and display.** The attached lanyard cable doubles as both an output lead and a recharge cable, so one fewer cord is needed. It adds only a few grams (the cable and holder took the weighed unit from 159 g to 166 g). The display is useful, though one owner noticed the percentage dropped from 100% to 91% quickly and then fell much more slowly. The plastic around the display scratches readily: MobileSyrup's reviewer reported three large scratches after a few nights in a purse. Bank and keys in the same pocket is a bad combination, so a small pouch is worth the weight.

**Limits.** There is no magnetic attachment or wireless charging, which Pack Hacker, which scored it 7.2 out of 10, flagged as a gap against rivals like the Ugreen Uno Magnetic. One Slickdeals commenter, who preferred the P50 over Iniu's thinner siblings, also noted it has less capacity than a similar-looking Qi2 model. Another commenter said their earlier experience with cheaper Iniu banks gave mixed results on longevity, though a TechRadar reviewer's two-year-old Iniu B6, built the same way, was still going strong.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 45W output with PPS handles flagship phones at full speed and tops up tablets and small laptops
- 160 g and 8.3 x 5.2 x 2.6 cm is compact for 36 Wh
- Built-in lanyard cable removes a separate cord
- Digital display shows remaining charge
- Recharges in roughly one to two hours with a 45W charger
- Two USB-C ports plus USB-A, and passthrough charging
- Often discounted to well under its $32.99 list price

**Cons**

- Thick body, though short, so it is less flat than MagSafe-style banks
- Plastic around the display scratches easily
- Capacity is only enough for about two phone charges
- No wireless or magnetic charging
- Output is shared when charging three devices at once
- Not enough for powering a laptop for any length of time

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend and section hikers, and thru-hikers who resupply every few days, will get the most out of the P50-E1: fast phone charging, a built-in cable and a small footprint. It also suits anyone with a Samsung phone that benefits from 45W PPS. Hikers who rely on a phone for navigation and photos for a week or more between outlets, or who want to charge a laptop, should look at a larger 20,000mAh bank. Those who want magnetic phone attachment should consider Iniu's own Qi2 model.

## Verdict

The Iniu P50-E1 delivers 45W at 160 g, and it is easy to carry. It is a very good everyday and short-trip battery, held back mainly by modest capacity and a scratch-prone shell. Buy it for weekend trips and quick refuels, and pair it with a pouch.
