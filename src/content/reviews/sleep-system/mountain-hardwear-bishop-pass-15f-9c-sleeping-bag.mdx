## Overview

The Bishop Pass 15F/-9C is Mountain Hardwear's value-minded cold-weather down bag: a mummy-cut, 650-fill sleeping bag for backpackers and climbers who want a durable shell and a full feature set without paying 800-fill prices. It is not a gram-counter's bag. At 1,060 g, it sits in the lightweight tier rather than the ultralight one, and its 15°F number is a limit rating, not a promise of comfort.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The Bishop Pass earns its reputation as a three-season bag that stretches into shoulder-season cold. One reviewer put its comfort limit at about 26°F, which lines up with how most owners describe it: reliably warm through mountain and desert nights, with a warm pad and clothing carrying it lower. Winter-camping owners of the colder-rated Bishop Pass models report staying comfortable well below freezing, but the 15F version is a different animal, and the reports at the edge of its range are mixed. A Sierra hiker crossing Bishop Pass in early October, with nights in the upper 20s, called the bag a disappointment on REI's site. A Backpacker magazine tester, using the Gore-Tex version of the bag, woke cold repeatedly with tent temperatures near 26°F. The takeaway is consistent: treat 15°F as a survival-adjacent number and plan on temperatures in the 20s and up for a good night's sleep. Cold sleepers should add a layer or buy a step down in rating.

**Weight and bulk.** This is the bag's weak point. The 650-fill down is cut with feathers, and the 20D nylon shell is sturdier than the thin fabrics found on ultralight bags. Reviewers at The Equipment Guide listed heavy, bulky, lower-quality down among the cons, and Adventure Alan's review noted the bag only just qualifies as lightweight. Stuffed, it measures 8 by 15.5 inches, roughly the size of a football. That is manageable in a 50-liter pack, but it will eat more space than an 800-fill bag with the same rating.

**Durability and features.** The heavier shell has an upside: reviewers describe it as highly resistant to tears and snags, which suits climbers and anyone who sleeps in rough campsites. The #5 YKK zipper has anti-snag guards and glow-in-the-dark pulls, and owners say it runs smoothly in the dark, a small but real nicety at 2 a.m. A draft collar seals the neck, an internal stash pocket holds a phone or contacts, and loops along the side let a sleeping pad be clipped in place so it doesn't wander. Trail Sisters' testers, reviewing the women's version, liked the plow-shaped zipper pull but wished the stash pocket closed with a zipper. Left- and right-zip versions can be paired for a double bed.

**Fit.** The cut is a performance mummy, narrower through the legs to save weight. A 5'11" tester found it comfortable, if slightly tight through the torso for broader sleepers. Stomach and side sleepers on REI reported enough room, and the footbox sits in a natural position that helps hold warmth. A long length is offered for taller sleepers.

**Value.** Retail has run roughly $285 to $335, and sales have pushed the 15F version to under $100 at times, at which point reviewers and deal hunters call it a hard bag to beat.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tough 20D shell shrugs off snags and abrasion
- Full feature set: draft collar, stash pocket, pad loops, glow zipper pulls
- Smooth, anti-snag #5 YKK zipper
- RDS-certified down
- Low price, especially when discounted

**Cons**

- 1,060 g and a football-sized stuffed volume are heavy for a 15°F bag
- 650-fill down with 20% feathers is the least efficient insulation in its class
- Some users report cold nights in the mid-to-upper 20s
- Narrow cut can feel snug for broader sleepers

## Who Should Buy This

Car-camp-adjacent backpackers, Sierra and Rockies weekenders, climbers who are hard on gear, and first-time down buyers who want a warm, rugged bag on a budget are the target. Anyone planning long thru-hikes, where every 100 g counts, should look at 800-fill bags or a quilt instead. Cold sleepers who expect true 15°F comfort should step down to the 0°F model or add a liner.

## Verdict

The Bishop Pass 15F/-9C is a durable, feature-rich bag that delivers solid three-season warmth at a modest price, and it is a poor fit for weight-focused hikers. Buy it on sale for rough-use trips and treat its rating as a limit rather than a comfort number.
