## Overview

The Enlightened Equipment Revelation is the quilt that a generation of Appalachian Trail and PCT hikers swapped their mummy bags for. It is a 20°F down top quilt with continuous U-shaped baffles, a quarter-length zipper and a footbox that opens flat like a blanket or cinches shut like a cocoon. The headline trade-off: the weight-to-warmth ratio is hard to beat, but the 20°F rating is a limit figure, so cold sleepers need to buy colder than the number on the label.

## Performance

### Warmth and the temperature rating

Enlightened Equipment rates its quilts to the EN/ISO limit standard, which means the 20°F figure marks the point where a sleeper is cold but safe, not where they are cozy. Retailers selling the quilt put the comfort temperature roughly 10°F above the limit. Owners' reports line up with that. CleverHiker's testers, who logged hundreds of nights in the quilt over five years, advise cold sleepers to choose a rating 10–20°F lower than the temperatures they expect. A reviewer at The Trek took the 20°F model out at a recorded 12°F and ended up a little chilly, concluding that the rating is honest but not a license to go below it.

In practice, that makes the 20°F Revelation a quilt for nights in the 30s and above, with a puffy and a beanie extending the range for people who run warm. Below freezing, CleverHiker's testers found it drafty. Hikers who regularly see the 20s should step down to the 10°F or 0°F models in the lineup.

### Baffles, fill and shell

The U-shaped continuous baffles give the down room to loft and keep it from sliding to the edges. The cost is that fill can't be shifted between torso and feet. Older owner complaints about thin fill and cold spots led EE to add more down, and current production quilts draw fewer of those reports.

Fill power is the main weight lever. SectionHiker's comparison of the Custom Revelation 20 found that 850fp duck down comes in at 22.54 oz and 950fp goose down at 20.88 oz, with the goose version costing more. Both perform the same per gram of fill-power-adjusted down; the 950fp quilt simply needs less of it. EE doesn't treat its down with a hydrophobic finish, so a wet footbox in a damp climate is a real concern. Condensation under a tarp is the usual culprit.

The standard 10D shell is thin enough to show its weight savings and its fragility. Minor down leakage is common with shells this light, and anyone cowboy camping or sleeping on rough ground should look at the 20D option.

### Footbox, collar and pad straps

The footbox is the Revelation's signature. Unzipped, the quilt becomes a blanket for summer nights. Zipped and cinched, it closes off drafts at the feet, and the neck drawcord seals the top. Few quilts cover such a wide span as comfortably, which is why active sleepers tend to favor it over a sewn-closed design.

The weak point is the neck. A draft collar is an extra on the Revelation, while some competitors build it into the base price. Retailers who stock EE quilts, including Farlite in Finland, include the collar on every unit they sell, which says something about where most buyers land. Custom buyers should add it at checkout.

The included pad straps, one straight and one looped, hold the quilt to the pad and cut drafts at the sides. Owners say the spacing takes a night or two to dial in, particularly on pads that are narrower or wider than average. Once set, the system works well.

### Packability

The Revelation compresses small in its silnylon sack. CleverHiker puts the 20°F version at about 20 oz in 950fp, and calls it one of the lightest in its class. A cotton storage bag comes in the box to keep the loft intact between trips.

### Lead times

Custom builds ship on EE's lead-time schedule, which runs weeks, not days. Stock quilts come with a fixed feature set and ship faster. A first-time buyer who needs a quilt for a trip next month should look at stock.

## Choosing a Version

The four listed configurations differ mainly in fill power and how far the build has been customized.

- **592 g:** The lightest option, built with 950fp Grey Goose Down at the 20°F rating. It saves roughly 48 g over the 850fp version and costs more. Best for gram counters who already know the quilt suits their sleep style.
- **660 g 20°F (639 g):** A 20°F Revelation that lands within a gram of the 850fp Regular/Regular quilt below. EE notes that down weights can vary by about 3% between units, so these two are effectively the same product.
- **660 g 20°F, Regular/Regular, 850FP (640 g):** The baseline. Duck down, standard 10D shell, and a size that fits hikers up to about 6 feet with a 56-inch girth. Best value for most buyers.
- **660 g Custom (660 g):** The made-to-order route, at the top of the weight range. Options such as a wider or longer cut, a heavier shell or added features push the weight up a notch. Choose this if the stock sizing doesn't fit, or if a draft collar and 20D shell are on the list.

Size matters too. On the 850fp 20°F model, a Short is about 595 g, Regular/Regular about 640 g, Regular/Wide about 669 g, and Long/Wide about 723 g. Taller or broader sleepers should go wide rather than squeeze, since gaps at the shoulders are where cold air gets in.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Weight-to-warmth ratio at the top of the class, down to 592 g with 950fp
- Footbox converts from open blanket to sealed cocoon
- Continuous baffles keep down from migrating
- Pad straps included
- RDS-certified down
- Deep customization of fill, shell, size and rating

**Cons**

- The 20°F rating is a limit figure; comfort sits about 10°F higher
- Draft collar costs extra
- The 10D shell is delicate and can shed some down
- Untreated down is a liability in persistent damp
- Pad straps take some fiddling to seal properly
- Custom orders take weeks to arrive

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season thru-hikers, weekend backpackers and anyone leaving a mummy bag behind for the first time will get the most from the Revelation. It suits restless sleepers and hikers who want one quilt to cover summer heat and shoulder-season chill. Those who sleep cold, or who regularly camp below freezing, should buy the 10°F or 0°F model instead. Hikers who need gear immediately should stick to stock, and anyone who wants a hood or an integrated collar without extra cost should look at competitors that include one.

## Verdict

The Revelation earns its reputation: very light, very packable, and flexible enough to span a wide range of nights. Treat the 20°F number as a floor, add the draft collar, and choose the 850fp version for value or the 950fp for the last 48 grams.
