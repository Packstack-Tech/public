## Overview

The MSR Titan Fork & Spoon is about as simple as backpacking gear gets: a titanium fork, a titanium spoon, and a little cable to keep them together in your food bag. It's aimed at thru-hikers and weekend backpackers who want to ditch a flimsy plastic spork or a heavier steel utensil from the kitchen drawer without overthinking it. If you've ever melted a plastic spoon into your dinner or snapped a fork on a frozen Snickers bar, this is the fix.

## Performance

At 28 grams for the pair, this set isn't going to win any ultralight utensil weight-weenie contests — a bare titanium long spoon alone can come in under 20 grams — but it's still a fraction of what you'd carry from a home flatware drawer, and it beats most plastic sporks on durability while costing barely more.

The fork and spoon are sold separately in size but shipped as a linked pair, and <cite index="10-2">they come on a screw-locked cable so they're as easy to keep track of as they are to carry</cite>. That cable is a nice touch for keeping the two pieces from wandering off into different corners of your food bag, though it's not bomb-proof — one reviewer on a gear forum noted that <cite index="16-2">the braided metal cord broke at one point when hanging from a pack, so they'd recommend using a spare small biner to pair these together</cite> instead of relying on it long-term.

On actual eating performance, opinions split a bit depending on what people are used to. Several users describe being pleasantly surprised: one Backcountry.com reviewer said <cite index="13-11,13-12">"I've been surprised by how much I like these. The finish isn't as nice as similar Snow Peak but they're slightly longer and a better fit in my hand,"</cite> and another noted <cite index="13-2">"the spoon has a surprisingly good cutting edge on it without risking your lips and tongue."</cite> On the flip side, a few reviewers found the spoon's bowl on the shallow side, with one writing <cite index="13-8,13-9">"the bowl of the spoon really isn't deep enough. It's almost like trying to eat with a tineless fork."</cite> Another comment summed up the size trade-off bluntly: <cite index="13-26">"A little too big for minimalist UL use."</cite>

That's a fair characterization — this isn't a dedicated long-handled spoon designed to reach the bottom of a dehydrated meal pouch. If that's your priority, MSR's own Titan Long Spoon is built specifically for that job. The Fork & Spoon set is more of a general-purpose pairing: good for eating out of a pot, a bowl, or a standard meal bag, but you may still need to tip the bag or use your fingers to get the last bites out of deep pouches.

Durability has been a non-issue in the reports I found. Titanium doesn't corrode, doesn't hold onto food odors or flavors, and shrugs off the abuse of being tossed in a pot or ground into dirt. <cite index="13-32,13-33">Titanium is non-reactive, so it won't cause weird flavors in your food or drink.</cite> One long-term user mentioned buying their set in the early 2000s and still using it decades later, only replacing it after losing a piece — which says more about human error than the gear itself.

One legitimate knock is cleaning: unlike some plastic or coated combo utensils, this set is not dishwasher-safe on the top rack the way certain competitors are, so if you're using it at home between trips, that's a minor inconvenience.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Titanium construction — doesn't rust, doesn't hold flavors, essentially indestructible under normal use
- Reasonably priced for titanium gear
- Fork and spoon linked by a cable, so you're less likely to lose one in your food bag
- Long enough handle to comfortably eat out of a pot without dunking your knuckles
- Decades of proven use reported by longtime owners

**Cons:**

- At 28 g combined, it's not the lightest titanium utensil option on the market
- Spoon bowl is shallower than some users want for deep meal pouches
- The connecting cable isn't fully reliable long-term — consider a small carabiner as backup
- Not dishwasher-safe
- No knife in the set, so you'll want a small blade elsewhere in your kit for cutting food

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for backpackers who want a simple, no-drama utensil upgrade from disposable plastic or heavy steel, and who eat mostly out of pots, bowls, or standard-depth meal bags rather than deep dehydrated pouches. If your meal prep involves rehydrating food in tall freezer bags and you want to scrape the corners clean, look at a dedicated long-handled titanium spoon instead.

## Verdict

The MSR Titan Fork & Spoon does exactly what it promises — light, durable, no-nonsense eating utensils that will outlast just about everything else in your pack. It's not the featherweight champion of the utensil world, and the spoon bowl could be deeper, but for the price and reliability, it's an easy recommendation for anyone tired of babysitting plastic sporks. I'd put this at a 7.5/10: a dependable staple, just not a category-leading standout.
