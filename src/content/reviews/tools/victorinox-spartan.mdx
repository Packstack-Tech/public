## Overview

The Victorinox Spartan is the plain-clothes workhorse of the Swiss Army lineup: a 91 mm, 12-function folder with two blades, a corkscrew, openers, screwdrivers and a few small utilities. It suits campers, section hikers and anyone who wants one knife for the trail and the kitchen drawer. The trade-off is weight. At 59 g it carries more tools than most ultralight kits need, and it costs several times the weight of a minimalist blade.

## Performance

**Tool lineup.** The Spartan shares its tool list with Victorinox's original Officer's Knife, in a more streamlined body. The tools are:

- a large blade and a small blade
- a corkscrew
- a combined reamer, punch and sewing awl
- a can opener with a 3 mm screwdriver
- a bottle opener with a 6 mm screwdriver and wire stripper
- tweezers and a toothpick, both removable
- a key ring

Owners note that the small screwdriver on the can opener is useful for eyeglass screws, and that the reamer handles the occasional boot or pack repair. Reviewers who have carried it camping say the two-blade setup is underrated. One blade can stay razor sharp for food and first aid while the other does the dirty work.

**Cutting.** Both blades have a standard V grind in Victorinox's stainless Inox steel. That steel resists corrosion well and is easy to touch up in the field, which matters on a wet trail where a carbon blade would start to speckle. The large blade is short at 61 mm. It is enough for cheese, salami, cordage, moleskin and fuzz sticks, but it is not a tool for batoning kindling. One reviewer found a little give in the blades, though nothing alarming. Build quality is the brand's calling card, and long-term owners describe knives that have served as daily carry for a decade or more.

**The lock question.** The blades and tools do not lock. That is the knife's most consistent criticism, and it is a real one. Pressure on the spine of the open blade can fold it onto your fingers. With a light touch and cutting strokes that move away from the hand, it is manageable for camp chores. It is not a knife to lean on.

**In the pocket and pack.** At 14 mm thick and 26 mm wide, the Spartan is slim for a 12-function knife. Reviewers describe it as the right size to forget it is there, and it has no tactical look that unsettles strangers when it comes out at a trailhead picnic table. The Cellidor scales are slick when wet but light, and they have held up well for owners.

**Weight in context.** At 59 g, it is light for its capability and heavy for an ultralight kit. Victorinox's own Classic SD, a keychain-sized 58 mm knife, weighs roughly a third as much and covers most of what a thru-hiker cuts in a day. Within the 91 mm family the Spartan is also the lean option, well below the Hiker at 77 g and the Huntsman at 98 g. The corkscrew and bottle opener are, as one reviewer put it, impractical for serious trekkers. They are also nearly dead weight on a resupply-to-resupply route. The can opener and wire stripper rarely come out on a trail either.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Two blades keep one edge pristine for food and first aid
- Corrosion-resistant stainless that tolerates rain and river crossings
- Slim 14 mm profile that disappears in a pocket or hip belt pouch
- Swiss build quality, with owners reporting a decade or more of regular use
- Screwdrivers, awl and tweezers cover basic gear repairs and splinters
- Inexpensive for the quality, and parts and replacements are easy to find

**Cons**

- 59 g is heavy against ultralight alternatives
- No blade lock
- Short 61 mm main blade limits heavier tasks
- Several tools (corkscrew, can opener, bottle opener) rarely earn their place on a backpacking trip
- No scissors, which many ultralight hikers prefer to a second blade

## Who Should Buy This

The Spartan fits weekend backpackers, car campers, section hikers and anyone building a single do-everything knife for travel and daily carry. It also suits hikers who like a repair-capable kit: awl for stitching, screwdriver for trekking pole tips, tweezers for ticks. Gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere, to a Classic SD or a small fixed or lockback blade, depending on what they cut. Anyone who expects to do real food prep or wood processing should pair this with something sturdier or choose a locking model.

## Verdict

The Spartan is a well-made, honestly sized knife that does what a Swiss Army Knife should, and it ranks among the better values in the lineup. For ultralight trips, its 59 g is hard to justify against the Classic SD, and the missing lock keeps it from being a hard-use tool. Buy it for general camping and everyday carry, and leave it home on a pared-down thru-hike.
