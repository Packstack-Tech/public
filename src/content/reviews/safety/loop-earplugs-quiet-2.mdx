## Overview

The Loop Quiet 2 is a reusable silicone earplug built for sleep, focus, and everyday noise sensitivity rather than shooting ranges or job sites. It's the second generation of Loop's original Quiet plug, with a redesigned, smaller-nozzle tip aimed at reducing pressure for side sleepers. If you're a light packer who wants to shave weight off your "just in case" hostel/tent-city noise kit, these are worth a look — though they're a comfort-and-sleep product first, not a technical hearing-protection tool.

## Performance

**Noise reduction:** On paper, 24 dB SNR / 14 dB NRR puts the Quiet 2 well below foam plugs, which routinely claim 29-33 dB NRR. In practice, that gap matters less than the spec sheet suggests for sleep applications. One tester who lives next to a nightclub noted that <cite index="8-2,8-3,8-4">she was dubious at first because she could still hear shouting and singing from the street, since her usual industrial-style earplugs reduce noise up to 37dB compared to the Loop's 24dB</cite>. But she found <cite index="8-6,8-7">the Quiet 2 muffles bedroom sounds just enough for her brain to ignore them, and she fell asleep quickly and comfortably even side-sleeping</cite>. That's the core trade-off with these: they're designed to take the edge off noise, not create a silent vacuum. Loop is upfront about this positioning — the earplugs are <cite index="10-9">designed to reduce ambient noise without completely blocking it out, for those who want to minimize disruptions without total silence</cite>.

**Fit and comfort:** This is where the Quiet 2 earns its reputation. The redesign brought a smaller nozzle specifically to cut down on ear canal pressure, and Loop states this makes them <cite index="12-17">more comfortable to sleep in</cite> compared to the original Quiet. Independent coverage backs this up, describing the flush fit as the plug's signature trait: <cite index="17-2,17-3,17-4">it sits flush with the ear canal with nothing protruding, so when you lie on your side the earplug doesn't dig in, shift, or make you rip it out at 3 AM</cite>. That's a real advantage over foam, which tends to push out of the canal and create a pressure point against the pillow.

That said, comfort isn't universal. One eBay buyer reported the plugs weren't comfortable during sleep, and a Tom's Guide tester who otherwise liked the fit still <cite index="8-8">woke up more than once in the night to readjust them, and at one point took them out in a half-conscious moment because they'd become uncomfortable</cite>. Ear canal shape varies enough that even with four tip sizes, some people will need to hunt for the right fit or accept the Quiet 2 isn't their forever solution.

**Durability and upkeep:** Silicone doesn't degrade the way foam does, and Loop markets these as reusable and washable long-term. A third-party review estimated <cite index="17-13">a single pair lasts 3-6 months of nightly use with occasional soap-and-water cleaning</cite>, though I'd treat that as a rough estimate rather than a guarantee — Loop doesn't publish an official lifespan figure, and actual mileage will depend on how often you clean them and how much ear wax buildup you're dealing with.

**Everyday ratings:** Aggregate retail reviews land in solidly-good-not-great territory — Walmart's listing shows <cite index="2-1">3.9 stars out of 5,817 reviews</cite>, which tracks with a product that a lot of people love but that has a real minority of side-sleepers or small-ear-canal users who bounce off the fit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Flush, low-profile design genuinely reduces the "digging into my ear" problem that plagues foam plugs for side sleepers
- Reusable and washable — no ongoing disposable-foam waste or cost
- Four tip sizes (XS-S-M-L) give you a real shot at dialing in fit
- Comes with a carry case, which matters if you're tossing these in a hip belt pocket or stuff sack
- Certified hearing protection (EN 352-2:2020, ANSI S3.19-1974), not just a marketing claim

**Cons**

- 24 dB SNR / 14 dB NRR is noticeably lower than dedicated foam plugs (29-33 dB NRR) — don't expect these to fully block loud snoring, generator noise, or a rowdy hostel common room
- Some users still report them working loose or needing readjustment overnight
- Fit is genuinely hit-or-miss for smaller or unusually shaped ear canals despite the size range
- At around 30 g with the case, they're not meaningfully lighter than a handful of foam pairs, so the weight argument for thru-hikers is marginal — the appeal here is comfort and reusability, not grams saved

## Who Should Buy This

The Quiet 2 makes the most sense for backpackers who prioritize sleep quality over maximum decibel blocking — think shared shelters, hostel dorms, or car-camping next to a noisy family, where you want to soften the edges of ambient noise without going deaf-in-a-jar silent. Side sleepers who've bounced off foam plugs digging into their ear on the pillow are the clearest use case. If you're dealing with heavy snoring, close-proximity road noise, or need reliable occupational-grade hearing protection, look at a higher-NRR foam or a Loop Dream (27 dB SNR) instead.

## Verdict

The Loop Quiet 2 isn't the loudest-blocking earplug you can buy, but it doesn't need to be — its job is comfortable, reusable, all-night wearability, and on that front it delivers for most side sleepers and light sleepers. If you want maximum noise cancellation on a job site or firing range, keep shopping; for taking the edge off camp noise so you can actually fall asleep, it's a smart, low-maintenance addition to a gear closet. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — solid and well-engineered, docked slightly for the fit variability some users report.
