## Overview

The Anjan 3 is Hilleberg's Yellow Label answer to the question of how much storm resistance a three-season tent needs. It is a single-entrance tunnel built from Kerlon 1000 and 9 mm poles, and the company says it weighs roughly 20% less than the all-season Nallo it was derived from. The trade-off is familiar for Hilleberg: Swedish construction, a lifetime warranty and weather-proof stability, paid for with a high price and a weight that sits well above modern ultralight shelters.

## Performance

**Weather.** The Anjan's reputation rests on how it behaves when conditions turn. Backpacker's testers pitched it in Colorado's Roosevelt National Forest, where it withstood three inches of wet snow. They credited the low, aerodynamic profile and four guy-out points, each anchored at two spots on the fly. Owners on Rokslide and Reddit describe it as sturdier than a three-season rating suggests, and one hunter-owner called it amazing in wind. Hilleberg itself positions it for snow-free months, and a tent with this much wall area and this little insulation from the ground is not a shoulder-season winter shelter. It will take a beating in rain and gusts. It is not designed to sit under a snow load for days.

**Pitching.** The tunnel layout needs only four pegs for a basic pitch, and a continuous pole sleeve with a pole tensioner keeps setup quick. Owners single out the ability to pitch it in a small space on just four stakes. The inner and outer are linked, so both go up at once, and a rainy-day pitch keeps the inner dry. Looped guy lines attach at twin points on each side of every pole for extra stability when the wind rises.

**Ventilation and condensation.** This is where the three-season design earns its keep. The fly stops short of the ground, the inner has an almost all-mesh door and a large mesh rear vent, and the rear wall of the fly can be rolled up on warm nights. A Rokslide owner reported that condensation was handled well, helped by that mesh door. The catch, noted by Backpacker's testers, is that venting is limited when weather forces the vestibule doors shut. An Australian owner on Bushwalk also reported rain splashing in under the high fly and some flapping in wind, even with a careful pitch.

**Space.** The three-person floor is generous for two and snug for three. An owner who used it mostly for two described it as noticeably roomier than the Anjan 2, while calling three occupants tight but doable. Backpacker's testers found width and headroom modest but length luxurious, which is typical of a tunnel with a 105 cm peak. The 15 sq ft vestibule holds packs and boots while leaving room to get in and out.

**Weight and pack size.** At 1,900 g, the Anjan 3 is not an ultralight tent by cuttingedge standards. Divided between two hikers it comes to roughly 950 g each, which is competitive for a double-wall shelter this sturdy. Carried alone, it is a different story, and solo backpackers on Reddit single out the weight as the reason to look elsewhere. The poles weigh around 340 g and fold to 43 cm, so they sit neatly inside most packs. An optional footprint adds about 320 g.

**Durability.** Kerlon is the headline. The fabric is thin to the touch, yet owners report miles of use without rips or holes, and the metal friction adjusters and non-absorbent Spectra guy lines hold up to long-term abuse. Hilleberg backs the tent with a lifetime warranty against manufacturing and material defects, and spare parts are available.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Stable in wind and heavy rain, with a low profile and four double-anchored guy points
- Pitches on four pegs, with fly and inner going up together
- Highly ventilated in warm weather, with a mesh door and a roll-up rear wall
- Roomy for two, with a 15 sq ft vestibule
- Kerlon fabric and lifetime warranty give a very long service life

**Cons**

- 1,900 g is heavy for solo use and for ultralight standards
- Expensive, at roughly €1,139 in Europe
- Venting suffers when the vestibule has to stay closed
- Single door and a tight fit for three adults
- Not freestanding, and not built to carry snow loads
- Some owners report splash-back and flapping beneath the raised fly

## Who Should Buy This

The Anjan 3 suits two hikers who want a spacious double-wall shelter that stays calm in exposed, windy terrain, and who plan to keep it for a decade or more. It also fits couples who split the weight and anyone who values fast, stake-light pitching on rough ground. Solo thru-hikers counting every ounce, three adults who want proper elbow room, and anyone planning snow camping should look elsewhere. The first group will find lighter shelters at a fraction of the cost. The other two should step up to Hilleberg's heavier all-season tents.

## Verdict

The Anjan 3 is a well-made, storm-capable shelter for two that sits between lightweight and bombproof. It costs far more than most comparable tents and weighs more than ultralight alternatives, so it only makes sense for hikers who will use that stability and durability. For couples on exposed three-season routes, it is a sound long-term purchase.
