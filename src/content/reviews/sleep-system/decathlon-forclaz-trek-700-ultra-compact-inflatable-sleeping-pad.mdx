## Overview

The Forclaz Trek 700 is Decathlon's entry-level inflatable pad for backpackers: a 2.2-inch-thick, mummy-shaped air mattress that rolls down to roughly the size of a large water bottle and sells for around $38 in the US. It is aimed at warm-season hikers who want a real cushion without paying cottage-industry prices. The trade-off is plain: it is heavier than the premium pads it imitates, and its insulation stops at R 1.6.

## Performance

**Comfort.** At 2.2 inches, the Trek 700 sits well above the ground, and its vertical baffles spread weight evenly. One reviewer at 75 kg described sleeping comfortably on it over varied terrain, and a long-term owner who switched from foam pads credited it with relieving back discomfort. Fully inflated, the pad is on the firm side. Terradrift's testers found they needed to let some air out to get the feel right, which the valve allows.

**Inflation.** This is where the Trek 700 stands out. The 2-way valve is large, and one tester counted about 15 breaths and under 45 seconds to fill the pad. The anti-return mechanism keeps air in between breaths. Terradrift judged it the quickest of three inflatables it compared. The catch is placement. Both valves sit on the underside, so topping off or bleeding air while lying on the pad means folding it back to find the valve.

**Noise and surface.** Testers note that the fabric stays quiet when a sleeper shifts, unlike the crinkle of many ultralight pads. The silicone patches on top grip a sleeping bag, and Terradrift reported that together with the mummy taper they kept the bag in place without side rails. Quilt users get a different result. A reviewer of the short version said the patches are better suited to bags than to quilts or blankets, and he found their top-side placement odd.

**Warmth.** An R-value of 1.6 puts this pad in summer territory. Owners and reviewers class it as a spring-to-autumn pad, and the "above 8°F" figure on retail listings should not be read as a guide to sleeping comfort. That number assumes a very warm bag and extra ground insulation. Buyers on Slickdeals made the same point: a warm bag does not make up for lost insulation underneath. Some stack it on a foam pad for car camping, where R-values add up, but that undoes any weight savings on the trail.

**Weight and packed size.** At 510 g (one tester measured 523 g), the Trek 700 is on the heavy side of ultralight. Reviewers have pointed out that pads around 300 g sell at similar prices. Decathlon's counter is the packed size, which is small for a full-length pad, and a two-year warranty backed by a large store network for repairs. Walmart shoppers also report that the pad can be tricky to fit back into its stuff sack.

**Durability.** One owner reported eight months of use without a puncture and described the material as thick. The pad should still go on a groundsheet or tent floor, as one tester advised against placing it directly on bare ground.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Inflates in under a minute, with an anti-return valve that holds air between breaths
- 2.2 inches of cushion with quiet fabric
- Packs to 3.9 in x 10.2 in
- Silicone patches keep a sleeping bag from sliding
- Around $38 at retail, with a 4.4-star average across 160 Walmart ratings
- Sold in Large, XL and short versions

**Cons**

- 510 g is heavy by ultralight standards
- R 1.6 limits it to warm nights
- Valves on the underside are awkward to adjust mid-night
- Firm when fully inflated, so it needs tuning
- Silicone patches get in the way for quilt users
- Narrow 20.5-inch width at the shoulder, with a taper toward the feet. A 5'7" Slickdeals buyer wished for the wider size

## Who Should Buy This

Summer and shoulder-season backpackers who want a cheap, comfortable, compact pad for weekend trips or a first thru-hike kit will get good value here. It also suits anyone who sleeps in a bag rather than a quilt and values a short inflation routine. Cold-weather hikers, quilt users and gram counters should look elsewhere. Anyone expecting temperatures near freezing needs a pad rated at R 3 or higher, and people shaving base weight can find lighter pads for similar money.

## Verdict

The Forclaz Trek 700 is a well-made budget air pad that does the basics well: it fills quickly, sleeps quietly and packs small. Its weight and thin insulation keep it from being a serious option for ultralight or cold-season use. For warm-weather trips at this price, it is an easy recommendation.
