## Overview

The Half Dome is the helmet that shows up at the base of every crag, on guide-service gear racks and in a lot of canyoneering duffels. It pairs a hard ABS outer shell with a co-molded EPS foam liner, adds a low-profile suspension with a one-handed fit dial, and costs $69.95. The trade-off is plain: it is a tougher, heavier helmet than the all-foam models ultralight climbers tend to covet, and it does not pretend otherwise.

## Performance

**Protection and durability.** The hard shell is the reason guides keep buying it. Reviewers who work with the helmet in programs describe years of use without cracking or showing wear after drops, and a climbing-school buyer cited how well it survives being banged around. Owners on REI and Black Diamond's own site credit it with taking rockfall hits, and one reviewer who rejected lighter options said the added protection was worth the small weight penalty. The helmet is rated to CE/EN and UIAA standards. As with any EPS-lined helmet, a significant impact means retirement.

**Fit.** The redesigned suspension is the main upgrade over older Half Domes. The rear dial adjusts with one hand, including over gloves or a thin beanie. Black Diamond's aggregated customer feedback lists the dial, the room for a hat and the comfort as the main praise. The complaints are consistent: some wearers find it rides high on the head, fit varies between sizes, and a few prefer the previous version's fit. One critical REI reviewer found it tiring over a long belay because the interior has no padding. A few owners also reported adjustment straps popping out of their slots.

**Headlamp clips.** Four clips hold a headlamp band firmly in place, and owners describe them as quick to use. They are one of the better arguments for the Half Dome on alpine starts and for cavers and canyoneers.

**Ventilation.** This is a middling area. Black Diamond's own summary of customer feedback notes that ventilation is limited in heat. A reviewer with thick hair said the same. Others call it adequate for cragging and belaying, so it suits moderate temperatures better than summer sun on a south face.

**Weight.** At 312 g in S/M and about 340 g in M/L by the catalog figures, the Half Dome sits well above the all-foam helmets that dominate ultralight kit lists. Black Diamond's current product page lists higher figures, 340 g and 356 g, so a kitchen scale may land above the catalog numbers. Either way, the gap to a foam helmet is meaningful for a thru-hiker clipping a helmet to a pack for a few alpine sections. The shell also resists the crushing and scuffing a pack inflicts on foam.

## Choosing a Version

The Half Dome comes in two sizes, S/M and M/L. The only substantive differences are head-circumference range and a weight difference of roughly 30 g. Black Diamond's size range for each is wide, and the dial covers a lot of that range, so measuring matters. Measure the largest part of the head, about an inch above the eyebrows. S/M suits smaller adults and teens. M/L fits most regular adult heads, with room for a beanie underneath, which canyoneers working cold water appreciate. Someone near the overlap, around 22–23 in, should try both, because owners report that fit isn't perfectly consistent between sizes. A separate women's version with a ponytail-friendly cradle exists, but it is a different product.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tough ABS shell that holds up to drops, pack abuse and rockfall
- One-handed dial that is simple to adjust and works over a beanie
- Four secure headlamp clips
- $69.95 with a two-year warranty
- CE/EN and UIAA certified

**Cons**

- Heavier than all-foam helmets
- Limited ventilation in hot weather
- Some wearers find it sits high; sizing is inconsistent
- No interior padding, which some find uncomfortable over long belays
- Retailer and manufacturer weights disagree by 25–45 g

## Who Should Buy This

The Half Dome is for climbers who want a certified helmet that survives years of abuse for under $75: gym-to-crag climbers, multi-pitch trad parties, canyoneers, cavers and instructors outfitting groups. It also suits hikers who need an occasional helmet for scrambles and want it to survive being strapped to a pack. Ultralight purists who count grams on alpine routes, or anyone climbing in sustained heat, should look at an all-foam or in-mold helmet with more venting.

## Verdict

The Half Dome is a durable, simple, fairly priced helmet with an easy fit and good headlamp clips. It does not win on weight or ventilation, and it is not trying to. Buy it as the dependable default, not as the lightest option.
