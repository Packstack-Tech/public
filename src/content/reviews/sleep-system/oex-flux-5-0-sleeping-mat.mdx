## Overview

The OEX Flux 5.0 is a budget inflatable sleeping mat for three-season camping, built from nylon ripstop with triangular baffles and sold with a drawstring pump bag. It is aimed at people stepping up from a foam pad or a bulky self-inflating mat, not at gram-counting thru-hikers. The trade-off is plain: it packs small and sleeps warm for the money, but at 540 g it is no featherweight, and its slick surface annoys some sleepers.

## Performance

**Warmth.** An R-value of 3 puts the Flux 5.0 in three-season territory: spring through early autumn, and shoulder-season nights where the ground is cool but not frozen. Owners back that up. One buyer who found the mat too narrow and too slick for side sleeping still noted that no cold came up through the ground. Another described it as a warm, comfortable mat for three-season use. It is not a winter pad, and the R-3 rating means it should not be the only barrier between a sleeper and frozen ground.

**Comfort.** At 5.5 cm thick and 181 cm long, the mat gives back sleepers a stable, cushioned platform, and the triangular baffle layout is meant to spread support evenly. The limits show up on the side. A reviewer who tried it as a side sleeper reported that it isn't very thick and that they felt like they were rolling off the edge during the night. A buyer with a bad back said they still needed to slide a small seat pad under their spine when lying on their back. That is a fair summary: adequate for most back and combination sleepers, marginal for side sleepers who need to keep their hips off the ground.

**Surface grip.** The most repeated complaint is slipperiness. Reviewers describe the mat sliding out from beneath them overnight, and one said they slid around inside their sleeping bag. The fix is cheap and familiar to anyone who has used a slick nylon pad: a few dabs of silicone sealant or grip tape on the top surface, or a sleeping bag with a pad-attachment system. Out of the box, though, the mat is a slider.

**Inflation and packing.** The included drawstring pump bag does the work of inflation without lung power, which keeps moisture out of the baffles and spares cold-morning breathlessness at altitude. Buyers call it easy to use. Packed, the mat measures 25 x 10 cm, roughly the size of a large water bottle, and owners single out how small it rolls up as a highlight.

**Weight.** At 540 g, the Flux 5.0 sits well above what premium inflatables deliver for similar warmth. For weekend trips, wild camping and shoulder-season hiking, that penalty is easy to absorb. For a long-distance base weight build, it is a clear compromise.

**Durability.** The ripstop nylon shell is marketed as tear- and abrasion-resistant, and owners report no widespread failures. Used listings turn up the occasional repaired puncture or leaking unit, which is normal for any inflatable. Carrying a patch kit is the sensible habit, and sleeping on a groundsheet or footprint helps.

**Value.** Value is the Flux 5.0's strongest card. Buyers describe it as much cheaper than competing brands and a reasonable first inflatable for someone starting out with wild camping. One owner said it performs as well as other mats they use without costing more. Its recommended retail price is £80, and it has been seen discounted well below that. Amazon UK shows an average of around 4.2 stars across 15 ratings.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- R-3 insulation covers three-season use
- Packs to 25 x 10 cm
- Pump bag included, so no lung inflation
- Tear- and abrasion-resistant nylon ripstop shell
- Low price compared with name-brand inflatables
- 181 cm length and 5.5 cm thickness suit back sleepers

**Cons**

- 540 g is heavy for an R-3 inflatable
- Slippery top surface; mats slide out from under sleepers
- Side sleepers report it feels thin and narrow
- Not suitable for winter use
- Brand has a limited track record with thru-hikers

## Who Should Buy This

The Flux 5.0 suits newcomers to inflatable pads, wild campers, festival-goers and weekend backpackers who want a warm, compact mat without paying premium prices. It also works as a spare or guest pad. Side sleepers, anyone building a sub-4 kg base weight, and anyone heading into sub-freezing conditions should look elsewhere: a thicker pad, a higher R-value, or a lighter mat will serve them better.

## Verdict

The OEX Flux 5.0 is a sensible budget buy that does the basics: it insulates adequately for three seasons, packs small and inflates without fuss. Its weight, slick surface and modest thickness keep it from being a serious thru-hiking pad. Buy it for casual trips and first outings; skip it if grams or side-sleeping comfort matter.
