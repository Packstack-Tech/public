## Overview

The Osprey Aura AG is a women's-specific backpacking pack built around AntiGravity suspension, a tensioned mesh that wraps from the shoulders across the back and into the hipbelt. It comes in 50L and 65L versions for weekend-to-week-long trips. It is not an ultralight pack. It's a full-featured comfort hauler that trades roughly 4.3 to 4.6 pounds of empty weight for fit, airflow and organization.

## Performance

### Suspension and comfort

The AntiGravity system is the reason the Aura has the reputation it does. The mesh is soft, stays under tension and conforms to the back, and it carries through into the hipbelt without a seam where pressure can build. Owners hauling 30 to 35 pounds describe a pack that seems to float. One camper at that weight said no other pack would have let them do it. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers rank the 65L among the most adjustable women's packs they've tested.

The system has a ceiling. Because the mesh holds the pack off the back, the load sits farther from the spine than it does in a conventional foam-panel design. That's the price of the airflow. Past about 40 pounds the carry gets less stable, and the 65L's 30–40 lb rating is honest. Hikers who routinely go heavier belong in Osprey's Ariel 65, which is rated to 60 lbs.

### Fit and adjustability

The torso length slides on a cam-lock system that takes seconds to change, so a trailside tweak doesn't mean unpacking. The Fit-on-the-Fly hipbelt extends and changes angle in small increments. Outdoor Gear Lab measured about three and a half inches of torso adjustment within each frame size.

The frame is rigid, though, and that makes the initial size choice matter. The harness can't make up for a shell that's too tall. One reviewer ended up with a pack that towered over her head even when empty. A shopper on the small end who bought an XS/S still reported the top of the pack hitting the back of her head. Reviewers also note the pack runs slightly large, and the hipbelt is stiff out of the box. It softens after a few shakedown miles.

### Ventilation

Ventilation is the other strength. The suspended panel, vented hipbelt and airy shoulder straps keep sweat down on long climbs, and a cross breeze can be felt moving between pack and back. No pack eliminates a sweaty back, but the Aura takes the edge off better than most.

### Organization

Both versions have a floating lid, a bottom sleeping bag compartment, a stretch front pocket, tall mesh side pockets and roomy hipbelt pockets that take a phone and snacks. The front mesh stretches for layers and stays flat when empty. Curved side zips on current models let hikers reach the main compartment without digging from the top, which older Aura generations lacked. The lid can come off to save a few ounces, with the integrated FlapJacket covering the opening. The rain cover is built in.

### Dynamic terrain

The top of the pack can shift when scrambling, crossing logs or bending over. It doesn't ruin the carry, but this is a suspension designed for sustained trail walking, not technical movement.

### Durability and weight

Owners generally report the pack holding up over years of use, and the lifetime guarantee covers repair or replacement. There are outliers: one owner of an older-generation Aura had a shoulder strap fail at 25 lbs. Outdoor Gear Lab weighed the 65L at 4.5 lb. Against the field, that is heavy. The ULA Ultra Circuit 68 weighs 2 lb 4.8 oz. Osprey's own Aura AG LT trims more than 10 percent of the weight by dropping some pockets and organization.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same suspension, harness and feature set. What changes is volume, load rating and a small weight penalty.

**Aura AG 50 (1,947 g).** The load range is 25–35 lbs, and the volume is 47L in XS/S and 50L in M/L. It suits weekend and 1–3 night trips, and the smaller shell keeps the center of mass lower and discourages overpacking. It's the better pick for a shorter torso, or for anyone whose kit is already compact.

**Aura AG 65 (2,086 g).** The load range steps up to 30–40 lbs, with 62L in XS/S and 65L in M/L. The extra 139 g buys room for bulky gear, a bear canister, cold-weather insulation or longer resupply carries. Choose it for week-long trips and for winter shoulder-season kit.

The weight gap between the two is small enough that it shouldn't drive the decision. The load a hiker expects to carry should. Someone who packs 35 pounds into a 50L is better served by the 65L. Someone who would only half-fill the 65L is carrying volume she doesn't need.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- AntiGravity suspension spreads load evenly and moves a lot of air
- Highly adjustable torso and hipbelt cover a wide range of body types
- Generous organization, including large hipbelt pockets and curved side zips
- Integrated rain cover and removable lid
- Recycled fabrics and a lifetime repair guarantee

**Cons**

- Heavy at 4.3–4.6 lbs, well above the current ultralight norm
- Comfort tapers past about 35 lbs (50L) or 40 lbs (65L)
- Top of the pack can sway on scrambles and uneven terrain
- Rigid frame makes the initial size choice critical
- Stiff hipbelt needs break-in time

## Who Should Buy This

The Aura AG is for women carrying real loads, 25 to 40 pounds, who want a pack that fits well and stays cool in warm weather. It also suits hikers with shoulder or back sensitivity who want the hips doing the work. Beginners get a forgiving, adjustable fit without much fuss. Thru-hikers and gram counters should look elsewhere: with a base weight under 12 lbs, the pack becomes a disproportionate share of what's on the back, and the Aura AG LT or a lighter frameless or framed design makes more sense. Those regularly above 40 lbs should step up to the Ariel.

## Verdict

The Aura AG delivers on its central promise: a breathable, finely adjustable carry that makes moderate loads feel lighter than they are. Weight is the cost, and it's worth paying for recreational backpackers who value comfort over ounces. Choose the 50L for weekends and the 65L for anything longer or bulkier, and get measured before ordering.
