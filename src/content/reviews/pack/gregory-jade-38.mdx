## Overview

The Gregory Jade 38 is a women's-specific framed pack built for overnights and short multi-day trips, with a pivoting FreeFloat hip belt, a ventilated mesh back panel and a front U-zip for digging into the main compartment. It earns its following on comfort and carrying ability rather than grams. At 1,260 g (about 44 oz), it sits well above the weights thru-hikers chasing a sub-10-pound base weight tend to accept.

## Performance

**Comfort and carry.** The Jade's reputation rests on the hip belt. Gregory's FreeFloat flex panels let the belt pivot with the hiker's stride rather than fight it, and the belt itself is a women's-specific 3D-molded design with dual-density foam. Owners on retail sites describe a pack that carries well on hips and lower back; one reviewer with lower-back pain called the fit a clear win, and another switched from an Osprey Tempest 34 after trying it. A travel blogger reviewing the pack against the Osprey Ariel called it the most comfortable bag tried in store. The shoulder harness is die-cut for a woman's chest, with load lifters that let wearers shift weight between shoulders and hips.

**Load capacity.** Gregory rates the pack to 16 kg. Reddit commentary on the Jade line is broadly positive, with owners saying it handles weight well and reduces shoulder strain, though the community's sweet spot for the pack is closer to 20 lb or less. That is the right framing: the suspension is more than the 38-liter body needs, which suits a hiker who carries a heavy bear canister, a winter sleeping bag or a camera kit, and is overbuilt for someone with a 10-pound base weight.

**Ventilation.** The open-air mesh back panel holds the pack off the spine, and reviewers cite it as a real advantage in hot weather. One long-term user wrote it up as a good tropical-climate pack. The trade-off is standard for tensioned-mesh designs: the load rides a little farther from the back than on a flush-fitting pack, and the profile is slightly bulkier on technical terrain.

**Access and organization.** The front U-zip opens the main compartment like a suitcase, which makes camp unpacking easy and ends the dig-to-the-bottom routine for a sleeping bag or stove. Stretch side pockets are deep enough to reach a water bottle without taking the pack off, per an owner's review. The hip belt pockets take snacks and a phone, and the shoulder harness has a sunglasses quickstow, a hydration clip and a sternum strap with an integrated whistle. A rain cover is included at 68 g. The pack accepts a hydration reservoir, which is sold separately.

**Durability.** Reviewers describe the construction as solid, with sturdy zippers and no stitching pull. One reviewer used it on a roughly 500-mile trek in Spain with no reported problems. Minor complaints on Reddit involve small components such as cords and clips, not structural failures. One owner reported carrying the same Jade 38 for nearly two decades.

**Weight.** This is the pack's weak point for the Packstack audience. At 1,260 g with a rain cover that adds 68 g, it weighs more than frameless and light-frame packs of similar volume by several hundred grams. The weight pays for the frame, the padding and the ventilation, and whether that trade is worthwhile depends on what goes inside.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Women's-specific hip belt and shoulder harness, with a 16–20 in torso range and a hip range up to 46 in
- FreeFloat belt moves with the hiker's stride
- Ventilated mesh back panel suits hot climates
- Front U-zip makes camp unpacking easy
- Rain cover included
- Reports of long service life

**Cons**

- Heavy for the volume at 1,260 g
- Mesh suspension pushes the load away from the back
- Rated to 16 kg, but best felt in the mid-teens of pounds, not at the limit
- Small hardware items such as cords have failed for some owners

## Who Should Buy This

The Jade 38 suits women who want a comfortable, well-ventilated pack for weekend trips, hut-to-hut routes or travel, and who carry enough weight (say 15–30 lb) that a real frame and padded belt earn their keep. It also works as a carry-on-friendly travel pack if the top lid stays light. Hikers who have already cut their base weight below 10 lb, or those who want a minimalist frameless pack for long-distance thru-hiking, should look at lighter 35–40 L packs instead.

## Verdict

The Jade 38 is a comfort-first pack with a deserved following among women who find unisex packs ride poorly on their frames. It is not a thru-hiker's tool, and at 1,260 g, it asks for a payload that justifies the frame. For weekenders and moderate-load backpackers who prioritize fit, it is a sound buy.
