## Overview

The Iceline is a minimalist carbon trekking pole from Durston Gear, built to the Canadian brand's spec by Austrian maker Komperdell. At 289 g (10.2 oz) a pair, it sits at the very light end of adjustable poles, and testers say it is stiffer and tougher than that number suggests. The trade-off is a stripped-down feature set: no baskets, no extended grip, and a lower section that detaches entirely.

## Performance

Stiffness is what reviewers mention first. Backpacker's testers spent about six months on the Iceline in Hells Canyon and the White Mountains, plus some ski tours, and found less flex than nearly any telescoping pole they had used, even when leaning hard on steep descents. Most of them counted that as a plus. One long-distance user who logged more than 1,000 km on the Tour du Mont Blanc and the Walker's Haute Route described the effect as being propelled forward on each plant. Switching back to aluminum poles, that user said, mostly brought vibration.

The durability reports are unusual for a carbon pole this light. Part of the reason is the thick 16 mm lower section and the aluminum hardware at the joint. Better Trail's tester jammed the poles into crusty snow and wedged them between rocks on a Montana elk hunt, and judged them to outperform carbon poles of similar weight. CleverHiker's testers, who took them through snow and brush in Arizona's Chiricahua Mountains, saw less bending than in other ultralight carbon poles. They also cautioned that carbon will always be more likely to snap than aluminum. That holds here too.

The design is a hybrid. The upper section telescopes with a single external flick-lock, and the lower section clicks on through an aluminum quick-connect joint. A button releases it. A Rokslide user who tried the poles on a short rock-slide hike liked the snap-together lower section and found it simple and solid. The same user wondered whether grit could eventually jam the release button, but that was a hypothetical concern, not a reported failure. The other cost is deployment speed. Because the poles come apart into two loose pieces, they are slower to set up than a Z-fold pole, and there is more to lose. Better Trail's testers also found they pack less compactly than folding models.

The wide adjustment range suits trekking-pole shelters. The stiff shafts are a good match for tarps and tents, and reviewers who used them to pitch in a storm reported no trouble. Better Trail's tester described the EVA foam grip as basic but well contoured, with etched slits that kept a hold when hands got sweaty. The grip has no extension, so steep sidehilling means choking up on the shaft. The mesh wrist straps are an optional add-on at roughly $10. They are thin and breathable, but not plush, and CleverHiker flagged them as less comfortable than those on other poles.

Baskets are not included. That leaves the Iceline without a snow solution, which matters for shoulder-season routes. The included Dyneema stuff sack weighs 5 g and is cut from scrap fabric left over from Durston's Pro-series packs.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 289 g a pair is among the lightest adjustable carbon poles available
- Very stiff, with little flex under load
- Thick 16 mm lower shaft and aluminum hardware hold up better than most ultralight carbon
- 95–127 cm range works for hiking and trekking-pole shelters
- Carbide tips and a 5 g Dyneema stuff sack are included
- Made by Komperdell, a century-old pole maker

**Cons**

- No baskets in the box, and no extended grip
- Lower section separates completely, so it is slower to deploy and easy to misplace
- Packs longer than folding poles
- Carbon remains vulnerable to a hard lateral impact
- Straps cost extra and are not padded

## Who Should Buy This

The Iceline suits thru-hikers and gram-counters who want a stiff pole that survives rock and brush, and anyone who pitches a trekking-pole shelter. Hikers who want a pole they can deploy mid-stride, one that folds down to pack size, or one with a long foam grip for sidehilling should look at Z-fold poles instead. Winter and snowshoe users will have to buy baskets elsewhere. Anyone who wants plush straps should plan on swapping them.

## Verdict

The Iceline is a carefully engineered ultralight pole that gives up convenience in exchange for stiffness and strength at 289 g. At $179, it is a sound purchase for anyone who counts grams and abuses their gear. Hikers who want folding speed or a long feature list should buy something else.
