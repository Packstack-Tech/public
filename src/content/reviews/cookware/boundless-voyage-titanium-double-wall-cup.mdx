## Overview

The Boundless Voyage Titanium Double-Wall Cup (model Ti3027D in the 120 ml size) is a thimble-sized titanium vessel for espresso, tea and small servings of anything hot or cold. At 42 g it weighs about 1.5 oz, and the double wall keeps the outer surface comfortable to hold. The trade-off is capacity. At 4 fl oz it is a sipping cup, not a mug you can cook in or build a full breakfast around.

## Performance

**Weight and size.** At 60 mm wide and 58 mm tall, the cup is roughly the footprint of a shot glass. It disappears into a pot or a side pocket. The maker also sells it in larger sizes up to 450 ml, and those larger cups are the ones designed to stack, so this size is best read as an accessory to a main cook pot rather than a replacement for one.

**Heat handling.** The cup uses a double wall with an air gap rather than a vacuum seal, and the maker says so plainly. Titanium conducts heat poorly compared with aluminum or steel. Add a second wall and the outside of the cup stays easier to hold than a single-wall titanium cup filled with boiling water. That is the anti-scalding claim, and it is a fair one for a cup this small. It is not vacuum-flask insulation, though. Expect a modest edge in how long a drink stays warm, not hours of heat retention. A 4 oz pour also loses heat fast no matter what the cup is made of.

**What it can't do.** This is a drinking vessel, not a cook pot. The maker sells a separate single-wall line that it describes as direct-heat compatible, which points to the double-wall version being meant for pouring into, not for putting on a flame. Boil water in a pot, then pour. The small volume also rules out instant oatmeal, a freeze-dried dinner or a full cup of coffee. On a cold morning, a 120 ml refill runs out in a few swallows.

**Taste and durability.** Titanium is inert, so it adds no metallic taste to tea or coffee. The material doesn't rust or dent easily, and the cup carries no coatings that could wear off. The maker lists it as hand-washable and machine-washable. For a cup at this price, the seamless inner wall is a welcome detail because it leaves no crevice to trap coffee grounds or tea residue.

**Where it fits.** The 120 ml size suits three kinds of use: a trail espresso routine, gongfu-style tea tasting, and a small cup for sharing a dram or a sip of broth. Hikers who split a pot between two people can also use it as a second cup without carrying a full mug.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 42 g, which is lighter than most plastic or steel cups of any size
- Double wall keeps the outside comfortable for hot drinks
- Grade 1 titanium, so no taste, no corrosion, no coating to chip
- Compact 60 × 58 mm footprint that tucks inside most cook pots
- Around $17–19, cheap for titanium

**Cons**

- 120 ml is too small for use as a main trail mug
- Non-vacuum construction offers only modest heat retention
- Not intended for direct flame; a separate single-wall cup is the cookware option
- No handle on the standard model, so the rim is the only place to grip it
- Heavier than a single-wall titanium cup of the same size, which saves grams

## Who Should Buy This

Buy this if you carry a stove-top espresso setup, drink tea in small servings, or want a second cup for a hiking partner that weighs less than a snack bar. It also suits hikers who already own a titanium pot and want a cool-to-the-touch drinking cup that nests inside it.

Look elsewhere if you need one cup for everything. A solo thru-hiker who wants to boil water, rehydrate meals and drink from the same vessel should buy a 400–450 ml single-wall titanium mug and skip the double wall. It saves weight and works over a flame.

## Verdict

The Boundless Voyage 120 ml double-wall cup is a well-made, inexpensive piece of titanium for people who want a small, comfortable cup for hot drinks. It does that job well, but its size means it is a supplement to a cook kit and not a substitute for one. It is a good buy for the coffee and tea crowd, and a poor one for everyone else.
