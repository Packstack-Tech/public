## Overview

The Renegade is DMM's do-everything harness. It has a floating, padded waistbelt, adjustable leg loops, seven gear loops and slots for four Vault ice-screw clippers. It's aimed at climbers who want one harness for a Welsh sea cliff in July, a frozen gully in January and a long alpine ridge in between. The trade-off is that it isn't a minimalist. At 415 g in a medium, it carries more structure and more kit-hauling capacity than a sport-day harness, and it only has as much padding as DMM thought a mixed-use harness needed.

## Performance

**Fit and layering.** The Renegade's main selling point is how much it adjusts. Slide Lock buckles on the waist and both leg loops let the harness go over a base layer at the crag or a belay jacket on a winter stance. The waistbelt is free-floating: the padding isn't stitched in place and slides along the webbing. Reviewers at Mountain Misadventures call this a standout feature. It lets a climber centre the belay loop and tie-in points however tight or loose the belt is set. A UKHillwalking forum user who sat down with the harness to check the claim confirmed the whole assembly can be slid around to even up the gear loops.

**Comfort.** Owners generally rate it as a harness that can be worn all day. A UKC reviewer who had used it for four years said he'd worn it more than any other harness he'd owned, and it still looked good. The internal load-spreading plate is meant to take the sting out of hanging belays and long days. Not everyone agrees. One Mountain Project poster found the waist uncomfortable on hanging belays and rappels, and dropped it in favour of something else. Mountain Misadventures notes that the padding is thinner than on some competing harnesses, particularly on the leg loops. The Renegade is a good choice for most all-day mountain use. It is less plush than the heavily padded harnesses that big-wall and route-working climbers favour.

**Racking.** Seven gear loops is the headline number, and the rear loop keeps belay kit away from the main rack. The four ice-clipper slots mean a full set of screws doesn't have to share space with cams. The updated version has a narrower belay loop for better compatibility with small belay karabiners. DMM also revised the racking system to work with both summer and winter racks. The loops are the most debated feature. They sit flat against the harness rather than sticking out. One UKH forum user hated them, reporting that gear bunched and was awkward to unclip over multiple winter layers. He kept the Renegade as an indoor training harness and went back to an older Petzl for real routes. Others find the loop count and spacing more than make up for it.

**Weight.** At 415 g (M), this isn't an ultralight alpine harness. It's a sensible weight for what it does. Climbers who want a harness for a single long multi-pitch day, with a glacier approach and no ice, can save weight with a stripped-down model. Those who also climb in winter will want the Renegade's racking and the adjustment range.

**Durability.** The Renegade's reputation is for longevity. One reviewer at Climbers Shop used it for 1.5 years of multipitches, alpine ascents and ghyll scrambles and said he could not recommend it highly enough. That's a single owner's report, but it's consistent with the harness's wider reputation as a workhorse.

## Choosing a Version

The three sizes differ in fit range and weight. Small covers waists of 64–79 cm and legs of 45–58 cm. Medium covers 75–91 cm waists and 50–63 cm legs. Large covers 87–104 cm waists and 55–70 cm legs. Weight rises by about 20–35 g from one size to the next.

The sizing overlaps, so check where you fall. Climbers near the top of a size's waist range should be careful. A UKH forum user with a 34-inch (about 86 cm) waist found the medium a little tight over a jacket and swapped for a large. He then found the large too loose in summer clothing, so he had to re-route the strap through the guides. Those in the overlap zones should size to the thickest clothing they will wear most. The slide-lock waist has room to tighten for summer, but not to expand over a puffy.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Floating waistbelt centres padding and gear loops at any adjustment
- Fully adjustable waist and legs fit over thin or thick layers
- Seven gear loops plus four ice-clipper slots cover summer and winter racks
- Internal load-spreading plate for hanging belays
- Slide Lock buckles are quick to adjust
- Strong reputation for durability

**Cons**

- Gear loops lie flat and can bunch gear, especially over bulky layers
- Padding, particularly on the leg loops, is thinner than on some rivals
- At 395–450 g, it's heavier than a minimalist alpine harness
- Vault ice clippers are not included
- Some climbers find the waist uncomfortable when hanging for long periods

## Who Should Buy This

Climbers who want a single harness for trad, winter climbing and mountaineering. It suits those who change clothing layers through the year, and who value a large rack and adjustment range over gram-counting. Gym-and-crag climbers will find it comfortable enough.

Climbers who spend long hours hanging in a harness, such as sport projectors and big-wall aid climbers, should look at something more heavily padded. Alpinists chasing the lightest possible kit should look at a slimmer, lighter design. Anyone who dislikes flat, non-protruding gear loops should try the harness on with a loaded rack before buying.

## Verdict

The Renegade is a well-sorted all-rounder. Its floating waistbelt, wide adjustment and four-slot ice racking make it the harness to buy if one harness has to cover every season. It isn't the lightest or the plushest option, but it covers more ground than most.
