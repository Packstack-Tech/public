## Overview

The knife in Sea to Summit's Frontier Ultralight line weighs 9 g and is made of hard-anodized aircraft-grade aluminum. It is for backpackers who want a real table knife on the trail without paying the weight of a folding blade or the price of titanium. The trade-off is the metal itself. Aluminum keeps the weight down, but it gives up the stiffness and abuse tolerance of titanium.

## Performance

**Weight and carry.** Nine grams is roughly the weight of two sugar packets. Sea to Summit sells the knife as part of the Frontier cutlery family. The two-piece spork-and-knife set is listed at about 19 g, and the three-piece knife, fork and spoon set at 25–26 g. A mini carabiner ties the pieces together and gives a clip point for a pack strap or a pot bag. Owners mention the carabiner often, mostly because loose utensils are easy to lose in a tent.

**Handle design.** The handle is the main design idea. Its profile adds stiffness to what would otherwise be a thin strip of aluminum, and it lets the knife nest against the other Frontier utensils. Packed, the three-piece set measures 6.6 x 1.7 x 0.8 in. It slides into a pot, a bowl or a side pocket without taking up room.

**Cutting.** This is a camp knife, not a bushcraft tool. Reviewers describe it as sharper than they expected for a utensil this light, and it handles cheese, summit sausage, tortillas and the occasional avocado without trouble. It will not do much with a frozen block of salami or a firm apple, and a 9 g aluminum handle is not meant to be leaned on. For cold-soaking, freeze-dried meals and simple trail lunches, it does the job.

**Durability and finish.** Hard anodizing forms a protective shell over the aluminum and keeps the base metal from leaching into food. Sea to Summit lists the material as free of BPA, PFOA and PTFE. Customer reviews at big retailers generally call the set sturdy and easy to clean, though a minority report that aluminum bends more readily than they would like. Because the pieces are light and thin, that complaint is plausible when they are used as pry tools or on frozen food. Anodized pieces should not go in the dishwasher, so hand washing is required. Over time the finish can scratch, which is cosmetic and typical of anodized aluminum.

**Versus titanium.** The Frontier line is often lighter than comparable titanium cutlery. Titanium is stronger, however, and it carries a higher price. A Frontier two-piece set typically sells for about $21 and the three-piece for about $35, depending on the retailer.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 9 g knife weight is difficult to beat in a full-size utensil
- Ribbed handle adds stiffness and lets the pieces stack with the rest of the Frontier line
- Mini carabiner keeps the set together and clips to a pack
- Hard-anodized finish is easy to clean and does not leach metal into food
- Lifetime guarantee from Sea to Summit

**Cons**

- Aluminum bends more readily than titanium
- Hand wash only; the finish can scratch with use
- Not a tool for heavy cutting
- Best value comes from buying it as part of a set rather than on its own

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend and thru-hikers who cook freeze-dried meals, cold-soak, or build simple dinners and want a proper knife next to their spork will get the most from it. It also suits gram-counters who already carry a Frontier spork or spoon and want a matching, nesting knife. Hikers who carve, process fish or game, or cut hard foods daily should carry a small folding knife with a locking blade. Anyone who treats utensils roughly and wants a piece that never bends should pay for titanium.

## Verdict

The Frontier Ultralight knife is a light, tidy and well-thought-out camp utensil that fits neatly into the rest of the Frontier line. Its aluminum construction limits how hard it can be pushed, but for the cutting most backpackers do at camp, it is enough. It is a good choice for ultralight cooks who want something better than a plastic knife.
