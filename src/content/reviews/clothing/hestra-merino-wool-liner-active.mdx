## Overview

The Merino Wool Liner Active is Hestra's thin knit liner glove, a 70% merino, 30% polyamide jersey that weighs 50 g. It is built to slip under a shell glove or mitt and add a little warmth for almost no weight or bulk, and it can double as a standalone glove on calm, cool days. The trade-off is built into the design: it is a minimal-warmth, high-dexterity layer, and Hestra's own scale rates its durability as modest.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Hestra rates this liner 2 out of 8 for warmth and describes it as minimal lining for cool conditions or high-exertion use. Owners' accounts match that. Reviewers at European retailers describe it as an effective base layer under winter gloves, and some wear it alone on calm days. It takes the edge off a cold morning start. It won't carry a hiker through a freezing, windy ridge without a proper outer glove over it. Merino's usual advantage applies here: the wool keeps insulating when damp, which matters on a day of sweaty climbing followed by a wet descent.

**Dexterity.** Mobility is the category where this liner scores highest, 8 out of 8 on Hestra's scale. The jersey is thin and stretchy enough to behave like a second skin, so stuffing tent stakes, working zippers and handling a stove are all possible with the liner on. Buyers repeatedly mention the movement as a plus, and several note the fit is trim enough to disappear inside a lined glove. Sizing runs snug. One reviewer with a thin hand found a size down fit better than the size they expected, so sizing by measurement is wiser than by guess.

**Moisture and drying.** Customer reviews across European retailers frequently call out quick drying, which fits with the wool-synthetic blend. Hestra's pitch is that the merino stays warm when damp while the polyamide adds hardwearing strength. The liner can go in a washing machine, which is rare enough among merino liners that it counts as a real convenience on multi-week trips where hand-washing gloves is a chore.

**Durability.** This is the weak point on paper. Hestra rates durability 2 out of 8 and says the liner suits gentle everyday wear with minimal abrasion. The polyamide makes it more hardwearing than Hestra's 100% merino liner, which is thin and fine, and retailers describe it as somewhat more durable than that one. That is a relative improvement, not a promise of longevity. Worn as a bare glove with trekking poles, scrambling or pack straps, a thin jersey like this will wear at the fingertips and palm first. Under another glove, where the shell takes the abuse, it should last far longer.

**Features.** The pull loop at the cuff helps get a snug liner on over sweaty hands. There is no palm grip print or reinforcement, and Hestra sells separate Touch Point liners for people who want touchscreen function. For phone use, this isn't the liner.

**Weight and packing.** At 50 g it is easy to justify as a "just in case" item. Retailers pitch it as something to keep in a pocket in case the weather turns, and that's a fair description of its best use for three-season hikers.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 50 g with almost no packed volume
- Top-of-scale mobility (8/8); fingers retain feel
- Polyamide content makes it sturdier than Hestra's 100% merino liner
- Machine washable; ZQ-certified wool
- Pull loop at the cuff makes on-off easier
- Owners rate it highly, with scores around 4.8 out of 5 at European retailers

**Cons**

- Only 2/8 for warmth: a modest boost, not a stand-alone cold-weather glove
- Durability is also 2/8, so it's a poor bare-hand glove for rough use
- No grip print and no touchscreen function
- Snug cut can frustrate people between sizes
- Priced at about $40 list, which is steep for a glove that can wear out

## Who Should Buy This

This liner suits three-season and shoulder-season backpackers who already own a shell glove or mitt and want to extend its temperature range for under 2 oz. It also suits cold-start hikers who need a light glove for the first hour and then stash it in a pocket, and anyone running a layered system of liner, shell and mitt. People who plan to wear it as their only glove for hard use, who need to operate a phone constantly, or who expect real warmth in winter conditions should look at a thicker fleece or wool glove, or one of Hestra's Touch Point liners.

## Verdict

The Merino Wool Liner Active does what a liner should: it adds a small, reliable layer of warmth without costing dexterity, weighs next to nothing and goes in the wash. Treat it as an insulating layer under a real glove rather than a glove in its own right, and it earns a place in a pack.
