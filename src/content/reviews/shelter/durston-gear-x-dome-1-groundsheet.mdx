## Overview

The X-Dome 1+ Groundsheet is a factory-cut polyester footprint built to match the floor of Durston's freestanding solo tent, and it clips to the four floor corners so it travels with the shelter. It's for X-Dome owners who camp on gravel, rock or damp duff and would rather not improvise with a piece of Tyvek. The trade-off is simple: $49 buys a nicely made 105 g accessory for a tent whose floor is already tougher than most.

## Performance

**Fit and setup.** The groundsheet is cut to the floor's outline, which is the whole point. There's no oversized rectangle of sil-nylon to fold, tuck or trim, and no overhanging edge to funnel rain under the floor. Durston's instructions are brief: logo side up, slide it beneath the floor, clip the corners. The company suggests leaving it attached in normal use and detaching it only when drying the tent, which keeps pitching a single motion. Because the X-Dome is freestanding, the sheet can also stay clipped in while the tent is shaken out and repacked, so there's no separate item to lose at a trailhead.

One owner who tested an X-Dome 1+ in the UK, writing on the Bushcraft UK forum, ran into a fit quirk on the first pitch. With the groundsheet clipped to the pole ends, a large section of the inner floor was left uncovered. Once the sheet was unclipped and the tent re-pitched more taut, he clipped it to the inner's straps leading to the pole docking points instead, and the coverage problem went away. The lesson for new owners is to spend a few minutes learning where the clips belong, then check coverage after the pole tension is set.

**Protection.** The 20D silicone/PE polyester is thin, but it has a respectable 3,500 mm waterproof rating, which is more than enough for a sheet that sits under a floor rather than overhead. Owner feedback on Durston's groundsheet line is consistently positive. A buyer of the X-Mid 2 version, posting on a retailer's page, reported that the sheet survived a rough campsite without holes even after the ground had been cleared, and said the polyester had initially made them worry about durability. Across the 342 reviews on Durston's own page, ratings skew heavily toward the top of the scale. The sheet's purpose is to catch abrasion, grit and the occasional stray stick, not to make bad sites safe for a tent floor.

**Construction.** This is where the sheet departs from a typical footprint. Durston says its groundsheets are fully double stitched, with double-folded edges that hide the raw fabric and generous bartacking at every connection point. That's tent-grade finishing on a 105 g accessory, and the company is open about its being more than strictly necessary. In practice, the clip points are where a groundsheet usually fails, and reinforcing them is the right place to spend thread.

**Weight and packing.** At 105 g, the sheet weighs about the same as Durston's X-Mid 1 groundsheet and a little under the 145 g X-Dome 2 version. It's about 10 percent of the weight of the X-Dome 1+ itself, which Durston lists at 985 g. Bikepacking.com's testers weighed the fly at 408 g and the inner at 301 g. The sheet is thin enough to fold flat against the inner in the stuff sack.

**Coverage limits.** The sheet matches the floor and stops there. The vestibule is bare ground, so wet boots, a muddy pack or a stove stay on whatever the site provides. Bikepacking.com's reviewers found a spare groundsheet handy for the extra coverage in the vestibule, which means some hikers will end up carrying a second piece of ground cover anyway.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Cut to the exact shape of the X-Dome 1+ floor, with no trimming or overhang
- 105 g (3.7 oz), light for a sheet this well finished
- Double stitching, folded edges and bartacked clip points that should outlast a DIY footprint
- Clips to the tent and can stay attached through pitching and packing
- 3,500 mm waterproof rating handles wet ground and puddling

**Cons**

- $49 is a lot for a sheet that a Tyvek or polycryo cutout can mostly replace for a fraction of the weight and cost
- No vestibule coverage
- Clip placement takes some learning, as at least one early owner discovered
- Stock has been intermittent; Durston has listed it as awaiting a mid-October restock

## Who Should Buy This

X-Dome 1+ and X-Dome Pro 1+ owners who regularly camp on gravel, rock, roots or abrasive soil, and who want the protection integrated rather than improvised. It also suits anyone who wants a cleaner floor to pack away, or who sells and resells gear and wants the tent to keep its condition. Hikers who camp mostly on forest duff and already carry a cut-down piece of polycryo, or who guard their pack weight to the gram and are comfortable inspecting their pitch, can skip it. So can anyone who needs a groundsheet that also covers the vestibule.

## Verdict

This is a well-made, properly fitted accessory that does one job and does it neatly. It isn't the cheapest or lightest way to protect an X-Dome floor, but it's the most convenient, and the construction quality matches the tent it's built for. Buy it if you want a no-fuss, permanently attached floor guard; otherwise a $5 cutout will do the same work.
