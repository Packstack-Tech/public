## Overview

The Light My Fire Titanium Spork is the metal answer to one of the most common failures in a backpacking kit: the plastic spork that snaps in a cold-soaked jar or a deep pot. It fuses a deep spoon, a four-tine fork and a serrated edge into a single 19–20 g piece of polished titanium designed in Sweden. The trade-off is a utensil that is nearly impossible to break but also compact, a bit stubby for freezer-bag meals, and priced well above the plastic originals it replaces.

## Performance

The case for this spork starts with what happens to its plastic sibling. One Trailspace reviewer reported that three of four plastic Light My Fire sporks eventually broke. The same reviewer's titanium replacement showed no wear, weakness or damage after more than a hundred uses over nine months. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers gave it a 9 out of 10 for durability. The scorecard summed up the appeal in one line: the titanium version is better than the plastic because it simply does not break.

Eating is the more nuanced part. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers praised the large, deep bowl, which handles soup and oatmeal well. They also found the fork tines sharp enough to spear chunks of meat. The double-ended layout is a mixed blessing. Some users like the versatility, and others find the shape awkward in the hand. Testers also noted that hygiene-minded eaters tend to commit to one end per meal. The overall eating score was a middling 6 out of 10, and the spork ranked fifth of ten in that test.

The serrated edge is a convenience, not a knife. Testers described the "knife" claim as a stretch. It will saw through a soft tortilla or a cooked potato, but owners who plan to eat cheese, salami or steak still pack a small blade. One scout-troop leader writing on Trailspace made the same point about thick cuts of meat.

Length is the recurring complaint. At 170 mm, the spork reaches the bottom of a mug or a short pot, but long-handled spoons are better suited to rehydrating meals in tall freezer bags and food pouches. Reviewers with large hands also found it small. One reviewer wished it were a little longer, and another found it a touch long for tidy packing. Light My Fire's plastic range includes longer sizes, but no longer titanium version has been offered.

The finish is the pleasant surprise. The titanium is polished smooth, which reviewers say feels better in the mouth than the grainy plastic. It is also easy to wipe clean, and it carries no metallic taste. It tolerates boiling water and fire, and it goes in the dishwasher. Titanium also won't corrode, so it can ride in a damp pot or a wet pocket without trouble.

## Choosing a Version

The two listings, 19 g and 20 g, describe the same spork. Both are 170 mm long and share the same polished titanium construction and the same spoon-fork-serrated-edge layout. The difference is one gram, about 0.04 oz. That is far too small to affect a pack weight or a base-weight spreadsheet. Buy whichever is in stock at the better price.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Titanium won't snap, bend or crack the way plastic sporks do
- 19–20 g, light enough to ignore in a pack
- Deep spoon bowl holds a generous bite of soup or oatmeal
- Polished finish is smooth and tasteless, and it wipes clean easily
- Dishwasher safe, non-corrosive and hypoallergenic
- Handles boiling water and fire without melting

**Cons**

- The serrated edge is weak and doesn't replace a knife
- 170 mm is short for tall freezer bags and deep food pouches
- The double-ended shape divides users, and some find it awkward to hold
- Costs more than most utensils, and a four-pack of plastic ones is cheaper
- Dedicated titanium long-handled spoons are lighter and reach deeper

## Who Should Buy This

This spork suits hikers who want a single utensil that survives years of abuse and will never go through the break-and-replace cycle of plastic. It also suits anyone who eats from a pot or mug, cooks real meals on trail, or wants one durable set for both camping and everyday use. Hikers who live on freezer-bag meals or cold-soaked jars in tall containers should look at a long-handled titanium spoon instead. Anyone who wants the lowest possible weight should do the same, since a plain spoon undercuts this by several grams.

## Verdict

The Light My Fire Titanium Spork delivers on its main promise: a utensil that lasts and works for most meals. Its limits are modest. The length is short, and the knife edge is mostly decoration. For pot-and-mug cooks, it's a one-time purchase that earns its price.
