## Overview

Gilde Turmat Biffgryte is a freeze-dried beef stew from Nortura's Gilde brand, packed in a vacuum-sealed pouch that doubles as the cooking vessel. It is aimed at Norwegian weekend hikers, hunters and hut-to-hut walkers rather than ultralight gram-counters, and the trade-off is plain: a mild, family-friendly stew that is available at an ordinary supermarket, in exchange for flavor that plays it safe.

## Performance

**Preparation.** The routine is the standard one for this kind of meal. Boiling water goes into the pouch up to the fill line, then a stir, a sealed bag and a wait of roughly 8–10 minutes. No pot is needed, so cleanup is a rinse-free fold of the empty pouch. One tip from Norwegian hikers who tried the first Gilde Turmat meals: the fill line is a small marking along one side of the back panel, not the white band that wraps the pouch. It is easy to miss with a hungry crowd around the stove.

**Flavor and ingredients.** Gilde has said it designed the range to be mild and suited to all ages, and the Biffgryte follows that brief. The recipe reads like a home-cooked Norwegian stew: potato leads the ingredient list, followed by beef at 17%, mushrooms, carrot, onion, lentils, celery root and parsley root, rounded out with garlic, leek, beef extract and red wine extract. There is no heat and no spice-rack ambition. That suits cold evenings and mixed groups, and it will bore anyone who wants the punch of a curry or taco meal.

**Origin and dependability.** The beef comes from Nortura's Norwegian farmer-owners, and the pouches are freeze-dried and packed at the Rondane Produksjon plant in Otta, at the foot of the Rondane mountains. Gilde developed the range with the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT), which stocks it at some of its self-service cabins. The series launched in 2021 as a domestic rival to Real Turmat, the better-known Norwegian freeze-dried brand, and owners at the time noted that Gilde's pouches were cheaper and could be bought at the neighborhood Coop rather than ordered online.

**Weight and packability.** At 100 g dry, the pouch is among the lighter single-serving dinners in Gilde's own range, and it flattens easily in a pack. Real Turmat's comparable beef stew weighs 128 g dry, though the two are not a like-for-like swap in portion size. The vacuum seal means no repackaging for most trips.

**Dietary profile.** The product is marketed as gluten-, soy-, egg-, lactose- and dairy-free, which makes it one of the few mainstream supermarket trail meals that works for hikers avoiding several of those at once. Celery is the allergen declared on the label. The ingredient list also includes a rapeseed, sunflower and soybean oil blend, so anyone with a serious soy allergy should read the pouch itself before packing it.

**Texture.** Reviews of the Gilde line are thinner than for Real Turmat, and the earliest hands-on write-up, of the Pasta Bolognese, found the texture softer than expected, with the flavor acceptable and the portion filling. The stew format, with potato and chunked vegetables, is a more forgiving fit for freeze-drying than pasta.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 100 g dry weight keeps dinner light and compact
- Mild, familiar beef-and-potato recipe that suits children and mixed groups
- Free from gluten, soy, egg, lactose and dairy per the manufacturer's labeling
- Eaten from the pouch, so no pot to clean
- Sold in Norwegian supermarkets and some DNT cabins, generally cheaper than Real Turmat
- Up to 2 years of shelf life

**Cons**

- Mild flavor with little punch
- Shorter shelf life than Real Turmat's five years
- Hard to find outside Norway
- Contains celery, and the oil blend includes soybean oil
- Fewer independent reviews than competing brands, so texture is less proven
- The fill line is easy to overlook on the pouch

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for Norwegian weekend hikers, hunters and families heading to cabins, and for anyone who needs a dairy- and gluten-free hot dinner that can be picked up on the drive to the trailhead. It also suits cold-weather days when a bland, warming stew is exactly what is wanted. Thru-hikers resupplying outside Norway, and anyone chasing maximum flavor or a long shelf life for emergency stockpiling, should look at Real Turmat or one of the stronger-flavored freeze-dried brands.

## Verdict

Gilde Turmat Biffgryte does what a trail dinner needs to do: it is light, simple to make and free from the common allergens, and it comes from a Norwegian plant that knows the terrain it is aimed at. It is a sensible, inexpensive staple for Norwegian trips and an unremarkable one everywhere else.
