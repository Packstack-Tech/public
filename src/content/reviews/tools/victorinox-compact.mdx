## Overview

<cite index="24-11,24-12">
  The Victorinox Compact is not simply a reduced model — introduced as the
  Golfer in 1986 and renamed in the early 1990s, it refined the 91mm format by
  removing excess while preserving versatility.
</cite>
The result is a 64g, 2-layer Swiss Army Knife that still manages to squeeze in
15 functions, including scissors and a pressurized ballpoint pen — a combination
no other current 91mm SAK pulls off at this thickness. It's best suited for
hikers, travelers, and urban EDC users who want a capable multi-tool without the
bulk of a 3-layer knife.

## Performance

### The Combo Tool — the Compact's defining feature

The Compact's most architecturally interesting decision is its "Combo Tool," which merges the bottle opener and can opener into a single, thinner implement. <cite index="21-2,21-3">The Combo Tool completely replaces the bottle opener and can opener, but the question is whether it can handle everything those two tools do.</cite> In practice, it handles both functions well enough for camp use — opening trail beers and tuna cans without complaint — but it is notably thinner than the standalone bottle-opener layer found on a Spartan or Tinker, and <cite index="8-13,8-14,8-15,8-16">it has almost zero Phillips capability, and the Combo tool doesn't give much confidence as a prying tool, owing to how thin it is.</cite> Worth knowing before you buy.

The payoff for that tradeoff, though, is real: <cite index="8-31">the Victorinox Compact is the thinnest medium-size Swiss Army Knife (91mm model) to carry a pair of scissors.</cite> Those scissors, like all Victorinox scissors, are spring-loaded and precise — legitimately the best scissors I've found in any pocket-size multi-tool.

### The Plus Scales

<cite index="8-44">
  In addition to the tweezers and the toothpick, the Plus scales house two more
  tools — a pressurized ballpoint pen, and a stainless steel pin.
</cite>
The pen works reliably at altitude and in cold weather (pressurized cartridge),
and I've found myself reaching for it more than expected on trail — permit
lotteries, gear notes, and emergency contact info don't care that you're trying
to go light. The pin is a niche tool, but it works for splinter removal when the
tweezers are too blunt.

### The Multipurpose Hook

This is where opinions diverge most sharply. <cite index="21-9,21-10">The multipurpose hook in the Compact is unique — it has a nail file on the back edge — but it's a niche tool that is not produced in volume.</cite> <cite index="25-14">The hook is not as useful as a reamer; however, it does have a nail file on the back of it which can be handy for grooming or lighting strike-anywhere matches.</cite> As a parcel carrier or bag hanger, it sees occasional use at trailheads and resupply boxes. For most hikers, it will be the least-used tool on the knife — but that nail file is a quiet bonus that earns its keep on longer trips.

### The Blade

The large blade (the only blade on this model) is a standard Victorinox clip point in their chrome-molybdenum stainless steel. <cite index="6-16,6-17">Special chrome-molybdenum steel is used for Victorinox blades; after tempering, the blades reach a hardness of 56 HRC.</cite> It takes and holds a decent edge, sharpens easily on a ceramic rod, and handles food prep and cordage cutting without trouble. You won't be doing heavy bushcraft with it — the lack of a lock is the limiting factor, not the steel.

### What's Missing

The Compact has no awl/reamer and no Phillips head screwdriver. <cite index="3-12,3-13">It's a great tool with 90% of the options most people want, but trading the parcel hook and corkscrew for a Phillips would make it perfect</cite> — a sentiment I've seen echoed across forums repeatedly. The combo tool can be coaxed into Phillips screws in a pinch, but it's awkward. If screw-turning is a regular need on your trips (tent poles, stove repairs), factor that in.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Only 2-layer 91mm SAK that includes scissors — genuinely useful on trail
- Pressurized ballpoint pen in the scales works reliably in cold and altitude
- At 64g, lighter than most 3-layer SAK alternatives (e.g., Super Tinker is ~3 layers)
- Combo Tool keeps the profile slim without sacrificing bottle/can opening
- Multipurpose hook's integrated nail file is a subtle but practical bonus
- Lifetime warranty; backed by 100+ years of manufacturing consistency
- Spring-loaded scissors are best-in-class for any pocket multi-tool

**Cons**

- No Phillips screwdriver — a real gap for gear repairs
- No awl or reamer — can't punch holes in leather or patch kits
- Combo Tool is thinner and less confidence-inspiring as a pry tool than standalone openers
- No blade lock (common to all SAKs, but worth noting)
- No one-hand blade opening
- Pricier than comparable SAKs due to Plus scales and low-volume Combo Tool production
- Multipurpose hook is genuinely low-utility for most users

## Who Should Buy This

The Compact earns its place in a pack for hikers and travelers who want scissors, a pen, and a blade without lugging a 3-layer knife. <cite index="8-9">The Compact can be the ideal EDC tool for people who need a set of useful tools in a small and light package that is extremely handy in many day-to-day activities and situations.</cite> If you've previously carried a Spartan and found yourself wishing for scissors — this is the natural upgrade that won't add much bulk. It's less compelling if you frequently need a Phillips or awl, in which case the Tinker or Super Tinker might serve you better despite the extra layer.

## Verdict

The Victorinox Compact is one of the most cleverly engineered SAKs in the current lineup. The Combo Tool compromise is real and worth understanding before you buy, but for most hikers and travelers, the tradeoff delivers: scissors and a pen in a 2-layer, 64g package is a hard combination to beat. I'd rate it **8/10** — docking points only for the absent Phillips and awl, which will genuinely matter to some users. If those aren't in your top-five needs, this is as close to a perfect pocket companion as Victorinox makes.
