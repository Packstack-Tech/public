## Overview

The Rab Ultrasphere 4.5 is a tapered, mummy-shaped inflatable pad that gets its warmth from two layers of reflective TILT film and offset air chambers rather than from bulky insulation. At 370 g in the Regular size, it is among the lightest pads that still reach three-season warmth, and it packs down to roughly the size of a large coffee mug. The trade-off is a narrow, 8 cm-thick platform and a fabric that owners have questioned over the long haul.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The name is a small trap: the 4.5 refers to the model line, not the R-value, which is 4.3. That figure is solid three-season territory, and testers at Live for the Outdoors called the warmth impressive for the weight. The mechanism is two layers of TILT, a reflective film that works like an emergency blanket to send radiant heat back toward the sleeper, paired with internal cutouts that disrupt convection currents and keep warm air from circulating. Rab positions the pad for milder conditions rather than deep winter, and the 4.3 rating backs that up: this is a shoulder-season workhorse, not a snow-camping pad.

**Weight and packed size.** The 370 g claim covers the pad alone. Live for the Outdoors weighed the full kit, including pump sack, storage bag and repair kit, at 463 g, so hikers who ditch the stuff sack and carry the pump sack only when needed will land somewhere in between. Packed size is the real party trick. Testers measured about 16 x 9 cm, and one r/Ultralight commenter reported that it compresses to a half to a third of the volume of a Nemo Eclipse in the same size. Anyone building a frameless-pack kit or a bikepacking handlebar roll will notice the difference.

**Comfort.** Simple horizontal baffles keep the surface unfussy, and reviewers describe the pad as comfortable for side and back sleepers alike. It is not a luxury mattress. One r/Ultralight owner who has used the Ultrasphere and the Tensor Elite said "fantastic" oversells it, though both beat an XLite for comfort. The 51 cm width is the common complaint: testers describe it as narrow, with limited room to roll. Another reviewer, 6 ft 2 in tall, found the Regular length more generous than expected. The fabric is also on the slick side, so a slippery bag or quilt can slide around.

**Inflation.** The two-way valve is low-profile and straightforward, and the included pump sack keeps moist breath out of the chambers. Testers found it a little slower than the best pump sacks on the market, but it does the job without drama.

**Durability.** This is the pad's soft spot. A thin 20D fabric rewards careful campsite selection and a footprint or ground sheet. One r/Ultralight owner who logged about 50 nights reported waking to dozens of tiny holes across the top surface, which they attributed to the weld points tearing the fabric. Another commenter noted the first production batch had pinhole problems and speculated the issue may have since been fixed. Live for the Outdoors scored durability the lowest of its categories, flagging long-term questions. The included repair kit is welcome, and a spare patch is worth carrying.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 370 g with an R-value of 4.3: a strong warmth-to-weight ratio
- Packs to about 15 x 9 cm, smaller than most competitors at similar warmth
- Quiet-enough, unfussy baffle design with easy two-way valve
- Pump sack, stuff sack and repair kit in the box
- Priced below many premium ultralight pads, with a UK RRP around £170

**Cons**

- Narrow 51 cm width and 8 cm thickness limit comfort for restless sleepers
- Thin 20D fabric; owners report pinholes and weld-point failures
- Slick surface
- Pump sack is slower than some rivals
- Model name suggests an R-value of 4.5, which it does not have

## Who Should Buy This

Weight-focused thru-hikers, bikepackers and mountain runners who want three-season warmth without giving up pack space will get the most from the Ultrasphere. It suits careful campers who pitch on forgiving ground and are comfortable with a mummy taper. Side sleepers who spread out, hikers who drag their pad across rough bivy sites, and anyone planning true winter trips should look elsewhere. Wider sleepers can step up to Rab's wider cuts, though that costs weight: the Regular Wide runs 444 g and the Long Wide 505 g.

## Verdict

The Ultrasphere 4.5 delivers on its central promise: real three-season warmth in a package that disappears into a pack. Its narrow platform is tolerable for most solo hikers, but the durability reports are serious enough that buyers should treat the pad with care and carry a patch. For the gram-counter who sleeps in a mummy bag anyway, it is an easy recommendation.
