## Overview

The Ghost Whisperer/2 is Mountain Hardwear's featherweight down sweater. It has been a fixture in thru-hiker packs since the original launched in the early 2010s. It weighs 226 g in a men's medium, stuffs into its own pocket, and carries enough 800-fill down to handle cold camps in three-season conditions. The trade-off is the 10-denier shell, which reviewers describe as fragile enough to demand some care around branches, Velcro and rock.

## Performance

**Warmth.** A jacket this light invites skepticism, but testers consistently find it warmer than its weight suggests. The down is lofty, and the narrow stitched baffles leave little room for it to migrate. One retailer review pegs it as comfortable to around 0°C (32°F) with a decent base layer. Thru-hikers on the Pacific Crest Trail report wearing it with just a base layer below freezing. It is not a winter jacket. Reviewers who put it through chillier shoulder-season days say it falls short once temperatures drop well below that range, and Adventure Alan notes it is less warm than the average sweater-weight puffy. What it does well is stay in the useful middle: warm enough for a cold camp, and not so warm that it overheats on a brisk climb or in a hostel common room. Runners and day hikers say the same.

**Weight and packability.** This is the reason the jacket sells. Gear reviewers who compare it with mainstream down sweaters generally find it lighter and better on warmth-to-weight than nearly all of them. Treeline Review named it the best ultralight pick in its down jacket roundup after what it described as thousands of trail miles. It compresses to a small bundle in its pocket, though Montbell's Plasma 1000 and a few cottage-brand options still beat it on pure grams.

**Wet weather.** The Q.Shield-era down treatment is gone from this build; the spec here is a DWR-finished recycled shell around RDS-certified down. Mountain Hardwear's updates to the /2 added a moisture-resistant outer and a longer hem. The DWR will shed light drizzle and condensation, but that is all it will do. One long-distance reviewer reported the down wetting out under a shell in very wet weather. As with any down jacket, a dry bag liner or a trash compactor bag is cheap insurance.

**Durability.** Here the jacket loses points. A 10D shell is thin, and owners say it shows wear faster than heavier-faced jackets. One PCT and Himalayan reviewer scored durability a middling 3 out of 5. Another long-term owner with a decade-old original reported it kept most of its loft and feathers, helped by washing it as rarely as possible. The lightweight coil zipper has drawn complaints too. One Trailspace owner saw the bottom of the zipper fail on an older hoody. Reviewers also report that elastic cuffs loosen with use. A nice touch: the last baffle at the cuff and hem is synthetic, since those areas take the most abuse and get wet first.

**Fit and features.** Sleeves run slightly long relative to competitors, which keeps the cuffs over the wrists when reaching. Some testers found the shoulders a bit narrow. The torso is roomy: reviewers describe it as true to size to slightly loose, with space for a fleece or two layers underneath, and several buyers say it runs a touch big. The two hand pockets are functional but awkward to use, and there is no chest pocket. The jacket packs into one of them. Minimalism has its costs: an adjustable hem is the only fit control.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 226 g in a medium, among the lightest full-zip down jackets from a major brand
- Packs down small enough to disappear in a pack's lid pocket
- 800-fill RDS-certified down with narrow baffles that keep loft evenly distributed
- Recycled shell and a DWR finish for light moisture
- Synthetic final baffles at the cuff and hem
- Long sleeves; comfortable as a mid-layer or outer layer

**Cons**

- The 10D shell snags and abrades more easily than thicker fabrics
- Lightweight zipper and elastic cuffs have drawn wear complaints
- No chest pocket, and the hand pockets are awkward
- Not warm enough on its own for deep-cold shoulder season
- Full retail price is high; owners recommend waiting for sales
- No hood on this version

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and fast-and-light backpackers who want a camp and sleep-system layer at the lowest weight, and who will treat the shell with some care, are the ideal buyers. It also suits travelers and runners who want a puffy that vanishes into a bag. Anyone who will use one jacket for winter, bushwhacking or scrambling should look at a heavier, thicker-faced jacket with more insulation. Cold sleepers who need a warmer layer should consider a hooded model.

## Verdict

The Ghost Whisperer/2 remains one of the easiest down jackets to recommend for three-season ultralight use, with real warmth for under 230 g. Buy it for the weight and compressibility, carry a repair patch, and accept that the shell won't forgive rough treatment.
