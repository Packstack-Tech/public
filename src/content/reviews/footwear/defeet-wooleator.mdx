## Overview

The DeFeet Wooleator is a thin, unpadded, USA-made Merino sock with a 3-inch quarter-height cuff. It started as a cycling sock and has been adopted by long-distance hikers who want low bulk, quick drying and a close fit. The trade-off is cushioning and toughness: there is almost no padding underfoot, and the thin knit does not last as long as a heavier hiking sock.

## Performance

**Fit and feel.** The Wooleator's best-known advocate is Andrew Skurka. He wore the 3-inch version for the entire 6,875-mile Great Western Loop in 2007, and a decade later still called it his go-to sock. By his own count he has logged at least 20,000 miles of hiking and running in the model. His praise centers on fit: with only 2 percent spandex, he says, it fits like a glove, without the sagging or bunching that plagues many thin socks. A Backpacking Light forum poster who compared it with Darn Tough's quarter-height mesh sock also preferred the Wooleator's fit and liked having a slightly higher cuff at the ankle.

**Moisture and temperature.** The knit is a mesh construction, and the mesh on top of the foot helps heat and moisture escape. Because the sock is thin, unpadded and made of hydrophobic fibers, it holds little water. Skurka reports that after sweat, creek crossings or a rinse at a rest stop, the sock dries quickly. In the 3-inch height it handles three-season hiking comfortably, and Skurka has also used the model for winter running in single-digit temperatures and as a liner under thicker wool socks for skiing and snowshoeing. DeFeet markets it as a four-season sock, thin enough for summer and warm enough for winter.

**Odor.** Merino does most of the work here. Long-term owners describe the Wooleator as good at keeping smell down over multi-day use, which matters on a resupply stretch where laundry is a theory. One caution from a Backpacking Light user: thin socks of this kind get crusty after a day of use without washing, and the Wooleator is no exception.

**Durability.** This is the weak spot, and opinions vary with how the sock is used. Skurka reports that two new pairs held up to trips of 150 to 200 miles on rugged off-trail routes and were still in good condition at the finish. On the Great Western Loop he replaced pairs every 600 miles. Other owners report less. One Backpacking Light poster found holes after about 300 miles, and a commenter on Skurka's review wore through big-toe areas on three pairs, but only on road runs of 20 miles or more, and had no problems on trails. The same Backpacking Light thread notes that Darn Tough's comparable sock lasted longer but felt stiffer. Treat the Wooleator as a consumable, with a lifespan measured in hundreds of miles and not thousands.

**Cushioning.** There is none. On long days in firm, thin-soled trail runners, that means less padding underfoot than a Darn Tough or Smartwool hiker. Hikers who like a plush feel should look elsewhere, while those who want a close fit and good ground feel will like the thin knit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 43.6 g with a thin profile that leaves room in snug shoes
- Close, glove-like fit with little bunching
- Mesh upper and low-bulk knit dry fast after sweat or creek crossings
- Merino keeps odor in check over multi-day trips
- Made in the USA, with a 5-year warranty
- Works from summer hiking through cold-weather running and as a liner

**Cons**

- No padding; not for hikers who want cushioning
- Wears faster than heavier wool socks; owners report holes anywhere from roughly 300 miles to a few hundred more, depending on use
- Toe-area wear on long road runs
- 3-inch cuff leaves ankles exposed to debris and cold
- Gets crusty after a day without washing

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers and trail runners who wear low-volume shoes, want a sock that fits precisely and dries quickly, and don't mind replacing pairs every few hundred miles. It suits thru-hikers in three-season conditions, especially in trail runners, and cyclists and everyday wearers who want a thin wool sock. Anyone who wants cushioning, or who expects one pair to last an entire long trail, should buy a padded, more durable sock such as Darn Tough's. Those who need more ankle coverage can choose the longer cuff heights of the same sock.

## Verdict

The Wooleator is a light, well-fitting, fast-drying Merino sock with a long record among serious long-distance hikers. It is a sock for people who want thin and snug, and who accept that it will wear out sooner than a thicker one. For three-season hiking in trail runners, it remains one of the most proven thin wool socks available.
