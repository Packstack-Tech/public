## Overview

The Akto Footprint is Hilleberg's factory-matched groundsheet for the Akto (it also fits the newer Enan). It's not a generic tarp cut to size — it's a shaped, waterproof-coated fabric piece that toggles directly into the same attachment points as the inner tent, covering the full outer tent footprint including the vestibule. It's aimed at Akto owners who camp on rough, abrasive, or wet terrain often enough to want a sacrificial layer between the tent's floor and the ground.

## Performance

The core job of this footprint is simple — keep sharp ground features away from the Akto's floor and keep moisture from wicking up into your vestibule — and by most accounts it does that job well. Owners on Trailspace point out that the biggest threat to an otherwise bomber tent is a punctured floor, and one reviewer noted <cite index="4-9,4-10">"the quickest way to destroy a tent like this is to start working holes in the floor on hard ground" while "everything else on this tent will handle most anything you (or the weather) can dish out except a torn floor from a sharp rock."</cite> That's exactly the failure mode this footprint is designed to prevent.

Where it earns its keep beyond floor protection is in the vestibule. Because coverage extends over the full outer tent area rather than just the inner tent footprint, it gives you a dry, clean spot to stage a pack, boots, or a stove instead of setting them on bare dirt. One long-term user described it as making <cite index="2-11,2-12">"a clean/dry area in the vestibule" where they "could keep my pack, boots etc dry and of the ground," and said it "also extended the 'living' area inside made it easier to have the inner fly rolled back in the pouring rain."</cite>

Setup-wise, the attachment system is a genuine convenience rather than an afterthought. The footprint <cite index="2-6">attaches through the same loops as the inner tent, which has black toggles</cite>, so on dry trips you can leave it clipped to the tent permanently and pitch the whole assembly — outer, inner, and footprint — as one unit. One reviewer who left it attached on dry days but detached it when packing wet gear called it <cite index="2-7,2-8">"easier when I had to remove the inner tent when packing it down wet," while on dry days he "left the inner tent and footprint all attached, quick and easy to pack down and set up as one unit."</cite> Another owner echoed this, saying the footprint <cite index="4-16,4-17">"pitches with the tent, and doesn't add too much more weight for the value of getting gear off the wet ground," and that "the Akto packs away easily in its bag, even with the footprint attached."</cite>

Weight is the one spec that's genuinely inconsistent depending on where you look — third-party retailers list it anywhere from about 255 g to 320 g, while one long-time Akto user described it as weighing <cite index="4-2">"only 40g or so"</cite> (almost certainly a misremembered figure, since that's far lighter than any listing I found). Take the 240 g figure from Hilleberg as the most trustworthy baseline, but expect some batch or regional variance. Either way, multiple users report it's negligible on the shoulders — one summed it up as adding weight that <cite index="2-13">"is negligible it certainly made no difference to me."</cite>

The durability angle cuts both ways. On one hand, it's there specifically to take the abuse so your tent floor doesn't have to. On the other, at least one long-term owner reported pinholes developing in the footprint itself over time, though they noted it <cite index="2-9,2-10">"added extra protection to the sewn in groundsheet," and even with "pin holes in it, although it doesn't leak it will prevent further damage."</cite> That's arguably exactly what you want from a sacrificial layer — it's cheaper and easier to patch or replace than the tent's integrated floor.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Toggles into the exact same attachment points as the inner tent, so it pitches as one unit with zero extra fuss
- Full vestibule coverage means a genuinely dry, clean gear-storage area, not just floor protection
- Can stay clipped on permanently for dry trips, or be stripped off in seconds when packing a wet tent
- Extends the practical life of the Akto's floor, which is the tent's single most vulnerable point
- Packs small enough to still fit in the stock Akto stuff sack

**Cons**

- Adds real weight and cost to an already premium, already-not-ultralight setup
- Published weight figures vary noticeably by retailer (roughly 240–320 g), so double-check before you buy if grams matter to you
- It's a wear item itself — expect eventual pinholes or abrasion with heavy use
- Hilleberg doesn't publish granular fabric specs for this accessory, so you're buying on brand trust rather than a spec sheet

## Who Should Buy This

This is an easy add for anyone who already owns an Akto and camps regularly on scree, gravel, hard-packed dirt, or anywhere ground moisture is a persistent issue. If you're mostly pitching on soft duff or established tent pads and you're chasing every gram, you could reasonably skip it — the Akto's tub floor is tough on its own. But if you're doing multi-week trips where the tent gets pitched and struck daily on variable terrain, the combination of floor protection and a genuinely usable dry vestibule area makes this one of the more sensible accessory purchases in the Hilleberg lineup.

## Verdict

It's a simple product that does exactly what it says: protects the floor, dries out your porch, and pitches as one unit with the rest of the tent. It won't transform your Akto experience, but it meaningfully extends the life of an expensive tent and earns its weight on trips with rough or wet ground. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — solid, purpose-built, and worth it if you're logging serious nights in the field, less essential if your Akto mostly sees manicured campsites.
