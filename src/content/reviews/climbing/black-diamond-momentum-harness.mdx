## Overview

The Momentum is Black Diamond's do-everything harness, and the one most likely to be hanging on the wall at a rental counter or clipped to the pack of someone at the crag. It trades a few grams for generous padding, adjustable leg loops and a waistbelt buckle that is hard to misuse. At 336.5 g it is not a harness for people counting grams on a long alpine route, but it suits nearly everything else.

## Performance

**Comfort.** Comfort is the Momentum's reputation, and reviewers keep returning to it. Dual Core Construction runs two bands of nylon webbing through a CLPE foam insert inside an abrasion-resistant shell, spreading load across the waist instead of concentrating it on a single strap. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers called the waistbelt and leg loops well padded, and owners on Black Diamond's own site most often praise how it handles long hangs and falls. One owner said it spreads weight across hips and legs better than any other harness tried. Reviewers elsewhere reported hanging for more than 30 minutes with no pressure points.

**Speed and ease of use.** The Speed Adjust buckle arrives pre-threaded, so the harness can go on in seconds and the classic mistake of a backed-out buckle is designed out. Reviewers who stepped up from fixed-size or strap-style harnesses singled this out. The trakFIT leg loops adjust with a simple sliding buckle, which helps when a climber swaps between thin summer layers and winter pants. A few owners wished the leg loops opened wider for getting in and out over bulky clothing.

**Gear loops.** The four molded loops are stiff and easy to clip, and Treeline Review's testers liked their sturdiness. Outdoor Gear Lab found that they make a multi-pitch rack manageable, and that the same harness works for a sport day with a handful of quickdraws. Some owners climbing trad want more capacity, and a couple of reviewers on Black Diamond's site wished for extra loops. There are no ice clipper slots, so winter use means improvising.

**Bulk and weight.** The padding that makes the Momentum comfortable also makes it chunky. Outdoor Gear Lab noted that it is a little bulky and not something to wear around camp, and REI reviewers made the same point. A long-time owner who works as a climbing instructor and rescue technician said it falls short for all-day wear on longer routes and extended belay sessions. Some testers also found that it rides up when top-roping. Early owners noted it runs about 10–20 g heavier than comparable budget harnesses, which they considered immaterial for gym and crag use.

**Durability.** Reports are consistently good. One REI owner said it held up through three years of roughly three sessions a week, and another said the harness they bought as a teenager had shown nothing to suggest low quality. Outdoor Gear Lab observed that many experienced climbers wear it despite its entry-level label.

## Choosing a Version

This listing covers two sizes. The L fits a 33–36 in waist and a 22–26 in leg loop. The XL fits a 36–39 in waist and a 24–28 in leg loop. Pick by waist measurement first and check the leg loop range second. Because the leg loops adjust, there is less guesswork than with fixed-loop harnesses, but the waist range is firm. Climbers who land between sizes should size according to how much they layer: the XL gives room over a winter shell, and the L gives a snugger feel in the gym. Smaller sizes and a women's version also exist, with a women-specific rise, for climbers outside these ranges.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Thick, supportive waistbelt that owners say stays comfortable through long hangs and falls
- Pre-threaded Speed Adjust buckle makes tying in fast and mistakes less likely
- trakFIT adjustable leg loops handle changing layers and different body shapes
- Rigid, easy-to-clip gear loops
- Strong durability record and a retail price of roughly $60 in major listings
- Black Diamond's site shows a 4.8-star average across 65 reviews

**Cons**

- Bulky compared with minimalist alpine and trad harnesses
- Four gear loops fill up quickly on long trad routes
- Leg loops can be awkward to open over bulky pants
- No ice clipper slots for winter use
- Some testers found it rides up when hanging or top-roping

## Who Should Buy This

New climbers, gym regulars and weekend sport and top-rope climbers will get everything they need here, and many will never outgrow it. It also works as a second harness for canyoneering or occasional multi-pitch days. Alpinists, thru-hikers adding a via ferrata or glacier day, and anyone who wants a harness that disappears in a pack should look at lighter, more stripped-down designs. Dedicated trad climbers who carry a full rack should consider something with more loops and wider padding.

## Verdict

The Momentum is a durable, comfortable and simple harness that costs less than most competitors and rarely lets its owner down. It is heavier and bulkier than anything built for weight-conscious mountain objectives, so the rating reflects its strengths as an all-rounder rather than as an ultralight piece. For gym, sport and moderate multi-pitch use, it is an easy recommendation.
