## Overview

<cite index="4-21,4-22">
  The Outdoor Research Helium is an ultralight, waterproof shell designed for
  hikers and backpackers who prioritize weight and packability, and it has long
  been a favorite of thru-hikers and the ultralight crowd.
</cite>
At 179g (about 6.3 oz),
<cite index="2-10">
  it's built with a 2.5-layer Pertex Shield Diamond Fuse membrane and a thin
  30-denier shell — clearly designed for weight savings, not for riding out
  long, wet storms.
</cite>
Think of it as the jacket that lives in the bottom of your pack until you need
it: an emergency layer first, a storm shell second. Worth noting upfront:
<cite index="2-5,2-6,2-7">
  for spring 2026, Outdoor Research replaced the Helium Rain with the Helium UL,
  which features an updated 2.75-layer construction and 15-denier shell,
  dropping the weight to 5.7 oz and retailing for $210.
</cite>
The original Helium Rain is now available as final sale stock, often at a
discount.

## Performance

### Weather Protection

This is where the Helium's limitations become the most important thing to understand before you buy. <cite index="1-1,1-2">In field and shower tests conducted by OutdoorGearLab, the jacket couldn't handle much precipitation beyond 5–10 minutes of consistent rain; in a more gentle drizzle, testers stayed mostly dry until around the 25-minute mark.</cite> That squares with real-world experience from The Trek, where <cite index="17-9,17-10">the jacket fended off rain for a good half-hour on the front, back, and shoulders in a heavy downpour, with water beading on the fabric for almost that entire time.</cite>

<cite index="3-6,3-7">
  Ultralight rain shells aren't typically intended for serious alpine conditions
  or day-long deluges, and the Helium is no exception — with a 2.5-layer build
  and thin materials throughout, the face fabric will get overwhelmed in a
  strong downpour and start to absorb moisture, particularly noticeable at the
  shoulders and forearms.
</cite>
<cite index="8-7">
  The Diamond Fuse technology uses yarns with diamond-shaped filaments that lock
  together, lending the fabric relatively good durability and snag-resistance
  for its weight and improving water-beading properties.
</cite>
But no amount of clever construction fully overcomes the physics of 30D nylon in
a sustained downpour. Treat the DWR well —
<cite index="13-12">
  OR recommends cleaning it regularly with Nikwax Tech Wash to maintain water
  repellency.
</cite>

### Breathability

Honest assessment: it's not good, and it's a consistent complaint across every long-form review I found. <cite index="2-26,2-27">The 2.5-layer construction features a plasticky laminate interior that feels sticky and clammy against the skin — especially during movement or in humid conditions. Unlike 3-layer jackets, which typically use a fabric liner to create a softer barrier, the Helium's interior is bare-bones.</cite> <cite index="10-25">There are no pit zips or other ways to dump heat, so things warm up quickly during any kind of sustained activity.</cite> The only real ventilation option is cracking the front zipper. <cite index="17-12,17-13">On the AT, one tester found that heading up any steep hill required fully unzipping the jacket or removing it entirely to avoid being soaked from the inside out.</cite>

### Packability & Weight

This is where the Helium earns its reputation. <cite index="2-28,2-29,2-30">Where the jacket truly excels is in weight and packability — it packs down into its own chest pocket, complete with a carabiner loop for clipping to a harness, and compresses small enough to fit in the palm of your hand.</cite> <cite index="9-30,9-31">One tester measured the stowed jacket at 7 inches wide at the widest point and 7 inches long by about 2 inches deep.</cite> That's genuinely small. <cite index="9-32,9-33">There's also a key hook inside the pocket for when you're wearing the jacket; when you stuff and pack it, this hook moves to the outside so you can clip the jacket to the outside of your pack.</cite> Small detail, but a handy one.

### Fit & Mobility

<cite index="1-28,1-29">
  When wearing only minimal layers underneath, the Helium offers decent freedom
  of movement without a great deal of bunching, though testers experienced a
  slight bit of resistance in the upper back when arms were crossed and some
  tightness in the chest when arms were positioned slightly behind the body.
</cite>
<cite index="1-30">
  There is some stretch in the Pertex Shield to minimize restriction, but if you
  need to wear more layers underneath, sizing up is recommended.
</cite>
<cite index="12-25,12-26">
  With an athletic fit, the Helium is designed for active outings — because of
  its trimmer build, it's difficult to layer underneath, making this jacket more
  ideal for summer activities than fall or winter.
</cite>

### Durability

<cite index="15-19,15-20,15-21">
  The Helium isn't the most robust design out there, and the Pertex 2.5L fabric
  is fairly thin, requiring mindful movement in abrasive environments — OGL
  testers even experienced a few minor tears during testing.
</cite>
That said, one user on Exploring Wild used her original Helium II for eight
years across multiple thru-hikes before replacing it.
<cite index="8-8">
  This fabric will stand up to wearing a backpack over it but is likely to tear
  too easily for hiking off-trail in forest or rock climbing.
</cite>
Use it on maintained trails and it'll last; take it through brush and expect
consequences.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Sub-180g weight — genuinely one of the lightest fully taped waterproof shells available
- <cite index="16-4,16-5">
    All seams are fully seam-taped, and the jacket still incorporates an
    adjustable hood, full-length weather-resistant front zipper, elastic cuffs,
    and an elastic drawcord at the hem
  </cite>
- <cite index="16-7">
    The chest pocket doubles as a stuff sack with a built-in carabiner loop
  </cite>
- <cite index="16-6">
    Reflectivity on the chest and forearms provides a measure of safety in low
    light
  </cite>
- Effective wind layer when rain isn't the primary concern
- Long, proven track record on the AT, JMT, PCT, and beyond
- Bluesign-approved materials

**Cons**

- <cite index="10-11">
    Not breathable — no options for venting; the jacket will grow clammy in a
    hurry
  </cite>
- Wets out in sustained heavy rain (roughly 10–30 minutes depending on intensity)
- <cite index="4-28">
    Lacks pit zips and hand pockets (men's version), limiting breathability and
    in-use utility
  </cite>
- <cite index="10-12">Trim fit can only be worn over light layers</cite>
- 30D fabric requires care; avoid abrasive terrain
- Replaced by the Helium UL — buy the closeout knowing you're getting an outgoing model

## Who Should Buy This

<cite index="5-11,5-12">
  The Helium is a good choice for warmer weather trips where gear weight is a
  priority and you're unlikely to need a rain jacket, but feel you need to carry
  one anyway — a calculation that backpackers armed with weather forecasts make
  about shorter trips all the time.
</cite>
It's the right call for Sierra summer trips, desert Southwest travel, or any
route where rain is a possibility but not a forecast certainty. If you're
heading into the Pacific Northwest in October or planning a week in the Scottish
Highlands, look elsewhere. It also works well for fastpackers and trail runners
who want protection that disappears when not needed. Just make sure a base layer
is always between you and that laminate interior.

## Verdict

The Helium Rain Jacket has earned its decade-plus of loyalty from the ultralight crowd — not by being the best rain jacket, but by being the best "might rain" jacket. <cite index="7-26,7-27">It's a favorite thanks to its featherlight weight, tiny packed size, and functional-yet-minimalist design — though it's not the best choice for gnarly alpine conditions or back-to-back soggy days, it really shines as a wind and occasional rain layer in moderate 3-season conditions.</cite> With the Helium UL now on shelves as the updated successor, the original is available at a discount — and at closeout prices, the value proposition gets even stronger for the right buyer. Rating: **7/10**.
