## Overview

<cite index="4-25,4-26">
  In 2024, Nitecore finally gave its customers what they'd long been asking for:
  a proper update to the beloved NU20, called the Classic. It's virtually
  identical in size and shape to the original, but lighter, with improved
  functionality and a much-anticipated USB-C charging port.
</cite>
At 38g and around $25,
<cite index="14-18">
  it's the right pick for anyone whose headlamp use is mostly camp tasks and
  in-tent reading rather than sustained night hiking.
</cite>

## Performance

**Weight and size.** <cite index="2-22,2-23,2-24">The main feature of this ultralight headlamp is its weight. Out of the box, the headlamp weighs just 38 grams — with the headlamp unit itself at 28g and the headband adding 10g.</cite> That puts it firmly in the "you'll forget it's on your head" category. <cite index="15-12">Gram-counters can shave it further by swapping the stock dual shock-cord band for a Litesmith Spiderlite UL Headband, which gets the total down to 1.14 oz (32.3g).</cite>

**Output and beam.** <cite index="6-2,6-3">The NU20 Classic provides 100° wide-angle flood illumination, and the light tilts down up to 70° for reading or close-up work.</cite> 360 lumens on Turbo is solid for such a small package, but don't expect it to hold there. <cite index="10-36,10-37">The brightness steps down the longer you run it to prevent overheating and extend battery life — in practice, the 360-lumen Turbo mode dips to around 200 lumens after 30 minutes.</cite> For camp use and short trail sections that's a non-issue; for extended night hiking, it's worth knowing upfront.

**Three light sources.** <cite index="8-4">The NU20 Classic is equipped with a primary white light (6,000K), a high CRI auxiliary white light (CRI90, 4,500K), and an auxiliary red light.</cite> <cite index="6-28,6-29">The auxiliary white's high CRI is great for reducing eye strain during close-range work, like reading a map.</cite> <cite index="5-8">The red light protects your night vision with three modes: Low, High, and Slow Flashing.</cite> That's a well-rounded trio, and the 1-lumen floor on the white modes is worth calling out specifically — <cite index="16-30,16-31">Nitecore included the good ol' 1-lumen mode here after users pushed back on the 2022 NU25 update, which had removed it. The 1-lumen mode is very useful for camp tasks when you don't want to obliterate your night vision or wake tent-mates.</cite>

**Controls.** Two buttons, one for white and one for red. <cite index="1-10">A single button press cycles you through the white or red mode intensities.</cite> That's it for the bread-and-butter usage. <cite index="1-12">The control logic for the SOS and Beacon modes is more nuanced and easy to forget</cite> — but realistically, most hikers will never need those modes in anger anyway. <cite index="15-38,15-39,15-40">Press both buttons simultaneously to lock the headlamp; it flashes three times to confirm. Press both again to reactivate in Low mode.</cite> It's a common idiom across headlamps and easy to internalize.

**Battery life and charging.** <cite index="5-11">A full charge takes just over 1 hour via USB-C.</cite> <cite index="6-40,6-41,6-42">The light maintains full functionality while charging. To check remaining battery without turning the light on, press the Red button and four blue power indicators show the charge level in 25% increments.</cite> The 500 mAh cell is small by any measure — this isn't a lamp you'll want to rely on for three consecutive nights of heavy hiking without a top-up. It pairs naturally with whatever power bank you're already carrying for your phone.

**Weather resistance.** <cite index="2-16,2-17,2-18">The IP66 rating means it's completely dust and waterproof against high-pressure water jets. One reviewer tested it under a shower with significant water flow, and it held up as expected.</cite>

**Build quality.** <cite index="2-12,2-13,2-14">The headlamp itself is made of plastic but feels solid — Nitecore officially rates it as drop-proof to 1 meter. However, the bracket that attaches the headband to the light feels a bit flimsy</cite>, and a few users have flagged it as a potential long-term durability concern. That's worth monitoring over a full season.

**Headband.** The stock dual shock cord works, but it's polarizing. <cite index="10-7">The shock cord isn't as comfortable as a wide strap, but if you don't make it too tight, it's not particularly bothersome.</cite> On the other end of the spectrum, <cite index="13-7,13-8,13-9">some users couldn't stand the corded headband — particularly those with smaller heads — and ended up having to purchase a separate cloth headband at additional cost.</cite> <cite index="13-21">The headlamp is so lightweight, though, that you hardly notice it's there at all</cite>, which offsets a lot of the comfort argument.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- <cite index="2-22,2-23">Featherlight at just 38g out of the box</cite>, with
  room to go lighter with an aftermarket band
- <cite index="16-7,16-8">
    Ultralight and ultra-affordable at $24.99, with an ultra-simple user
    interface
  </cite>
- <cite index="8-4">
    Three distinct light sources: primary white, high CRI aux white, and red
  </cite>
  — all genuinely useful
- <cite index="5-11">Charges fully in just over 1 hour via USB-C</cite>
- <cite index="6-40">Maintains full functionality while charging</cite> — handy
  when you're running off a power bank mid-camp
- <cite index="5-13">IP66 waterproof and 1-meter impact resistant</cite>
- <cite index="13-22">
    Reflective cord is a bonus safety feature for road crossings or night camp
    approaches
  </cite>

**Cons**

- <cite index="10-37">
    Turbo mode steps down from 360 to ~200 lumens within 30 minutes
  </cite>
- <cite index="10-26">Built-in battery is not replaceable</cite> — a dead cell

- <cite index="10-1">
    The bracket attaching the headband to the light feels a bit flimsy
  </cite>
- <cite index="13-7,13-8">

- 500 mAh cell requires frequent top-ups for multi-day trips with heavy headlamp use
- <cite index="1-12">SOS and Beacon mode control logic is easy to forget</cite>

## Who Should Buy This

<cite index="14-27">
  If budget or absolute minimum weight is the deciding factor and your headlamp
  is mostly a camp light, the Nitecore NU20 Classic is the pick.
</cite>
It's ideal for the ultralight backpacker who relies on natural light most of the
day, uses the headlamp primarily for tent tasks and pre-dawn starts, and
recharges from a power bank at resupply or camp.
<cite index="4-18,4-19">
  It's a small, inexpensive headlamp with good brightness and the easiest on/off
  and brightness toggle in its class. If you need longer battery life, more
  brightness settings, and flood/spotlight modes, step up to the NU25 — but
  accept that it's heavier and more complex.
</cite>

## Verdict

The NU20 Classic is the rare piece of gear that overcomes its spec-sheet limitations through sheer execution. It won't out-run a Petzl Swift or a Nitecore NU27 in raw output or sustained runtime, but it doesn't need to — it does exactly what a sub-40g, sub-$25 headlamp should do, and it does it with minimal fuss. The flimsy bracket and shock-cord headband are legitimate concerns for long-haul thru-hikers, but for three-season weekend trips and hut-to-hut travel, it's hard to argue against this much capability for this little weight and money. Rating: **8/10**.
