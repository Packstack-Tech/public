## Overview

The Exped Ultra 6.5R is a four-season inflatable pad that gets its warmth from a hybrid of recycled microfiber fill and reflective foil, a combination Exped calls SynMat Plus. It is built for backpackers who camp in shoulder season and winter and don't want to carry a brick to do it. The trade-off is a thin 20D shell that rewards careful campsite selection, and a price that makes little sense if the pad only sees July.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The 6.9 R-value is the headline, and testers report it holds up. Treeline Review's testers logged more than 50 nights across all four seasons, including sleeping on snow in 15°F conditions, and never felt cold coming through the pad. A GearJunkie tester reached a similar verdict on 0°F and 2°F nights, adding a layer only because the sleeping bag was at its limit, not the pad. Backpacker, which tested the pad on release, noted the improvement over Exped's earlier Ultra 5R: lighter, smaller packed, and a better tested R-value. CleverHiker named it a favorite cold-weather pad, and cited warm Grand Canyon camps as well as chilly Rocky Mountain nights.

The flip side shows up in summer. One owner on Reddit says a 6.5R pad can feel like a lot of insulation in genuine heat. That is the cost of a pad built for winter.

**Comfort.** At 3.5 inches thick, the Ultra sits well above the bony-hip threshold. The vertical baffles produce a softer, more cradling surface than the stiffer horizontal tubes on many competitors, and the slightly raised outer rails help keep a sleeper centered. CleverHiker rated it 4.7 out of 5 for comfort and singled it out for side sleepers. Better Trail's tester reported sleeping comfortably on the side, on the stomach and in a figure-four. UK reviewers at Trek & Mountain were just as enthusiastic. The pad is also quiet, without the crinkle of foil-lined designs that draws complaints from light sleepers. Not everyone likes vertical baffles, though, and Live for the Outdoors flagged that as a matter of taste.

**Weight and packability.** At 544 g, this rectangular, full-width configuration is a mid-weight winter pad, not an ounce-counter's piece. CleverHiker's verdict was that warm-weather hikers can find lighter options, but that the weight looks impressively reasonable for what it delivers in the cold. The pad rolls easily and packs to 8.3 x 4.3 inches. Exped also makes a tapered mummy shape in the family. Backpacker clocked the regular mummy at 12.7 ounces, and Treeline Review put it at 12 ounces, which is where the pad's reputation as a warmth-to-weight standout comes from. Hikers who want that figure should expect to give up shoulder room.

**Inflation.** A one-way "IN" valve and a separate deflate valve keep setup simple, and a lightweight pump sack is included. Testers generally found it easy to inflate. Better Trail's reviewer found the pump sack less intuitive than some competitors', while Trek & Mountain counted it among the best.

**Durability.** This is the pad's soft spot. The recycled 20D fabric is Bluesign-approved and DWR-free. GearJunkie's tester found it durable enough for snow camping, where puncture hazards are minimal, but noted it isn't the most puncture-resistant. A Reddit commenter comparing it with the 30D Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite mentioned the thinner denier and occasional baffle-weld failures. An REI buyer also reported cold spots from gaps in the reflective layer, though that sits among seven reviews averaging 4.0 stars. In practice, that means a footprint or a Tyvek sheet and a quick sweep of the tent floor for pine needles.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 6.9 R-value handles snow camping and sub-freezing nights
- 3.5-inch thickness with vertical baffles and raised rails suits side sleepers
- Quiet, with a soft-feeling fabric
- Pump sack included; simple valve system
- Priced below many winter-rated competitors at about $199

**Cons**

- Thin 20D fabric needs care around sharp ground
- Too warm and heavy for summer-only use
- 544 g is not ultralight for a rectangular pad; the mummy is far lighter
- A few owners report weld failures or gaps in the reflective layer
- Vertical baffles are not to everyone's taste

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season-plus backpackers who sleep cold, side sleepers who find stiff, narrow pads unforgiving, and anyone planning snow camping or winter trips will get the most out of the Ultra 6.5R. It also suits hikers wanting one pad for the whole year, with the caveat that it will run warm in summer. Hot-weather thru-hikers and gram-counters should look at a lower R-value pad or the mummy cut. Anyone who regularly sleeps on rough ground without a ground sheet should pick a thicker-fabric alternative.

## Verdict

The Exped Ultra 6.5R is one of the best-reviewed cold-weather backpacking pads, delivering a winter-capable R-value and real comfort at a competitive price and moderate weight. Buy it for shoulder season and winter, and protect the shell. Skip it if the plan is mostly warm-weather trips.
