## Overview

The MSR Elixir 2 sits in that awkward-but-useful middle ground: not an ultralight tent, but not a heavy car-camping dome either. It's a freestanding, double-wall, 3-season backpacking tent aimed at people who want MSR's build quality and weather resistance without paying Hubba Hubba or FreeLite prices. If you're a weekend backpacker, a first-time thru-hiker, or someone who prioritizes a bombproof shelter over shaving grams, this is the tent MSR built for you.

## Performance

**Weather resistance and stability.** This is where the Elixir 2 earns its reputation. It uses an unusual interlinked two-pole geometry plus a third floating brow pole, rather than the simpler crossed-pole hub design you'll find on most tents in this price range. <cite index="12-1,12-2">This creates an intrinsically stable freestanding structure, with four pole crossing points.</cite> Reviewers consistently point to this as the tent's standout feature: <cite index="11-4,11-5">it earns a nod for its overall stability and durability in wind and rain, with its unique pole structure providing extra rigidity and myriad stake points and guy points to anchor it securely.</cite> One long-term user on a UK gear blog noted the tent standing strong through one of the windiest nights they'd experienced. That said, it's not bulletproof — one Reddit user reported a gust that left the tent "no longer standing properly," so treat the freestanding stability as good-for-its-class rather than mountaineering-grade.

**Livability and interior space.** At 40 inches of peak height with steep, near-vertical walls, the Elixir 2 feels roomier than its 30-square-foot floor suggests. <cite index="11-11,11-12">Its symmetrical footprint has a slightly below-average 50-inch width for the category, but paired with the 40-inch peak height, two people can sit up at the same time without issue.</cite> The catch is length: <cite index="11-13,11-14">the 84-inch length can feel tight once you factor in gear, a pad, and a sleeping bag — taller campers may find their head or feet bumping the tent walls.</cite> That's a real consideration if you're over 6 feet tall and sleep with a lot of kit inside.

**Condensation.** Because of that snug length, condensation management is a known weak point. <cite index="11-15">When that happens, the foot of your sleeping bag can end up wet from condensation that's collected on the tent wall overnight.</cite> Good site selection and keeping the vestibules cracked for airflow help, but don't expect FreeLite-level ventilation from a tent at this fly waterproof rating.

**Setup.** Multiple users describe a learning curve on the first pitch thanks to the unusual pole architecture, but say it becomes quick and intuitive after a try or two. One reviewer called the first setup "a bit tricky" but easy once you get the hang of it, and MSR's official product reviews echo this — one buyer described it as easy to set up solo in about five minutes once familiar with it.

**Durability.** This is the tent's other headline strength. The heavier-denier fabrics and robust pole set mean it shrugs off rough handling — dog paws, rocky sites, careless packing — better than most tents in its price bracket. It's not going to win any weight-weenie awards, but it's built to survive years of seasonal use rather than a single thru-hike.

**Weight tradeoffs.** At 2,240 g minimum weight (2,770 g packed), the Elixir 2 is noticeably heavier than MSR's own Hubba Hubba or FreeLite 2, which sit closer to 1,400–1,700 g. If you split it with a tent-mate, you're looking at around 1,150–1,400 g per person — reasonable for a budget backpacking tent, but well outside true ultralight territory. The Fast & Light setup (poles, fly, footprint, no inner) drops that to 1,610 g if you're willing to sacrifice bug protection and full weather sealing.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent stability in wind thanks to the interlinked pole geometry and multiple guy-out points
- Genuinely roomy headroom (40 in peak) for two people to sit up simultaneously
- Durable materials that tolerate rough handling and repeated use
- Footprint included at no extra cost
- Two doors and two vestibules for a true 2-person livability setup
- Fast & Light mode offers a lighter-weight option for fair-weather trips

**Cons**

- At 2,240–2,770 g, it's heavy compared to true ultralight 2P tents
- 84-inch interior length is tight for taller campers once gear is loaded in
- Condensation tends to collect on the foot-end wall, risking a damp sleeping bag
- First-time setup has a learning curve due to the unconventional pole system
- Fly waterproof rating (1,500 mm) is modest compared to pricier MSR shelters

## Who Should Buy This

The Elixir 2 makes the most sense for weekend backpackers, new thru-hikers building their first kit, or car campers who want a genuine backcountry-capable tent without an ultralight price tag. If you're chasing sub-2-lb base weights for a long-distance hike, look elsewhere — but if you value a tent that will survive years of abuse, handle a surprise storm with confidence, and not empty your wallet doing it, this is a strong pick.

## Verdict

The MSR Elixir 2 is a dependable, weather-tough tent that punches above its price point on durability and stability, but it asks you to carry extra weight and tolerate a snug interior length in return. For anyone prioritizing reliability and value over grams, it's an easy 7/10 recommendation — just go in knowing it's a workhorse, not a featherweight.
