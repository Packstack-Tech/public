## Overview

The Bessport 4 Season Camping Tent is a freestanding, double-wall dome with two D-shaped doors and two vestibules, sold to backpackers and campers who want a roomy two-person shelter for very little money. Its appeal is floor area, headroom and a pitch that takes minutes. Its limits are weight, which is well off ultralight territory at 2,350 g, and a "4-season" label that the mesh-heavy design and light fabrics don't fully back up.

## Performance

**Space.** The floor is the headline. At 220 cm long, the tent has room for tall sleepers. One owner who is nearly 2 m tall called it more than adequate for a single person. Another, 6'4", said he usually can't fit in one-person tents but sleeps comfortably in this one. Two people fit, though the 123 cm width is snug for two wide pads, and owners who bring a lot of gear tend to describe it as a roomy solo tent or a tight duo. One reviewer who bought the 3-person version did so specifically to get bags inside. The 110.5 cm peak height lets most people sit up and change clothes without contortions.

**Setup.** This is where the Bessport is easiest to like. The tent is freestanding and clip-attached, and one review timed the pitch at about six minutes. Because it stands without stakes, it can be shifted around a campsite or shaken out before packing. A budget roundup described the color-coded clip system as intuitive enough for a first-timer to manage in the dark. Owners also single out zippers that don't snag, a small virtue that matters at 2 a.m.

**Weather.** Reports split, which is typical for tents at this price. Several owners describe riding out multiple rainstorms and staying dry, and one backpacker reported a night of driving rain and gusts with no wall flex. Others found it did not keep them dry. A budget-tent roundup lists a 3,000 mm waterproof rating and cautions that the pole design can sway in gusty conditions. That fits the fabric: 68D polyester at 190T is a light, entry-level material, not something built for sustained alpine wind or snow load. Seam taping and a welded floor are part of the design, but the tent is best treated as a three-season shelter that handles moderate rain.

**The "4-season" claim.** The listing says 3–4 season, and that's the more honest half. Mesh walls help ventilation and cut down on condensation, but they also let in spindrift and cold air. One winter user said the tent worked well in cold conditions but had a condensation problem. Anyone expecting a true four-season mountaineering shelter, with a stiffer pole structure, solid fabric walls and a bomber guy-out system, will be disappointed. For a calm, cold, snow-free night, it will do.

**Weight and packability.** At 2,350 g, the tent is heavy by the standards of this site's readers, and well above what many double-wall two-person backpacking tents weigh. Split between two hikers, it's about 1.2 kg each, which is reasonable. Carried alone, it's a lot of shelter. The packed size of 42 x 15 cm is compact; owners report it strapping easily to the top of a pack or saddlebag, and one fit it into a compression bag with a sleeping bag for a three-day trip.

**Durability.** Owners praise the aluminum poles and describe the overall build as reasonable for the money, comparable to Naturehike-type budget tents. Nobody is reporting thru-hike mileage on it. The fabrics suggest it's best suited to a few weekends a season rather than months of daily pitching.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Long, tall interior: 220 cm of length and 110.5 cm of headroom suit tall hikers
- Two doors and two vestibules, so each occupant gets their own exit and gear storage
- Freestanding, simple pitch of around six minutes
- Compact packed size of 42 x 15 cm
- Very low price for a double-wall tent with this layout

**Cons**

- 2,350 g is heavy for backpacking, especially solo
- Not a genuine four-season shelter; mesh walls and light fabric limit it in snow and wind
- Mixed owner reports on staying dry in sustained rain
- Condensation reported in cold weather
- Narrow 123 cm width is tight for two people with full-width pads

## Who Should Buy This

Budget-conscious hikers and campers who want a spacious, easy-to-pitch two-door tent for fair-weather weekends, shared trips where the weight is split, or as a first tent. Tall solo hikers who value room over grams will also find it a bargain. Ultralight and thru-hikers should look elsewhere, as should anyone planning winter camping, exposed ridgelines or high-mileage seasons. A tent built for those jobs costs several times as much, and the difference shows in fabric, poles and weight.

## Verdict

The Bessport is a roomy, easy, inexpensive tent that does best when the forecast is mild. It isn't light, and the 4-season label overstates what it can handle. A fair deal for casual three-season use; not a tool for serious weather or long-distance weight savings.
