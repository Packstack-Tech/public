## Overview

The REI Co-op Sahara Shade Hoodie is a long-sleeve, UPF 50+ pullover built for people who would rather wear sun protection than reapply it. The women's version weighs 201.6 g and lists at about $50, though REI has regularly discounted it to the mid-$30s. The trade-off is simple: a soft, stretchy knit that is comfortable enough to wear all day, in exchange for a fabric that runs warmer than the thinnest, most open-weave sun shirts.

## Performance

**Sun coverage.** The hood is what separates this from a plain long-sleeve tee. It has three panels, an internal drawstring and a crossover-style opening at the neck that covers the collarbone area. Even with the hood down, the overlapping neckline covers more skin than a standard crew. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers praised the hood for staying in place once cinched. They also rated the sun protection as good. Switchback Travel noted that many sun hoodies have a non-adjustable hood, which makes the drawcord a real advantage. One reviewer on REI's site logged six hours on exposed summits and found the shoulders, back and forearms fully covered. That same reviewer still put sunscreen on face and neck whenever the hood was down.

**Comfort and fabric.** The knit is the hoodie's best feature. Reviewers describe it as soft, smooth and stretchy. One women's reviewer called it close to buttery, and another compared the thickness to an ordinary polyester workout tee. Raglan sleeves keep the shoulders from binding on scrambles. Thumbholes hold the cuffs in place, which is useful on exposed ridgelines and for covering the backs of the hands. A change to the thumbhole design on the newer regular-fit men's version drew complaints from at least one longtime owner who preferred the original.

**Heat and breathability.** Opinions divide here. Outdoor Gear Lab's Colorado testing found breathability better than the amount of UV protection would suggest, and thru-hikers praise the cooling in hot weather. One reviewer reports finishing the entire PCT in it. Switchback Travel's Wyoming tester found the fabric a bit warm for high-output hiking in the heat. A critical REI owner called the shirt constrictive in hot sun. Another buyer considered it middle of the pack for airflow and pointed to the Ketl Nofry and REI's own Terramar Ventilator as cooler alternatives. There are no vents, and the Sahara isn't offered in white. Expect it to be comfortable in warm, sunny conditions and to feel snug in true desert heat.

**Durability and care.** Switchback Travel's tester put close to two months of near-daily hiking, trail running and climbing on it. It looked nearly new apart from a few loose threads at the cuffs. The hang loop at the collar is thin and one owner suspected it would tear with rough handling. Drying is fast, which suits multi-day trips. Odor resistance is weak, as with most polyester, so a long trip means a noticeable smell.

**Fit.** The cut is relaxed but not baggy, with a scooped hem that works as a casual layer in town. Some buyers say it runs true to size but slightly snug. The hood sits a little long for some wearers and the slick fabric can cause it to slip until it is cinched. There are no pockets, which Outdoor Gear Lab marked as a drawback.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 201.6 g with UPF 50+ coverage for head, neck, arms and hands
- Soft, stretchy knit that stays comfortable over long days
- Cinchable hood with a crossover neck for fuller coverage
- Thumbholes and raglan sleeves suit hiking and paddling
- Good value, especially on sale
- Dries quickly and survives repeated use

**Cons**

- Runs warmer than lightweight, open-weave sun shirts
- No vents and no pockets
- Weak odor resistance
- Slick fabric lets the hood slip until it is cinched
- Thin hang loop
- Hood is too warm for cold temperatures. A reviewer felt chilled in the 50s °F

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers, paddlers and travelers who want a comfortable, affordable sun layer for warm-weather trips. That includes thru-hikers on sunny stretches and anyone tired of reapplying sunscreen to their neck and ears. The soft fabric also suits wearers who plan to use it around camp and in town. People who do most of their hiking in extreme heat or humidity, or who want the most airflow possible, should look at lighter open-knit hoodies. Anyone who needs pockets should also look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Sahara Shade Hoodie covers the basics well: reliable UPF 50+ coverage, a hood that actually stays put, and a soft fabric at a fair price. It is a better all-rounder than a hot-weather specialist. At 201.6 g it earns a place in most warm-weather packs.
