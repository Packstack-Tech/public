## Overview

Within Macpac's lightweight range, the Hesper has the greatest capacity and
the lightest harness — the HeliumAir.

Its construction balances a minimal design with maximum carry capacity, and
the internal frame is removable so you can cut weight and fine-tune your
pack's fit to everything from day missions to thru-hikes.

Think of it as Macpac's answer to the fastpacker who doesn't want to sacrifice
capacity — a pack that works for a long weekend out of the box and can be
stripped back for faster, lighter objectives.

## Performance

**Load Carry & Comfort**

Wilderness Magazine's tester carried a three- to four-day load and found the
harness supportive, stable and comfortable — though noted the shoulder straps
might provide more stability if they were slightly wider, given the pack's
fastpacking-style fit. The waist belt conforms well to the hips thanks to its
minimal padding and flexibility.

That tracks with what you'd expect from a harness marketed as "lightest to date"
— it's competent and breathable, but you're not getting the bombproof load
transfer of a beefier, heavier suspension system.

The recommended load tops out at 16 kg; beyond this would be uncomfortable due
to the torsional flex of the harness, as well as stressing the minimal design.

Keep that ceiling in mind if you're packing for resupply legs or genuinely wet
conditions where everything gets heavier.

Breathable mesh and airflow allowance throughout the harness and back panel
help keep clothing dry

— a meaningful feature in the notoriously humid trail environments of New
Zealand and Australia's east coast.

**Access & Organization**

The pack is secured by a roll-top closure and detachable lid, and an
additional strap runs from the base to the lid to lift loads and provide
greater compression.

That base-to-lid compression strap is genuinely useful when the pack isn't fully
stuffed — it stops gear shifting around on shorter trips.

The removable and extendable lid allows the pack to be customised to suit
weekend trips or expanded for, say, resupplying on a thru-hike.

The pack is hydration compatible and has an extendable and removable lid as
well as a roll-top closure with a long cinch strap. The frame is removable for
smaller loads and weight savings. There are three external stretch pockets for
carrying overflow, poles or wet gear, and dual ice tool attachment points.

Stretchy front and side pockets let you store bottles and snacks outside the
pack, and the detachable shoulder strap fits a 500ml soft flask.

The soft flask attachment on the shoulder strap is a nice touch —

the way the shoulder strap is designed means you can keep the mouthpiece close
by without it flapping as you walk.

Hip belt storage is a weaker point. The hip belt has pockets on each side, but they are too small to be useful for anything larger than an energy bar — and ideally they'd be bigger and able to be securely shut. For a pack aimed squarely at thru-hikers who live out of their hip pockets, this is a real miss.

**Fit**

The pack is available in two sizes, and with the hip belt interchangeable
in-store, it should fit a wide range of body types. At 1.76m, the S2 fitted
well; taller hikers would require the S3.

Note that the torso length is not adjustable — the back length is fixed in each
size.

Users report the length is not adjustable, so it pays to check fit in person
before buying.

If you're between sizes or buying blind online, that's a genuine risk.

**Durability & Materials**

The 81% recycled nylon ripstop material makes this pack both durable and
lightweight, and able to handle a carrying capacity of up to 13–16 kg.

The UTS coating adds water resistance but this isn't a waterproof pack — use a
liner in sustained rain.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roomy external stretch pockets are well-suited for quickly stuffing a
  mid-layer or jacket
- Removable frame and lid let you shave real weight on lighter trips
- The indented foam back panel doubles as a removable seat

  — a clever trail feature

- Interchangeable hip belt available in-store for a dialled-in fit
- Hydration bladder compatible with a dedicated hose port at the top of the
  harness
- Recycled nylon construction is a genuine sustainability plus
- While the base price is steep, Macpac Club membership brings it to a more
  competitive price point

**Cons**

- Hip belt pockets are too small to be useful for anything larger than an
  energy bar
- Shoulder straps could offer more stability if slightly wider, especially
  under heavier fastpacking loads
- Torso length is fixed — no on-the-fly adjustment; in-store fitting is strongly recommended
- At 1,170 g, it sits in the "lightweight" camp rather than "ultralight" — you'll find sub-700 g options at similar volumes
- Single main compartment only; organization relies entirely on the external pockets

## Who Should Buy This

The Hesper 52L is built for multi-day trampers and aspiring thru-hikers — particularly those in Australia and New Zealand — who want a capable, trail-tested pack without jumping to a full expedition pack weight. It sits at the top end of the capacity range for fastpacking-style packs, and with that size comes added versatility for thru-trampers and long-trail aspirants. If you're planning Te Araroa, an extended hut-to-hut circuit, or a mixed-terrain multi-day where conditions can vary, the Hesper earns its keep. It's less suited to sub-8 kg base-weight purists who'll find lighter options at a similar price, or anyone who can't try it on first — the fixed torso sizing makes blind online purchases a gamble.

## Verdict

The Macpac Hesper 52L is a well-rounded, durable pack that hits a smart balance between capacity and carry weight for the AU/NZ trail market. The HeliumAir harness carries comfortably up to its 16 kg ceiling, the removable frame and lid add genuine versatility, and the recycled materials are a plus — but the undersized hip belt pockets and fixed torso length are real trade-offs you should weigh before buying. At full retail it's a stretch; at Macpac Club pricing, it's solid value for a pack you can take from weekend missions to thru-hike starts without swapping gear. **Rating: 7.5/10.**
