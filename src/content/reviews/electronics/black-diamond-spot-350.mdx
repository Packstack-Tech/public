## Overview

The Black Diamond Spot 350 is a three-AAA headlamp that puts 350 lumens, an IPX8 waterproof body and a fool-proof interface into an 85 g package. It suits backpackers, climbers and trail runners who want one light that handles pre-dawn starts and camp chores without a manual. The trade-off is weight and power source: it's heavier than the minimalist lamps thru-hikers favor, and it runs on disposable cells rather than a USB-rechargeable battery.

## Performance

**Brightness and beam.** Peak output is 350 lumens, good for a throw of 86 m on high. That is enough to pick out trail junctions on a fast night descent or read terrain on an alpine approach. Black Diamond's multifaceted lens is meant to squeeze more usable light from the LED, and the company pitches it as a way to save battery too. The honest number to remember is the regulated burn time: 3.75 hours on high under the FL1 standard, with a reserve mode that stretches to 36 hours at a much lower output. Anyone planning to hike at full blast for a whole night will go through batteries fast.

**Controls.** This is where the Spot earns its reputation. Two buttons sit on top of the housing: a large one cycles brightness, and a second switches between beam modes. PowerTap lets a swipe or tap move instantly between full and dimmed output, and Brightness Memory means the lamp comes on at the level it was last set to instead of snapping back to full. Reviewers at Adventure Pending noted how few controls there are compared with many competitors, and owners on retail sites describe it as usable without instructions. A digital lock mode keeps the lamp from switching on in a pack, a small feature that saves a lot of dead-battery grief.

**Close-range and red light.** A proximity mode spreads light wide and low for cooking, sorting gear or reading in a tent, while the red night-vision setting keeps camp dark for neighbors. Strobe is there for emergencies. The low setting is the real workhorse: about 8 m of reach and a rated 200 hours, which is more than a week of nightly camp use before a battery swap.

**Weather.** IPX8 is a stronger waterproofing rating than many headlamps in this price bracket carry. Black Diamond says it is tested to operate at least 1.1 m underwater for 30 minutes. The company also notes that water can enter the battery compartment if the lamp is submerged, so it should be dried thoroughly afterward. For rain, river crossings and wet-weather scrambling, that is more than enough.

**Battery life and weight.** Three AAAs keep the Spot easy to resupply, since the cells are available at any trailhead town, and cold-weather users can swap in lithiums. Retail reviewers describe battery life as fine for moderate use but quick to drain with heavy, high-output use. At 85 g, it is heavier than the featherweight lamps many thru-hikers carry. Adventure Pending flagged the weight and the lack of a recharge option as its main drawbacks. Black Diamond's own SpotLite 160 is the company's lighter, smaller alternative.

**Comfort.** The body was redesigned with a lower profile for better balance than earlier Spots, and the elastic headband is adjustable. Owners generally describe it as comfortable and stable for running and climbing.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Simple two-button interface with PowerTap and Brightness Memory
- IPX8 waterproof housing
- 200-hour low mode and a three-LED battery meter that shows what's left
- Lock mode prevents accidental activation in a pack
- Red light and proximity modes cover camp tasks
- Three-year warranty and a low street price, often $25–40
- AAA cells are easy to replace anywhere

**Cons**

- 85 g is heavy for an ultralight kit
- No rechargeable option
- Only 3.75 hours of regulated output on high
- Heavy high-output use drains batteries quickly

## Who Should Buy This

The Spot 350 fits backpackers, climbers and runners who want one inexpensive, durable headlamp for everything from pre-dawn starts to wet-weather camp use, and who value controls that work with gloves on. It also suits anyone on a long route who wants to resupply batteries in town. Gram-counting thru-hikers who use a light only for camp chores should look at the SpotLite 160 or a similar sub-50 g lamp. Night-riding mountain bikers and anyone who runs at full output for hours should choose a rechargeable lamp with a higher-capacity battery.

## Verdict

The Spot 350 is a dependable, waterproof workhorse with the most intuitive controls in its price class. It isn't light enough to please a dedicated ultralighter, but for most backpackers it delivers more headlamp than it costs. Recommended as a do-everything light, and not as a base-weight trimmer.
