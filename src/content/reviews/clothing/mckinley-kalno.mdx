## Overview

The McKinley Kalno is a men's short-sleeve hiking tee from Intersport's in-house outdoor label. It combines recycled polyester with cotton and a Dry Plus® moisture-wicking treatment, and it usually sells for about €10. The trade-off is that the cotton makes it comfortable and cheap, but it also makes it a mediocre pick for sweaty, multi-week mileage.

## Performance

**Fabric and moisture handling.** The Kalno's composition decides most of what follows. At 65% recycled polyester and 35% cotton, it belongs to the same family as the cotton-poly tees McKinley sells elsewhere in its range. The Dry Plus® finish is a surface treatment meant to pull moisture off the skin and speed up evaporation. A treatment on a blended fabric has limits, though. Cotton holds water, and a tee that holds water on a long climb dries slowly and clings. Pure synthetics designed for thru-hiking avoid that.

**Comfort and fit.** This is where owners are most positive. Of the 10 reviews on Intersport France, the product averages 4.1 out of 5, and 80 percent of buyers recommend it. Reviewers describe a good cut and a true-to-size fit, though one found it just slightly small. The graphic print comes up repeatedly as a selling point. One buyer kept the shirt for the mountain design alone, despite doubts about the fabric.

**Weather suitability.** One reviewer called it a good shirt for shoulder-season temperatures but not light or breathable enough for hiking in hot weather. That fits the fabric blend. Other buyers describe the cloth as very light, so impressions of its weight vary. At 180 g, the Kalno is not a heavy shirt, but the lightest dedicated synthetic hiking tees weigh less. The saving is more noticeable in a ten-day trip's worth of wear than on a day hike.

**Durability.** Build quality draws the most criticism. One owner liked the look but called the seams poor. Another praised the design but feared the print would wear off in the wash. A third rated the fabric quality as unremarkable. These are single reports rather than a pattern. Still, a €10 shirt with a screen-printed graphic is not a garment to run through a season of trail grime and repeated washing and expect it to look new.

**Odor and care.** The Kalno doesn't carry the Polygiene® odor treatment that McKinley puts on some of its pricier tees, such as the Riggo and Rogin. The listing for this model mentions only Dry Plus®. On a multi-day trip, a shirt without an antimicrobial finish picks up smell sooner, and the cotton content doesn't help.

**Value.** Intersport France has run club-member deals that dropped the Kalno to €6.49. Few hiking shirts come cheaper. The listing currently shows it out of stock, and Intersport now lists a Kalno II in eight colors at the same €9.99, so buyers may end up with the newer version.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low price, with deals as low as €6.49
- Recycled polyester makes up most of the fabric
- Owners report a flattering cut and true-to-size fit
- Graphic designs get consistent praise
- Comfortable for cool-weather and shoulder-season walking

**Cons**

- 35% cotton slows drying and holds sweat
- At 180 g, heavier than the lightest synthetic hiking tees
- A reviewer found it not breathable enough for hot-weather hiking
- Isolated reports of weak seams and a print that may fade
- No odor-control treatment listed
- Listed as out of stock at Intersport France, with a Kalno II replacing it

## Who Should Buy This

The Kalno suits day hikers, weekend walkers and travelers who want a presentable, inexpensive tee that also works on trails in mild weather. It also works as a spare town shirt for a hiking trip. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers should look elsewhere. Anyone logging long days in heat, or washing a shirt in a sink every few days, will want a pure synthetic or merino shirt that dries faster and resists smell.

## Verdict

The Kalno is a decent budget casual tee with a hiking label, not a piece of technical kit. Buy it for short outings and cool days at a price that makes durability a minor concern. For multi-day or hot-weather hiking, spend a little more on a lighter all-synthetic shirt.
