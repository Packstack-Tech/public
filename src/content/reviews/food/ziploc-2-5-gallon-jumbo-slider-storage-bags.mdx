## Overview

Ziploc's 2.5 Gallon Jumbo Slider Bag is the largest bag in the company's slider line. It's built for roasts, garden harvests and batch-prepped meals, not for trails. Backpackers have still found uses for it. At 19 g and roughly fifty cents a bag, it works as a food-bag liner, a resupply-box organizer and a bulk-repacking sack. The trade-off is that it's a kitchen product. It's thin, and it isn't designed for hard abrasion or for being submerged.

## Performance

**Capacity and fit.** The bag's reason for existing is volume. Ziploc pitches it at a whole roast or a mountain of produce. On a trail, that translates to a full multi-day food haul in one sack: tortillas, bars, bagged dinners and a bulky bag of trail mix, all without the Tetris of stacking smaller bags. Ziploc says the size makes filling easy. The wide mouth is a real advantage when the contents are lumpy or the hands doing the packing are cold.

**Weight.** At 19 g, the bag adds about two-thirds of an ounce to a food carry. That's more than a quart bag and less than most purpose-built food sacks, which are heavier and cost many times more. Per gram of storage volume, though, it's very efficient. The film is thin, and a rodent, a sharp corner or an aggressive stuffing job will find that out.

**Durability.** Ziploc says the Power Shield film is tougher against punctures and tears. It cites ASTM D4272 dart-drop and ASTM D2582 puncture-propagation testing, with the comparison made against its gallon-size bag. The claim is relative, not an absolute rating. Retail listings for the jumbo slider average about 4.9 stars across a few hundred reviews. Those buyers are mostly shoppers using the bags for freezer and pantry work, and their experience says little about a pack's abrasive interior. Treat each bag as a consumable. A bag that survives one section of trail may not survive a whole season.

**Closure.** The slider is easy to run with gloves or in the dark, which is a genuine upgrade over pinch-seal zippers when fingers are numb. Slider closures on oversized bags do demand a full sweep to the end. A partly closed corner is the usual failure. The bags are marketed for food storage, not waterproofing, so they're not a substitute for a roll-top dry bag. They do keep crumbs, moisture and mild spills contained, and they're a sensible secondary layer inside a pack liner.

**Reusability.** Ziploc says the bags are reusable after hand-washing and air-drying. The company advises against reuse after raw meat, fish or eggs. On a thru-hike, the practical rule is to rinse, dry on a pack lid and keep going until a seam or the slider gives out. A 12-count box can easily cover a long trail's worth of resupply organizing.

**Where it fits in a resupply.** The most useful job for the jumbo bag is in town. Dump a bulk bag of oatmeal, couscous or nuts into it, portion out meals, and mail or carry the lot. It also works as a sleeping-bag-stuff-sack-sized wet-gear isolator and as a trash bag, though both uses are improvised.

**Odor and bears.** The bag isn't an odor-proof barrier, and the manufacturer doesn't claim it is. Anyone hiking in bear or critter country should use it inside a bear canister or hang bag, not as the defense.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 19 g for 2.5 gallons of volume
- Roughly 50 cents a bag, with replacements at any grocery store
- Slider closure works with cold or gloved hands
- Reusable with washing and drying
- BPA-free polyethylene
- Wide opening makes bulk food easy to load

**Cons**

- Thin film that will puncture on rough ground or sharp food
- Not marketed as waterproof, and not odor-proof
- Not designed for trail abrasion or repeated stuffing in a pack
- Raw-protein reuse is not recommended by the maker
- Large slider needs a full sweep to seal reliably
- Sold only in 12-count boxes, which is more than a single trip needs

## Who Should Buy This

Section hikers and thru-hikers who want a cheap, large liner for a food bag, a resupply-box organizer or a bulk repacking sack will get good value here. Budget-minded ultralighters who already keep a stash of freezer bags will find the jumbo size fills a gap those smaller bags can't. Anyone who needs to keep gear dry in a river crossing, or who needs a durable, replaceable food sack for a whole thru-hike, should look elsewhere. A roll-top dry bag or a dedicated odor-resistant sack is the better tool for those jobs.

## Verdict

The Ziploc 2.5 Gallon Jumbo Slider is a good trail accessory for very little money. It's a light, roomy, replaceable bag for sorting food and resupply, and it makes no claims it can't back up. Buy it for repacking and liner duty, and keep a roll-top bag for anything that must stay dry.
