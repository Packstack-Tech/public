## Overview

The Merino 260 Tech Thermal Leggings are icebreaker's midweight, no-frills merino base layer bottom — a slim-fit, 100% merino wool legging meant to go directly against skin under ski pants, snowshoeing gear, or a puffy on cold camp mornings. This is the classic "cold weather workhorse" piece in icebreaker's lineup: not the ultralight summer option, and not the zone-mapped technical version with ventilation panels, just a straightforward warm layer for people who run cold or who need serious insulation once the temperature drops into the teens or below.

## Performance

**Warmth.** This is a genuinely warm piece — 260gsm puts it at the heavier end of icebreaker's base layer spectrum, and it reads that way on the body. One reviewer who wore them under lined nylon pants cross-country skiing in the teens with single-digit windchill <cite index="11-13,11-14">described great 4-way stretch and said they didn't feel cold at all</cite> in those conditions. Another owner echoed the same sentiment for backcountry use, saying <cite index="11-19">these kept them just the right temperature whether skiing or snowboarding</cite>. If you run cold or you're standing around more than moving, this weight class is where you want to be — icebreaker's lighter 200 or 175 options won't cut it in genuinely cold, low-output conditions.

**Comfort and feel.** Merino has a reputation for being scratchy at the wrong micron count, and multiple reviewers specifically called out that these are not itchy. One reviewer said they were <cite index="11-16">worried that 100% wool would be itchy, but these were not itchy at all</cite>, and a men's version reviewer put it plainly: <cite index="9-7">"I've had some Merino that feel scratchy, these are smooth as silk."</cite> The flatlock seams and gusseted crotch construction that runs across icebreaker's whole 260 line show up here too, and in practice that translates to minimal chafing and decent range of motion for a slim-fit garment.

**Breathability and odor control.** This is merino's home turf, and the reviews back it up — a related 260 Tech kids' version reviewer noted their son could wear the leggings <cite index="13-1,13-2">for several days without any complaints of discomfort or smell</cite>, which tracks with what I'd expect from a straight merino jersey fabric with no synthetic blend to trap odor. For multi-day backcountry trips where you're not doing laundry, that's a real practical advantage over synthetic base layers.

**Fit.** The slim fit runs true to icebreaker's normal sizing quirks — several reviewers noted these run small and recommended sizing up, with one saying they <cite index="11-24,11-25">run small, so size up... usually wear a small, but returned them for a medium and they fit perfectly</cite>. Worth ordering a size up if you're between measurements, especially if you plan to size for extra room to layer over other base pieces.

**Durability — the real weak spot.** This is where reviews get split. Most owners report long service life, with one saying <cite index="11-3,11-4">the fit is perfect and they are incredibly warm and comfortable, and they can tell these will last a very long time</cite>. But there's a vocal minority with the opposite experience — one reviewer with two decades of merino base layer experience reported a hole blowing out <cite index="11-10,11-11">after wearing them 3 or 4 times, just from putting them on</cite>, and a French-language customer review of a related 260 model described stitching coming undone after only one winter of use. Merino at this weight is a fine, closely-knit fabric, and it simply doesn't have the abrasion resistance of a synthetic or a wool/nylon blend. Treat these as a next-to-skin layer only — not something to wear solo under a pack's hipbelt or against rough gear for extended periods.

**Waistband.** A recurring minor complaint across the 260 Tech line (including the men's version) is waistband rolling. One reviewer flagged it directly: <cite index="9-2">"My only complaint is the waistband.... it rolled up immediately when I put these on and stayed rolled forever."</cite> It's not universal, but if you've had this problem with past icebreaker base layers, it's worth trying on before committing, or looking at icebreaker's high-rise version which uses a different waistband construction that some reviewers reported holds up better over time.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely warm at 260gsm — suited for real cold, not just "brisk" days
- Merino softness holds up; multiple reviewers surprised by lack of itch
- Strong odor resistance for multi-day wear without laundry access
- Flatlock seams and gusseted crotch minimize chafing and restriction
- Good moisture-wicking performance for a non-synthetic fabric

**Cons**

- Durability is inconsistent — some report holes or blown seams within a handful of wears
- Runs small; sizing up is generally recommended
- Waistband tends to roll for some body types
- Not a do-everything layer — best worn only next-to-skin, not as a standalone hiking pant
- Slower to dry than any synthetic base layer, a real consideration on multi-day trips without a way to dry gear

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right pick for winter hikers, backcountry skiers, snowshoers, and cold-weather backpackers who need serious next-to-skin insulation and want merino's odor and moisture management for trips where laundry isn't an option. It's less suited to people who run hot, who need a do-everything base layer that can also take abuse as a standalone bottom, or who are chasing the lightest possible base layer setup — icebreaker's lighter weight options exist for that.

## Verdict

The Merino 260 Tech Thermal Leggings deliver on warmth, comfort, and odor control in a way that's hard to beat for genuinely cold conditions, and the fabric feel is a clear step up from budget merino blends. Durability is the asterisk — most people get years out of these, but there's a real subset of buyers reporting premature failures, so treat them gently and avoid using them as anything but a dedicated base layer. I'd put this at a 7.5/10: excellent performance for the intended use, docked for inconsistent longevity and a fit that runs small.
