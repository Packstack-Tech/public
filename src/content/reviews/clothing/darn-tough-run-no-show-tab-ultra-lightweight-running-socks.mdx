## Overview

The Run No Show Tab Ultra-Lightweight (style 1039) is Darn Tough's thinnest cushioned sock, cut low enough to disappear inside a trail runner. It suits hot-weather hikers, trail runners and thru-hikers who wear low-volume shoes and want a sock that outlasts the shoes. The trade-off is that it's a $20 sock with almost no ankle coverage, and a low cuff gives sand, grit and brambles an easy way in.

## Performance

**Fit and feel.** The sock is built on fine-gauge yarns that give it the lowest in-shoe profile in Darn Tough's running line. Owners describe a snug, close-to-skin fit that stays put. One reviewer noted that the sock holds firmly in place from the first run. Another, a trail-runner-wearing hiker, logged a month of cool-morning, hot-afternoon hikes with no hot spots. Cushioning is a terry loop pad isolated under the forefoot and heel. It is thin, but testers say it is hard to find in a sock this light. The brand's own reviewers frequently say they get through long runs without blisters.

**Heel tab and cuff.** The small padded tab at the back of the heel keeps shoe collars off the Achilles and stops the sock from slipping down. Buyers cite it as a reason to choose this model over a plain no-show. The cuff is silky and doesn't dig in, though at least one buyer found it a little tight around the ankle.

**Durability.** This is where Darn Tough earns its price. A near 50% nylon share gives the sock a sturdiness that most ultralight merino socks can't match. Advnture's testers ran several hundred kilometers in the sock and washed it dozens of times without seeing any deterioration. Long-term owners report earlier pairs lasting three years or more. Adventure 52 called the long life the main advantage of Darn Tough's lightweight running socks, since many thin competitors wear through quickly or struggle with the washing machine. If one does fail, the lifetime guarantee covers a replacement with no conditions attached.

**Temperature and moisture.** Merino's wicking and quick drying handle sweaty summer miles well, and the thin knit moves heat out of the shoe. Hikers also like the odor resistance on multi-day stretches, and one buyer reported three days of wear between washes with no complaints. It is a warm-weather sock. In cold or wet conditions it provides little insulation.

**Where it falls short.** A low cuff offers no defense against stinging plants, brambles or ticks. The Advnture testers said as much. One trail runner reported blisters after sand worked in at the heel and recommended a taller sock for that terrain. A single reviewer complained of pilling and lint pickup. That complaint is rare against more than 2,200 reviews on Darn Tough's site, which average 4.9 out of 5.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 57.6 g a pair, with cushion under the foot
- Heel tab stays put and protects the Achilles
- Exceptional durability for such a thin sock
- Wicks, dries quickly and resists odor over multi-day wear
- Lifetime guarantee with no conditions
- Made in Vermont

**Cons**

- About $20 a pair, several times the price of generic running socks
- Almost no ankle coverage against debris, brush or ticks
- Sand and grit can enter at the heel on loose terrain
- Cushioning is minimal for hikers who want a plush feel
- Offers little warmth in cold weather
- Some wearers report a tight fit at the ankle and occasional pilling

## Who Should Buy This

Hot-weather trail runners and fastpackers will get the most from it. So will thru-hikers in trail runners who rotate two pairs and want socks that last the whole trail. Buyers who want a no-show sock that stays up and doesn't blister will also be happy. Anyone hiking through tick country, thorny scrub, or soft sand without gaiters should choose a quarter-height or crew sock instead. People who want heavy cushioning or cold-weather warmth should look to Darn Tough's thicker hiking lines.

## Verdict

The 1039 is a thin, tough, quietly comfortable no-show that justifies its price over several seasons of use. It is a better choice than any budget running sock if your feet live in low-cut shoes. Pair it with gaiters or step up to a taller cuff if your route is sandy or overgrown.
