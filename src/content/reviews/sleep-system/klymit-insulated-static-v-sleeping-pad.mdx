## Overview

The Insulated Static V is Klymit's budget cold-weather air pad: a 72-inch, 2.5-inch-thick mattress with V-shaped chambers, side rails and synthetic insulation, aimed at backpackers who want a warm pad without a premium price. It is durable, wide and quick to inflate. It is also heavier and bulkier than the ultralight pads thru-hikers usually carry, and its 4.4 R-value draws more skepticism from owners than the spec sheet suggests.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Reviewers who have used Klymit's insulated Static V pads in the cold generally call them adequate rather than exceptional. A Gear Junkie tester found the 4.4 rating enough for cold weather but felt a chill creep in at zero degrees Fahrenheit. A thru-hiker writing for The Trek used the pad on a shelter floor during a snowy, windy night and said it did its job of cutting heat loss to the cold boards, calling it a good match for cold sleepers. SectionHiker, reviewing the lighter Ultra Light variant, rated it a solid shoulder-season pad and recommended adding a foam layer underneath for snow camping.

The R-value deserves a caveat. Reddit commenters broadly like the pad for three-season use, but a recurring complaint is that the 4.4 figure seems generous, and some owners say they feel misled by it. Retailer listings for the pad also cite an ASTM-tested R-value of 1.9, a very different number. Anyone planning real winter nights should treat this as a pad that needs a closed-cell foam sheet beneath it, not as a standalone four-season solution.

**Comfort.** The 2.5-inch thickness is the pad's best feature. Reviewers praise the cushioning for back and stomach sleepers, and the V-chamber baffles and side rails are designed to keep the sleeper centered. The Trek's reviewer found the rails supportive for side sleeping because air can't escape to the edges when you shift, though a Backpackers.com tester considered it less than perfect for side sleepers. At 23 inches it is about 3 inches wider than a standard 20-inch backpacking pad, which owners appreciate. One Trailspace reviewer who bought it for its compact footprint still described feeling like they were on a balance beam, and noted that a slick sleeping bag shell slides on the brushed fabric.

**Inflation and packing.** Klymit quotes 10 to 15 breaths, and reviewers' counts land in that range: 11 for The Trek's thru-hiker, and 12 for Gear Junkie on the related Peak model. Deflation is the slower half of the job. Testers describe rolling the pad into its stuff sack as a multi-fold, time-consuming process, though the wide-opening flip valve helps. The packed size of 8 x 5 inches is respectable but not class-leading; Backpackers.com called it very respectable without suggesting it would win awards.

**Durability.** The 75D polyester face fabric is the pad's quiet strength. Gear Institute's testers called it tough and expected it to survive several seasons of hard use. The Trek's reviewer slept on it directly on shelter floors without a ground sheet and reported no damage from nail heads or splinters, and a Backpackers.com reviewer used it for cowboy camping on bare dirt. Reddit owners do mention occasional leaks or punctures after long heavy use, which is par for the course with any inflatable.

**Weight.** Reviewers' scales and listings put the insulated Static V at 24 to 25 ounces. The Trek's author put it plainly: at that weight, it is not for gram-counters.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ by 29 g, listed as 680 g for the Regular and 709 g for the Standard, roughly one ounce. That gap is too small to matter on the trail. Buy whichever is in stock at the better price. The pad's R-value, fabric, inflation system and 72-inch listed length are described for the product as a whole, so the choice comes down to availability rather than performance.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 2.5 inches of cushioning, with a 23-inch width that gives restless sleepers room
- Tough 75D fabric that owners and testers trust on rough ground
- Inflates in roughly 10–15 breaths, with a wide flip valve for fast deflation
- Insulated for shoulder-season and cold use at a budget price
- Quiet, without the crinkle of some competing air pads

**Cons**

- At 680–709 g, it is heavy for an ultralight kit
- The 4.4 R-value is questioned by some owners, and retailer listings cite a much lower ASTM number
- Slow to roll up, with a bulkier packed size than premium pads
- Slick surface can leave side sleepers sliding in a slippery bag

## Who Should Buy This

The Insulated Static V suits backpackers who value comfort, durability and price over base weight: shoulder-season hikers, cold sleepers who will pair it with a foam pad, and anyone moving up from a thin foam mat who wants a thick, stable surface. Gram-counting thru-hikers and anyone who needs a verified high R-value for true winter camping should look elsewhere, toward lighter pads with independently tested ratings.

## Verdict

The Insulated Static V is a tough, comfortable and affordable pad that works well in cool conditions and rewards sleepers who want width and cushion. It loses points for its weight and for a warmth rating that real-world owners don't fully back, so it fits best as a three-season workhorse, not a winter specialist.
