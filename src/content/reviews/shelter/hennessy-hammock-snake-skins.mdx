## Overview

Snake Skins are a pair of tapered silnylon tubes that sit gathered on the suspension ropes at each end of a hammock. When it's time to pack, they slide inward and swallow the hammock, bug net and rainfly in one motion. They are built for hammock campers who are tired of stuffing wet nylon into a sack in a downpour. The trade-off is 90 g of extra carry weight for a fix that only works with a gathered-end hammock.

## Performance

The idea is simple, and the system is patented. At camp, the hammock is tied between two trees with the skins still bunched on the ropes. Hennessy's instructions have the user slide each skin off the hammock and onto the rope at the end of the rainfly, then clip out the side guylines. Hennessy says the slide-off takes under ten seconds. In the morning the process runs in reverse: drop the side cords into the folds of the fly, pull the skins back over the whole assembly, and the shelter is a long, skinny snake with only the ropes showing.

Hennessy quotes about 30 seconds for the full collapse. Owners land in the same range. One reviewer put pack-down of hammock, net and fly at 38 to 45 seconds. That is a real gain over the roughly five minutes SectionHiker says it takes to get a full Hennessy system sorted in good conditions.

The biggest payoff is in bad weather. Because the hammock never has to be stuffed or laid out, it can't touch mud, and the fly stays attached and adjusted. Trailspace's reviewer, who gave the product five stars, highlighted that the hammock material stays out of the way and out of the wet. In dry conditions, hammock and fly can be packed together. In rain, the skins let the wet fly travel with the hammock instead of contaminating a sleeping system's stuff sack.

The rest of the packing logistics are modest but real. The finished snake is long, about 82–84 in, and it is usually stuffed into a side pocket or the bottom of the pack. SectionHiker notes this method works with any maker's gathered-end hammock and tarp, not only Hennessy's. Owners have used these with a Warbonnet Superfly and an 11-foot Dream Hammock Sparrow with an underquilt attached.

Fit is the main failure point. The 70D fabric and 6 in opening mean the skins handle standard hammock-and-fly combinations, but the bulkier 70D poly hex fly needs a second pair, per Hennessy. A buyer who tried the narrower XL version with a 12 x 12 ft nylon tarp found it nowhere near wide enough, though the build quality drew no complaints. Hennessy itself says the wider XXL is the easier fit for its larger Safari and Explorer hammocks. Another Trailspace complaint is minor: with everything in the tubes, it is hard to tell which end of the hammock is which.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Collapses hammock, net and fly in about 30 seconds
- Keeps the hammock off the ground and out of mud
- Works with other brands' gathered-end hammocks and tarps
- 90 g and flat when not in use
- Inexpensive at about $20, and included free with hammocks ordered through Hennessy's online store

**Cons**

- Adds weight that a stuff-sack-free ultralighter may not want
- Oversized tarps and the 70D hex fly need a second pair or a different fit
- Ends are hard to tell apart once packed
- Only suits hammocks with gathered ends

## Who Should Buy This

Hennessy owners who don't already have a pair, and anyone using a gathered-end hammock in wet or buggy country, will get the most from them. Section hikers and thru-hikers who break camp in the rain, or who want to leave camp in minutes, are the natural audience. Skip them if the shelter is a flat tarp with a separate hammock, if the hammock has a non-gathered design, or if the goal is a base weight so low that 90 g has to justify itself against a plain stuff sack. Those hikers also lose little by packing the old way.

## Verdict

Snake Skins do one job well and cheaply: they turn the worst part of hammock camping, breaking down in a storm, into a half-minute chore. For anyone with a compatible hammock, the 90 g is easy to justify. Check the fly size before buying, and order a second pair if it's the hex.
