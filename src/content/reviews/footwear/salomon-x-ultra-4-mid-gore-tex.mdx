## Overview

The X Ultra 4 Mid GORE-TEX is Salomon's answer to a common request: a boot with ankle coverage and a waterproof membrane that still moves like a trail runner. At 854 g for a men's size 9 pair, it sits well below traditional leather boots. The trade-off is a soft, lightly cushioned platform that suits maintained trails better than heavy loads or rough off-trail travel.

## Performance

**Comfort and break-in.** Nearly every reviewer lands on the same word: soft. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers gave it high marks for out-of-the-box comfort, noting that the leather-and-textile upper conforms to the foot easily. GearJunkie's testers hiked 5 miles on day one with no break-in. The padded heel tab, collar and tongue get repeated praise, and the toe box is roomy enough that testers reported no toe compression.

**Weight and agility.** Outdoor Gear Lab's men's testers called it light enough that they reached for it about as often as their low-cut trail runners, because it weighs nearly the same but adds ankle coverage. For a waterproof mid, 854 g a pair is unusually low. On a long climb or a fast descent, that shows up as less swing at the foot and a pace closer to trail-runner than boot.

**Traction.** The Contagrip outsole is the other consistent strength. Testers describe confident bite on rock, scree, roots and mud, and the lugs shed muddy trail well. A hiker logging 72 miles on wet granite reported a single minor slip. The dissenting view comes from an owner scrambling a wet Welsh ridge, who found the grip less convincing than Salomon's marketing suggested. Like most 4.5 mm lugged outsoles, it is a hiking tread, not a mountaineering one.

**Waterproofing.** The GORE-TEX membrane does its job. Testers report dry feet through heavy rain and creek crossings, and one owner describes submerging a foot up to the tongue without a leak. The usual caveat applies: the membrane limits airflow, and breathability is rated moderate. Once water comes in over the collar, it stays in.

**Support and protection.** This is where opinions split. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers credit the Active Support straps and chassis with making the boot steadier off-trail, and one UK reviewer said her feet felt well supported. GearJunkie's testers found the soft collar left the boot feeling a little unstable and recommend it mainly for hikers who stay on trail. Several reviewers also point to thin midsole cushioning: the boot lets a hiker feel the ground, closer to a hiking shoe than a padded boot. The reinforced toe cap and chassis shield the forefoot from rock strikes without adding bulk.

**Fit.** Sizing reports conflict. GearJunkie's testers found it a half-size large and suggest sizing down. Treeline Review found it true to size with an average width, and Outdoor Gear Lab's women's testers describe a generous fit that favors wider feet. Hikers with narrow feet should try a pair on first. A forward-sliding foot on descents is the likely result of a size too big.

**Durability.** The synthetic-heavy upper is lighter than full leather and gives up some longevity for it. Outdoor Gear Lab lists it as less durable than heavier boots. Owner reports vary: one Appalachian Trail hiker said the boots were still in good shape at roughly 500 miles, while a daily wearer said the outsole and GORE-TEX wore out after about a year. The fabric lace eyelets are another point reviewers flag as likely to wear faster than metal.

**Lacing.** The most common owner complaint is laces that loosen and need retying through the day. Swapping in aftermarket laces is the usual fix, but it is an annoyance on a $175 boot.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 854 g a pair is light for a waterproof mid
- Comfortable straight out of the box, with a plush collar and tongue
- Contagrip outsole grips well on rock, mud and wet roots
- GORE-TEX stays dry in rain and shallow crossings
- Reinforced toe cap and chassis protect without bulk

**Cons**

- Thin midsole cushioning transmits trail texture
- Soft collar feels less stable on rough or off-trail terrain
- Laces loosen, and fabric eyelets may wear sooner
- Sizing reports conflict, so fit is worth trying in person
- Upper and outsole durability trail heavier leather boots

## Who Should Buy This

Day hikers, weekend backpackers and three-season thru-hikers who stick mostly to maintained trail and want dry feet and ankle coverage without boot weight. It is also a sensible pick for wet climates where a mesh trail runner stays soaked all day. Hikers carrying heavy loads, those who spend a lot of time off-trail on uneven ground, and anyone who wants a stiff, supportive platform should look at a heavier boot. Ultralighters who prefer to let shoes drain and dry should skip the membrane entirely.

## Verdict

The X Ultra 4 Mid GTX does what it sets out to do: a comfortable, grippy, waterproof mid at a weight few boots match. Its softness and modest durability keep it from being a do-everything backpacking boot, but for trail-bound hikers who want the lightest waterproof ankle-high option from a mainstream brand, it is an easy recommendation.
