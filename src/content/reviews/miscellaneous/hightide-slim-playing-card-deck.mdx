## Overview

Hightide, a Fukuoka stationery brand that has been making desk goods since 1994, has applied its design sense to the camp deck. The Slim Playing Card Deck is a full 54-card set in water-resistant PVC, cut to roughly half the width of a standard deck so it slides into a hip-belt pocket, a bikepacking feed bag or a shirt pocket. The trade-off is that it saves volume, not weight, and it costs about $20.

## Performance

**Packability.** This is the whole argument for the deck. The retailer's pitch is a bikepacking one: how many times has a standard deck failed to fit in the space available? At 3.6 x 9.0 x 2.1 cm packed, the Slim slots into places a regular tuck box never will, such as the side pocket of a frame bag, a mesh pocket on a pack strap, or the lid of a food-storage bag. A conventional box sits wider and stubbier and tends to end up crushed at the bottom of a stuff sack. The long, narrow shape of the Slim is easy to fit alongside a phone, a lighter and a headlamp.

**Weather and grime.** The PVC construction is the second selling point. Hightide says the cards can be rinsed under water when they get dirty, which is a real advantage after a night of games with dirt, sunscreen and trail-mix residue on everyone's fingers. Paper decks warp and go soft when damp, while plastic cards shrug off condensation, spilled coffee and wet tent floors. For camping, beach and river trips, that durability is the reason to choose plastic at all.

**Weight.** Here the Slim gives little back. At 85.9 g, it weighs about as much as a standard paper deck, which typically falls somewhere around 90 to 100 g. Anyone counting grams on a base-weight spreadsheet gets no savings; they get a smaller object that weighs the same. Plastic cards are denser than paper, and the narrow format doesn't offset that. Thru-hikers who carry 30-gram paper mini decks or a handful of cut-down cards will find this a heavier option.

**Handling.** At 1.4 in wide, the cards are about the width of a thumb pad and a half. Hightide pitches the narrow shape as easy for small hands to hold, and in a tent vestibule or on a sleeping pad it takes less space than a full spread. The same narrowness makes a full 54-card shuffle a different exercise from a standard deck, and anyone who wants a casino-style riffle will need to adapt. Games that need a wide fan of cards, like big-hand rummy variants, suit the format less well than quick two-player games such as cribbage, gin or speed.

**Value.** The Slim costs $20 at SimWorks, and a UK stockist lists it at £16. A full-size waterproof PVC deck from the usual outdoor-gear suppliers costs far less. The extra money buys the format and the Hightide design polish, not a performance upgrade.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Packed footprint of 3.6 x 9.0 x 2.1 cm fits pockets and bike bags that standard decks do not
- PVC cards rinse clean and resist rain, condensation and spills
- Full 54-card deck, not a trimmed or novelty set
- Cleanly designed packaging from a respected stationery brand

**Cons**

- 85.9 g is no lighter than a standard deck, so there are no weight savings
- $20 is a lot for a deck of cards
- Narrow cards change the feel of shuffling and holding large hands
- PVC adds weight over paper alternatives

## Who Should Buy This

The Slim suits bikepackers and campers who have more trouble with space than with grams: people with tight frame bags, small hip-belt pockets or a car-camping kit that has to double as a day bag. It also suits anyone who wants cards that survive a wet trip and can be rinsed off in a stream or sink. Ultralight hikers who track every gram should look elsewhere, and so should anyone who wants to play with a standard-width deck or just wants the cheapest card game available. A paper mini deck in a zip bag weighs less, and a full-size plastic deck costs less.

## Verdict

The Hightide Slim Playing Card Deck does one thing well: it makes a durable, wipe-clean deck fit into places a normal one cannot. It does not save weight, and the price is steep for what is a novelty-format item. Buy it for the pocketability and the weather resistance, and skip it if the goal is a lighter pack or a cheaper deck.
