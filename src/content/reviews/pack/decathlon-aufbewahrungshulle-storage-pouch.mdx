## Overview

Decathlon's Aufbewahrungshülle is a zippered, mesh-topped fabric pouch that sorts clothing and small kit inside a backpack or travel bag. This 30 × 20 × 9 cm, 5.4 L pouch is the small size in the Forclaz Commute organizer set, which pairs two of them with one larger pouch. The trade-off is classic Decathlon: very low cost and sensible design, paid for with weight that ultralight packers will notice.

## Performance

The pouch is a simple box with a big job: stop a pack from becoming a single undifferentiated pile. The zipper opens wide, so the contents are accessible without excavating, and the mesh top panel lets the owner see what's inside without unzipping it. At 5.4 L, it holds roughly a base layer top and bottom, a pair of socks and a hat, or a day's worth of small items. A pair of them splits "worn" from "clean" or "sleep" from "hike" with minimal fuss.

The shape is the point. At 30 × 20 × 9 cm, the pouch is a flat brick that stacks against the back panel of a 40–60 L pack and slides into the footprint of a carry-on. Loose stuff sacks tend to fill dead space unevenly; a defined rectangle packs more predictably. The flip side is that the structure is the weight. Decathlon lists each small pouch at 60 g, and the full three-piece set at 200 g. A plain stuff sack of similar volume weighs a fraction of that, so anyone counting grams pays a measurable penalty for tidiness.

Weather protection is nil. The polyester carries a polyurethane coating, but Decathlon states plainly that the pouches are not waterproof, and a zipper plus a mesh panel is not a barrier against a river crossing or a day of sideways rain. Clothing that must stay dry still needs a pack liner or a dry bag, with this pouch serving as the organizer inside it.

Owner sentiment is strong. The set carries a 4.9 out of 5 rating across roughly 2,500 reviews on Decathlon's German site, a volume of feedback that is unusual for an organizer costing less than a sandwich-and-coffee lunch. That scale of positive response points to a product that does what it says, holds up to ordinary travel use, and rarely disappoints at the price. It does not suggest a tested thru-hike pedigree, and the polyester-and-zipper construction is the kind of thing that wears at a seam or a zipper pull before it fails outright.

Weight-conscious hikers who want to keep the organizer but shed some grams will find it a hard sell. Those who value quick access and clean packing over the last 50 g will not.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low price, about €3.33 per pouch when bought as the set
- Mesh top panel shows contents without opening
- Wide zipper opening makes loading and retrieval easy
- Rectangular 5.4 L shape stacks neatly and uses pack space predictably
- Folds flat when empty
- Highly rated by owners, with a 4.9 average across roughly 2,500 reviews

**Cons**

- 60 g per pouch is heavy next to simple stuff sacks
- Not waterproof
- Only sold as part of a three-piece set
- Zipper and fabric are less proven on long trails than on holiday trips

## Who Should Buy This

Section hikers, weekend backpackers, and travelers who split time between a trekking pack and a carry-on will get the most from it. It suits anyone who wants to separate clothing categories in a 40–60 L pack and prefers a visible, structured pouch to a shapeless sack. It also rewards people who are tired of paying 20 to 30 euros for branded packing cubes that do the same job.

Gram-counting thru-hikers and anyone who needs weather protection should look elsewhere: a silnylon or Dyneema stuff sack for weight, or a roll-top dry bag for wet-weather clothing and sleep systems.

## Verdict

The Aufbewahrungshülle is a competent, inexpensive organizer that makes a travel or trekking pack easier to live in, and its mesh window and rectangular shape are genuine conveniences. At 60 g per 5.4 L, it is a comfort item rather than an ultralight one, and it earns a recommendation for casual and mixed-use packers, not for base-weight chasers.
