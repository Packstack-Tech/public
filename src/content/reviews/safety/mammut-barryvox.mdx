## Overview

The Mammut Barryvox S is the Swiss brand's top-tier avalanche transceiver: a three-antenna digital unit with an analog mode, a big graphic display and a deep menu aimed at guides, patrollers and serious ski tourers. It earns its place on a Packstack page for the same reason it earns a place in a pack: at 210 g with batteries, it is one of the lighter full-featured beacons, and it is the only piece of kit here that exists to be used by someone else on the worst day of your trip. The trade-off is that much of what it offers goes unused by anyone who isn't running multiple-burial scenarios for a living.

## Performance

**Search speed and range.** Reviewers who have run the Barryvox S side by side with older Mammut models describe a clear jump in processing speed. Fine search no longer requires creeping along while the display catches up. Ski Magazine's tester picked up signals on the Barryvox S ahead of a BCA Tracker 3 on every comparison run. Mammut's 70 m search strip is among the widest in the category, and HikeForPow found it held up to the claim. Whitelines adds a caveat: readings at the very edge of range aren't always accurate, though the reach still helps on a large debris field.

**Multiple burials.** This is where the S pulls away. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found it beat every other model they tried at multiple-burial scenarios. It also posted the best results in simulated AMGA, ACMG and Pro Level 1 rescue exercises, which involve three or more burials with at least two close together. Once a signal is marked, the unit points to the next closest one.

**Interface.** The display is large, bright and legible in direct sun and through polarized lenses. Audio prompts back it up, and the unit can be operated one-handed and with gloves on. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers liked changing settings on the device itself instead of through an app, and the unit accepts firmware updates. Backcountry Skiing Canada's reviewer found the menu easier to navigate than the old Pulse's despite the added options.

**Rescue Send.** If a second slide catches a rescuer mid-search, the beacon reverts to send mode after being motionless for four minutes. The delay is adjustable. It is a small feature with a large payoff in the rare event it's needed.

**Battery and build.** Owners report roughly 300 hours of send time on alkalines, and Mammut rates lithiums at 350–400 hours. The casing is thick plastic with rubberized sides that grip in wet conditions. Reviewers call it tough.

**Weak points.** The rigid holster-style harness holds the beacon securely, but Beacon Reviews found it very hard to extract with gloves on and nearly impossible to re-seat while wearing gloves and a bulky jacket. One owner review flags the harness clip as fragile. In spike-handling tests, the same site saw the S occasionally report a momentarily short distance when it wasn't directly over the buried transmitter. Its analog range is also less usable than the spec suggests: the extra distance is a faint beep you have to be quiet to catch.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 210 g with batteries; smaller and lighter than several rivals, including the BCA Tracker 2
- 70 m search strip and fast signal processing
- Class-leading multiple-burial handling in Outdoor Gear Lab's tests
- Large, sun-readable display with intuitive prompts
- Adjustable Rescue Send auto-revert
- Firmware updatable; Pro Mode and fleet settings via W-Link for guides
- Long send life on common AAA cells

**Cons**

- Expensive; the standard Barryvox covers most of the same ground for less
- Pro-level search options go unused by most recreational users
- Holster is awkward to work with gloves on and a thick jacket
- Edge-of-range readings are imprecise
- Occasional spike reporting in independent testing

## Who Should Buy This

The Barryvox S suits ski tourers, mountaineers, guides and anyone who regularly travels in avalanche terrain with a partner or group, and who will practice until the interface is second nature. Ski Magazine's reviewer recommends it even for newer skiers who expect their backcountry days to climb. Thru-hikers crossing early-season Sierra or Cascade snow, or hikers who only wander into avalanche terrain a few times a year, should look at the standard Barryvox. Outdoor Gear Lab and HikeForPow both point to it as the sensible step down. A beacon is only as good as its owner's practice, and neither model replaces a probe, a shovel and a formal avalanche course.

## Verdict

The Barryvox S is among the fastest, most capable transceivers on the market, and at 210 g it adds little to a pack. It is the right choice for frequent backcountry users and professionals. Occasional users will do just as well, and spend less, with the standard Barryvox.
