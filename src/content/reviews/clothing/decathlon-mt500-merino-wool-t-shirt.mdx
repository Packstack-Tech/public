## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is Decathlon's women's short-sleeve merino trekking tee. It is a 91 g shirt built for hikers who want wool's odor control without paying Icebreaker or Smartwool prices. The headline trade-off is the polyamide reinforcement: it makes the shirt tougher and cheaper, and it also lets odor creep in sooner than it does in pure merino.

## Performance

**Weight and warmth.** At 115 gsm, this is a true warm-weather merino. It sits well below the 190 gsm used in the MT500 long-sleeve, a shirt reviewers describe as a cool-weather piece that runs too warm for July. The short-sleeve is the one to pack for summer trekking and shoulder-season layering. At 91 g, it weighs about what a lightweight synthetic tee does, which is not something most merino shirts can claim.

**Softness.** The wool is 18.5 micron, fine enough that testers of the MT500 line consistently report no itch. The softness has limits. Reviewers comparing the MT500 to Icebreaker, Arc'teryx and Devold put it a step behind on hand-feel. They also say it holds up better than Smartwool in durability. Buyers who want a silky next-to-skin feel will notice the difference. Hikers who want a shirt that survives a season of pack straps probably won't care.

**Durability.** The polyamide is spun around the wool fiber rather than blended in loosely, and that is the point of the Beta-Spun construction. The wool takes the skin contact and the nylon takes the abrasion from hip belts and shoulder straps. Testers of the long-sleeve version saw no premature wear. One long-term reviewer of the MT500 boxers reported that the stitching and fabric outlasted a pair of Icebreakers. Pilling is possible over time. Reviewers of the line also recommend air drying and no tumble dryer.

**Odor.** The shirt is lab-tested to ISO 17299-3, the standard Decathlon cites for odor control. On trail, it is good, not magical. Testers say the polyamide content means odor eventually breaks through. A two- to four-day stretch between washes is realistic, and a rinse and a hang-dry resets it. Pure merino lasts longer. A thru-hiker rotating one shirt for a week without washing will notice the difference.

**Moisture and drying.** Wool absorbs moisture without feeling clammy, and it holds some warmth when wet. Testers of the MT500 line report that it dries quickly enough to wash mid-trek and wear again the same day. At this fabric weight, the shirt should dry faster than the heavier long-sleeve does.

**Fit.** Decathlon cuts MT500 tops close to the body for moisture transfer. Listings on its US site for MT500 long-sleeves flag that they run small, and customers there recommend sizing up. Owners who want a looser, hang-off-the-body feel should do the same here.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 91 g with a 115 gsm fabric suits summer and warm-weather trekking
- 18.5-micron wool is itch-free for most wearers
- Polyamide-wrapped yarn adds abrasion resistance under pack straps
- Multi-day odor control, with ISO 17299-3 lab testing behind it
- Price sits well under premium merino brands

**Cons**

- Less soft and refined than Icebreaker or Devold
- Synthetic content means odor eventually breaks through on long stretches without washing
- Fitted cut; sizing up is common advice for MT500 tops
- Pilling is possible, and air drying is the safe care routine
- Muted color range

## Who Should Buy This

This shirt suits warm-weather hikers, section hikers and travelers who want merino's multi-day wearability on a budget. It fits those who put a pack on daily and want a tee that survives hip-belt abrasion. It also works for thru-hikers who wash every few days and don't mind rinsing in a creek. Gram-counters who want the lightest possible wool or a premium hand-feel should spend more. Hikers who go a week or more between washes should buy pure merino.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 doesn't pretend to be a luxury shirt. It is a light, durable, fairly priced merino tee that handles several days of trail sweat before it needs a wash. For most hikers it is the better buy over pricier brands, and it earns a 7.5 out of 10.
