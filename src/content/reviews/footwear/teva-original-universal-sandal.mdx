## Overview
The Original Universal is the sandal that grew out of a 1984 Grand Canyon river trip: a flat EVA footbed, a grippy rubber outsole and two hook-and-loop straps that do most of the work. It suits hikers who want a cheap, adjustable camp shoe or water-crossing sandal, and it works as a daily driver on mellow terrain. Its limit is the same as its appeal. It has almost no structure, so it is comfortable and light on the foot but offers little support once a pack gets heavy.

## Performance

**Comfort.** The soft, squishy EVA needs no break-in. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found the sandals comfortable from the first wear, thin enough to feel the ground and cushioned enough to protect from it. The flip side is a footbed with no arch support and no contouring. Testers say hikers with high arches, or anyone who wants a firm platform under a loaded pack, should look elsewhere. GearJunkie's reviewer found the foam pleasant for short outings but expected foot fatigue over a full day. The fit runs slightly narrow, and wide-footed wearers report a tight squeeze. There are no half sizes.

**Straps and fit.** The dual-strap system is the sandal's best feature. CleverHiker's tester notes that the straps make the sandals quick to put on and take off, and that they can be readjusted without sitting down or taking off a pack. That matters at river fords, where shoes come off and go on several times in a mile. The forefoot strap also takes up slack on narrow feet. The one common complaint is that the webbing can feel scratchy on bare skin during the first few wears. Outdoor Gear Lab says it softens with use.

**Traction and stability.** The varied tread grips well on dirt, gravel, grass and sand. Testers say the sandals handled easy mountain trails and dry surfaces. Steep, slick or rocky ground is a different story. The sole is flexible enough that RunRepeat's lab rated its torsional rigidity 1 on a 5-point scale. Its verdict was that these are not serious hiking sandals, especially with a backpack on rough terrain. The straps do keep the foot from sliding around on the footbed. Outdoor Gear Lab did note that wet EVA can feel slick underfoot, which makes the Original Universal a poor choice for scrambling over wet rock.

**Water and drying.** The open design keeps water from pooling, and the polyester webbing dries faster than leather or padded uppers. Testers handle mellow stream crossings in them without trouble. One reviewer found drying slower than expected, so they are not as quick as a bare-bones foam sandal.

**Durability.** Reports are mostly favorable. CleverHiker's tester wore one pair for three years without visible wear and described it as durable for its weight. Other long-term owners report similar longevity. GearJunkie's reviewer was less convinced, saying the sandals do not feel especially robust. Heavy daily wear will wear down the rubber outsole first.

**Weight.** At 306 g for a women's average size and 369 g for the men's, the Original Universal is not an ultralight camp shoe. It is fine for a hiker who wants a real sandal rather than a foam slide, but the weight adds up for gram counters. Most of the difference between the versions is simply a larger sandal.

## Choosing a Version
The two versions differ in size and fit rather than features. Both use the same EVA midsole, rubber outsole, recycled webbing and strap system.

- **Standard (306 g):** The women's cut, on a narrower last. It suits smaller and narrower feet, and the forefoot strap handles any remaining slack. It is the lighter of the two.
- **Men's (369 g):** The same design scaled up for larger feet. The extra 63 g reflects the extra foam, rubber and webbing, not a different build. Hikers with wide feet should still try a pair first, because testers consistently call the footbed narrow.

Neither version comes in half sizes, so wearers between sizes need to decide whether to go up or down.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Dual hook-and-loop straps adjust quickly, even with a pack on
- No break-in period
- Soft EVA cushioning that is easy to wear for hours
- Good traction on dirt, gravel, sand and grass
- Quick-drying recycled webbing handles mild stream crossings
- Long-term owners report years of use
- Low price compared with technical hiking sandals

**Cons**
- No arch support and a very flexible sole
- Not suited to loaded miles, steep terrain or wet rock
- Straps can scratch until they soften
- Runs narrow, with no half sizes
- Heavier than minimalist camp-shoe options

## Who Should Buy This
Buy it if you need a cheap secondary shoe for camp, hostel floors, river fords and warm-weather town days, or a travel sandal that will double as a casual walking shoe. It suits hikers with neutral or narrow feet who value easy adjustment over structure. It is not a primary trail shoe. Anyone planning all-day miles with a pack, or who needs arch support, should look at a sandal with a stiffer, contoured platform and a more aggressive outsole. Gram-counters will find lighter options for camp use.

## Verdict
The Original Universal is a well-liked, long-lived, inexpensive sandal that does what it was designed for: easy walking, easy straps and easy water. It falls short as a main hiking shoe, because the support is too thin for a loaded pack. For camp, crossings and casual days, it is a sound buy.
