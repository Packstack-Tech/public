## Overview

The DJI Neo Intelligent Flight Battery is the only power source for the DJI Neo, the 135 g palm-launch drone that has become a popular way to get aerial footage without carrying a camera-drone kit. For backpackers, the battery is the real range limiter: one pack is about 18 minutes of flying, and the trade-off is a spare that costs 45 g and a few dollars per minute of airtime. It is a purpose-built, no-frills part. It does its job and asks for nothing else.

## Performance

**Flight time.** DJI's 18-minute figure comes from a laboratory run: forward flight at a steady 2 m/s, no wind, near sea level, photo mode, 100% to 0%. DJI itself notes that results vary with environment, use and firmware. Real trail flying is rarely that polite. Ridgeline wind, climbing, Sport-mode sprints and subject tracking all draw more than a lazy glide in a lab, so a working figure shorter than 18 minutes is the sensible planning number. The Neo's rated wind resistance is 8 m/s, and a battery working against that is not sipping. Mounting the propeller guards costs about a minute of rated flight, bringing the figure to roughly 17.

**Weight in context.** At 45 g, the battery accounts for about a third of the Neo's 135 g takeoff weight, which is why a spare is such a small burden: three batteries together weigh 135 g, the same as the aircraft itself, and represent a rated 54 minutes of flying. At 10.5 Wh, the pack works out to roughly 233 Wh per kilogram. A spare adds less weight than most people's phone charger and cable and nothing like the heft of a camera-drone setup.

**Takeoffs and landings.** DJI says a full charge supports at least 20 palm takeoffs and landings in succession, which suits the way the Neo is actually used on trail: launch from a hand, grab a 20-second orbit or follow shot, land, repeat. Short clips use the battery more efficiently than a single long hover.

**Charging.** The weakest part of the workflow is the wait. Charging inside the drone through USB-C tops out at 15 W and takes about 50 minutes for a full cycle. The Two-Way Charging Hub, which comes in the Fly More Combo, pushes 60 W across three batteries in about 60 minutes, which is the better option in a hostel or hut with a wall outlet and a USB-PD brick. In the backcountry, the practical answer is to charge from a power bank able to deliver PD output. The charging window of 5° to 40° C means a battery that spent a night in a frozen tent should be warmed in a jacket or sleeping bag before it goes on a charger.

**Pricing and availability.** DJI lists the battery at $59. Retailer prices have ranged from roughly $39 at B&H and some specialty shops to $75 and $89 at others, with stock at several dealers intermittently sold out. A $59 spare buys about 3.3 dollars per rated minute of flight; at the lower prices, closer to 2.2. Buying from DJI or an authorized dealer keeps the warranty intact.

**Travel.** At 10.5 Wh, the pack sits far below the 100 Wh threshold that triggers airline restrictions on spare lithium batteries, so a handful of them can ride in a carry-on. Spares should travel in carry-on luggage, not checked bags, as with any lithium cells.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 45 g per spare, a third of the aircraft's own weight
- Rated 18 minutes of flight and at least 20 palm takeoffs and landings per charge
- 10.5 Wh capacity keeps carry-on air travel simple
- Charges in the drone at 15 W or three at a time on the hub
- Official $59 price is modest next to the cost of the drone

**Cons**

- Fits only the DJI Neo
- The 18-minute rating comes from still-air lab conditions; wind and aggressive flying shorten it
- Charging in the aircraft takes about 50 minutes, so spares only help if they are charged before the trip
- Charging is limited to 5° to 40° C, which complicates cold-weather use
- Retailer pricing is inconsistent and stock is spotty

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who already owns a Neo and wants to shoot more than one session per day should carry at least one spare. A thru-hiker or section hiker who films ridge, summit and camp footage in short bursts, then recharges from a power bank at town stops, gets the most from the weight. The battery makes less sense for anyone who flies casually once or twice a trip; the single pack that ships with the drone covers that. It is also not an option for owners of other DJI drones, since it is specified for the Neo only.

## Verdict

The Neo battery is a simple, light and fairly priced way to double or triple the drone's flying time at a weight cost most pack lists will absorb. It is not exciting, but it is the one accessory that most improves how the Neo performs on a long trip. Buy it at or near $59 from an authorized dealer, charge it before the trailhead, and plan on less than 18 minutes in wind.
