## Overview

The Terrebonne Trail Joggers are Patagonia's do-everything lightweight pant: a slim-cut, 2.7-oz recycled polyester ripstop jogger built for trail running, hiking, bouldering, and yoga, with enough polish to wear straight into a coffee shop afterward. They're aimed at anyone who wants one pair of pants that can pack down small, dry fast, and survive a sweaty trail day without feeling like technical gear.

## Performance

On the trail, the Terrebonne's main selling point is how little you notice it's there. At 170 g, it's genuinely light for a pant with pockets and a real waistband, and multiple reviewers echo that impression — one hiker who wore them for a week on Iceland's Laugavegur trail called them <cite index="1-1,1-2,1-3">breathable, dry quickly, and super light, with no complaints.</cite> That kind of unqualified praise shows up a lot in the field: another long-term owner said <cite index="1-20,1-21,1-22">they feel good on the skin, the cut and fit is great and it's perfect on hikes, backed by Patagonia, so the quality is unmatched.</cite>

The stretch is real and useful. The 100% recycled polyester ripstop has four-way give, and testers specifically called out that <cite index="2-2">the Terrebones are lightweight, breathable and generously stretchy, so you never feel restricted on a steep incline.</cite> One reviewer who put them through actual trail runs found <cite index="3-12,3-13">they feel comfortable while running, and while they do get a little tight when pushing hard, they flex and stretch just enough to not restrict your movements.</cite>

Weather resistance is modest but functional — this is a DWR-treated ripstop, not a hardshell. A tester in Rocky Mountain National Park noted <cite index="6-13">the fabric wicked moisture efficiently during sweaty climbs and dried swiftly after a brief encounter with an alpine rain shower.</cite> Another reviewer appreciated that <cite index="2-6">the quick-drying fabric and water-repellent finish handle stray raindrops or snowflakes with ease—without that stiff, crunchy feel some weather resistant fabrics have.</cite> Don't expect this to replace a rain shell in a downpour, but it'll shrug off drizzle and trail spray just fine.

Packability is a genuine strength for anyone counting grams. One retailer review noted <cite index="4-7">this plush fabric is also so light and compressible that these joggers are able to be packed down to the size of the back pocket for easy storage</cite> — useful if you're carrying them as an emergency layer rather than wearing them all day.

Fit and function details are where opinions get more textured. The elastic waistband is comfortable but loose enough that several reviewers flagged sagging during dynamic movement. One tester put it plainly: <cite index="3-16,3-17,3-18,3-19">there's a reason why Patagonia chose to include a tied waistband because you'll need it — untied, you'll really notice the backside getting low when squatting, but tie them up and this problem resolves itself.</cite> The same reviewer added a minor gripe that <cite index="3-20">the drawstring should definitely be longer.</cite> On sizing, at least one reviewer flagged the 30" inseam as a limiter, saying <cite index="1-36">I would not recommend these if you are long-legged.</cite>

Pockets are a quiet highlight. The construction earned specific praise: <cite index="3-21,3-22,3-23">front pockets are just the right size for whatever you need, and the pocket construction is unique in that mesh fabric for each pocket is continuous along the waistband, connecting each pocket — that design tends to keep the pockets in place and not easily turned inside-out.</cite>

On durability, the picture is mostly positive but not unanimous. Several long-term owners report the joggers holding up well over years of hard use — one Backcountry reviewer said they'd worn the same pair climbing, caving, and to the grocery store for two years straight without failure. But one reviewer with extended use flagged a fabric quirk: <cite index="3-1,3-2">like most Patagonia apparel, these feature recycled fabrics throughout, and the fabric quality, feel and performance is outstanding, but they have shrunk over time.</cite> If you're on the fence between sizes, that's worth factoring in — maybe don't size down.

One more caveat worth flagging for anyone shipping across state lines: at least one retailer notes <cite index="4-10">this item is made with PFAS chemicals and cannot be shipped to some states</cite>, which seems to conflict with Patagonia's own PFAS-free DWR claim — likely a labeling/compliance quirk rather than a fabric issue, but worth confirming with the retailer if you're in an affected state.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely light (170 g) and packs down small enough to stash in a pack or back pocket
- Four-way stretch ripstop moves well on climbs, scrambles, and trail runs without feeling restrictive
- DWR finish sheds light rain and snow without the stiff, crinkly feel of heavier shells
- Functional, well-constructed pockets that stay put during activity
- Versatile enough to go from trailhead to town without looking like technical wear
- Fair Trade Certified factory production

**Cons**

- Waistband sags under dynamic movement (squats, running) unless you cinch the drawstring, and the drawstring itself is on the short side
- 30" inseam runs short for taller hikers
- At least one long-term owner reported shrinkage over time
- Not a substitute for real rain pants in sustained wet weather
- Slim fit won't suit everyone — no relaxed or athletic-fit option mentioned in the lineup

## Who Should Buy This

These are best suited to hikers, trail runners, and travelers who want a single lightweight pant that can multitask — something to wear on the approach, toss in a summit pack as an emergency layer, or wear straight through a travel day. If you're doing serious bushwhacking or need heavy-duty abrasion resistance, this thin ripstop isn't the pant for that job; treat it as a warm-weather, fast-and-light piece rather than a rugged all-conditions workhorse.

## Verdict

The Terrebonne Trail Joggers deliver on the core promise — light, stretchy, quick-drying, and packable — and the bulk of user feedback backs that up enthusiastically. The waistband sag and short inseam are real but minor annoyances you can mostly engineer around with the drawstring and by sizing up if you're tall. For the price of a pair of do-anything pants that punch well above their 170 g weight, I'd rate these a solid 7.5/10.
