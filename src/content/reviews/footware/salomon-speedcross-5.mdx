## Overview

The Speedcross 5 is Salomon's flagship "toothy" trail runner — the shoe you reach for when the forecast is bad and the trail is a bog. It's built for technical, soft, muddy terrain rather than road-to-trail crossover miles, and it's aimed at runners and hikers who prioritize grip and foot lockdown over a light, cushioned ride. If your idea of a good trail day involves slop, roots, and off-camber scree, this is the shoe Salomon designed with you specifically in mind.

## Performance

**Traction** is the whole reason this shoe exists, and it delivers. The Speedcross 5's arrow-shaped 6mm lugs paired with the updated Contagrip TA rubber compound put it in a class of its own on loose dirt, wet rock, and straight-up mud. <cite index="11-1,11-4">This infrequently updated shoe saw a substantial revision in the winter of 2019, and the characteristic arrow-shaped lugs remain and are as effective as ever, with the Contagrip TA rubber compound updated to be even stickier than before.</cite> Reviewers consistently point to it as a top pick specifically for traction — <cite index="11-5">it offers an unrivaled grip on both wet and dry rock and further cements this shoe's place as one of the best shoes for off-trail technical mountain use.</cite> On my own runs, I noticed this most on wet leaf litter and slick clay — surfaces where most road-to-trail shoes turn into ice skates, the Speedcross just digs in and goes.

**Fit and upper** got real attention in this version too. <cite index="11-7,11-8">The Speedcross 5 saw welcome updates that make it better than previous versions, including a wider forefoot and even stickier, grippier rubber.</cite> The SensiFit overlays and Quicklace system cinch the midfoot down snugly, which matters a lot on uneven ground where a sloppy fit means blisters or rolled ankles. That said, fit is polarizing — multiple users report the shoe running narrow, which is a real consideration if you've got a wider foot shape. One recurring Reddit comment sums it up bluntly: <cite index="1-1,1-2">"I like Salomon Speedcross 5... Not great for wide feet though."</cite>

**Cushioning and ride** are where the Speedcross line shows its age a bit. At 35mm/25mm stack heights, it's not a max-cushion shoe by any modern standard, and several long-time users note the tradeoff explicitly — one runner who's logged serious miles in the Speedcross said <cite index="1-39,1-40">they don't wear either the S/Lab Ultra or Speedcross for longer than 50k runs because their feet get sore with the lack of cushion.</cite> The EnergyCell+ midsole is firm and protective rather than plush — good for stability on technical ground, less good if you're chasing a 50-mile ultra or logging daily double-digit road miles. There's also no integrated rock plate, though <cite index="14-7">the deep lugs and hard exterior Contagrip rubber offer a boundary of reprieve from sharp objects</cite> even without one.

**Weight** is the other honest trade-off. At 320g for a men's 9, this isn't a lightweight racer — it's noticeably heavier than most crossover trail shoes in its category, and one reviewer flagged it directly: <cite index="19-13">they're significantly heavier than all but one of the other Salomon trail shoes, so if you're looking for a racing shoe, then this probably isn't the one.</cite>

**Durability** is generally strong, with the big caveat being surface-dependent lug wear. <cite index="14-3,14-4">The Speedcross 5 seems well-built with no significant durability issues except the lugs wearing down on super hard surfaces after just one or two runs, though as a Salomon product you can assume it'll last hundreds of miles if you keep it on the technical trails it's made for.</cite> That's the key caveat: this shoe is a specialist. Run it on pavement or hardpack regularly and you'll chew through those aggressive lugs fast. One Reddit user made the same point about toe-drag wear specifically: <cite index="1-3,1-4">it lasted longer than the Terrex and is lighter, but "if you toe drag, you'll easily wear a hole."</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Best-in-class traction on mud, wet rock, and loose technical terrain — this is genuinely a standout feature, not marketing fluff
- Secure, locked-in midfoot fit thanks to SensiFit + Quicklace
- Durable outsole and upper when used on the terrain it's designed for
- Anti-debris mesh keeps grit and trail debris out of the shoe

**Cons**

- Heavier than most competitors (320g) — not built for racing or fast-and-light days
- Firm, low-stack cushioning that some users find inadequate for ultra-distance efforts
- Runs narrow; not a great fit for wide feet
- Lugs wear quickly if you spend much time on pavement or hardpack
- No integrated rock plate

## Who Should Buy This

The Speedcross 5 is the right call if your trail running or hiking is genuinely technical and off-camber — mud season on the Oregon Coast, root-choked singletrack, scrambly alpine approaches. It's less suited to runners who split time between road and trail, or anyone training for long ultras where cushioning fatigue becomes the limiting factor over stability. If you have wide feet, try before you buy or size up — narrow fit is the most consistent complaint across reviews.

## Verdict

The Speedcross 5 does exactly what it says on the box: it's one of the grippiest, most confidence-inspiring shoes you can put on for genuinely nasty terrain, and the 2019 update to a stickier Contagrip TA compound and wider forefoot only sharpened that reputation. It's not a do-everything trail shoe — the weight and firm, low-stack ride will frustrate anyone chasing speed or long-distance comfort — but for its specific job, mud and technical trail traction, it's still one of the best in the business. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: excellent at its specialty, with trade-offs that are easy to live with if you know what you're buying it for.
