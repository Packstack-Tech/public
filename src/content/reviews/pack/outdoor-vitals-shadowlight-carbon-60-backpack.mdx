## Overview

The Shadowlight Carbon 60 is Outdoor Vitals' overhauled flagship pack — a 60L, sub-2-pound hauler built around a new carbon fiber stay system instead of the single aluminum stay used in the original Shadowlight. It's aimed at thru-hikers, fast packers, and distance backpackers who want full-frame support and real load-carrying capacity without paying Dyneema-pack prices.

## Performance

**Carrying comfort.** The headline upgrade over the original Shadowlight is the frame. <cite index="2-4,2-5">The original Shadowlight had a single aluminum frame stay with an external foam sit pad, while the new Shadowlight Carbon 60 frame features two carbon-fiber frame stays and a sewn-in die-cut foam back panel.</cite> That's not a subtle change on the trail — reviewers consistently note that <cite index="1-18">two vertical carbon stays and a foam back panel transfer weight efficiently to the hip belt</cite>. One tester who tried it in winter conditions found <cite index="6-14">the carbon frame system distributes weight really well across the hips, eliminating the typical shoulder fatigue that plagues many frameless ultralight designs</cite>. Real-world load testing lines up with Outdoor Vitals' 40-lb rating, though most reviewers keep loads more modest in practice — one noted <cite index="5-22,5-23">the pack is very comfortable carrying 25 pounds, the top weight they like to carry, though Outdoor Vitals says the pack can carry a maximum of 40 pounds</cite>. Another tester pushed it further, saying <cite index="6-18">you can comfortably push this pack into the low-30-pound</cite> range without issue.

**Storage and organization.** This is where the Shadowlight Carbon really sets itself apart from typical 2-lb packs. <cite index="2-7">It's a 60L rolltop backpack with six external pockets and two large hip-belt pockets.</cite> The party trick is a full-length front-panel zipper — <cite index="5-9,5-10">you can stash rain gear, lunch, water filter, water bottle, navigation aids, phone, hat and gloves, snack, and outer clothing in the outside pockets, and if the pockets are filled there's a unique front-of-pack zipper that lets you easily access gear inside</cite>. That's a real advantage for anyone tired of digging to the bottom of a top-loader for a stove. It's not without a catch, though — one reviewer pointed out <cite index="9-13">the downside to this layout is that it's difficult to access the bottom half of these pockets without yardsale-ing the top half's worth of contents</cite>.

**Fabric and weather resistance.** The 100D/210D Robic nylon with Spectra ripstop is tough and proven, but it's not cutting-edge. As Adventure Alan put it, <cite index="3-11,3-12,3-13,3-14">the very best ultralight backpacks are made with woven UHMWPE fabrics like Dyneema, Challenge ULTRA, or ALUULA, which are stronger, lighter, and inherently waterproof — the ripstop Robic nylon this pack is made from is acceptable, functional, and proven, but relatively low tech, and its lack of UHMWPE precludes it from best-in-class consideration.</cite> With a 1500mm hydrostatic head rating, the fabric itself sheds light moisture fine, but SectionHiker flagged real limitations that matter in wet climates: <cite index="2-10">limited top compression, weak external attachment points, a non-waterproof zipper, and pocket tension that won't reliably hold tall bottles all reduce the pack's versatility for cold-weather layering, wet conditions, and multi-sport use where bulky items must be lashed on the outside.</cite> Their bottom line was pragmatic: <cite index="2-11,2-12">if you typically hike in drier climates, avoid carrying heavy external loads, and treat the front zipper carefully, the pack offers solid value at its price point — but for those who need robust external attachment, maximum weather protection, or heavier-duty zippers, a different model may better suit those requirements.</cite>

**Fit.** With 15 torso/hip-belt combinations, dialing in fit shouldn't be hard for most body types. One 6'1", 195-lb reviewer found <cite index="1-17">Regular/Medium mapped almost perfectly onto their body</cite>, and load-lifter placement got specific praise: <cite index="1-1">the pack carries medium weights surprisingly well for its lightweight build, thanks to the effective load adjustment straps positioned above the shoulder straps.</cite>

**Bulk when loaded.** Because pockets stick off both sides, the pack runs wider than a minimalist single-compartment design. <cite index="3-15,3-16">With top and bottom pockets sticking off both sides, the pack runs a bit wider than average when fully loaded — not a huge drawback, but it can be annoying if you have a smaller torso, are hiking in desert canyons, or while bushwhacking.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full carbon-fiber frame at under 2 lbs — rare combination in this price tier
- Comfortable up into the 30-lb range, rated to 40 lbs
- Excellent external organization: six pockets plus hip-belt pockets plus a full-length front zipper for mid-pack access
- 15 size combinations (3 torso x 5 hip belt options mentioned across sources) make dialing in fit easier than most UL packs
- One of the few sub-2-lb, 60L packs priced under $300

**Cons**

- Robic/Spectra nylon isn't as durable-per-ounce or waterproof as Dyneema or ALUULA fabrics used by pricier competitors
- Front zipper isn't waterproof — needs babying in sustained rain
- Limited top compression and weaker external attachment points than dedicated 4-season or bushwhacking packs
- Pack runs wide when fully packed, which can snag in tight brush or canyon terrain
- Side/front pockets can be awkward to fully empty without unloading everything above them

## Who Should Buy This

This pack makes the most sense for thru-hikers and long-distance backpackers who want big volume, easy on-trail access to gear, and genuine hip-belt load transfer, but don't want to spend $350+ on a Dyneema pack. It's especially well-suited to drier-climate trails (think CDT, PCT desert/high-country sections) where you're not relying on bombproof waterproofing or heavy external lashing for snowshoes and ice axes.

## Verdict

The Shadowlight Carbon 60 delivers a genuinely rare combination — real carbon-fiber structure, 40-lb load capacity, and thoughtful external storage — at a weight and price that undercut most of the ultralight category. It's not the toughest or most weatherproof pack out there, and the non-waterproof zipper and modest compression are real trade-offs, but for mixed-terrain, three-season thru-hiking it's one of the best value propositions in the sub-2-lb class. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: excellent for the price, with clear room for a next-gen fabric upgrade.
