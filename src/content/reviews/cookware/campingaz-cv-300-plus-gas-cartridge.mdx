## Overview

The Campingaz CV 300 Plus is a 240g valve-type gas cartridge filled with an 80/20 butane/propane blend, designed exclusively for use with Campingaz's proprietary Easy Clic Plus appliances. It's aimed squarely at recreational campers, festival-goers, and casual hikers in Europe — particularly those already invested in the Campingaz ecosystem. If you're a thru-hiker or ultralight backpacker shopping for the most capable or lightest fuel option, you'll want to read the caveats below before committing.

## Performance

### Gas Mix and Temperature Range

The 80/20 butane/propane split is where this cartridge has the most to answer for. <cite index="26-14">Butane has a boiling point of −1°C, just below the freezing point of water</cite>, which means it struggles to vaporise in cold conditions. The 20% propane content does push performance further than a pure-butane cartridge — <cite index="3-4">the CV 300 Plus can function down to approximately −5°C</cite> — but that's a fairly narrow margin for anyone camping in shoulder seasons or at altitude.

Compare this to the isobutane/propane blends used by most dedicated backpacking canisters. <cite index="25-1">If you want a high-performing canister and plan to camp at altitude or in winter, you need as high a proportion of propane as possible, blended with isobutane rather than butane.</cite> <cite index="26-16,26-17">Due to their lower boiling points, propane and isobutane vaporise more quickly at lower temperatures — but if you're camping in sub-zero weather, butane may not vaporise at all, reducing the flow of gas to the burner head.</cite> In practice, once you're reliably below 0°C, the CV 300 Plus will start losing pressure noticeably. For three-season, temperate-climate camping it's fine. For winter mountaineering or high-altitude use, it isn't the right tool.

### The Easy Clic Plus Valve

This is genuinely one of the better things about this cartridge. <cite index="22-3">The Easy Clic Plus connection allows the fixing of a valve cartridge safely on a stove with a simple 45° rotation.</cite> <cite index="2-2,2-3">The unique self-sealing safety valve allows secure and tight closure, and the cartridge can be removed and re-attached even when it is not yet empty</cite> — a meaningful advantage if you're running one cartridge across multiple devices at camp, or if you need to pack away mid-cook in a hurry. That's a real-world convenience that standard Lindal-valve canisters don't offer. <cite index="1-5">The cartridge also has an unlimited shelf life when stored properly.</cite>

### Compatibility: The Big Catch

Here's the problem for anyone shopping from the broader backpacking world: <cite index="23-19,23-20">if relying on a gas stove, you'll typically be looking for an EN417 canister with a Lindal valve, which is compatible with the vast majority of backpacking gas stoves and is found across the world.</cite> The Campingaz Easy Clic Plus system is a different standard entirely. <cite index="23-25">An MSR Pocket Rocket 2 stove, for example, cannot connect directly to a CV canister.</cite> You can find third-party adapters, but that adds complexity, cost, and a small point of failure to your kit. If you're committed to the Campingaz stove ecosystem — a Twister Plus, Bivvy, or similar — this is a non-issue. If you're not, it's a deal-breaker.

### Weight Efficiency

The numbers here aren't flattering for ultralight-focused hikers. At 410g total with 240g of gas, the empty canister shell weighs roughly 170g. A comparable 230g MSR IsoPro isobutane canister weighs around 340g total, with an empty shell of approximately 110g. That's 60g of extra canister weight you're carrying for a nearly identical gas payload — and the CV 300 Plus's gas mix is less cold-weather capable to boot. <cite index="22-8">Campingaz cartridges are 100% made and quality controlled in their own factory in Lyon, France</cite>, and the build quality is solid, but the valve mechanism does add real mass.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Resealable Easy Clic Plus valve is genuinely convenient — swap between devices without losing gas
- Secure, audible click connection removes guesswork at setup
- Reliable 3-season performance down to around −5°C
- Consistent supply in European camping and outdoor shops
- Unlimited shelf life when stored correctly
- Manufactured and quality-controlled in Europe (Lyon, France)

**Cons**

- 80/20 butane/propane mix underperforms isobutane/propane blends in cold or high-altitude conditions
- Proprietary valve locks you into the Campingaz ecosystem — incompatible with the vast majority of backpacking stoves without an adapter
- Heavy empty canister shell (~170g) penalises ultralight packers
- Meaningfully more expensive per gram of gas than standard isobutane canisters in many markets
- Not widely available outside Europe and Australia

## Who Should Buy This

The CV 300 Plus makes most sense for campers and light hikers already using a Campingaz Easy Clic Plus stove — particularly in the UK or continental Europe where these canisters are easy to find in supermarkets and hardware shops. It's also a smart pick if you're running multiple Campingaz appliances (stove, lantern, grill) from a single cartridge, since the resealable valve earns its keep in that scenario. It's not a good match for thru-hikers optimising for base weight, cold-weather mountaineers, or anyone outside the Campingaz ecosystem.

## Verdict

The CV 300 Plus does exactly what it says: it delivers reliable, clean-burning fuel for three-season camping in a convenient resealable package, and it's easy to find across Europe. But it carries a real weight penalty versus standard isobutane canisters, its 80/20 butane/propane mix is behind the curve for cold-weather performance, and the proprietary valve is a compatibility wall for anyone not already in the Campingaz world. Solid within its niche — just make sure that niche is yours before you buy in.
