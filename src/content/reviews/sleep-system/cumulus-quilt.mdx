## Overview

The Cumulus Quilt is a Polish-made, hoodless down quilt with a zippered, fully openable footbox. It comes in three fill weights: 150, 250 and 300 grams of goose down. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who sleep on a pad and don't need a hood will like it, because the quilt drops the weight of insulation they would crush flat underneath them anyway. The trade-off is a delicate shell and an overseas supply chain. The warmest version, the 300, is the one most hikers will buy.

## Performance

**Warmth for the weight.** The 300 is the version that shows what the design can do. It has 10.6 oz of 900-fill down inside a shell that weighs only 19 g/m², and the whole quilt comes in around 495 g. Cumulus rates it at 3°C comfort and -2°C limit. One hands-on reviewer put its sweet spot at nights from about 2°C to 15°C, which matches the 3-season label. Choose the fill by the coldest night you expect, not the average one. The 28°F limit is a number to survive at, and cold sleepers shouldn't plan around it.

**Baffles and drafts.** The H-shaped box baffles are more involved to build than simple sewn-through chambers. They keep down from migrating and help avoid cold spots. A two-strap pad attachment system handles the usual quilt weak point, the gap between quilt and pad. In the 300's case, the straps add 31 g and the stuff sack another 12 g. Even so, quilts are open at the back, so they run cooler at the margins than a closed bag with the same rating. Side sleepers who tuck the edges under tend to get the best results.

**The zippered footbox.** A short zipper at the foot lets the quilt cinch into a closed footbox on cold nights. On warm ones it opens into a flat blanket. Owners of the 250 describe using the open position mostly for venting or for draping the quilt around the shoulders at camp. They rarely unzip it all the way. It's a handy extra, but nobody buys the quilt for it.

**Packed size and shell.** The high-fill 300 stuffs into a 3.5-liter sack. The lighter 150 packs to roughly 1.5 liters, small enough to ride in a hip-belt pocket on a fastpacking trip. The 250 comes with a 3-liter stuff sack. Reviewers describe Airtastic as feeling impossibly thin but well made, with even down distribution and neat seams. That thinness has a cost. Zippers, rough rocks and dog claws can all damage it, and anyone who sleeps on gravel or in a shared shelter should pitch on a groundsheet and keep the quilt away from sharp edges.

**Customization.** Cumulus lets buyers adjust length, width, fill and fabric before the quilt ships. Bumping the 300 up to 350 g of fill buys roughly 2°C of extra comfort, according to one reviewer. That is a real advantage for tall, wide or very cold-sleeping hikers, who often can't find a good fit in off-the-shelf American quilts. Custom orders mean longer lead times, and buyers outside the EU should check import duties.

## Choosing a Version

The three versions share a pattern and a footprint. What changes is how much down sits in the baffles, and so how cold the quilt can go.

**Quilt 150 (380 g).** This is a summer and warm-night quilt: 5.3 oz of down, a 48°F comfort rating and a 39°F limit. It suits desert and low-elevation summer hikes, bikepacking, and hikers who plan to add layers rather than carry a heavier quilt. It also works as an insert for a heavier bag. Anyone expecting nights below the mid-40s°F should look higher in the range.

**Quilt 250 (480 g).** This one covers the middle: 250 g of down and a 39°F comfort rating, with a 32°F limit. It's built for spring and summer trips where nights drop to the low 40s°F, and owners report it holds up near freezing with a base layer and a hat. It's only 16 g lighter than the 300, so the extra warmth of the 300 costs almost nothing. Hikers who weigh every gram and don't expect cold snaps could still pick it.

**Quilt 300 (496 g).** This is the 3-season quilt: 10.6 oz of 900-fill down, 37°F comfort and 28°F limit. It's the version for thru-hikers who sleep through spring and fall shoulder weather and want a single quilt for most of a long trail. It's also the one with the most room to be tuned up through the custom-fill option.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- The 300 delivers a 28°F limit at roughly 500 g.
- H-shaped baffles and a two-strap pad system cut down on cold spots and drafts.
- The zippered footbox gives real ventilation and a flat-blanket mode.
- Three fill weights cover roughly 48°F down to the high 20s°F.
- Customizable length, width, fill and fabric.
- Neat seams and even down distribution, with a small packed size.

**Cons**

- The 19 g/m² Airtastic shell is fragile and needs careful handling.
- Made in Poland: import duties and longer lead times on custom orders outside the EU.
- A 28°F limit is a survival number, not a comfort margin, for cold sleepers.
- The 250 is only 16 g lighter than the 300, which makes it a hard sell.

## Who Should Buy This

Pad-sleeping thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who already know they like quilts should look at the 300. It covers the widest range of conditions and carries the best down and fabric. The 150 suits warm-weather trips where every gram counts. Those who toss and turn on cold nights, hikers who camp in sub-freezing weather, and anyone who wants a hood or a sturdier shell should look elsewhere, at a winter-rated bag or a quilt with a thicker fabric.

## Verdict

The 300 is a strong 3-season quilt: very light, packable and well built, with enough customization to fit unusual body sizes. Its fragile shell and the overseas ordering process keep it from being a no-brainer. The 150 is a good warm-weather specialist, but the 250 sits too close to the 300 in weight to make much of a case for itself.
