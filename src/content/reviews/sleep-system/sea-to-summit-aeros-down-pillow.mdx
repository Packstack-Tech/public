## Overview

The Aeros Down Pillow is Sea to Summit's air-supported camp pillow with a thin layer of duck down quilted onto a soft nylon face. It weighs 70 g in the Regular size and 85 g in the Standard, and it packs to roughly the size of a large egg. The trade-off is a modest comfort upgrade over the cheaper Aeros Premium in exchange for a higher price and a slicker surface than some sleepers like.

## Performance

**Comfort.** The down layer is thin, and testers describe its effect accordingly. CleverHiker's reviewers called the difference subtle rather than dramatic. They found it slightly softer against the skin than the standard Aeros Premium, and it compressed their ears less than other air pillows when they slept on their sides. They still ranked it mid-pack for comfort among the pillows they tested. The real draw is that it achieves that softness at almost no weight penalty.

**Weight and packability.** This is where the pillow earns its reputation. CleverHiker named the Regular the lightest and most packable pillow in its test, and its 2.5 oz (70 g) figure comes out ahead of the Therm-a-Rest Air Head Down, which that outlet ranked second among down-topped pillows. The fabrics are borrowed from Sea to Summit's Spark and Flame sleeping bags, which is how the weight stays so low. Stuffed in its sack, it disappears into a pot or a pocket.

**Inflation and firmness.** The mini XPRESS valve combines a one-way inflate port with a rapid-dump deflate port and a fine-tune button. Owners report that the pillow fills in a few breaths and that the one-way action stops air from escaping between puffs, which helps at altitude. At full inflation it reaches 12 cm, enough loft for side sleepers. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers noted that on the Aeros family the deflation port releases air all at once, so softening an overinflated pillow takes some care. Easing air out slowly takes a light touch on the valve.

**Staying put.** This is the most common complaint. Some REI reviewers say the current nylon face is slippery and crinkly compared with the older, softer-feeling top. One described their head sliding around "like it was on ice." Others get around it by wrapping a shirt or puffy over the pad or by using the included 3M PillowLock patches. Those patches stick to most sleeping pads, and they work best with Sea to Summit mats that have matching attachment points. Back sleepers, who have less shoulder contact to anchor the pillow, tend to notice the slide most.

**Durability.** The TPU bladder is hydrolysis-resistant, and the nylon face is thin by design. Most owners report no trouble, but a few REI reviewers describe seam leaks at the corners after limited use, and older Aeros pillows have drawn similar complaints on long-term threads. The down also loses its loft if it gets wet, so it needs to stay in the stuff sack or inside the tent on damp trips.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same 12 cm maximum loft, valve, materials and PillowLock system. What changes is the footprint, and 15 g separates them.

The Regular (70 g) is the version CleverHiker recommends for weight-focused hikers. It has the smallest sleeping surface among the pillows that outlet tested, so restless sleepers and anyone who ends up with their cheek half off the pillow will find it tight. It suits back and side sleepers who stay put, and anyone counting every gram.

The Standard (85 g) gives the head more room to move around for a 15 g penalty, which is less than the weight of a spare lighter. Hikers who toss and turn, or who tend to slide off the Regular on a slick pad, should take it. On a base weight scale, the extra 15 g buys more usable pillow than most gear swaps would.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Among the lightest down-topped pillows available at 70 g, with a very small packed size
- Down layer adds a soft, warm feel against the face and ears
- Multi-function valve inflates in a few breaths and deflates quickly
- Up to 12 cm of loft supports side sleepers
- PillowLock patches are included
- Lifetime-style durability claims backed by a hydrolysis-resistant TPU bladder

**Cons**

- Nylon face can be slick and crinkly, and some owners find it slides
- Down benefit is subtle and costs more than the Aeros Premium
- Regular has a small sleeping surface
- Down loses loft when damp
- Some owners report seam leaks, and the deflate port dumps air all at once

## Who Should Buy This

Ultralight and weight-conscious backpackers who want a real pillow instead of a stuffed jacket will get the most out of the Aeros Down. It suits side sleepers who want loft without bulk, and anyone already sleeping on a Sea to Summit mat with PillowLock points. Warm-sleeping back sleepers who move around a lot, or hikers who dislike crinkly fabrics, should look at the Aeros Premium's brushed face or a foam-topped option instead. Anyone who wants a pillow that tolerates rain and abuse without babying should skip the down.

## Verdict

The Aeros Down Pillow is one of the best weight-to-comfort bargains in camp sleep gear, as long as the slick nylon face and thin down layer don't bother you. Take the Regular if the grams matter most. Take the Standard if the pillow ends up on the ground by morning.
