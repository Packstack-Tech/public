## Overview

The Anker PowerPort III 3-Port 65W (model A2033) is a GaN wall charger built for people juggling more than one device — phone, watch, headphones, maybe a USB-C laptop — off a single outlet. It's not something you'd pack for a thru-hike, but it's the kind of charger that earns a spot in your resupply box or town-stop kit if you're running a phone, GPS unit, headlamp battery, and power bank off USB. For backpackers specifically, I'd frame this less as "trail gear" and more as "the thing you leave in your car or hotel room so you're not fighting over outlets before your next stretch in the backcountry."

## Performance

The headline number is 65W, but it's worth reading the fine print before you buy this expecting to charge a 16" MacBook Pro at full tilt: <cite index="10-11">the USB-C port maxes out at 45W of power for phones and laptops, with two USB-A ports for older Apple gadgets, and three interchangeable international plugs included</cite>. That 45W ceiling is fine for most ultralight laptops, tablets, and phones, but it means this isn't a full-speed charger for power-hungry 65W+ laptops if you're trying to top up fast.

Where it's genuinely useful is the multi-device juggling act. <cite index="10-12">With a USB-C Power Delivery port, two USB-A sockets, and interchangeable travel plugs, the Anker PowerPort III 3-port is a solid travel companion — especially for Apple users</cite>. On a resupply day, that's a phone on USB-C, a headlamp or GPS on one USB-A port, and a spare battery bank on the other — all from one wall socket, which matters more than you'd think when a hostel or gas station only has one free outlet.

The PPS support is a nice touch if you're running a newer Samsung phone — it'll hit Samsung's Super Fast Charging spec rather than falling back to a slower generic PD profile. But if you're deep into the PD PPS ecosystem or need the absolute fastest charging across a wide range of devices, reviewers note <cite index="10-2,10-3">the charger isn't exactly on the cutting edge, and those looking for a more up-to-date alternative with USB Power Delivery PPS support should look elsewhere</cite>. This is a "good enough for almost everyone" charger, not a benchmark-topping one.

Build quality and thermals seem to be non-issues for most owners. Aggregated customer feedback pulled from a large sample of Best Buy reviews for this charger found that <cite index="9-12,9-13">customers consistently note the charger for its fast charging speed and portability, making it convenient for travel, and appreciate its compact size and premium build quality, as well as the usefulness of having multiple ports</cite>. The flip side: <cite index="9-14">some customers have mentioned that the charger can get hot and is somewhat heavy</cite>. In my experience with GaN chargers in this power class generally, some warmth under full 45W+load is normal and not a safety concern — just don't be surprised if it's warm to the touch after an hour of charging a laptop.

At 162g, it's not a featherweight — that's noticeably more than single-port 20-30W GaN bricks that weigh 30-45g, so don't mistake this for an ultralight trail item. It earns its keep by replacing two or three separate chargers, not by being small itself.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Charges three devices simultaneously from one wall outlet — useful for resupply stops or hostel rooms with limited plugs
- GaN internals keep it reasonably compact for a 65W-class charger
- Interchangeable US/UK/EU plugs make it a genuine one-charger travel solution for international trips
- PPS support for faster charging on compatible Samsung phones
- Consistently praised for build quality and portability in user reviews

**Cons:**

- USB-C port is capped at 45W, not the full 65W — won't fast-charge higher-wattage laptops
- Not class-leading on PD PPS versatility compared to newer competitors
- At 162g, it's heavier than dedicated ultralight single-port chargers — not something to justify carrying on-trail
- Some users report it runs warm under sustained load

## Who Should Buy This

This makes the most sense for backpackers who need a single "home base" charger to manage between trail sections — someone running a phone, a rechargeable headlamp, a GPS device, and a power bank who's tired of packing three separate wall warts for town stops or international travel. If your main device is a power-hungry USB-C laptop that needs the full 65W, or if you only ever charge one device at a time, you can save weight and money with a simpler single-port GaN charger instead.

## Verdict

The PowerPort III 3-Port 65W does exactly what it says: it charges three things at once, reasonably fast, from a compact GaN body with travel-friendly interchangeable plugs. It's not the fastest or most flexible charger on the market, and the 45W USB-C cap will disappoint anyone expecting full 65W laptop charging, but as a multi-device travel and resupply hub it's a dependable, well-built pick. I'd rate it a 7.2/10 — solid and practical, just not spectacular.
