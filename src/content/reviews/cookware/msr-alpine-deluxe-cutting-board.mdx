## Overview

The Alpine Deluxe is a BPA-free polypropylene cutting board in two rigid halves joined by a hinge, so it folds to roughly the size of a paperback. It's built for campers and backpackers who cook real ingredients rather than just adding boiling water. The trade-off is plain: it's far sturdier than a flexible mat, and it weighs 176.6 g to prove it.

## Performance

**Cutting surface.** The board's selling point is rigidity. Owners describe a surface that holds up under a knife without gouging or tearing, and one long-term owner on Backcountry reported years of use without damage. That sets it apart from the thin flexible mats common in camp kitchens, which can slip and wrinkle under a blade. A Japanese reviewer who cooks solo meals found the unfolded area enough for chopping mushrooms, onions and potatoes one item at a time, though not enough for a big prep session. Reviewers sum it up as a size that's not stupidly small and not unwieldy.

**The hinge.** This is where the board gets its character, for better and worse. Most owners value the fold for getting chopped food into a pot: tilt the board, bend the hinge slightly, and the vegetables slide in. A UK buyer folded it to the size of a hand and planned to use it as a windscreen for an alcohol stove, then as a prep surface. Another reviewer says it's weighty enough to work as a windbreak. The hinge also draws the most criticism. A Japanese reviewer expects the crease to crack eventually, and a buyer on MSR's own site worried it might break from repeated folding. Another noted that the fold makes the surface feel a little less solid when chopping very hard items such as bones.

**Flatness.** The biggest complaint is that the board doesn't always lie flat. One Backcountry reviewer who stepped up from MSR's smaller, thinner board said the larger one was thick enough that it bowed up when set down. With light ingredients like herbs or sliced onion, there was nothing to pin it, so it had to be held. A Canadian Amazon reviewer made the same point, adding that the board is heavier than backpacking needs. On a rock or a log, a hand or a pot on one half solves it. Anyone cutting one-handed in a tent vestibule will find it more annoying.

**Cleanup and hygiene.** Polypropylene is non-porous and wipes clean, and owners consistently say it's easy to wash. The board is hand-wash only. Buyers say it can get slick with very wet ingredients, a minor issue.

**Carry.** At 0.8 cm packed thickness, it slides flat against a pack's frame sheet or inside a bear canister's neighbor pocket without fuss. Day-pack lunch carriers, bikepackers and motorcycle campers praise the compact footprint. Base-camp cooks are the group that finds it small.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rigid surface that stands up to knife work, with no tearing reported by long-term owners
- Folds to 18.8 x 13.2 cm and about 0.8 cm thick
- The hinge lets cooks funnel chopped food into a pot
- Easy to wipe clean; BPA-free polypropylene
- Double duty as a stove windscreen or lap table, according to owners
- Priced around $15

**Cons**

- 176.6 g is a lot of weight for a single-purpose item in an ultralight kit
- Can bow up at the hinge rather than lying flat, especially with light food
- Hinge is a potential wear point after many folds, according to some owners
- Compact working area is tight for group meals or base camping
- Hand wash only

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend backpackers, bikepackers and motorcycle campers who cook fresh vegetables, cheese, sausage or fish, and who are tired of balancing a pot lid on their knee, will get the most from it. It also suits anyone running a stove-based kitchen on a winter trip, where eating well is part of the point. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. A 176.6 g board is hard to justify when a knife and a pot or a plate can handle most trail food, and a sub-50 g flexible mat or a cut-down sheet of plastic covers the same job for a fraction of the weight. Group cooks and base campers will also want something bigger.

## Verdict

The Alpine Deluxe is a well-made, durable board that does what it claims and packs smaller than most alternatives of comparable stiffness. Its weight and the occasional rocking at the hinge keep it from being an ultralight recommendation, but for fresh-food cooking on short trips it earns its pack space.
