## Overview

Decathlon's Forclaz line has a reputation for putting serious trekking features at supermarket prices, and this roll-top dry bag follows the pattern. It weighs 40 g in the 7 L size and 55 g in the 13 L, costs roughly €10 to €13 in Germany, and is built for keeping a sleeping layer, spare socks or electronics dry in a pack rather than for being dunked. The trade-off is the waterproofing ceiling: this is a rain-protection sack, not a river-crossing one.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The bag pairs a 5,000 mm coating with sealed seams, and Decathlon rates it for one hour of storm-level rain. That is the right spec for the job most backpackers actually need done: keeping a down quilt or a spare base layer dry inside a pack that is getting rained on for a day. Decathlon's support documentation is explicit that the bag is not fully waterproof. It can't be submerged, and the company rules it out for diving, kayaking and canoeing. Hikers who want a bag for packrafting or for fording thigh-deep water should look at a welded, higher-rated sack instead.

**Closure and packing.** The roll-top works the usual way: press out the air, fold the top three times, clip the buckle. Because the bag is cut flat rather than as a tall tube, the contents squash down into a low, pack-friendly shape, which is the point of the design. The wide mouth makes it easy to stuff a quilt or a jacket in without the wrestling match that narrower sacks invite, and the black tab at the bottom gives a grip for shaking out the last sock at camp.

**Durability.** The 40-denier polyamide uses reinforced yarns, a step up in toughness from the gossamer fabrics on the lightest cuben-style sacks. Decathlon reports that 20 testers used the bag on two 16-day treks, covering 240 km with 12,000 m of climbing and 232 km with 13,100 m of climbing. Customer ratings at Decathlon run high: 4.7 out of 5 across 652 reviews for the 7 L and 4.8 across 444 for the 13 L.

**Camp use.** Stuffed with a puffy and a fleece, the flat 7 L doubles as a bivouac pillow. It isn't going to replace an inflatable pillow, but it adds a function to a bag that is already in the pack, for zero extra grams. It also keeps dust and sand off gear, which makes it a sensible organizer for desert and beach routes.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes share fabric, closure and construction. Only the volume and the weight change.

- **7 L (40 g):** Fits a quilt-weight insulation layer, a pair of socks and a hat, or a phone, battery and headlamp kit. It is the better pillow, and the cheaper way to wrap electronics and a first-aid kit.
- **13 L (55 g):** Holds a compressed 20°F-class quilt or a full clothing set, so it works as the primary liner for sleep gear in a smaller pack. The extra 15 g is a small price for the volume.

Many hikers will do best with one of each: the 13 L for the sleep system, the 7 L for clothes or electronics. The two together weigh 95 g and cost under €23.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 40 g and 55 g, light enough to ignore on the scale
- Sealed seams and 5,000 mm coating handle a full day of rain inside a pack
- Wide mouth and flat cut make loading and compressing easy
- Doubles as a pillow
- Roughly €10 to €13, cheap enough to buy several
- Two-year warranty and strong customer ratings

**Cons**

- Not submersible, and unsuitable for paddling or deep fords
- IPX5 and a one-hour storm rating are the ceiling, not a margin of safety
- 40 D fabric is tougher than premium ultralight sacks but still needs care around sharp gear
- Sold mainly through Decathlon, so availability outside Europe is patchy

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers and thru-hikers who want an inexpensive set of liners and organizers for rain protection, particularly Europeans with a Decathlon nearby. It suits three-season hiking where the threat is wet weather, not water immersion. Hikers who cross rivers with a loaded pack, paddle, or use a pack with no rain cover in sustained downpours should buy a fully waterproof welded dry bag.

## Verdict

For the price, these are some of the easiest dry bags to recommend: light, simple, sensibly sealed and honest about their limits. Buy the pair for a sleep system and a clothing kit, and keep them away from water that rises above the knee.
