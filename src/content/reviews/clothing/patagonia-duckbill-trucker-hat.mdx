## Overview

The Duckbill Trucker Hat is Patagonia's take on the classic five-panel trucker cap, reworked with recycled performance fabrics so it can pull double duty as a trail running/hiking hat and an everyday cap. It's aimed at anyone who wants trucker-hat styling but with a packable brim, better breathability, and a sweatband that actually manages moisture instead of just soaking through.

## Performance

The defining feature here is the packable brim. Unlike a typical trucker hat's stiff foam bill, this one folds flat into your pocket or pack pocket and pops back into shape afterward — genuinely useful if you're stashing it during a climb or a swim and don't want a permanent crease. <cite index="2-7">The crushable brim folds into a pocket for easy packing, and an internal sweatband helps keep you cool when you're working hard.</cite>

On breathability, the mesh back crown does what it's supposed to. One runner who <cite index="2-10,2-11,2-12,2-13,2-14">wore it during a 50k in Michigan during a warm September day found comfort outstanding, praising the sweatband for keeping sweat from running into their eyes, and calling it cool, breathable, and compatible with sunglasses</cite>. That tracks with what I'd expect from the Capilene Cool sweatband material — it's the same fabric family Patagonia uses in its running apparel, so it's built to wick rather than just absorb.

Not everyone has the same experience, though. One Backcountry reviewer had the opposite take, saying <cite index="6-2,6-3,6-4">they had very mixed feelings about the hat, that it sat on their head in a weird way and wasn't the most comfortable, and that its large front section was really good at holding heat and made their head very sweaty</cite>. That's a real contradiction worth flagging — head shape and how the front panel sits against your forehead seems to make a bigger difference in comfort than the mesh back does. If you run hot or have a rounder head shape, the front panel (which is solid nylon faille, not mesh) may trap more heat than you'd expect from a "breathable trail hat."

The foam brim itself has also drawn mixed reactions across seasons. Some buyers found the brim <cite index="1-3">a little flatish for my taste, but it's far from a deal breaker</cite>, while a review of a later production run noted the brim was <cite index="5-3,5-4,5-5,5-6">better in some seasons than others — this season being more stiff and angled down a bit</cite>. Patagonia appears to have tweaked brim stiffness between manufacturing runs, so don't be surprised if two hats from different years feel slightly different off the shelf.

Durability is a mixed bag on the one weak point: the rear buckle. It's a plastic adjustment clip rather than the more robust webbing-and-metal-grommet setup you see on heavier-duty trucker hats, and at least one long-term owner reported <cite index="5-9,5-10,5-11">a couple of buckle breaks and wished it was easier to get repaired, since something like that should be an easy in-store fix</cite>. That's a real ding against a $35 hat you're hoping will last years, though it's not a universal complaint — a separate long-term tester who put the hat through roughly 100 km of trail running and commuting reported it held up well.

Everything else about the build points to a hat meant for movement rather than sitting on a shelf. <cite index="2-10">The front crown and bill fabric is built of NetPlus 100% postconsumer recycled nylon faille made from recycled fishing nets, with a DWR finish made without intentionally added PFAS to shed moisture</cite>, so light rain and sweat bead off rather than soak in immediately. The high crown fit is generous — it's a one-size-fits-all with a rear elastic-and-buckle closure, which should cover most adult head sizes without feeling like it's pinching at the temples.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Packable foam brim survives being crushed in a pocket or pack without holding a permanent crease
- Mesh back panel and Capilene Cool sweatband genuinely wick sweat for most wearers
- Recycled NetPlus nylon front and recycled polyester back keep the sustainability story credible, not just marketing
- Fair Trade Certified construction
- Lightweight at 74 g — barely noticeable on the head during long runs or hikes
- Works well with sunglasses thanks to a brim that isn't overly rigid

**Cons**

- Comfort is inconsistent — some wearers find the front panel traps heat and sits oddly on certain head shapes
- Plastic rear buckle has been reported to break on at least one long-term owner's hat, with no easy repair option
- Brim stiffness seems to vary between production runs/seasons
- One-size fit means no adjustability for smaller heads beyond the elastic's range

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for trail runners and hikers who want a packable hat they can stuff in a hydration vest pocket without worrying about crushing the brim, and who prioritize sweat-wicking over maximum sun coverage (the bill is shorter than a dedicated running visor). It's also a reasonable everyday cap if you like the recycled-materials story and Fair Trade Certified label. If you know from experience that trucker-style caps run hot on your head, or you want bombproof hardware for years of abuse, this may not be the hat that changes your mind.

## Verdict

The Duckbill Trucker Hat delivers on its core promise — a packable, breathable trucker cap built from recycled materials — but comfort and hardware durability aren't universal. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: worth buying for the packability and wicking performance, just don't expect every head shape to love the fit, and keep an eye on that rear buckle over time.
