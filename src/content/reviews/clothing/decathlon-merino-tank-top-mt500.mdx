## Overview

The Decathlon MT500 Merino Tank Top is a women's sleeveless base layer built from 210 g/m² fabric that is 95% merino wool and 5% elastane. It is aimed at trekkers who want wool's odor resistance in warm conditions, either under a shirt or worn alone. The trade-off is the usual one for Decathlon's merino line: real, fine-fiber wool at a modest price, without the refinement of Icebreaker or Smartwool.

## Performance

**Fabric and softness.** The 18.5-micron fiber is the number that matters here. It sits in the superfine range, where wool generally stops itching against bare skin. Reviewers of other pieces in the MT500 range, including the long-sleeve shirt and the beanie, report the same result: soft enough for direct skin contact, with no itch on first wear. They also note the wool is a step below premium brands in softness and finish. A tank puts a lot of fabric against the shoulders, collarbones and upper chest, so that gap is easier to notice here than in a long-sleeve.

**Composition.** Much of the MT500 line blends merino with polyamide, a mix of about 86% wool and 14% synthetic that reviewers credit with extra durability. The tank takes a different approach. At 95% merino with 5% elastane, it is closer to a wool-first garment, and the elastane supports the fitted cut so the top stays put under a pack. The trade-off is that a higher wool content can mean less abrasion resistance than the nylon-reinforced shirts in the same line. Merino generally pills slightly with wear, and testers advise keeping it away from rough brush and abrasive pack straps.

**Odor and moisture.** Odor control is where Decathlon's merino has the strongest record. A long-term reviewer of the MT500 boxers called them excellent at staying stink-free. The same wool chemistry applies here. On multi-day trips, a tank that can be worn for days without a wash is the main reason to carry merino over a synthetic singlet. Wool also stays comfortable when damp. It dries more slowly than a polyester tank, though.

**Weight and warmth.** At 98 g, the tank is light for a 210 g/m² fabric, but it is not the lightest option. Ultralight merino tops and synthetic singlets built from 120–150 g/m² fabric weigh noticeably less and dry faster. The 210 g/m² weight also makes this a mid-weight wool. It will feel warmer on a summer afternoon than a lightweight tank would. It suits cool mornings, shoulder-season hiking, and use as a layering piece under a shirt. It is less suited to desert heat.

**Fit and use.** The cut is fitted, with a crew neck. A close fit is what lets merino wick and manage odor against the skin, but it also means sizing matters, and layering a tank over a bra and under a pack hipbelt leaves little room for error. Because the top is sleeveless, the shoulders are exposed to sun and to pack-strap rub. Hikers who sunburn easily or carry heavy loads usually add sleeves.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 18.5-micron merino that owners of the MT500 range describe as non-itchy
- 95% merino content, higher than the blended shirts in the same line
- 98 g, easy to carry as a second top
- Strong odor resistance, the main reason to buy merino
- Elastane keeps the fitted cut from sagging
- Budget pricing relative to premium merino brands

**Cons**

- Wool is less soft and refined than Icebreaker or Devold, based on reports from the wider MT500 line
- 210 g/m² is warm for a summer tank and heavier than ultralight alternatives
- High wool content may pill or wear faster under pack straps
- Sleeveless cut offers no sun protection for shoulders and arms
- Slower drying than a synthetic singlet

## Who Should Buy This

The MT500 tank is for women who want a merino base layer for three-season trekking and don't want to spend premium prices. It works well as a sleep-and-camp top, as a layering piece under a light shirt, or as a primary top on cool, dry hikes. Thru-hikers who rotate a few days of wear between washes will get the most out of the wool.

Hikers who run hot, who walk in desert or humid heat, or who want the fastest-drying kit should look at a lighter merino-blend or synthetic singlet. Anyone who carries a heavy pack, or who needs sun cover, will do better with a short-sleeve top.

## Verdict

The Decathlon MT500 Merino Tank Top is a sensible, low-cost way into wool next to the skin, with fine fiber, high merino content and a low carry weight. It does not match the softness of premium brands, and the 210 g/m² fabric is built more for cool and mild days than for heat. Buy it as a layering and camp top, not as a hot-weather trail singlet.
