## Overview

The FLEXTAIL Tiny Pump is a USB-C rechargeable air pump about the size of a chunky shot glass. It inflates and deflates sleeping pads, pillows and other inflatables. It comes in two versions: the plain Tiny Pump, and the Tiny Pump X, which adds a camping light on the bottom. At 80 g it will never help a base weight, but it swaps the nightly pump-sack routine for a button press. The trade-off is a high-pitched whine that carries across a quiet campsite.

## Performance

### Inflation speed

Speed is the reason to own it. Once a nozzle is seated on the valve, owners report a full sleeping pad in roughly 45 seconds. A pillow takes far less. One reviewer filled a Klymit pillow in about six seconds. After a long day, that replaces several minutes of lightheaded huffing into a dry bag.

The pressure is modest at 3.5 kPa. That is plenty to fill a pad. It will not firm one up the way a pump with higher output can, so most users finish with a few breaths or a hand press to set the firmness.

### Battery life

The 1,300 mAh cell is the one spec owners track closely. One tester got four inflations of a double pad plus four of single pads before the pump quit on the fifth night. The light was still working at that point. The practical guideline from owners is about ten single-pad inflations per charge. That covers a 3–4 night trip with room to spare.

Charging a fully drained pump takes around four hours. A 20-minute top-up from a power bank is enough for one full double-pad inflation. USB-C means the pump shares a cable with a phone and headlamp. A sealed lithium cell also copes with cold and damp better than AA or AAA pumps. Alkaline cells sag in freezing temperatures and corrode in wet ones.

### Valve compatibility

The five nozzles cover most pads. Owners confirm a good fit on Klymit and Exped valves, and on Therm-a-Rest pads with flip valves. Screw-type valves take more work. The rubber nozzle has to be pushed over the valve while the cover is pulled out of the way and held. Nemo Tensor UL owners report the nozzle leaking or popping off mid-fill. One fixed it by wrapping a small rubber ring around the nozzle tip. Nozzles can also back off the valve during a fill. Reseating one usually lets the pump finish.

### Noise

Noise is the most consistent complaint. One reviewer docked a star for the loud, high-pitched motor. The whine lasts under a minute, but it is harsh enough to draw looks at a busy shelter or a quiet campground at 10 p.m. Anyone sharing a tent early in the morning will notice it too.

### The light (Tiny Pump X)

The X puts a lantern on the base. It has three brightness levels: 40, 160 and 400 lumens. The listing describes 4500K white light from eight LEDs. Owners say it works as a tent light, and the hanging bail lets it light the inside of a shelter. One reviewer describes the pump line as a lantern that happens to inflate pads, and says it replaced the backup light they already carried.

The light and pump share a single button, with different press patterns for each. In the dark, starting the motor when you wanted the light is easy to do. Running the light also draws on the same battery that inflates the pad.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same pump: 3.5 kPa, 180 L/min, 1,300 mAh, USB-C, five nozzles. Inflation and deflation performance is the same. The only difference is the lamp.

- **Tiny Pump:** The pick for anyone who already carries a headlamp that works as a tent light, or who wants the battery reserved for inflating pads. It has one job and one battery draw.
- **Tiny Pump X:** The pick for anyone who carries a separate tent lantern or backup light. Folding that into the pump can retire a second item, which is the best argument for the pump's 80 g. It also suits anyone who likes a hanging light in the shelter. Expect a slightly smaller battery reserve if the light is used often.

For most backpackers, the X is the better buy. It adds a useful function for little extra cost, and the pump itself is unchanged.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fills a sleeping pad in under a minute and a pillow in seconds
- USB-C charging uses the cable and power bank hikers already carry
- Roughly ten single-pad inflations per charge covers most trips
- Five nozzles fit most common pad valves
- Small enough for a hip belt pocket
- The X's built-in lamp can replace a separate tent light

**Cons**

- High-pitched motor noise is loud in a quiet camp
- Screw-type and some Nemo valves need fiddling, and nozzles can pop off
- 3.5 kPa is modest next to newer FLEXTAIL pumps
- Needs a recharge on longer trips
- 80 g is more than a pump sack for gram counters
- Shared button on the X makes it easy to start the pump by accident

## Who Should Buy This

This pump suits backpackers on 1–5 night trips who sleep on an inflatable pad and are tired of the pump-sack routine. It pays off most for people who carry both an inflation aid and a tent light. The X version fits them best. Thru-hikers on long stretches without reliable charging should look elsewhere, as should hikers who already manage fine with a pump sack. FLEXTAIL's newer models are lighter and more powerful. The 3X weighs 67 g and moves 300 L/min at 5 kPa. Buyers paying full price should compare those before settling on this one.

## Verdict

The Tiny Pump does what it promises. It fills a pad quickly, charges over USB-C, and, in X form, doubles as a tent light. It is loud, the valve fit sometimes needs coaxing, and newer FLEXTAIL pumps beat it on paper. It is still an easy recommendation for weekend and short-trip backpackers, and the X is the version to buy.
