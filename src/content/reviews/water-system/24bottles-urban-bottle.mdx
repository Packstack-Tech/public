## Overview

The 24Bottles Urban Bottle is a single-wall, 18/8 stainless steel water bottle designed primarily for office, gym, and everyday carry — not backcountry travel. It comes in multiple sizes, but the 1000ml version reviewed here weighs 158g and is what you'd realistically consider if you wanted to press this into hiking duty. It's worth saying upfront: this isn't a product 24Bottles markets or engineers for backpacking, so I'm evaluating it here through the lens of "could a thru-hiker or weekend backpacker use this," not "how does this stack up against a Nalgene or Smartwater bottle on a thru-hike."

## Performance

Let's start with the obvious: at 158g for a liter of capacity, this is roughly 5x the weight of a 32oz Smartwater bottle (about 30-40g) and heavier than a comparable-capacity Nalgene HDPE bottle (around 178g for 32oz, so actually a slight win there, interestingly). Against dedicated ultralight hydration options — soft flasks, Smartwater reuse, or even a Nalgene — the Urban Bottle doesn't have an obvious niche on a backpacking gear list. It's a steel bottle built for aesthetics and daily reuse, not for shaving grams on trail.

That said, steel does bring some real advantages worth weighing. <cite index="7-3,7-4">Urban Bottle is 24Bottles' non-thermal, lightweight and stainless steel water bottle, with a refined design meant to be brought along at any time of day.</cite> The steel construction means no plastic taste, no BPA leaching concerns, and genuine durability — dent it and it'll still hold water, unlike a cracked Nalgene or a punctured soft flask. <cite index="12-16,12-17">It's worth noting this is a single-walled, non-insulated bottle, so it's likely to create condensation if filled with cold drinks, and it's not recommended for hot liquids since you could burn yourself handling it.</cite> That last point matters for backpackers: no hot cocoa or trailside coffee straight from this bottle, and if you're filling from a cold alpine stream, expect a wet ring on whatever it's touching in your pack.

The wide 3.3cm mouth is a genuine plus for backcountry use — <cite index="10-10">it allows comfortable drinking and easy ice filling</cite>, which also means it's easy to fill from a filter, dip in a stream, or scoop from a questionable water source without fumbling. It's also easy to clean, which some narrow-mouth ultralight bottles struggle with when things like electrolyte mixes or coffee residue start caking on the walls.

I couldn't find dedicated r/Ultralight, BPL, or CleverHiker threads specifically testing this bottle for backpacking — it lives almost entirely in the lifestyle/EDC/office-bottle review space (Trustpilot, Amazon lifestyle listings, university-student reviews in Italian praising it as "leggera" and "compatta" for campus use). That's a signal in itself: this product's actual user base is drinking coffee at their desk or carrying it to the gym, not filtering water on the AT. One Amazon reviewer specifically noted the tradeoff that matters here — <cite index="3-4">the only real drawback is the lack of double-wall insulation, though they understood the tradeoff was needed to keep the bottle compact and light</cite>.

On the trail specifically, the smooth steel exterior with no texture or grip pattern could get slippery in wet hands or with sunscreen residue, and the lack of any strap loop or carabiner attachment point (common on bottles like the Klean Kanteen or Hydro Flask lineups) means you're stuck stashing it in a side pocket rather than clipping it to a pack.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely food-safe, taste-neutral steel construction with no plastic liner
- Wide mouth makes filling, filtering, and cleaning easy
- Dent-resistant compared to plastic bottles — won't crack from a fall
- BPA/BPS/phthalate-free across the whole bottle and lid
- Reasonably light for a steel bottle at this capacity

**Cons**

- No insulation — condensation on the outside with cold water, unusable for hot drinks
- Meaningfully heavier than ultralight-specific hydration options (soft flasks, disposable-bottle reuse)
- No carry loop, carabiner clip, or webbing attachment for external pack storage
- Smooth steel body can get slick when wet
- Not marketed, tested, or designed with backpacking in mind — you're improvising a use case

## Who Should Buy This

This bottle makes the most sense for someone who already owns one for daily use — commuting, office, gym — and wants to bring the same bottle on a car-camping trip or an easy day hike where weight isn't the priority and durability, aesthetics, and no-plastic-taste matter more than ounces. If you're counting grams for a multi-day thru-hike, this isn't the bottle for that pack list; a Smartwater bottle or a purpose-built ultralight flask will serve you far better for a fraction of the weight.

## Verdict

The 24Bottles Urban Bottle is a well-made, good-looking steel bottle that does exactly what it's designed to do — just not for backpacking. If you need trail hydration, look elsewhere; if you want one nice bottle that pulls double duty between your desk and the occasional car-camping trip, it's a reasonable (if not remarkable) choice. I'd put it at a 5.5/10 specifically for backpacking use — solid product, wrong job.
