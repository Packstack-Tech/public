## Overview

The BIC Classic is the lighter everyone has owned: a disposable, isobutane-fueled, child-resistant pocket lighter that has been on shelves since 1973 and costs roughly a dollar or two apiece in multipacks. It earns its place in backpacking kits on reliability and sheer fuel capacity. The trade-off is weight. At 25.4 g, the full-size Classic is more than twice the heft of a Mini BIC, and gram-counters will notice.

## Performance

**Ignition and reliability.** This is where the Classic earns its reputation. Survival-gear testers who put it through repeated ignition trials scored it 10 out of 10 for ease of use and 9 for reliability, praising one-handed operation that still works with gloves on. Long-time backpackers say much the same in less formal terms: one veteran who has carried a BIC on every trip for more than four decades still reaches for one before anything else, and has dropped the cotton-ball fire kit, dryer lint and spare matches in its favor.

**Fuel capacity.** BIC rates the Classic at up to 3,000 lights, and the company claims up to twice the lights of the next leading full-size brand. Even if real-world numbers fall short of the rating, that is a deep tank. Lighting a stove twice a day, a single Classic can outlast a long thru-hike with fuel to spare. By comparison, the Mini BIC is generally cited at around 2,200 lights.

**Weather.** The Classic is a spark-wheel flame lighter, not a windproof torch. Testers rate its wind resistance at 6 out of 10 and its water resistance at 5, noting that the flame needs shielding in a breeze and that the lighter is not waterproof. The usual field fix works: blow water off the wheel, wipe it on a dry layer, and flick again. In deep cold, butane-class lighters can struggle to vaporize fuel, and owners who winter-camp keep the lighter in a pocket against the body until needed.

**Durability.** The plastic shell handles normal pack life, but testers flag that it can crack under pressure. A lighter wedged in a tight stuff sack or stored at the bottom of a loaded pack is the likeliest casualty. Wrapping it in a few turns of duct tape (which doubles as repair tape) or keeping it in a hip-belt pocket sidesteps most of that risk.

**Ergonomics.** The full-size body is where the extra grams go. Its longer barrel and larger spark wheel are easier to work with stiff, cold or gloved fingers than the Mini's tiny wheel, which is why some winter hikers carry the Classic despite the penalty.

**Weight.** At 25.4 g, the Classic is the heaviest part of most hikers' fire kits by a margin. The Mini BIC runs around 11.5 g and delivers essentially the same ignition performance. The "gram-weenie" consensus is that the full-size BIC works great but is heavier than it needs to be. Even the long-term devotee mentioned above calls it too heavy for backpacking and carries it only when a convenience store is out of Minis.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Proven ignition reliability, one-handed and glove-friendly
- Rated for up to 3,000 lights, enough for a season or more
- Sold nearly everywhere, from gas stations to grocery stores, at a trivial price
- Larger spark wheel and body are easier to use with cold or gloved hands
- Child-resistant design

**Cons**

- 25.4 g, more than double the weight of a Mini BIC
- Not windproof or waterproof; needs shielding and drying
- Plastic body can crack under pressure in a crowded pack
- Disposable, with no way to refill or repair when the fuel runs out
- Fuel level is hard to judge on opaque models, so a "dead" lighter can arrive unannounced

## Who Should Buy This

The Classic suits car campers, base-campers, day hikers, and cold-weather backpackers who struggle with a Mini's small wheel. It also works as a backup lighter, since it costs next to nothing and is easy to replace anywhere. Thru-hikers and ultralight devotees who are already carrying a Mini BIC should skip it. The Mini does the same job for less than half the weight, and a second lighter of any size is better insurance than a bigger first one.

## Verdict

The BIC Classic is a dependable, nearly foolproof fire starter that costs less than a cup of trail-town coffee. For anyone counting grams, though, the Mini BIC is the better lighter. Buy the Classic for easy handling and fuel capacity, not for weight savings.
