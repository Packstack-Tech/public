## Overview

The Archeos 2P is Blackdeer's entry-level double-wall backpacking tent — a freestanding, cross-pole design aimed at hikers who want a roomy 2-person shelter without paying Big Sky or MSR money. It's built for 3-season use with a mesh-and-poly inner, a PU-coated polyester fly, and 7001-series aluminum poles. This is a tent for budget-conscious backpackers and weekend campers who care more about interior volume and price-per-gram than brand cachet or cutting-edge fabrics.

## Performance

The listed 1,499 g weight is a genuinely interesting number for a freestanding 2P tent at this price point — it's competitive with far pricier double-wall shelters. That said, worth flagging: some retailer listings and the manufacturer's own product page list this same model at 2.45 kg, and even quote a body fabric spec of 190T Polyester with only PU450mm coating rather than a lighter fabric. <cite index="6-5">Inner tent material is listed as B3 mesh/190T Polyester/PU450mm W/R, tent bottom material as 150D Oxford/PU3000mm W/R.</cite> Given the discrepancy between the 1,499 g spec sheet and the ~2.45 kg figure that shows up elsewhere, it's worth weighing your own unit before you commit to using this on a long thru-hike — component weights can vary between production runs, and Blackdeer sells several closely-related "Archeos" variants (standard, UL, and 4-season with a snow skirt) that get confused across retailers.

Setup follows the classic cross-pole architecture that's been a mainstay in budget and mid-range tents for years — two poles cross at the peak to create a freestanding structure that pitches quickly without needing guy-outs to stand up. Blackdeer's own materials note that <cite index="7-9,7-11,7-12">the classic dual-cross pole design has been tested across countless conditions by hikers and backpackers over the years, and it's stable, reliable, and gives a surprising amount of interior space without weighing you down</cite> — which tracks with what I'd expect from this style of pole architecture generally, though I'd treat that specific framing as marketing language rather than independent testing.

At 215 x 130 x 100 cm, the floor plan is generous for two people — noticeably wider than many "2P" ultralight tarptents that pinch down at the foot end. The 100 cm peak height means you can sit up comfortably, which matters more than most spec sheets let on when you're waiting out an afternoon storm. The fly's PU2000mm rating is on the low end for sustained heavy rain — plenty of well-regarded 3-season tents sit at PU3000mm+ on the fly — so I wouldn't push this into consistently wet climates like the Pacific Northwest or Scottish Highlands without also seam-sealing carefully and pitching with a taut, low-angle fly. The floor's PU3000mm rating is more reassuring and should hold up fine on rocky or damp ground.

I wasn't able to find much in the way of independent long-term reviews from Reddit's r/Ultralight, Outdoor Gear Lab, or similar established review sites — Blackdeer is a smaller Chinese brand whose English-language footprint is mostly limited to Amazon and AliExpress listings and the brand's own marketing copy. That's worth noting plainly: the durability, seam quality, and zipper longevity claims here are largely unverified by third parties, so treat this as a budget gamble rather than a known-quantity purchase.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Competitive packaged weight (per spec sheet) for a freestanding double-wall 2P tent
- Generous floor dimensions and peak height for the price
- Freestanding cross-pole design pitches quickly, even for solo setup
- Floor fabric (150D Oxford, PU3000mm) is durable and well-suited to rough ground
- Low price relative to name-brand competitors with similar features
- Matching footprint available for extra floor protection

**Cons**

- Fly waterproof rating (PU2000mm) is modest for a 3-season tent; may struggle in prolonged heavy rain
- Conflicting weight and fabric specs across retailer listings make it hard to know exactly what you're getting
- Very little independent, long-term user testing or forum discussion to lean on
- Brand lacks the track record and customer support infrastructure of established ultralight makers
- 7.9mm poles are on the thinner side, which may be a concern in sustained high wind

## Who Should Buy This

This tent makes sense for budget-focused backpackers and car campers who want more interior space than a typical ultralight shelter offers and don't mind rolling the dice on a lesser-known brand to save money. It's a reasonable choice for mild 3-season trips — think established trails with decent weather windows — rather than exposed alpine routes or monsoon-season thru-hikes where fly waterproofing and pole robustness matter more. If you're chasing the lowest possible base weight with proven, battle-tested materials, look toward established ultralight brands instead; if you want solid square footage for two at a fraction of the cost, the Archeos 2P is worth a look.

## Verdict

The Archeos 2P offers a genuinely spacious, freestanding double-wall design at a price that undercuts most name-brand competitors, and the on-paper weight is respectable if the 1,499 g spec holds up in practice. But the modest fly waterproofing, the lack of independent long-term reviews, and inconsistent specs across listings mean this is a budget pick you buy with realistic expectations, not a giant-killer. I'd rate it a 6.5/10 — solid value for casual 3-season use, but not something I'd bet a thru-hike on without a season of field-testing first.
