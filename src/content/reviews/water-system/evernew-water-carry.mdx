## Overview

The Evernew Water Carry is a collapsible soft bottle that weighs less than a AA battery pair and has become a fixture on Sawyer-equipped thru-hiker kits. It comes in a 1.5-liter version at 36 g and a 2-liter Standard (EBY208) at 42 g. The trade-off is durability: it is thin plastic built to save grams, and it asks for gentler handling than a Nalgene or a recycled soda bottle.

## Performance

**Taste and odor.** Out of the package, the Japan-made Standard bottle has no apparent odor or residue. The low-odor, three-layer polyethylene holds that line over time: owners report no plastic taste and no microbial growth after months of continuous use. The spout and cap are polypropylene treated with silver powder, a germicide meant to help keep stored water fresh.

**Sawyer compatibility.** This is the reason most people buy it. The spout threads match Sawyer filters, so a Squeeze or Mini screws directly on. The bottle then works as a squeeze reservoir or a gravity-feed dirty bag without adapters. Hikers who have swapped out the stock Sawyer pouches describe the Evernew as noticeably tougher. For anyone already carrying a Sawyer, the bottle effectively becomes part of the filter system.

**Handling at camp.** The gusseted base lets a full bottle stand upright on flat ground. That helps when settling sediment, staging cooking water, or keeping a dirty bag from toppling into the dirt. The body tapers at the middle, which makes it easy to grab one-handed while filling at a shallow source. Compared with Platypus-style bottles, users describe the Evernew as stiffer and quicker to dry once emptied.

**Cap and storage.** The textured cap is tethered to the neck, so it stays attached while drinking or filling and can't be dropped in the dark. Some long-distance users report that the seal starts to weep when it isn't cranked down firmly, and a few see it worsen after extended thru-hike use. When empty, the bottle rolls into a tight cylinder, which an integrated shock cord holds closed. The Standard rolls to roughly the size of a pair of rolled-up boxer shorts, and it fits in a hip-belt or lid pocket without rattling.

**Gravity setups.** There is no hanging loop. Anyone suspending the bottle from a branch has to rig their own cord. Evernew marks an oval and a circle on the plastic as guides for boring attachment holes, so the manufacturer anticipates the modification. It takes a few minutes, and once rigged it works well.

**Durability.** Months-long thru-hikes have produced few reports of holes, delamination, or cracking in the body, and the occasional cap weep is the main complaint. The bottle still has limits. Carrying it full by the spout, direct high heat, and sharp objects can all damage it, and brush scrambles are harder on it than on a rigid bottle. The -20°C lower rating means cold mornings won't crack it, and the 90°C upper limit leaves headroom for warm fills. The 80 kgf pressure rating covers squeeze-filtering abuse, though hot-water bottle duty is not what it was designed for.

**Weight context.** At 42 g, the Standard is a hair heavier than some competing 2-liter soft bottles. The gap amounts to a few grams, and the Sawyer-ready thread is the reason most ultralighters accept it. The optional hydration tube is sold separately for anyone converting the bottle into a bladder.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share materials, cap, cord, gusseted base, and filter threading. Only the volume and footprint differ, and 6 g separates them.

**Standard (2.0 L, 42 g).** Evernew pitches this as the tent-site size: enough for dinner, breakfast, and a night's drinking without a refill. It also suits long dry stretches and groups that dirty-bag water for two. At 210 mm wide, it is the stable one when full.

**1500ML (1.5 L, 36 g).** The narrower bottle, about 6.7 in across by 13 in tall, saves 6 g and fits more easily in a side pocket or a snug pack. It suits hikers who filter often, camp near water, or run a Sawyer Mini for solo trips. Hikers who carry water for a full evening and morning routine will run short on 1.5 liters.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Threads fit Sawyer filters directly, with no adapters
- 36–42 g for 1.5–2 L
- Gusseted base stands upright when full
- Tethered cap can't be lost
- Built-in shock cord for compact rolled storage
- No reported taste or odor, even after months of use
- -20 to 90°C temperature range

**Cons**

- No hanging loop, so gravity setups need DIY holes
- Cap seal can weep over long use unless tightened firmly
- Less puncture and abrasion resistant than a rigid bottle
- Hydration tube sold separately
- A few grams heavier than some rival soft bottles

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone running a Sawyer Squeeze or Mini as a primary filter should start here. Thru-hikers who want a simple, low-odor soft bottle that costs little to replace mid-trail will find it a good fit, as will anyone who wants a stand-up camp water bag. Choose the Standard for dry sections and tent-site cooking, and the 1500ML for frequent water and tight packs. Skip it if the plan is hands-free sipping from a built-in hose, or if the route involves lots of brush and rock where a puncture-prone bottle would be a liability. In those cases, a tougher bottle or a purpose-built hydration reservoir makes more sense.

## Verdict

The Water Carry is a proven, Sawyer-ready soft bottle that gives up a little toughness for very little weight. The missing hanging loop and the occasional cap weep are minor irritations next to what it does well. It earns a clear recommendation for filter-equipped ultralight hikers.
