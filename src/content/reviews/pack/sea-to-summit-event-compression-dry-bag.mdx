## Overview

The Sea to Summit eVent Compression Dry Sack has been the default answer to a wet sleeping bag for close to twenty years. It's a roll-top stuff sack made from 70D nylon, with a breathable eVent panel in the base that lets air out while the load is cinched down. The 14-liter size fits a mid-weight three-season bag. It's durable and works as advertised, but at 148 g it weighs several times what a minimalist sack does.

## Performance

**Compression.** The design replaces the purge valve found on many compression sacks with a fabric vent. Stuff the bag, roll the top, and cinch the four straps. Air leaves through the eVent base, and water can't get back in. Sea to Summit rates the 14 L size to compress to 4.5 L, roughly a third of its open volume. With no valve to find, press, or break, there's less to fail. The straps pull evenly around the cylinder, so the finished bundle comes out as a tidy, round-bottomed shape that slides into the bottom of a pack. Outdoor Gear Lab's reviewer praised the venting design for making stuffing easier. The same review gave the sack a modest 5 out of 10 for ease of use.

**Water protection.** The 10,000 mm rating is more than enough for rain, creek splashes, and a pack dropped in a puddle. Testers at Outdoor Gear Lab held the 20 L version underwater and saw plenty of bubbles escape but no moisture inside. Sea to Summit's own guidance is more conservative. It says the sack isn't meant for submersion, and that the roll-top needs at least three folds before the buckle closes. Treat it as protection for a pack-liner failure or a soaked tent floor. Don't treat it as a packraft dry bag.

**Durability.** The 70D body is the point of this bag, and owners confirm it. A Trailspace reviewer with several sizes called the fabric durable enough to last years. Sea to Summit warns against sharp gravel and abrasion, which could compromise the waterproof coating. One owner noted that a bag damaged by a leaking fuel bottle was no longer fully watertight but was still usable. Since 2018, the buckle has been a field-repair design with stainless pins that can be swapped in seconds.

**Weight.** This is where ultralight hikers wince. The same Trailspace reviewer found the XS size, at about 100 g, much heavier than a lightweight fabric stuff sack of similar size. His advice was not to go ultralight with it. Sea to Summit sells a 30D Ultra-Sil version for that crowd, which it describes as 40 percent lighter than the 70D. The trade is fabric toughness: Ultra-Sil carries a 2,000 mm waterhead against the 70D's 10,000.

**What it holds.** Sea to Summit pairs the 14 L size with a mid-range three-season sleeping bag. Compression works best on lofty insulation like down or synthetic fill. Owners note that squeezing ordinary clothing gains little.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Valve-free venting is simple and has no parts to fail
- Compresses to roughly a third of its volume
- 70D fabric and taped seams hold up to years of use
- 10,000 mm rating handles rain and splashes comfortably
- Replaceable field-repair buckle
- Pull handle and round base make it easy to pack and extract

**Cons**

- At 148 g, it is heavy for an ultralight kit
- Not intended for submersion
- Little compression benefit for clothing
- Abrasion and sharp objects can compromise the coating
- Larger sizes are pricey, according to owners

## Who Should Buy This

This suits backpackers carrying a down or synthetic sleeping bag who want it shrunk and waterproofed in one piece of kit. That includes anyone in wet climates, on canoe or kayak trips, or on bikepacking routes where gear gets rough handling. It also suits hikers who would rather replace nothing for a decade. Gram-counting thru-hikers who already line their pack with a trash compactor bag or a roll-top liner should look at the Ultra-Sil version or a plain stuff sack instead. So should anyone who needs a fully submersible bag.

## Verdict

The eVent Compression Dry Sack is a rugged, simple, and proven way to keep a sleeping bag small and dry. Its 148 g weight puts it outside the ultralight mainstream, and there is no way around that. Buy it for a durable, no-nonsense setup, and pick the Ultra-Sil version if weight matters more.
