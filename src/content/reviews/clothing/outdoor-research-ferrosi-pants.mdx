## Overview

The Ferrosi Pants have been a fixture on American trails for years. They're a stretchy, wind-resistant nylon pant that works equally well on a three-season backpacking route and in an airport security line. At 360 g (12.7 oz) they're light for a full-length pant but not ultralight-specific. The trade-off is a relaxed straight-leg cut and a fabric that some owners say wears faster than its reputation suggests.

## Performance

**Comfort and mobility.** Stretch is the Ferrosi's calling card. With 14 percent spandex in a ripstop-style woven, the fabric moves with the wearer rather than against them. CleverHiker's testers praised the room through the knees and hips, and said the pants were good enough on the trail that they took them out for a light run. A brushed tricot waistband liner and an internal drawcord let the waist be cinched without a belt. CleverHiker's reviewers specifically credited the felted liner with reducing chafing.

**Breathability and weather.** The weave is airy, and the fabric is quick-drying and carries UPF 50+ sun protection, which makes the pants suitable for exposed ridgelines and desert hiking. The water resistance is a durable finish, not a membrane. It handles a passing shower or wet brush and nothing more. Owners on REI's site say the pants suit warm-weather hiking, but they need long underwear beneath them once temperatures drop. That matches the fabric's weight: it blocks wind and sheds light moisture, but it adds little insulation.

**Pockets.** Outdoor Research's own spec lists drop-in hand pockets and a zippered security pocket on the right hip. Reviewers count up to five pockets in total, including a zippered thigh pocket and two back pockets. A phone fits in the zipped hip pocket. Better Trail's tester found the front pockets sit too high and too centered, which made grabbing a phone mid-stride awkward.

**Fit.** The cut is generous. A reviewer on REI called the pants ridiculously baggy and noted that they run longer than the stated 30-inch inseam, so they bunch at the ankle. That is a recurring theme: the Ferrosi is a relaxed pant, and those who prefer a trim silhouette tend to look at the tapered Ferrosi Transit, a lighter 309 g sibling in the same fabric family. The 30, 32 and 34 inch inseams, along with half of the waist sizes in single-inch steps, help dial in the fit.

**Durability.** The fabric is rated abrasion-resistant, and the 4.5-star average across 476 REI reviews suggests most owners are satisfied. A minority report otherwise: one reviewer there said they had never had a pair of pants fray so quickly. Nylon with this much stretch will pick up pilling and scuffs at the seat and knees from scrambling and sitting on granite. Treat the pants as a hiking garment, not a bushwhacking one.

**Climbing use.** The waistband is built to sit comfortably under a climbing harness, with no bulky closure hardware pressing against the belay loop, which is useful for scramblers and alpine approaches.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent stretch with room through the hips and knees
- Breathable, quick-drying fabric with UPF 50+
- Wind- and water-resistant enough for passing weather
- Internal drawcord and tricot-lined waist make a belt optional
- Three inseam lengths and single-inch waist sizing from 30 to 36
- Harness-compatible waist for scrambling and climbing
- Recycled content in a bluesign® approved fabric

**Cons**

- Baggy cut that runs long for some, and it bunches at the ankle
- Front pockets sit high and are awkward for quick phone access
- Some owners report early fraying
- At 360 g, they are heavier than the Ferrosi Transit and than dedicated ultralight hiking pants
- Not warm enough for cold-season hiking without a base layer

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers, section hikers and travelers who want one durable, stretchy pair of pants that holds up for a week on trail and still looks acceptable in a town. They suit climbers and scramblers who need a harness-friendly waist. Gram-counters carrying a 5 lb base weight, or anyone who wants a tailored, tapered fit, should look at the Transit or a dedicated ultralight wind pant.

## Verdict

The Ferrosi Pants are a proven all-rounder: comfortable, breathable, sun-protective and versatile enough to wear for months at a time. The loose cut and occasional durability complaints keep it from a top score, but for most hikers it remains one of the safest buys in the category.
