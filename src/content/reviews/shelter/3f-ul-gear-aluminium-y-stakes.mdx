## Overview

The Aluminium Y Stakes are the stock pegs 3F UL Gear packs with its tents, and the company sells them one at a time so owners can replace a lost stake or build a set to their own count. At 9 g (0.32 oz) apiece and about $1 each on the manufacturer's site, they are among the cheapest ways to put a lightweight, Y-section aluminum stake in the pack. The trade-off is the usual one for budget aluminum: they are light and tough, but they are a general-purpose peg, not a specialist for sand, snow or very hard ground.

## Performance

**Weight.** Nine grams is a real number to compare against. 3F's own hollow aluminum stakes are listed at 10 g in the 15 cm length and 12 g at 20 cm, and they come only in packs of six or ten at $11 to $19. The Y stake undercuts the 15 cm hollow model by a gram and can be bought in ones and twos. Against third-party Y stakes, the gap is wider: Mons Peak IX's 7075 Y stake is listed at 17 g, nearly double. For a typical six-stake pitch, the 3F pegs come to roughly 54 g, a figure that is hard to beat without moving to carbon.

**Holding power.** The Y cross-section is the standard compromise in aluminum stakes. It puts three ridges of surface area against the soil, which grips better than a round rod in loam and forest duff while still slipping between stones better than a wide V. Owners of 3F's tents use these stakes as the baseline for pitching Lanshan-style shelters, and the brand's own guidance is that four-sided aluminum stakes suit most soft-soil camps, with cylindrical stakes held back for hard ground. That framing fits the Y: it is a soft-to-medium ground peg. In loose sand or snow, any slim aluminum stake pulls out easily, and hikers headed there generally bury the stake horizontally or carry wider pegs.

**Durability.** The 7-series alloy is the same family used in MSR's Groundhog-style stakes, and it resists bending far better than the 6000-series bargain pegs bundled with cheap tents. Reviewers who looked at an earlier 3F aluminum stake, an X-finned design in the same alloy family, described it as strong and bend-resistant, with the one complaint that it was a bit hard to push in by hand in firm soil. The same caveat applies to any narrow aluminum stake: in compacted trail-side ground, a boot heel or a rock is the driver of choice, and a stubborn stake can be tough to extract without a pull loop or a cord tied to the head.

**Value.** The sold-individually format is the underrated feature. Tent owners who have lost a stake mid-trip no longer have to buy a whole pack, and tarp and hammock users can mix in a few extras for guy-out points. The flip side is that shipping on a $1 item can erase the savings on small orders, so buyers do best adding several stakes, or the stakes alongside a tent or a guyline order.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 9 g per stake, lighter than 3F's 15 cm hollow stake and about half the weight of some Western Y stakes
- 7-series alloy resists bending better than commodity aluminum pegs
- Y profile grips well in soil and duff and splits gravel reasonably well
- Sold individually, so a lost stake costs about a dollar rather than a pack
- Same stake 3F packs with its own tents, so replacements match

**Cons**

- Slim aluminum offers limited holding power in sand, snow and very loose ground
- Narrow body can be hard to push in by hand and to pull out in firm soil
- Not a hard-ground stake; rocky or compacted sites call for a sturdier peg
- Overseas shipping on a $1 item can erase the price advantage on small orders

## Who Should Buy This

3F UL Gear tent owners are the obvious audience: these are the stakes that came with the shelter, and buying extras for guylines or replacing a lost one is cheap and simple. Budget-minded tarp campers and thru-hikers who pitch mainly in forest and meadow soils will also find them a good fit as a main set. Hikers whose routes cross desert sand, snowfields or alpine scree should look elsewhere, to wider-profile or specialty stakes, and anyone who wants a stake built for cobble-hard ground should carry a few heavier pegs in addition.

## Verdict

The Aluminium Y Stakes do what a standard ultralight stake should: they weigh next to nothing, hold in ordinary soil, and cost about as much as a candy bar. They are a smart buy for 3F tent owners and cost-conscious backpackers, and a poor primary choice for sand, snow or rock-hard camps.
