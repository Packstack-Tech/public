## Overview

The Exos Pro 55 is Osprey's answer to the cottage-industry frameless crowd: a 950 g, 55-liter frame pack that keeps the AirSpeed tensioned mesh back panel and adjustable harness of the heavier Exos 58 while shedding close to a pound. It suits hikers moving up from a conventional pack who want a ventilated back and a fit they can dial in. The trade-off is fabric and mesh that are thinner than a thru-hike's worst days might prefer.

## Performance

**Comfort and ventilation.** The AirSpeed panel is the reason to buy this pack. A curved, tensioned mesh sheet holds the load off the wearer's back, and reviewers keep returning to the same result: less sweat than packs that sit flat against the spine. A reviewer for The Great Outdoors noted it was the only pack in his test group built this way. Backpackingroutes.com called the ride a dream and said the suspension puts no pressure from gear on the back. UK writer Rudolf Abraham called it the most comfortable pack he had used, and he found it stable despite weighing less than many day packs. Perforated foam on the shoulder straps and hipbelt adds to the airflow. The shoulder straps are well padded and the hipbelt is a little less so.

**Load carrying.** The 3.5 mm LightWire frame carries more than its weight suggests. Load lifters on the shoulder straps let hikers pull the pack in as the food bag shrinks. Testers settle around 12 kg (roughly 26 lb) as the comfortable zone. Abraham carried near the top of Osprey's range on the High Scardus Trail, with a tripod strapped to one side, and the pack coped. Outdoor Gear Lab's tester went well past the manufacturer's limit with little complaint from his back. Even so, 35 lb is a ceiling to treat with respect, not a target for a long food carry.

**Fit.** The torso length adjusts by about four inches, and reviewers consistently call the system easy to change and more precise than the Exos 58's. The two sizes (S/M and L/XL) cover a wide range of torsos.

**Storage.** The pack has six pockets. The side pockets open from the top and the side, so a bottle can be pulled while the pack stays on. Outdoor Gear Lab found them among the most voluminous it had tested, with room for five 700 ml Smartwater bottles. REI owners report that a large Bear Vault canister fits vertically or horizontally. Hipbelt pockets are mounted far back, and one owner found the zipper awkward to close. The front pocket is solid fabric with mesh side panels, which stretches less than all-mesh designs and should wear better. Taking off the removable lid saves weight and cuts 9 liters from the capacity.

**Weight-to-volume.** At 950 g the pack works out to about 0.6 oz per liter counting all pockets, and 0.89 oz per liter for the main compartment alone. That is heavier than cottage frameless packs, and a few reviewers say so. The extra grams buy the suspension and the features.

**Durability.** Here the pack draws its most pointed criticism. The NanoFly fabric is thin, and reviewers warn against rough off-trail travel or sliding down rocks on it. Adventure Alan flagged the stretch mesh pockets and the frame as delicate. His site argued the pack falls short of the durability a full thru-hike demands. One REI owner raised a similar worry. Osprey's repair guarantee softens that, but trail repair is still on the hiker. The frame is also tall, which can bump the back of the head when looking up or crowd a brimmed hat.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- AirSpeed back panel keeps the back cooler than flat-panel ultralight packs
- Around 950 g with a real frame and load lifters
- Four inches of torso adjustment, easy to change
- Generous, easy-to-reach side pockets; fits a bear canister
- Removable 74 g lid saves weight on lean trips

**Cons**

- Heavier per liter than frameless cottage packs
- Thin fabric and stretch mesh raise durability concerns on rough terrain
- Tall frame can interfere with head movement and hat brims
- Hipbelt pockets sit far back and can be fiddly
- List price around $330

## Who Should Buy This

This is the pack for the backpacker leaving a 3–4 lb pack behind and unwilling to give up a ventilated back and structured carry. It suits warm-weather, established-trail hiking at base weights that keep the load around 25 lb. Hikers who want maximum durability for off-trail scrambles, or who are chasing the lowest possible weight per liter, should look at a Dyneema-style cottage pack instead.

## Verdict

The Exos Pro 55 is the most comfortable ultralight pack for its weight and the easiest to live with, if not the toughest. Buy it for maintained trails and loads of about 12 kg, and protect the thin fabric. Hikers who want a pack built for hard use should look elsewhere.
