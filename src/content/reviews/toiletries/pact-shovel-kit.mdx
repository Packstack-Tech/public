## Overview

The Pact Shovel Kit, sold by the company as the Lite Bathroom Kit, turns a hollow aluminum trowel into its own carrying case. The handle holds compressed plant-based wipes and mycelium tabs, so digging, wiping and burying waste all come out of one tube. It suits backpackers who want a single tidy bathroom system, and the trade-off is that the supplies are proprietary and the tube holds less than a full 20-trip refill pack.

## Performance

**Digging.** The trowel is a rigid aluminum tube with a serrated edge, and owners describe it as sturdy. Buyers report it doesn't flex in hard, root-laced ground, and one Colorado user called it burly in packed dirt. The handle has no sharp edges, so it stays comfortable through a deep hole. GearJunkie's tester noted that rocky soil will scratch the finish, so aim for dirt, not cobble. The 6-inch depth marker is a small feature that matters. A reviewer on REI said the hole needs to be deeper than most people expect.

**The system.** Each wipe is a dry, compressed cellulose disc that unfolds into a roughly 9-inch towel with a squirt of water. Pact says the wipes contain no plastics, additives or antimicrobial treatments, and lists them as breaking down 95 percent in 75 days. Three mycelium tabs go into the hole with the used wipe, and Pact claims its fungal species breaks waste down up to 10 times faster while neutralizing pathogens such as E. coli. That is the company's claim, not an independent test result, but the wipe-plus-tab routine is simple to follow in practice. The common complaint is logistical. One REI reviewer kept forgetting to bring water to expand the wipe, and another sometimes needed two wipes per trip.

**Capacity.** Pact pitches the handle as a week of supplies. GearJunkie's testing put the real range at 4 to 8 days, depending on how often the kit is used and by how many people. The tube holds a maximum of 15 wipes and about 30 tabs, so the full 60-tab, 20-wipe pouches are really a refill for the tube. Hikers on longer stretches either carry spares in a pouch or accept a second bag of supplies. Refill packs of 20 wipes and 60 tabs retailed for $22 at launch, against $35 for the whole kit.

**Carry.** At 4 oz stocked, the kit has little effect on pack weight. Everything is enclosed, so there is no trowel point to punch through a pack and no loose toilet paper. It slides into a side pocket or hip belt and has a small loop for clipping to a pack. Users of Pact's standalone shovel report that the loop's plastic cap can detach after heat exposure, such as in a hot car, and that dirt can occasionally pack into the cylindrical handle. The kit's own design hides that handle behind the storage cartridge, so it's less of an issue there.

**Weight in context.** At 120 g stocked, it is not the lightest way to bury waste. A bare ultralight trowel and a few sheets of toilet paper weigh less. Pact's own Ultra Lite kit comes in at 2.3 oz but holds only about three days of supplies. The Shovel Kit's appeal is that its weight includes the wipes, tabs and a sturdy digging tool.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Digging tool, wipes and tabs live in one 8.5-inch tube
- Rigid aluminum blade that holds up in hard soil
- 6-inch depth marker helps with proper cathole depth
- Plant-based wipes and mycelium tabs support Leave No Trace practice
- Lifetime guarantee on the shovel

**Cons**

- Proprietary supplies mean ongoing refill costs
- Tube holds only a portion of the 20-trip supply, with 4 to 8 days of real-world use
- Wipes need water to expand, which hikers occasionally forget
- Heavier than a bare ultralight trowel plus toilet paper
- Doesn't replace pack-out requirements in areas that mandate carrying waste out

## Who Should Buy This

This kit is for weekend and week-long backpackers who dislike the usual jumble of trowel, toilet paper, bags and sanitizer. It also suits anyone who finds the fiddling of a DIY kit annoying enough to pay for a neat one. It works well for hikers who don't mind a refill routine and want a trowel that holds up in hard ground. Gram-counters who already carry a minimal trowel and a small stash of toilet paper, and thru-hikers resupplying on a tight budget, will get less from it. Anyone traveling in river corridors, alpine zones or other places that require packing out waste needs a different system.

## Verdict

The Pact Shovel Kit is a well-built, convenient answer to the backcountry bathroom problem, and it's the neatest single-tube option for most backpackers. The weight and refill cost keep it from being a pick for the most extreme ultralighters. For anyone who values organization and a sturdy blade, it earns a place in the pack.
