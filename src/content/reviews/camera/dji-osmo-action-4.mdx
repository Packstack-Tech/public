## Overview

The Osmo Action 4 is DJI's fourth-generation action camera: a 145 g, metal-bodied, waterproof box that clips to a pack strap with a magnet and shoots stabilized 4K at up to 120 fps. Its headline upgrade over the Action 3 is a 1/1.3-inch sensor, which cleans up dim light and shadows. The trade-off is a longer minimum focus distance and a weight that, while modest for what it does, is still real money on a base-weight spreadsheet.

## Performance

**Image quality.** The bigger sensor is the reason this camera exists. TechRadar's reviewer found highlights such as bright clouds and evening lights hold detail the Action 3 could not manage, with cleaner shadows. Tom's Guide notes the sensor is a step above both the Action 3's 1/1.7-inch chip and the 1/1.9-inch unit in the GoPro Hero12 Black. Owners on Reddit are more split: the camera earns praise for reliable footage, but low-light performance is a recurring point of argument, and some users report weak 1080p quality, so 4K is the mode to shoot in. For a trail camera, that means golden-hour ridgelines and tent-door shots at dusk look good, while deep-woods dusk still shows noise.

**Stabilization.** This is where the Action 4 earns its reputation. RockSteady 3.0+ is described by TechRadar as incredibly effective, to the point that downhill bikers will not need a gimbal. An owner who upgraded from the original Osmo Action said the footage looks great straight out of the camera, with no post-processing needed. Hikers get the same benefit: footage from a chest-strap or pack-strap mount stays watchable at a walking cadence and holds up on scrambles. HorizonSteady keeps the frame level, though it is limited to 2.7K or 1080p at 60 fps or below.

**Durability and weather.** The body is metal, and Tom's Guide calls the build impressive. At 18 m bare, it is rated 8 m deeper than the Hero12 Black. For hikers, that means creek crossings, rain and a dropped camera in the mud are non-events, and the -20°C rating covers shoulder-season and winter trips. Reddit owners list reliability and durability among the camera's top strengths. File corruption has shown up in owner complaints, so a quality microSD card and a backup routine are worth the effort.

**Battery.** DJI quotes up to 2.5 hours of operation, with 160 minutes at 1080p/24 and about 150 minutes in extreme environments. Owners consistently rank battery life among the camera's best traits, and the battery is removable. A spare at 33.5 g is the lightest way to double capacity, which beats a power bank and cable for most itineraries. Fast charging reaches 80% in 18 minutes.

**Mounting and handling.** The magnetic quick-release system is the feature reviewers praise most. TechRadar says it outdoes the GoPros on convenience, and Reddit owners list it among the top pros. It lets a camera come off a shoulder strap for a quick summit selfie, then go back on in seconds. The dual touchscreens make framing easy, and the front screen is useful for vlog-style trail updates. Wearing gloves in cold weather is a separate matter, since small touch targets are no friendlier to mittens.

**Limitations.** The 155° lens is very wide, and the larger sensor raised the minimum focus distance from 0.3 m to 0.4 m. TechRadar notes this pushes users toward all-encompassing views rather than close details, so wildflower macros and gear close-ups are not what this camera does well. Digital zoom is limited to 2x in video. There is no resolution above 4K, which matters little for web delivery. The Action 4 has also been succeeded by the Action 5. Reddit users say the newer camera is similar overall, with somewhat better low-light handling.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 1/1.3-inch sensor gives cleaner low-light and shadow detail than the Action 3 and GoPro Hero12 Black
- Excellent stabilization that works without a gimbal
- 18 m waterproofing with no case, plus -20°C cold rating
- Removable battery with fast charging; a spare weighs 33.5 g
- Magnetic mounting is quick and easy on a pack strap
- 4K/120 slow motion and 10-bit D-Log M color

**Cons**

- 145 g is a real addition for a gram-counter, and it sits on the shoulder strap
- 0.4 m minimum focus rules out close-ups
- Low-light results are inconsistent according to some owners; 1080p looks soft
- Some owners report file corruption
- Superseded by the Action 5

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers, packrafters and bikepackers who want to film a trip as they move through it, in rain, water and cold, will get the most from the Action 4. It suits those who want footage they can use without a gimbal or heavy editing. Gram-counters who shoot a handful of photos a day should stay with their phone, which already weighs less than a dedicated camera and spare battery. Anyone who wants close-up detail, such as flowers, gear and food shots, will find the focus limit annoying.

## Verdict

The Osmo Action 4 is a tough, well-stabilized action camera with a sensor that gives it better image quality than the Action 3 or Hero12 Black, in a package that weighs about 5 oz. Hikers who want to film in the weather rather than around it should buy one, preferably at a discount now that the Action 5 has arrived. Those who shoot mostly stills and snapshots should stick with a phone.
