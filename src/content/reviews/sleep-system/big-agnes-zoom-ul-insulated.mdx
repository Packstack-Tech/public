## Overview

The Zoom UL Insulated is Big Agnes's lightest sleeping pad: a rectangular, three-season air mattress that weighs 397 g (14 oz) in the regular size and rolls down to roughly the size of a water bottle. It is aimed at ultralight backpackers who want the deep, stable platform of a heavier pad without carrying the weight. The trade-off is convenience. The valve is basic, the shell wants a clean campsite, and some testers found the warmth doesn't quite match the 4.3 R-value on the label.

## Performance

**Comfort.** This is where the Zoom UL earns its reputation. Most pads at this weight are tapered mummies. The Zoom UL keeps full rectangular corners, and the result is a sleeping surface that back, side and stomach sleepers can all use. CleverHiker's testers noted that the 3.25-inch center and 3.5-inch side rails give side sleepers enough depth, and that the raised rails help keep a sleeper centered through the night. A Better Trail tester, a 6'3" hiker used to narrow mummy pads, described the wide profile as luxurious. The honeycomb-style baffling kept hips and shoulders off rocky desert ground. One thru-hiker with three long trails on a NeoAir XLite switched to the Zoom UL for exactly this reason: more loft for side sleeping. Reddit owners echo the praise, with one calling it the most comfortable pad they had slept on. The 30D quilted top fabric is quiet, so there's less crinkling every time a sleeper rolls over.

**Warmth.** Big Agnes rates it at R 4.3, which on paper puts it a step behind the Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite NXT (4.5, 13 oz). Reports in the field are mostly favorable but not uniform. Owners describe it as comfortable down to about freezing, and some add a thin foam Z-mat below that. Gear Junkie's tester found it excellent for three seasons and only felt ground chill on a below-freezing night in a Utah snowstorm. Switchback Travel, however, said the pad doesn't fully live up to its stated R-value, and Better Trail's testers found it underperformed in colder temperatures. The practical read: it's reliable through spring, summer and fall, and shoulder-season nights near freezing with a solid bag. Winter use needs a foam pad underneath.

**Packability.** At 10 x 20 cm, it's smaller than a Nalgene bottle. That's among the better figures in the category. The included stuff sack doubles as a pump sack. Some owners like that it keeps breath moisture out of the pad. One reviewer called the sack frustratingly small, and CleverHiker's testers found it less efficient than their favorite inflation systems. Other owners skip it and use a small battery inflator.

**Valve and deflation.** This is the most consistent complaint. The single-port, dual-function valve handles both inflation and deflation, but there is no dedicated dump valve. Deflating means a lot of squeezing and rolling. Gear Junkie called the process tricky and time-consuming, and Better Trail's testers said the valve is slow to deflate and can leak. A Reddit owner reported a slow valve leak after about two years. Big Agnes replaced the pad even though the warranty had expired.

**Durability.** The pad uses a 30D top and a tougher bottom fabric, and TPU lamination around the welds and valve. CleverHiker's testers covered about 300 miles and 20 nights on the Arizona Trail with no major durability concerns. Gear Junkie's tester reported no visible wear or leaks after several months of regular use. Better Trail's testers disagreed, calling the shell fragile and slippery, which means a sleeping bag can slide around. As with any pad this light, thorns, sharp rocks and sticks are the enemy. A few owners report small leaks that were fixed with seam grip.

**Value.** Retail runs roughly $180–$200, which is less than the equivalent Therm-a-Rest and Nemo pads. Switchback Travel placed it between those two competitors in weight. It is also available in wide and long-wide sizes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 397 g with a 4.3 R-value
- Rectangular shape with deep side rails keeps side sleepers on the pad and comfortable
- Packs to about 10 x 20 cm
- Quiet top fabric
- Priced below comparable Therm-a-Rest and Nemo pads
- Pump sack included
- Recycled nylon shell

**Cons**

- No dedicated dump valve, so deflation is slow
- Warmth falls short of the rated R-value for some testers
- Shell needs careful campsite selection
- Reports of slow valve leaks
- The stuff sack/pump sack frustrates some users

## Who Should Buy This

Side sleepers and restless sleepers who are tired of narrow mummy pads but don't want to take on 20 oz or more will get the most from the Zoom UL. It suits three-season thru-hikers and weekend backpackers who pitch on duff or sand and are willing to put in a few extra seconds at the valve each morning. Cold sleepers and shoulder-season hikers regularly camping below freezing should look at a warmer pad or add a foam layer. Anyone who is hard on gear, or who wants one-handed quick deflation, will be happier with something sturdier or a pad with a proper dump valve.

## Verdict

The Zoom UL Insulated delivers one of the most comfortable sleeping surfaces at this weight, in a pack-friendly package that costs less than its rivals. The slow valve and delicate shell keep it from a higher score, and cold sleepers should treat the R 4.3 as optimistic. For three-season ultralight hikers who value sleep quality, it's a strong buy.
