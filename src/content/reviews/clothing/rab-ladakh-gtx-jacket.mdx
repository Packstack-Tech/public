## Overview

The Ladakh GTX is Rab's do-everything mountain hardshell, splitting the difference between a burly alpine shell and something you'd actually want to wear on a rainy weekend hike. It mixes 70D GORE-TEX Pro in the high-wear zones (shoulders, back, upper arms) with softer, quieter 40D GORE-TEX C-KNIT everywhere else, then tops it off with a genuinely helmet-compatible hood. It's aimed at hikers and alpinists who want one jacket that can handle a UK winter walk-in and a scramble up something steeper, without hauling around a full-on Pro shell like Rab's own Latok.

## Performance

**Waterproofing.** This is a 3-layer GORE-TEX jacket rated to 28,000 mm, and reviewers consistently report it holding up in genuinely miserable conditions. <cite index="1-1,1-2">One reviewer noted the jacket kept them dry in very wet weather, with internal condensation kept to a minimum due to its breathability.</cite> A UK-based tester who used the jacket through storms Ciara, Dennis, and Jorge during what was reportedly <cite index="1-6,1-7">the wettest February on record</cite> came away satisfied. Another reviewer who thrashed it through winter hikes said he came out of a session <cite index="4-1">95% dry, warm and satisfied that throughout the hike I'd not had to consider my protection for the elements</cite> — which is really the highest praise you can give a shell: you stop thinking about it.

**Breathability and comfort.** The C-KNIT backer is the standout here. Multiple reviewers commented on how much quieter and softer it feels compared to a full Pro shell — <cite index="5-3">the trade-off of the circular knit C-KNIT backer used for most of the jacket is that it's soft, quiet and very wearable</cite>. If you've worn an older-style Pro shell and winced at the crinkly fabric noise every time you move your arms, this hybrid construction is a noticeable step up in comfort, and one reviewer specifically called out that <cite index="5-14">if you've ever winced at the snap, crackle and pop soundtrack that goes with Pro, you'll be pleasantly surprised</cite>.

**Fit and cut.** At 80 cm center back length, this runs long — <cite index="7-2,7-3">the jacket has a longer front and back than average, so it fits nicely over the hips, and the back is also longer than the front</cite>. That's a deliberate mountaineering cut: good for layering and coverage when scrambling or carrying a pack, less ideal if you want a trim, athletic silhouette for fast hiking. One reviewer specifically preferred a slimmer jacket for lighter, faster days but reached for a comparable Rab shell (the Kangri) when conditions got serious — a pattern that likely holds for the Ladakh too. There's also room to layer: <cite index="7-1">in cold conditions, there is still enough space under the jacket for an insulating intermediate layer.</cite>

**Hood and helmet compatibility.** This is one of the most-praised features across reviews. <cite index="5-1">The hood is brilliant and gives you the option of wearing a helmet too.</cite> The 3-way adjustment plus wired laminated peak keeps the visor rigid in wind and rain rather than flopping into your eyeballs, and testers found the peak did a genuinely good job keeping the elements off their face.

**Ventilation and details.** The pit zips get consistent praise for being generously sized — one tester called them out directly, noting <cite index="4-7,4-8">working hard on the ascent I had the underarm vents wide open, and Rab have made them plenty big enough to give you maximum venting on this jacket</cite>. Small design touches also earned attention: the two-way main zip lets you crack the bottom open when a harness or a bathroom break demands it, and the stitchless hem/cuff construction was singled out as unobtrusive in the best way — <cite index="4-9">the stitchless hem and cuffs are brilliant, I didn't notice them until I watched a YouTube review... that's about the best compliment you can give such a detail.</cite>

**Durability concerns.** The main knock against the jacket isn't performance — it's transparency around sustainability. One independent reviewer flagged that <cite index="7-5">Rab must work harder on sustainable production and be open about which DWR layer is used on the jacket</cite>, and noted the DWR coating isn't PFC-free. If eco-credentials matter to your buying decision, that's worth knowing going in, even though the shell fabric itself is made from recycled GORE-TEX.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 3-layer GORE-TEX with a genuine track record of keeping people dry through sustained, nasty weather
- C-KNIT backer is noticeably quieter and softer against layers than a straight Pro shell
- Helmet-compatible hood with wired peak is a standout — rigid, adjustable, and well-regarded across multiple independent tests
- Generously sized pit zips actually move air when you're working hard on the ascent
- Thoughtful small details: two-way zip, stitchless cuffs/hem, harness-compatible hand pockets

**Cons**

- At 525 g and an 80 cm back length, this is not an ultralight rain shell — it's a mountain hardshell that prioritizes durability and coverage over minimal weight
- DWR coating isn't PFC-free, and one reviewer felt Rab could be more transparent about sustainability specifics
- The longer, roomier cut that layers well also means it won't suit hikers who prefer a trim, athletic fit for fast-and-light days
- 70D Pro panels, while more durable, still carry a bit of that stiffer, crinklier hand-feel compared to the C-KNIT sections

## Who Should Buy This

This jacket makes the most sense for hikers and alpinists who spend real time in sustained UK-style wet weather — walk-ins, scrambles, winter mountain days — and want one shell that can flex between casual hiking and more technical terrain without switching jackets. If you're strictly counting grams for a thru-hike and only need occasional rain protection, there are lighter, less burly options that'll serve you better. But if you want a shell that disappears into the background during an all-day soak and still has the helmet compatibility and durability for scrambling, the Ladakh earns its keep.

## Verdict

The Ladakh GTX delivers on its promise as a genuine mountain all-rounder — reliably waterproof, comfortably breathable, and detailed with the kind of small design touches that only show up after real trail time. It's not the jacket for gram-counters, but for anyone who needs a hardshell that can handle a proper mountain day and still feel wearable on the walk back to the car, it's a easy 8/10.
