## Overview

The Noaks Bag is a German-made pouch that looks like a freezer bag and behaves more like a miniature dry bag. A double-lip closure and a 100 µm composite film give it an IPX8 rating, odour containment and food-safe credentials, all at 4.1 g. The trade-off is scale: at 0.04 L, this format is built for the small, critical stuff, not for organising a pack.

## Performance

**Waterproofing.** Noaks rates the bags to IPX8, which the company and its retailers describe as protection to at least 10 metres for two hours. Owners mostly test it in less dramatic ways. One German reviewer carried valuables through a swim inside a stuff sack that let in roughly 100 ml of water; the Noaks bags inside stayed dry. A two-month test by the Austrian outlet Spartan found the bags fully waterproof as long as they were sealed properly. That last condition matters. The seal depends on the user running the double lip closed along its whole length, and an Italian buyer noted that it takes a patient pass to get it right. In a rainstorm at a trailhead with cold fingers, that is the failure point, not the film.

**Closure.** The double-lip design is wider and easier to grip than a standard press-seal, and reviewers describe it as quick to open and close. At 9 cm, the closure is short, which helps with the small footprint but leaves little margin for a bag that has been stuffed too full.

**Material and durability.** The 100 µm composite film is noticeably heavier-duty than a supermarket zip bag, and long-term owners call it robust. Spartan's testers observed the one cosmetic cost of use: the plastic wrinkles and turns slightly matte over time, which makes it harder to read a screen or label through it. A Belgian buyer of the largest size was less happy, describing a stiff, smelly bag that deformed after a few days in a pack. That complaint is an outlier against an average of 4.6 stars across more than 1,800 ratings on Amazon.de, where buyers praise quality, durability and odour resistance. Closure and price draw the most criticism.

**Odour and food use.** The film is certified food-safe and sealed against smell, a practical edge for stashing snacks, soap, or anything else a bear canister would otherwise have to handle. One caveat from owners: the narrow pouches are awkward to rinse out, so greasy food residue is a poor match.

**Touchscreen use.** Spartan reports that touchscreens work through the film, though phones with front sensors can throw up a cover warning. At this size, the point is moot, since a phone will not fit.

**On the trail.** At 40 mL of volume, the pouch suits a day's pills, a SIM card and microSD cards, a spare lighter flint, a small repair kit, or a copy of a permit. Customers on Amazon note a pre-punched pair of holes at the top edge, which allows a loop of cord for attaching the bag to a pack strap or a boat. At 4.1 g, the weight penalty is negligible, and several of them together barely register on a scale. Transparency is a modest bonus: contents are visible without opening anything.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 4.1 g, a weight nobody will count against a base weight
- IPX8 waterproofing, backed by long-term owner testing
- Transparent film makes contents easy to identify
- Odour-proof and food-safe
- Double-lip closure is easier to handle than a standard zip bag
- Top holes allow lanyard or cord attachment
- Inexpensive; a set of five of the smallest size starts around €10.95

**Cons**

- 0.04 L volume limits it to very small items
- Closure takes care to seal fully; a half-closed lip is not waterproof
- Film wrinkles and clouds with use
- Hard to clean after holding food
- A minority of buyers report stiffness or a plastic smell, especially on larger sizes

## Who Should Buy This

This is for hikers who want a tougher alternative to disposable zip bags for the small items that a wet pack can ruin: medications, cards, memory storage, ID, matches, a pinch of repair supplies. It also suits anyone who needs to keep food scents contained in a small space, and paddlers and packrafters who want a compact second layer around valuables. Hikers who want to waterproof a phone, a power bank or a stack of clothing should look at a larger size or a conventional roll-top dry bag, since this format will not hold them.

## Verdict

The Noaks Bag does what it claims at a weight that rounds to nothing, and its record with owners is strong. It is a small tool for small jobs, best bought in multiples, and the closure rewards a careful hand. Recommended for UL kits where a few grams of real waterproofing beat a flimsy sandwich bag.
