## Overview

The Luna is Big Agnes's women's hooded down jacket, a Steamboat Springs, Colorado staple that doubles as a camp puffy and a winter coat for running errands. It is built around a lot of down and a relaxed cut, which makes it one of the warmer packable jackets in its price range. The trade-off is that it is not a gram-counter's piece: it carries more weight and bulk than the featherweight hoodies that dominate thru-hiker kits.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Warmth is the Luna's calling card. In Outdoor Life's testing of the previous 700-fill generation, the jacket held about 6 oz of down and roughly 65 liters of volume. It was rated excellent for warmth in a cold-room test, and the tester ranked it among the two warmest jackets in a field of packable down pieces. The current version trades a notch of fill power for the new diamond quilting. Big Agnes says the pattern lets the down loft more fully with fewer stitch lines, which cuts cold spots at the seams. The same tester described it as a puffer for alpine camp when temperatures drop, not a summer midlayer for hiking at elevation.

**Fit and comfort.** The regular cut is roomy without being shapeless. REI reviewers describe it as cozy and flattering, with enough space for a fleece or sweater underneath. Taller owners single out sleeves that reach gloves or mittens, and the thumbholes close the gap at the wrist. A few buyers of the longer Luna parka noted the cuffs run snug over a watch, so the puffy sleeve volume is real. The hood is fully insulated and carries a built-in visor, and owners rank it among the jacket's best features. Drawcords at the hood and hem seal in heat.

**Weather resistance.** This is the weak spot. The DownTek treatment helps the fill shrug off damp, and one owner who was caught in the rain several times reported no change in loft. Even so, Outdoor Life's testers marked the Luna down for water resistance, calling it the jacket's only real ding in two separate roundups. On a drizzly shoulder-season trail, expect to wear a shell over it, and the roomy cut leaves space for one.

**Durability.** Testers rate the Luna as a strong performer here. One outdoor reviewer wore the same jacket for more than a year before it earned its Best for Women pick. Owners who bushwhack report it handles the occasional brush with a boulder or tree. Not every unit fares as well: a handful of REI reviewers report torn interior linings, pocket failures and seam problems within a season. Those reports are the minority in a 4.7-star average across a dozen reviews, but they point to the lining, not the shell, as the part to baby.

**Weight and packability.** The earlier 700-fill Luna was listed at 15 oz (425 g) in a medium. It stuffs into its own chest pocket, though the volume is more grapefruit-plus than apple. Outdoor Life flagged it as heavier than other jackets in its roundup. For a weekend in the Sierra shoulder season, that weight is a fair price for the warmth. On a multi-week thru-hike where every ounce is tallied, it is harder to justify.

**Care.** Owners say it is easy to live with. A reviewer new to down found it forgiving, and Big Agnes recommends a down-specific wash, a low-heat tumble dry and clean tennis balls to restore loft.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very warm for the price, with a generous down load
- Roomy regular fit that layers over a fleece
- Insulated, adjustable hood with a visor
- Thumbholes, hem and hood drawcords, and plenty of pockets
- Recycled shell fabrics and water-repellent down, with PFAS-free treatments
- Casual styling that works off trail

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than ultralight down hoodies
- Poor showing in water-resistance testing
- Some owners report interior lining and pocket failures
- 650-fill down is a step below the 700- and 850-fill versions Big Agnes sold earlier
- $299.95 MSRP, though it is often discounted

## Who Should Buy This

The Luna suits cold-weather campers, ski tourers and anyone who wants one down jacket that works at a winter trailhead and at the coffee shop afterward. It is a good match for cold sleepers who prefer a layer with real insulation over a minimalist one. Thru-hikers building a sub-10-pound base weight, or hikers who spend weeks in persistent rain, should look at lighter hoodies or synthetic insulation.

## Verdict

The Luna is a dependable, warm and comfortable winter puffy that earns its reputation in cold camps and on city sidewalks. It is a poor choice for weight-obsessed long-distance hiking, and it needs a shell when the weather turns wet. Buy it for cold-weather comfort and everyday versatility, ideally on sale.
