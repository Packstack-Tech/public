## Overview

The Cerium Hoody (formerly the Cerium LT) is Arc'teryx's answer to a question every alpine and backpacking puffy has to face: what happens when the down gets damp? The jacket uses 850-fill goose down through the body and swaps in Coreloft synthetic at the cuffs, underarms and collar, where sweat and condensation collect. It suits hikers, climbers and ski tourers who want a trim, packable layer and will pay for it. The trade-off is a thin shell and a slim cut that favor weight savings over durability and roominess.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Testers consistently describe the Cerium as warmer than its thickness suggests. Outdoor Gear Lab's reviewers credit the high-loft European white goose down for a mid-weight jacket that out-heats most others of similar profile, and Switchback Travel's tester found it held off a chill as snow began falling in the mountains. The insulated hood and a tall collar do real work here. Hood and hem drawcords plus elastic cuffs let the wearer seal out wind. Better Trail rated warmth at 3.5 out of 5. That fits a jacket built as an alpine midlayer or camp layer, not a deep-winter parka.

**Moisture management.** The hybrid construction is the main reason to choose the Cerium over a pure-down competitor. Better Trail lists 100 g and 80 g Coreloft in the cuffs, underarms and collar. GearJunkie's tester found the mix kept those zones resistant to wetting and said the jacket suited high-output days. The FC0 DWR is a different matter. A FeedTheHabit reviewer notes it sheds light moisture but won't hold up in sustained wet weather. On a rainy trail, the Cerium belongs under a shell.

**Weight and packability.** At 338 g, the Cerium isn't the lightest puffy on the market. Better Trail points to Mountain Hardwear's Ghost Whisperer, at 8.5 oz in a women's small, as lighter but with thinner fabric and less insulation. Better Trail calls the Cerium the better balance of weight, warmth and features. It stuffs to just over the size of a 40-ounce water bottle, according to GearJunkie, and the stuff sack stows in a breast pocket. CleverHiker says the jacket is light enough for all but the most committed ultralighters.

**Fit and mobility.** Reviewers like the cut. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers praised sleeves that stay put over the wrists with arms raised, and the hem sits low enough to cover the seat of the pants. The three zippered pockets sit above a pack's hip belt, a detail that matters on the trail. The trade-off is that the slim silhouette fits snugly over a base layer and won't accommodate bulky midlayers underneath. An Outside Bozeman reviewer found the sizing true to fit and suggested going up a size for more layering room. GearJunkie noted that earlier Ceriums drew complaints about unpredictable sizing, and that this generation fixed it.

**Durability.** The move to 15D Arato from the previous 10D shell is a real improvement. GearJunkie's tester brushed through desert scrub without snagging, though cacti are another story. Better Trail still flags the thin shell as something that needs care, and calls the center front zipper thin and fragile. An owner of both jackets on a Slickdeals thread said the Patagonia Down Sweater's heavier shell is less prone to leaking down, and that the Patagonia is boxier and more relaxed in fit. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers also complained about the hem drawcords on an earlier version of the jacket. With no cord lock, they were hard to loosen one-handed or with gloves on.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Strong warmth-to-weight ratio from 850-fill down
- Coreloft synthetic in cuffs, underarms and collar guards against sweat and damp
- Warm, adjustable, insulated hood
- Trim cut that moves well and fits under a shell
- Packs to about the size of a water bottle, with a stuff sack included
- PFAS-free DWR

**Cons**

- Expensive, with a list price around $400
- Thin shell fabric needs careful handling
- Slim fit limits layering underneath
- Center zipper described as thin and fragile
- DWR won't handle sustained rain
- Hem drawcords are awkward to loosen with gloves

## Who Should Buy This

The Cerium Hoody is made for ski tourers, alpine climbers and three-season backpackers who want one dependable insulating layer. It works as a midlayer under a shell on cold days and as a standalone puffy at camp or on a summit. It also looks clean enough for town use. Hikers who shed a lot of heat on the climb will appreciate the synthetic panels.

Buyers on a budget should skip it. So should anyone who drags a jacket through brush or wears heavy layers beneath it, and so should hikers who want the lightest possible down jacket. A sturdier, roomier puffy, or a shell-first insulation system, is the better choice for those users.

## Verdict

The Cerium Hoody is one of the best-balanced technical down jackets available, with the warmth, packability and hybrid construction that mountain users value. The price and the delicate shell are the main reasons to hesitate. For users who fit the profile above, it's an easy recommendation.
