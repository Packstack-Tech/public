## Overview

The Katadyn BeFree Gravity pairs the company's 0.1-micron EZ-Clean hollow-fiber membrane with a 3-liter HydraPak soft reservoir. Fill it at the source, hang it from a branch, and clean water runs through a hose while camp chores get done. It's built for solo hikers who want a camp water station and for couples or small groups. The trade-off: it's fast and effortless, but it clogs sooner than a Sawyer and offers no virus protection.

## Performance

**Flow.** Katadyn rates the filter at up to 2 liters per minute, and independent timing lands close to that. One tester drained a full 3-liter reservoir in just under two minutes. Another clocked roughly 1.5 minutes per liter in pure gravity mode. Either way, it runs at more than double the pace of a Sawyer Squeeze setup, which is why the BeFree Gravity is a realistic option for two or three people sharing one filter. The reservoir can also be squeezed to push water through faster, and standing the bottle upright stops the flow. Solo hikers in a hurry can use it that way.

**Filling and camp use.** The 43 mm opening is the system's quiet advantage. It takes water from a stream pool, a seep or a faucet more easily than a Sawyer's narrow pouch mouth does. An integrated bail handle and carry loop make it easy to hang, and the reservoir doubles as the carrying vessel back to camp. A quick-release hose fills a hydration bladder or bottle. One reviewer found the 3-liter size right for an evening's cooking and drinking with enough left over to fill a bladder in the morning. Hanging requires a branch, a pole or a bit of cord, and the system is awkward if the only collection vessel has a narrow mouth.

**Cleaning and clogging.** Katadyn's selling point is that the membrane cleans by shaking or swishing, with no syringe or backflushing. That's a real convenience compared with Sawyer, which has a reputation for slowing to a trickle in silty water. The BeFree's record is mixed. Backpacking Light forum posters describe the BeFree as the fastest option for a while, but say it clogs sooner than a Sawyer. Some report cycling through several filters. Others, including a long-term Section Hiker reviewer, say they've never seen flow drop and rarely bother to swish the filter. The pattern in owner reports is consistent: clear alpine water goes fine, and murky or silty sources are where flow degrades and doesn't fully recover. A bandana or coffee filter as a pre-filter helps. Katadyn's manual also advises choosing the best available fresh-water source and avoiding stagnant water or areas with heavy human or animal use.

**Filter life.** The 1,000-liter rating is well short of the Sawyer's much longer figure. In practice, one Trailspace reviewer accepted the trade because the BeFree requires no backflushing and fills faster. For a few weekends a year, 1,000 liters is plenty. For a thru-hiker filtering 4–5 liters a day, it's a few months of use at best, and fewer if the water is silty.

**Durability and cold.** The TPU reservoir stands up to normal handling, but multiple owners report pinhole leaks, and the soft bag does best when treated with care. Freezing is a hard limit. Katadyn's manual warns that a frozen membrane can let microorganisms bypass the filter, so on cold nights it belongs in a sleeping bag. If the filter dries out between trips, soaking and flushing may be needed to restore flow.

**What it doesn't do.** The 0.1-micron membrane handles protozoa and bacteria. It doesn't remove viruses, which matters for international travel or heavily used water sources.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Flow of about 2 L/min in gravity mode, fast enough for groups
- 192 g and collapsible, light for a 3-liter system
- Shake-to-clean membrane; no syringe or backflushing needed
- 43 mm mouth makes filling from shallow sources easy
- Doubles as a squeeze filter
- Hose for filling bladders and bottles

**Cons**
- Clogs in silty or cloudy water, and flow may not fully recover
- 1,000 L filter life is modest
- Doesn't remove viruses
- Soft reservoir is prone to pinhole leaks
- Can't be exposed to freezing temperatures
- Needs a hang point

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right filter for couples, small groups and solo hikers who like to do their water chores at camp rather than at the source. It suits three-season trips in the Rockies, Sierra and Cascades, where sources run cold and clear. Thru-hikers on silty desert or lowland routes, winter campers and travelers headed anywhere that calls for virus protection should look elsewhere. A Sawyer-based setup with a pre-filter, or a purifier with a virus rating, would suit them better.

## Verdict

The BeFree Gravity delivers fast, hands-free filtering at 192 g, and its shake-to-clean design is easier than backflushing when conditions cooperate. It's a smart pick for clear-water camps and group trips, but a careful one: owners need to guard against cloudy sources, freezing and punctures. Buy it for speed and convenience, not for longevity.
