## Overview

The Cascade Wild Ultralight Folding Table is a one-piece corrugated polypropylene platform that folds flat for packing and snaps open into a low camp table in seconds. At 62g (about 2.2 oz), it targets backpackers who want a clean, stable surface for meal prep without meaningfully denting their base weight. Sadly, the original is now discontinued — <cite index="4-4,4-5">in early 2024, founder Jim Hamel made the decision to retire, and once the last production run was completed, the remaining tables sold out quickly.</cite> If you find a new-old-stock unit or a used one, it's worth understanding exactly what you're getting.

## Performance

The table's concept is disarmingly simple. <cite index="16-9">The corrugated plastic construction uses air-filled flutes similar to cardboard, giving it a very high strength-to-weight ratio.</cite> <cite index="16-11">Folding grooves are set in with a jig, and the package is held together with hook-and-loop circles and #20 plastic snaps.</cite> Setup is genuinely fast — snap open, done. No poles, no stuff sack, no assembly ritual.

**Surface and height.** The 12 × 8-inch top is enough room for a canister stove, a mug, and a few odds and ends at once. That said, <cite index="18-7">it only elevates the work surface 3.25 inches off the ground, so you'll likely have to kneel to do any work on it.</cite> That's a legitimate ergonomic trade-off you should think about before buying — this is a low platform, not a proper table. <cite index="19-7">An ergonomic workaround: the design allows the table to straddle a log either lengthwise or crosswise to create a flat surface at a more convenient height.</cite>

**Heat resistance.** The polypropylene has a melting point of 324°F / 162°C, which is comfortably above boiling water (212°F). <cite index="1-30,1-31">That heat tolerance makes it practical for setting down two coffee mugs as they await hot water.</cite> Users on Backpacking Light also recommend using a heat shield if you do place a canister stove directly on the surface — <cite index="1-22,1-23">some product photos show stoves running atop the table, but the snap-together design doesn't feel entirely stable for that application,</cite> and most experienced users treat it as a gear-resting surface rather than a stove platform.

**Packability.** <cite index="2-2,2-3">Its one-piece design means no loose parts and no stuff sack required, and it's compact enough to fit in a side water bottle pocket.</cite> In practice this is a genuine win — it's one of those items that disappears into your pack so completely that the "weight vs. reward" math starts looking very favorable.

**Cleaning.** <cite index="1-32,1-33">Both the table and its companion cutting board are easy to clean — even with spilled coffee, a damp camp towel handles the job.</cite> The corrugated polypropylene is waterproof and stain-resistant, which matters when you're wiping down messy meal prep in the field.

**Multi-table scaling.** <cite index="2-8">Two or more tables snap together for a larger surface,</cite> which is a clever detail — a pair of solo hikers traveling together could each carry one and combine them at camp.

**Durability.** The BPL community's consensus is frank: <cite index="5-2,5-3">the tables are good but not especially durable, with the most practical use case being food prep while cooking on the trail.</cite> Corrugated plastic holds up fine for its intended job, but don't expect it to survive being sat on or overloaded.

**Origins and legacy.** <cite index="4-2">The business took off when the table was featured in Backpacker Magazine's 2018 Annual Gear Guide,</cite> and <cite index="12-10">by the end of the run, Cascade Wild had shipped 54,639 tables to customers in more than 40 countries.</cite> <cite index="4-12">From 2017 onward, all tables were assembled by hand at the vocational rehabilitation division of Oregon State Hospital.</cite> For what amounts to a stamped piece of corrugated plastic, that's a surprisingly meaningful story. <cite index="4-14,4-15">The brand's South Korean partner, Moonrocket, has worked to produce a replica Cascade Wild table with the original founder's blessing, and plans to continue the tradition of employing people with disabilities in production.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely ultralight at 62g — barely registers in the hand or on a scale
- One-piece design: no loose parts, no stuff sack, packs flat in a water bottle pocket
- Fast setup — snap open and you're done
- Heat-resistant to 324°F, safe for boiling water spills
- Multiple tables snap together to scale up surface area
- Reflective tape on ends helps locate the table in the dark
- Doubles as a light-duty cutting board in a pinch
- Wipes clean in seconds

**Cons**

- Only 3.2 inches tall — you're kneeling, not sitting, to use it comfortably
- Snap assembly can feel loose; not ideal as a primary stove platform
- 10 lb weight limit is adequate but not generous
- Discontinued — you'll need to hunt for NOS stock or look at the Moonrocket replica
- Straddling a log for extra height is a workaround, not a solution

## Who Should Buy This

This table earns its 62g most reliably for hikers who cook real food — think cheese and crackers, tortilla wraps, or anything that benefits from a clean, flat surface — and for anyone who camps on sloped or muddy terrain where a clean ground surface is hard to find. <cite index="1-15,1-16">Beyond meal prep, users have found it useful for setting down a cup of coffee, playing cards, or even propping up a phone for in-tent entertainment.</cite> <cite index="5-10,5-11">Hardcore gram-counters who can reliably find a flat rock or clean ground spot may find it less compelling,</cite> but for those who actually want a dedicated prep surface, the weight penalty is negligible. Hammock campers on uneven terrain get particular mileage out of it.

## Verdict

The Cascade Wild Ultralight Folding Table is a well-considered piece of gear that solves a real (if modest) camp kitchen problem with minimal weight cost. The 3.2-inch height keeps it firmly in "platform" territory rather than "table" territory, and the snap assembly isn't stiff enough to trust under a live stove — but for meal prep, gear organization, and coffee-cup staging, it quietly earns its spot in the pack. I'd rate it **7.5/10**: a smart, niche-appropriate product that just barely misses a higher mark on account of its low height and the fact that you'll now need to track down used stock or the Moonrocket successor to get one.
