## Overview

The NEMO Disco 15 is a men's regular-length, 650-fill down bag cut in NEMO's Classic Spoon shape: wider at the elbows and knees than a mummy, so sleepers can curl up and roll over without wrestling the fabric. It is aimed at side sleepers and restless sleepers who find mummy bags claustrophobic. The trade-off is plain. The roomy cut and mid-grade down make it heavier and bulkier than the bags ultralight backpackers usually carry.

## Performance

**Comfort.** This is the Disco's reason for existing, and reviewers are nearly unanimous about it. CleverHiker's testers put it at the top of their backpacking-bag list for comfort, calling it a dream for side sleepers and tossers and turners. Better Trail described it as one of the most luxe-feeling backpacking bags it has used. The baffles run vertically over the upper body and horizontally below, which that review credits with letting the bag drape naturally as the sleeper shifts. The shell fabric isn't especially soft, and Better Trail suggests a liner for texture-sensitive sleepers.

**Warmth and draft control.** The bag has a full-length draft tube along the zipper. A "Blanket Fold" replaces the usual mummy-bag collar: a flap of down that can be pulled over the shoulders and neck. Testers found both effective at blocking chills. The catch is the spoon cut itself. Section Hiker notes that the larger interior volume takes longer to warm up. Outdoor Gear Lab, testing the women's version, advised against sleeping in it near its comfort rating because the spacious interior increases drafts. Owners on Reddit echo the point and describe the 15°F rating as optimistic. The ISO comfort figure of 25°F is the more honest number. Several owners call it a shoulder-season bag, ideal on cool nights even when it isn't freezing, and too warm for summer.

**Ventilation.** The Thermo Gills are the standout feature. These are two zippered vents near the torso, with both internal and external zippers. Opened, they expose jersey mesh that dumps heat without letting cold air rush in. Better Trail, which scored the bag 5/5 for features, hadn't used another bag with this kind of venting. A Treeline Review tester who found the bag warm on a late-summer night vented it and slept with a leg out, without waking sweaty or clammy. That flexibility stretches the bag's usable range on trips that mix warm and cold nights.

**Moisture.** The shell has a DWR treatment, and the hood and footbox use a PFAS-free waterproof material. Several testers singled out the waterproof footbox for handling tent-wall condensation. The down is hydrophobic, and the bag is RDS-certified and fully recyclable through NEMO's Endless Promise program. Down never becomes waterproof, but in damp shelters the bag holds up better than most.

**Weight and packability.** The listed 1,220 g is heavy for a 15°F down bag. In practice, one REI owner weighed theirs at 1,290 g with the stuff sack, and Better Trail's testers put it at almost 3 pounds. At 7.2 liters compressed, it takes up serious pack volume. 650-fill down is cheaper than the 800-plus fill used in premium bags, but less lofty and less compressible. CleverHiker, Outdoor Gear Lab and Live for the Outdoors all flag the weight and bulk. CleverHiker specifically doesn't recommend the bag for long trips or thru-hikes. Outdoor Gear Lab judged the women's version too heavy for backpacking altogether, though it rated the men's design's comfort highly.

**Fit and durability.** The regular size is listed for sleepers up to 6 feet. One owner who is exactly 6 feet found it a bit tight. Reddit owners also mention feather leakage and occasional zipper snags. Other users praise the zipper's durability against trail grit, and NEMO backs the bag with a lifetime warranty.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Spoon shape gives real room at the elbows and knees for side sleepers
- Thermo Gills vent heat without admitting drafts
- Blanket Fold and full-length draft tube block drafts well
- Waterproof footbox and hood, plus DWR shell, handle condensation
- Hydrophobic, RDS-certified down; fully recyclable under Endless Promise
- Lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- 1,220 g and 7.2 L compressed are heavy and bulky for a 15°F down bag
- The roomy cut is less thermally efficient and slow to warm
- The 15°F rating runs optimistic; the 25°F comfort rating is the one to plan around
- 650-fill down is less lofty and compressible than premium alternatives
- Reports of feather leakage and occasional zipper snags
- Regular length is snug at 6 feet

## Who Should Buy This

The Disco 15 suits side sleepers and restless sleepers who sleep poorly in a mummy and are willing to pay a weight penalty for a night's rest. It works for weekend trips, shoulder-season camping and car camping, and its vents suit anyone who runs warm. It is a poor fit for thru-hikers and gram-counters, who can find lighter, more compressible 15°F bags for similar money. Cold sleepers expecting a 15°F rating to mean comfort at 15°F should also look at a warmer bag.

## Verdict

The Disco 15 is among the most comfortable backpacking bags available, and its ventilation and draft management are well thought out. That comfort comes with a weight and pack-volume cost that rules it out for ultralight and long-distance use. Buy it for short trips and shoulder-season camping if you sleep on your side. Skip it if base weight drives your gear choices.
