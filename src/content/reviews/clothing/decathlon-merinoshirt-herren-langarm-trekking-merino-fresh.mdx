## Overview

The Merino Fresh is Decathlon's lightest men's merino long-sleeve for trekking: a 155 g/m² jersey that weighs 184 g in size L. It is a 70/30 merino-acrylic blend, so it gives up some of the pure-wool feel in exchange for faster drying and a tougher fabric. For three-season hikers who want a wool shirt without paying Icebreaker or Smartwool money, it's one of the better-value entries in the category.

## Performance

**Weight and warmth.** At 155 g/m², the Merino Fresh sits in the lightweight tier of merino jerseys. It is a clear step below the 190 g/m² Forclaz MT500, which weighs 222 g in size L, so this shirt saves roughly 38 g and covers a wider temperature range. The MT500 is a spring-and-autumn shirt that runs too warm for July. The Fresh's thinner knit is better suited to summer alpine days, shoulder-season hiking and layering under a wind shell. It is not a winter base layer, and anyone expecting real warmth below about 5°C will need to pair it with a midlayer.

**Odor resistance.** This is the reason to buy any merino shirt, and Decathlon backs the claim with ISO 17299-3 lab testing. That standard measures how well a fabric limits the formation of sweat odor. The company says the shirt was also put through a sensory test for softness by ten trained testers. Its internal field testing involved 20 testers on two trekking trips. Decathlon's own pitch is that the shirt can be worn several days in a row, which means fewer shirts in the pack. Owners of Forclaz's merino line commonly report multi-day wear with no smell, and the Fresh's 30% acrylic content is the one variable to watch: it is a synthetic fiber, and a blend never matches pure wool for odor resistance.

**Moisture and drying.** Merino absorbs moisture and keeps the skin feeling drier than a damp synthetic does, and the acrylic fraction is there specifically to speed drying. The practical result on trail is a shirt that can be rinsed at camp in the evening and worn again the next morning. That is a real advantage over 100% merino jerseys, which stay damp noticeably longer.

**Comfort and durability.** The wool is 19.5 microns, a fine grade, and Decathlon describes the hand as supple. That is slightly coarser than the 18.5-micron wool in the MT500, so people with sensitive skin should try it on before a long trip. The wool-synthetic blend is marketed as very robust, a sensible claim given that acrylic adds abrasion resistance at pack-strap and hip-belt contact points. Decathlon lists the shirt at 4.7 out of 5 across roughly 1,400 reviews on its Austrian site, a high score for a product with that much volume behind it.

**Care.** Machine wash at 30°C on a normal cycle, ideally with a wool-specific detergent. The shirt should not be tumble dried, ironed or dry cleaned. Lay-flat or line drying is the only option, which suits trail life anyway.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 184 g in size L, about 38 g lighter than the 190 g/m² MT500
- Lab-tested odor control (ISO 17299-3)
- Acrylic content speeds drying compared with pure merino
- Fine 19.5-micron wool
- Very high customer satisfaction on Decathlon's site (4.7/5 from about 1,400 reviews)
- Low price relative to name-brand merino

**Cons**

- 30% acrylic means it is not pure wool, and it will not match 100% merino for odor control or natural-fiber feel
- Too thin for cold-weather use on its own
- No hand-warmer thumb loops or other cold-weather details
- Wool-care limits: no tumble drying, 30°C maximum wash

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers building a three-season layering system on a budget. Section hikers and thru-hikers who want a wool shirt that can be washed in a creek and dry by evening will get the most out of it. Weekend trekkers who want a single shirt for travel and trail will also find the odor control useful. People who dislike synthetic content in their clothing, or who want a wool shirt for cold-weather sleeping and winter use, should look at Forclaz's heavier MT500 or MT900 shirts.

## Verdict

The Merino Fresh is a light, fast-drying and inexpensive wool-blend long-sleeve that does the main job of a trekking base layer well. The acrylic share puts it a notch below pure merino, but it also makes the shirt lighter, tougher and quicker to dry. A solid buy for warm-to-cool conditions.
