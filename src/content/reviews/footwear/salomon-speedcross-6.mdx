## Overview

The Speedcross has been Salomon's mud-specialist trail shoe since 2006, and the sixth generation keeps the formula: deep, widely spaced lugs, a closed and protective upper, and a one-pull Quicklace closure. It suits hikers and runners who spend their miles on soft, wet, loose ground. The trade-off is a firm, 10 mm-drop platform with limited breathability and a weight that sits above most ultralight trail runners.

## Performance

**Traction.** This is the reason the shoe exists, and testers are nearly unanimous. Outdoor Gear Lab scored the Speedcross 6 a perfect 10 for traction and called it the traction specialist of its trail-runner lineup, with nothing else close on soft dirt, mud, and steep grass. Runningshoesguru's tester, working through early-season Teton mud, described a shoe that fully kept mud and debris out of the upper even as the lugs collected a heavy layer of muck. CleverHiker's testers noted that the widely spaced lugs bite into everything from sandy washes to sloppy mountain trail and shed debris easily. The 5 mm lugs are less happy on hard-packed trail and pavement, where owners describe the ride as harsh.

**Fit and comfort.** Outdoor Gear Lab gave the shoe a 9.0 for comfort and fit. Its testers credit the padded interior, the Ortholite insole, and an anti-debris mesh tongue, and say the upper holds the foot securely even when laced loosely. CleverHiker described a glove-like, true-to-size fit with good arch support and a cushy ankle collar. Reddit owners and retail reviewers repeatedly flag the toe box as narrow, even in the regular width, and a Wide option exists for those who need it. A few women's-version owners say the latest generation fits wider than earlier ones.

**Ride and stability.** Cushioning is firm. RunRepeat's lab testers called the midsole stiff and low on energy return, and an owner with 225 miles on a pair described the foam as stiff enough to make the shoe less nimble on roots and rocks. Outdoor Gear Lab rated stability only 5.8, and CleverHiker noted that the 10 mm drop can feel unbalanced on steep descents. On the plus side, the rigid outsole gives rock protection without a plate, though one owner said rocks are still felt underfoot.

**Quicklace.** A single pull snugs the shoe, and the excess cord tucks into a pocket on the tongue. Owners like the speed on and off and say it doesn't loosen mid-run. It also has a learning curve, and anyone who wants to adjust tension zone by zone for swollen feet at mile 20 will find it blunt. A snapped cord is a field repair that standard laces don't require.

**Breathability and drying.** The closed upper that keeps grit out also keeps heat in. RunRepeat's testers rated breathability extremely poor, and CleverHiker's testers found the shoe stuffy above 90°F on long climbs. Owners in wet climates report it dries reasonably fast, and the shoe is not waterproof.

**Durability.** Reports conflict, and the pattern is worth noting. Runningshoesguru's tester saw no visible lug wear after more than 100 miles, and one owner reported little wear at 225 miles. CleverHiker's testers, though, found the soft Contagrip rubber gives great grip for roughly the first 200 miles before the lugs wear faster than they'd like, and Reddit owners list quick sole wear among the top complaints. Hikers on abrasive rock should expect a shorter life than the upper suggests. RunRepeat measured the outsole base at a thin 1.9 mm, which is by design given the lug height.

**Weight.** The men's version at 296 g is lighter than its bulk suggests, and RunRepeat called it surprisingly manageable. Next to ultralight trail runners, though, it is heavy. CleverHiker's testers noticed the extra mass on days over 10 miles with a lot of climbing, and Outdoor Gear Lab gave weight just 4.1.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions are cut on different lasts rather than being a simple size split. The men's weighs 296 g and the women's 261 g, a 35 g gap that mostly reflects the smaller sizing range. Both share the same 10 mm drop, 5 mm lugs, EnergyCell+ midsole, and Quicklace closure. The practical choice is by foot shape. Women with narrow feet and men with high-volume feet should consider the width options, since the regular fit runs snug in the toe box. Anyone with wide feet should go straight to the Wide.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Class-leading grip on mud, wet grass, loose dirt, and snow
- Snug, secure fit that owners find comfortable out of the box
- Upper keeps debris and mud out
- Quicklace is fast and the lace tucks away
- Doubles as a rugged, protective everyday hiking shoe on soft terrain

**Cons**

- Poor breathability in warm weather
- Firm, low-rebound midsole and a high 10 mm drop
- Soft outsole rubber wears quickly on abrasive trail for some owners
- Narrow toe box in regular width
- Heavier than most ultralight trail runners

## Who Should Buy This

The Speedcross 6 fits hikers and runners in wet, muddy, or snowy terrain: Pacific Northwest, British hills, early-season alpine, and shoulder-season trail with slop that would send smoother lugs skating. It also suits anyone who wants a locked-in fit and doesn't mind the Quicklace system. Desert and dry-trail thru-hikers, high-mileage walkers who prize a plush ride, wide-footed hikers who dislike a snug toe box, and gram counters should look at lighter, more breathable trail runners with a lower drop and more forgiving foam.

## Verdict

The Speedcross 6 is a specialist that does its job better than almost anything else: it grips soft ground with confidence and fits like a glove. It's a poor all-conditions thru-hiking shoe, with its heat retention, firm ride, and uncertain lug life, but an excellent pick when the forecast is wet and the trail is soft.
