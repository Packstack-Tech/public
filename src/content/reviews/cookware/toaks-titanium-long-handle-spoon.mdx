## Overview

The TOAKS Titanium Long Handle Spoon is a one-piece, uncoated titanium spoon built for a single job: getting to the bottom of a freeze-dried pouch without putting a knuckle in dinner. It comes in two versions at the same 19 g, a Standard with a fully matte finish (SLV-03) and a Polished Bowl with a matte handle (SLV-11). The trade-off is small but real. The Standard's brushed bowl is slightly less pleasant to eat from and harder to clean than the polished one.

## Performance

**Reach and scraping.** The long handle keeps hands out of hot water and out of the sauce at the bottom of a meal bag. The flat, squared head matters as much as the length. It sits flush against a pot wall or the corner of a pouch and scrapes more surface per pass than a rounded bowl does. CleverHiker's testers also point out that a spoon has no spork tines for food to slip through. They measured the bowl at roughly 6 mL, about average for a backpacking spoon. It looks shallow, but they say it holds more than it appears to.

**Grip.** TOAKS' brushed titanium handle is grippy even when wet or greasy. That texture is the reason the Polished Bowl version keeps the matte handle and polishes only the bowl.

**Durability.** There is no coating, so nothing can chip into a meal, and titanium adds no metallic taste. Owners describe cramming the spoon into packs for thousands of trail meals without it bending. One Trailspace reviewer logged more than 6,000 thru-hiking miles with theirs across three long-distance routes. The handle has a stiffening ridge. The metal will bend before it snaps. Uncoated titanium can pick up scratches, but those never turn into flaking, which is the failure that sends anodized aluminum spoons to the trash.

**Edge finish.** The spoon is stamped, and some owners report sharp spots along the handle edges. Not every unit has them. A quick pass with a thumb and a nail file when it arrives takes care of the problem. It shouldn't be necessary on a utensil meant to last, but it takes about two minutes.

**Weight in context.** At 19 g, the spoon isn't TOAKS' lightest. The short SLV-05 spoon weighs 12.5 g. That saves 6.5 g and gives up the reach entirely, which defeats the purpose for anyone who eats from the bag. CleverHiker's scale put the polished version at 20 g, which is within rounding of the claimed weight.

**Odd jobs.** The spoon stirs coffee and cold-soak jars. One user reported that the flat tail end can work the latches on a bear canister. That isn't a design feature, but it's a useful extra from a rigid piece of metal.

## Choosing a Version

Both versions weigh the same, use the same pure titanium, and have the same flat head and matte handle. Only the bowl finish differs. The Polished Bowl listing also gives a longer overall length, 8.6 in against 7.5 in for the Standard. The extra inch is welcome with tall, narrow meal pouches.

**Polished Bowl (SLV-11).** This is the version most reviewers recommend. CleverHiker rated it 4.8 out of 5 and prefers it to the matte-bowl model. The polished bowl rinses cleaner, feels closer to household silverware in the mouth, and is less prone to streaks and leftover residue. It is the better buy for nearly everyone who plans to eat from it daily.

**Standard (SLV-03).** The fully matte spoon works fine, and plenty of hikers have run it for thousands of miles without complaint. It suits people who want the same texture from handle to bowl. It also suits anyone who finds a polished bowl slippery or dislikes the shine. Anyone else is giving up a little comfort for no gain in weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Pure titanium with no coating, so nothing chips, flakes, or tastes of metal
- Long handle keeps hands clean in deep pouches and tall pots
- Flat, squared head scrapes pot walls and pouch corners well
- Matte handle holds securely when wet or greasy
- Multi-year durability, with owners reporting thousands of miles of use
- Both versions weigh 19 g, and the Polished Bowl adds length at no weight cost
- Priced at around $11

**Cons**

- The Standard's matte bowl is harder to clean and has a rougher mouthfeel than the polished bowl
- Some units arrive with sharp stamping burrs on the handle edges
- Drab gray makes it easy to leave on a log at a rest stop
- No attachment point or carabiner hole
- Not a spork, so there is nothing to spear food with

## Who Should Buy This

This spoon is for backpackers who eat mostly from freeze-dried pouches or deep pots and want one utensil they won't have to replace. It is a clear improvement on a spork for soft or wet food. Gram counters who eat from a mug or a wide bowl can save 6.5 g with the short SLV-05. Anyone who needs to spear food should carry a fork or a spork instead.

## Verdict

The TOAKS Titanium Long Handle Spoon is a cheap, one-piece, buy-once utensil that does its job without fuss. Buy the Polished Bowl version, which costs the same, weighs the same, and eats and cleans better. Choose the Standard only if you want a uniform matte finish. Check the handle edges for burrs when it arrives.
