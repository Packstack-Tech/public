## Overview

Gilde Turmat is a line of freeze-dried dinners from Nortura's Gilde meat brand, vacuum-sealed in pouches that double as the bowl. It is aimed at Norwegian weekend hikers, hunters and hut-to-hut walkers who would rather pick up dinner at the Coop than order it online, not at gram-counting thru-hikers. The trade-off is plain: cheap, mild, widely stocked meals in exchange for flavor that plays it safe.

## Performance

**Preparation.** The routine is the standard one for this kind of meal. Boiling water goes into the pouch up to the fill line, then a stir, a sealed bag and a wait of roughly ten minutes. No pot is needed, so cleanup amounts to folding up an empty pouch. Norwegian hikers who tried the first Gilde meals warn that the fill line is a small marking on the back panel, not the white band wrapping the pouch. It is easy to miss with a hungry group around the stove.

**Flavor.** Gilde developed the range with the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT) and pitched the flavors as mild and suited to all ages. The Biffgryte follows that brief. Potato leads the ingredient list, followed by beef at 17%, mushrooms, carrot, onion, lentils, celery root and parsley root, with garlic, leek, beef extract and red wine extract in support. There is no heat and no spice-rack ambition. A Norwegian tester who tried the launch lineup gave the beef stew a 4 on the Scandinavian six-point die scale. He found the meat and potato pieces tiny, as freeze-dried pieces tend to be, and needed a pinch of salt to bring out a proper beef-stew flavor. His verdict was that all three launch meals were fine to carry. The Tacogryte is built on beans, tomato, onion, pepper and corn, with a cheese aroma and beef extract. Its description promises a mild taco flavor, which suits cold evenings and mixed groups and will bore anyone who wants the punch of a proper curry.

**Origin and availability.** The beef comes from Nortura's Norwegian farmer-owners. The pouches are freeze-dried and packed at the Rondane plant in Otta. DNT stocks the meals at some of its self-service cabins. The series launched in 2021 as a domestic rival to Real Turmat, and the pitch was convenience: Gilde's pouches sit on the dry-goods shelf of an ordinary grocery store. The Tacogryte retails for about 69 NOK at Felleskjøpet, and owners at launch noted that Gilde undercut Real Turmat on price.

**Weight and packability.** At 86 g and 100 g dry, both pouches are light and flatten easily in a pack. Real Turmat's comparable beef stew weighs 128 g dry, though the two are not a like-for-like swap in portion size. A 405 kcal Tacogryte is a modest dinner by thru-hiking standards, and big appetites will want a second pouch or a side. The vacuum seal means no repackaging for most trips.

**Dietary profile.** This is where the two meals diverge. The Biffgryte is marketed as free of gluten, soy, egg, lactose and dairy, which makes it one of the few supermarket trail dinners that works for hikers avoiding several of those at once. Celery is the declared allergen. The ingredient list also names a rapeseed, sunflower and soybean oil blend, so anyone with a serious soy allergy should read the pouch itself. The Tacogryte does not share that profile. Its label declares milk protein including lactose, from the cheese aroma, plus celery. Those avoiding dairy should pass on it.

## Choosing a Version

The 100 g Biffgryte is the heavier, more filling pouch, with chunks of beef and potato in a brown, homestyle stew. It is also the only version that fits the lactose-free and dairy-free profile, so hikers with those restrictions should stick with it. The 86 g Tacogryte saves 14 g, runs on beans and vegetables, and has a mild taco seasoning. It suits hikers who want a change from beef stew and have no dairy restrictions. Neither is spicy.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 86–100 g dry weight keeps dinner light and compact
- Mild, familiar recipes that suit children and mixed groups
- Biffgryte is free from gluten, lactose and dairy by the manufacturer's labeling
- Eaten from the pouch, so no pot to clean
- Sold in Norwegian supermarkets and some DNT cabins, generally cheaper than Real Turmat
- Up to 2 years of shelf life

**Cons**

- Mild flavor with little punch, and some salt may be needed
- Small meat and vegetable pieces after rehydration
- Shorter shelf life than Real Turmat's five years
- Hard to find outside Norway
- Tacogryte contains lactose, and the Biffgryte oil blend includes soybean oil
- The fill line is easy to overlook on the pouch

## Who Should Buy This

This suits Norwegian weekend hikers, hunters and families heading to cabins. It also suits anyone who needs a gluten- and dairy-free hot dinner that can be bought on the drive to the trailhead, provided they pick the Biffgryte. Thru-hikers resupplying outside Norway, and anyone chasing maximum flavor, bigger portions or a long shelf life for emergency stockpiles, should look at Real Turmat or one of the stronger-flavored freeze-dried brands.

## Verdict

Gilde Turmat does what a trail dinner needs to do: it is light, simple to make and cheap, and it comes from a Norwegian plant close to the terrain it targets. The Biffgryte is the better pick for its dietary range and heartier build. Both meals are sensible staples for Norwegian trips and unremarkable almost everywhere else.
