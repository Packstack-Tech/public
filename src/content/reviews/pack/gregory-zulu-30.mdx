## Overview

The Gregory Zulu 30 is a 30-liter technical daypack built around a perimeter frame, a tensioned mesh back panel and a structured hipbelt. It is made for hikers who carry a full day's worth of gear, and the extra water, layers and camera kit that come with one. The trade-off is plain: it carries a load better than nearly any pack its size, and it weighs about 1.4 kg to do it.

## Performance

**Comfort and load carrying.** The FreeFloat suspension is why the Zulu exists. CleverHiker's tester, who ranked it against a field of daypacks, called it the strongest heavy hauler in the lineup and said it carries substantial loads more comfortably than any other pack in that guide. Owners tell the same story at lower weights. One Yosemite hiker with 10 to 15 pounds in the pack reported staying comfortable through 8 or 9 hours on the trail, crediting a hipbelt that could be re-tightened on the move to keep weight off the shoulders. A long-distance walker on the Camino forums, carrying 5 to 6.5 kg before food and water, described the fit as a favorite coat and used it on a London-to-Rome walk with no complaints. Trailspace's reviewer found the foam soft but supportive, with a smooth transition from hipbelt to back panel.

**Ventilation.** The tensioned mesh panel holds the pack off the spine and leaves a gap for air. Reviewers in hot climates, including Southern California hikers, say it keeps sweat from pooling. Outdoors Magic found the gap narrow enough that it doesn't pull the load backward. The mesh can feel scratchy against bare skin, so wear a shirt.

**Weight and bulk.** This is where the Zulu loses ultralight sympathy. CleverHiker put it at roughly 3 pounds and called it the heaviest pack in its ranking. The perimeter frame also means the pack barely compresses, so it won't fold or roll down when half empty. On a short, lightly loaded outing, reviewers say the frame and padding are always there. Gear and Home's verdict is that it's overkill for minimalists, and that's accurate.

**Capacity and organization.** Owners describe the interior as roomy for day hiking. One Trailspace reviewer never filled all 30 liters while carrying a puffy, extra clothes, lunch, 3 L of water and emergency gear. Walkers report that trekking poles fit inside the pack for flights. Side bottle pockets can be reached without taking the pack off. Complaints are small: one REI reviewer called the top pocket small and wanted more internal dividers.

**Durability.** Outdoors Magic called the fabrics tough and the seams solid, but suspected the stretch mesh pockets would wear faster than the rest of the pack. Outdoor Gear Lab also noted the construction is more complicated than most daypacks, and that there is a learning curve in using all the features. Trailspace's longer-term reviewer found a few unbound seams, which he considered normal for the category.

**Fixed points.** Trailspace's reviewer flagged the rigidity, a fixed-length hipbelt, and no pad attachment points. The same reviewer said it fits slender men well, and fit is worth checking in person: one retail buyer settled on the Zulu after trying several packs with about 12 pounds in each.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes differ in torso fit, not in features. The S/M weighs 1,378 g and the M/L weighs 1,417 g, a 39 g gap that shouldn't decide anything. Both have an adjustable torso length, so the size choice sets the range you can adjust within. At least one reviewer lists the S/M at about 28 L and the M/L at 30 L.

- **S/M:** Shorter torsos, and hikers who fall between sizes and want the hipbelt to sit properly.
- **M/L:** Longer torsos, and anyone who wants the full 30 liters.

Because the suspension depends on a good hip and torso fit, try both with weight in them if you can.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Carries heavy and bulky loads with real comfort
- Ventilated back panel keeps sweat down in warm weather
- Adjustable torso length, with easy fit adjustments
- Well-reviewed organization, with easy-access bottle pockets
- Solid construction and fabrics from a reputable maker

**Cons**

- 1,378–1,417 g is heavy for a 30-liter pack
- Perimeter frame barely compresses
- Rigid feel and a fixed-length hipbelt
- Stretch mesh pockets may wear faster than the body
- Top pocket and internal dividers are modest

## Who Should Buy This

The Zulu 30 suits day hikers and walkers who regularly carry more than a minimalist kit: cold-weather layers, 2 to 3 liters of water, camera gear, family supplies, or a heavy emergency setup. It also works for multi-day hut or Camino-style walks with a light base weight. Ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers hunting for grams should look elsewhere. Anyone whose daily load is under 8 pounds will carry frame and padding they don't need.

## Verdict

The Zulu 30 is a specialist: a rigid, ventilated, heavy-duty daypack that does its one job better than lighter alternatives. If you carry real weight on day hikes, it's among the most comfortable options at this size. If you're counting ounces, it isn't the pack for you.
