## Overview

The CWM400 is Naturehike's budget down bag for three-season backpacking. It pairs a mummy-style top with a roomier envelope footprint, and it can be zipped to a second bag to make a double. The headline trade-off is price against temperature performance: it costs a fraction of what the established cottage brands charge for down, but its 6 °C comfort rating makes it a summer and shoulder-season bag, not a cold-weather one.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The number to read is the comfort rating, 6 °C (43 °F), not the -10 °C extreme figure. The extreme rating describes survival, not sleep. Owners who have tested the bag outdoors back that up. One reviewer comparing Naturehike's 800-fill bags found that above roughly 5–10 °C it sleeps fine, while below freezing it needed layers underneath and delivered a mediocre night. A bushcraft-gathering reviewer in Georgia went the other way: on a mild night the bag was too warm to zip up. Cold sleepers should treat it as a bag for lows above 5 °C. Anyone expecting winter use will be disappointed, whatever the extreme rating says.

**Construction.** Naturehike's cheaper CW280 uses sewn-through baffles, which let cold spots and drafts through. A hammock camper who bought a CWM400 found baffle walls inside, along with stable down that resisted migrating when shaken. That is a meaningful step up for a bag at this price. The 20D nylon shell and stitching draw generally positive remarks on build quality, with the occasional loose thread end. The YKK two-way zipper is a sound choice at this level.

**Weight and packability.** At 790 g with 400 g of down, the CWM400 carries a lot of insulation for a bag rated only to 6 °C. Premium brands get colder ratings out of similar down weights. What the CWM400 offers is a bag that packs to 16 x Ø35 cm, roughly the size of a loaf of bread by one owner's description, and that weighs under 800 g at a price that undercuts most competitors by hundreds of dollars. It is not the lightest way to stay warm at 6 °C. A quilt does the same job for less weight.

**Fit and comfort.** The 200 x 80 cm cut is generous by ultralight standards. A reviewer who received a sample from Naturehike reported a good fit and judged it suitable for people up to about 6 ft 3 in. Side sleepers and restless sleepers will appreciate the extra shoulder and hip room. Weight-obsessed hikers will see the same room as wasted volume and fabric. The envelope footprint also unzips fully to work as a quilt on warm nights, which suits the three-season brief.

**Fill variants.** Naturehike has sold the CWM400 name in more than one configuration, including 650-fill duck down and 750/550 goose listings from third-party retailers. The 800-fill goose version is the one this review covers. Buyers should confirm the product listing states 800 fill-power goose down and 400 g of fill before ordering.

**Value.** Street prices for the CWM400 have run from about $100 on sale to around $135 at list. At that price, reviewers keep landing on the same verdict: a well-made budget bag, and one that its owners expect to replace once they know what they want. The same Canadian reviewer put the gap bluntly, saying the difference between a cheap and an expensive down bag is "enormous". Even so, he found the CWM400 hard to beat for the money.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 400 g of 800-fill goose down for around $100–135
- 790 g and a compact 16 x Ø35 cm packed size
- Roomy 200 x 80 cm cut; fits taller sleepers
- Baffle walls reported on owner-inspected bags
- YKK two-way zipper
- Splices into a double bag; opens up for warm nights

**Cons**

- 6 °C comfort rating limits it to summer and shoulder-season use
- The -10 °C extreme rating invites misuse
- Heavier than a quilt with equivalent warmth
- Down quality and detailing sit below premium brands
- Multiple fill configurations sold under similar names, so check the listing

## Who Should Buy This

The CWM400 suits new backpackers building a first down kit, summer hikers who want a compressible bag for mild nights, and couples who want a bag that doubles up with a partner's. It also works for car campers and travelers who value a small pack size. Cold sleepers, shoulder-season hikers who expect frost, and gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere. A true 0 °C bag or a down quilt will serve them better.

## Verdict

The CWM400 is a competent, inexpensive down bag that does its job above 5 °C and costs far less than the name brands. Buy it for warm-weather trips and as an entry point into down. Pass if the forecast drops below freezing or if every gram matters.
