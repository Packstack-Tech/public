## Overview

The Quechua MH500 40L is Decathlon's mountain-hiking pack, designed by the brand's team at the foot of Mont Blanc for regular trail use rather than gram-counting. It has a tensioned mesh back panel, a preformed hip belt, nine pockets and a rain cover stowed in the base, all at a price that undercuts most cottage-brand alternatives. The trade-off is weight: at up to 1,390 g, it sits well above where ultralight packs live.

## Performance

**Carry comfort.** The MH500's reputation rests on its suspension. The tensioned mesh back holds the pack off the wearer's spine, and a wire frame gives it enough structure to keep the load from sagging. A buyer reviewing the pack on a retailer page praised the frame, back panel and hip belt as a comfortable all-day carry, and said the pack moves a fully loaded weight onto the body effectively. The same owner noted that the shoulder straps adjust easily and stay put. Preformed hip belt, foam shoulder straps and thumb rests round out a setup that Decathlon aims at mountain day hikes and multiday trips with modest loads.

**Organization.** Nine pockets is a lot for 40 liters. Two quick-access pockets take water bottles, and a reservoir compartment sits inside. Two belt pockets hold a phone or snacks. The side zip into the main compartment is the feature owners tend to appreciate most on the trail: it lets a hiker pull a layer from the bottom of the pack without unloading the top. One owner noted that the main compartment keeps a regular boxy shape, which makes packing straightforward.

**Weather protection.** The coated polyester shrugs off light rain and morning dew. It is water-repellent, not waterproof, so the integrated rain cover in the base does the real work in a downpour. For a pack at this price, having the cover built in is a practical touch: it cannot be left on the kitchen table.

**Volume and fit.** Walkers on the Camino de Santiago forum describe the 40L as carrying smaller than its rating, since it is light and compresses well, and they were comfortable running it half-full. At 66 cm tall it is a slim, tall profile that Decathlon rates as cabin-baggage compatible, which suits hikers who also use it for trips that begin with a flight.

**Value.** The MH500 has long drawn praise for price-to-performance. A widely read DPReview forum thread called the 30L version the best-value daypack around, with the 40L described as the same pack with extra room for a modest price premium. List prices run roughly £65–70 in the UK and CHF 94.90 in Switzerland, and the 10-year guarantee against manufacturing faults adds to the appeal.

**Weight.** Here the pack is out of step with this site's usual audience. A 1.26–1.39 kg empty weight for 40 liters is a conventional-pack number. A hiker moving to a sub-5 kg base weight will typically want a frameless or minimal-frame pack that weighs a fraction of this. The MH500 earns its heft with the frame, the mesh back and the pockets, but thru-hikers counting every gram will feel the difference.

## Choosing a Version

Only the size changes. Volume, pockets and features are identical.

- **Size S (1,260 g):** For hikers under 170 cm. The shorter back length keeps the hip belt on the hips rather than the ribs, and it saves 130 g.
- **Size L (1,390 g):** For hikers 170 cm and taller. Anyone near the 170 cm cutoff should choose by torso length and try both if possible, since a wrongly sized frame pack undermines the comfort that is the pack's main selling point.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Ventilated tensioned mesh back with a frame that carries loads well
- Nine pockets, including two bottle pockets reachable without removing the pack
- Side zip into the main compartment
- Rain cover built into the base
- Low price and a 10-year guarantee
- Two sizes for better fit

**Cons**

- 1.26–1.39 kg is heavy for an ultralight setup
- Coated fabric is water-repellent, not waterproof
- Mesh-back design pushes the load away from the spine, which costs some internal volume
- Pockets and zips add weight that minimalists will not use

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who want a comfortable, well-organized 40L pack for weekend trips, hut-to-hut routes, camino-style walks or travel, and who care more about price and carry comfort than shaving grams. It also suits anyone moving up from a daypack who is not ready to spend on a cottage-brand pack. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers with a lean kit should look at lighter frameless or minimal-frame packs instead.

## Verdict

The MH500 40L is a sensible, comfortable and inexpensive hiking pack with a feature set that outclasses its price. It is not an ultralight pack, and it makes no claim to be. For general backpacking and travel it is a strong buy; for a thru-hiker chasing a low base weight, it is not the right tool.
