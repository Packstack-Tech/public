## Overview

<cite index="5-8">
  REAL Turmat (Tomatsuppe in Norwegian) is made in Tromsø by Drytech, a company
  that has supplied field rations to the Norwegian Armed Forces since 1989.
</cite>
That military lineage matters:
<cite index="5-4,5-5">
  these are freeze-dried meals developed and tested in arctic conditions — light
  to pack, quick to prepare, reliable where it counts.
</cite>
The Tomato Soup is one of the lighter entries in the lineup — a vegan,
herb-seasoned soup built around tomatoes, pasta, and soy protein. It's aimed
squarely at hikers who want something warm, familiar, and easy on the stomach at
lunchtime or during a mid-day break.

## Performance

### Flavor & Texture

<cite index="6-7">
  The soup is made with tomatoes, pasta, and onions, seasoned with a blend of
  mild herbs
</cite>
— think classic Norwegian home cooking rather than anything bold or exotic.
<cite index="1-8">
  The tomato base feels familiar and close to a classic Norwegian home-cooked
  soup.
</cite>
That's either reassuring or underwhelming depending on what you're after on the
trail.

<cite index="12-13,12-14">
  REAL Turmat meals are freeze-dried, which means ingredients retain their shape
  and structure better than other forms of dehydration — the end result is meals
  that taste a bit more like real meals and often have better texture and
  mouthfeel.
</cite>
That reputation holds here: the pasta rehydrates properly rather than turning to
mush, and the soup has an honest broth character rather than a thin, powdery
aftertaste.

<cite index="1-9">
  Soy protein gives the meal more substance without making it heavy
</cite>
— it's a sensible way to keep the dish vegan while adding a bit of staying
power. Don't expect a thick, creamy consistency though. This is a brothy soup.
Expect it to behave like one.

### Preparation

<cite index="2-34,2-35,2-36">
  Tear open the package at the top and add approximately 310 ml of hot water up
  to the marked line, stir well, close the packet, and let it stand for 10
  minutes. Open and stir well before eating.
</cite>
That's genuinely it. No separate pot required, no guesswork on water volume
thanks to the external fill line.

<cite index="9-4">
  The packaging is well considered, with a clear volume marking so you know
  exactly how much water to add, a resealable top for while the meal is
  rehydrating, and a second tear-off level for easier eating out of the packet.
</cite>
After years of fighting with foil pouches in the dark, I appreciate that Drytech
actually thought through the eating experience. A long-handled spoon helps — the
pouch is tall and narrow before you tear down to the second notch.

### Calories: The Honest Conversation

This is where you need to calibrate expectations. 286 kcal from a 62 g dry serving is actually solid calorie density — roughly 4.6 kcal/g, better than most bars. But 286 kcal total is a light lunch at best. For context, REAL Turmat's own main-course Field Meals clock in around 700 kcal. <cite index="8-9">The soup is lighter than the well-known stews in the range, which makes it ideal for midday breaks</cite> — and that's exactly how I'd use it. Treat it as a warm, restorative mid-hike fuel stop, not a standalone dinner.

If you're running a tight calorie budget on a long day, pair it with a tortilla or some olive oil and nuts. <cite index="19-13">It can be enjoyed on its own or used as a base for adding other ingredients, allowing you to customize your meal according to your taste and nutritional requirements</cite> — and that's a practical tip worth taking seriously.

### Packaging & Shelf Life

<cite index="12-16,12-17,12-18">
  One thing REAL Turmat does that most other brands don't: the packages are
  vacuum packed. This has two benefits — pack size is smaller, and no oxygen
  absorber is needed.
</cite>
<cite index="2-15">
  When vacuum-sealed, these meals can last a long time — up to over 5 years,
  even without the use of preservatives.
</cite>
That makes them a legitimate emergency food option as well as a trail staple.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Freeze-dried quality: honest texture, proper rehydration, no powdery off-notes
- Vacuum-packed pouch is genuinely compact and well-designed
- Fully vegan, no artificial preservatives
- Military-grade provenance — tested in conditions far worse than most of us will see
- Easy on the digestive system; good for high-output days
- 5-year shelf life with no oxygen absorber needed

**Cons**

- 286 kcal is too low for a standalone dinner or even a substantial lunch on a hard day
- Contains both gluten and soy — limits its use for those with dietary sensitivities
- Mild, familiar seasoning won't excite anyone looking for bold trail flavors
- <cite index="16-4">REAL Turmat is well known for being expensive</cite> — and
  for a sub-300 kcal meal, the cost-per-calorie is unfavorable compared to
  heavier-hitting options in the lineup
- Brothy consistency means it won't keep you warm or full for long in cold conditions

## Who Should Buy This

This soup works best for hikers who want a vegan, easy-to-digest warm option for lunch breaks, or as a light starter before a more calorie-dense dinner. It's a natural fit for plant-based backpackers who already know the REAL Turmat range and trust the brand's quality. It also earns a place in a longer trip food kit as a lower-effort meal on rest days or short-mileage days when you don't need 600+ kcal from a single pouch. If your primary goal is maximum calories per gram, look to the Field Meal range instead.

## Verdict

The REAL Turmat Tomato Soup is a well-made, honest freeze-dried soup that delivers on flavor and convenience — just don't confuse it with a full meal. The brand's freeze-drying pedigree and thoughtful packaging design are genuine differentiators, and the vegan, herb-forward profile is a welcome change from the meat-heavy norm. At 286 kcal, it earns its place in a trip menu as a purposeful midday warmup, not a calorie anchor — plan accordingly and it won't let you down.
