## Overview

Nearly every freeze-dried brand sells a breakfast skillet, and Peak Refuel's version is the one that keeps winning side-by-side comparisons against Mountain House. It's a pouch of scrambled eggs, pork sausage, diced potatoes, peppers and onions that weighs 138 g and carries 31 g of protein. The trade-off is flavor: the texture and meat content impress, but the seasoning is mild enough that many hikers carry hot sauce to finish the job.

## Performance

**Texture and contents.** This is where the Breakfast Skillet earns its reputation. One reviewer who ate it next to Mountain House's version reported that the pouch was stuffed, the potatoes were diced instead of shredded, and the peppers came as actual strips rather than specks too small to taste. Practical Self Reliance's comparison of the major breakfast skillets found Peak Refuel's has the most meat by far. Mountain House's version leans heavier on potato, and Backpacker's Pantry's vegetarian option was judged less filling. The freeze-dried eggs get unusual praise too: one buyer called them the best dehydrated scrambled eggs he had tried, and a Top Shelf Backpacking review noted they beat most backpacking eggs.

**Flavor.** Here the reports split. Some owners describe a savory, peppery dish with a kick from the sausage. Others, including Top Shelf Backpacking, found it bland enough to need a lot of salt and hot sauce, and one Peak Refuel customer docked a star because the sausage added less flavor than expected. The fixes are cheap and light: salt, pepper, green chili flakes, or a few packets of hot sauce. One reviewer also found it was a touch heavy on onion.

**Rehydration.** Peak Refuel's directions call for boiling water and about 10 minutes. Most reports say everything rehydrates fully, though one Scheels reviewer who waited five extra minutes still found a quarter of the eggs a little crunchy. A thru-hiker who ate it as dinner on a 40-mile trek suggested using slightly less water than the pouch calls for, which avoids a soupy scramble.

**Portion size and calories.** The label's two servings are generous. One reviewer was stuffed after eating a whole pouch alone, and another found half a pouch plenty for breakfast. Eaten by one person, the pouch delivers 540 calories. That's a modest number for a thru-hiker burning 4,000 or more a day, but the protein is higher than most freeze-dried breakfasts. Older reviews list 640 calories and 39 g of protein for the same meal. Those figures predate the current label. A couple eating one pouch between them gets about 270 calories each. For that reason, one reviewer recommends stretching it with a flour tortilla, a stick of cheese and salsa to make a breakfast burrito.

**Weight and pack efficiency.** At roughly 111 calories per ounce, the skillet is well below the 125-plus calories per ounce that ultralight food planners often target. Eggs and vegetables are bulky and light on fat, and a pouch weighing 138 g buys a lot of volume but not much energy. Hikers counting every ounce will notice.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 31 g of protein per pouch, high for a freeze-dried breakfast
- Chunky, recognizable ingredients: diced potato, pepper strips, real sausage
- Eggs that rehydrate better than the usual rubbery crumbles
- Widely praised in head-to-head tests against Mountain House
- Just add boiling water; no cooking required

**Cons**

- Seasoning is mild, and several reviewers needed salt or hot sauce
- About 111 calories per ounce, low for a trail food
- 540 calories per pouch is light if eaten solo and heavy if split
- Eggs can stay slightly firm if rehydration is rushed
- About $15 per pouch adds up on a long trip

## Who Should Buy This

This suits weekend and section hikers, hunters, and anyone who wants a protein-heavy hot breakfast and will tolerate a few extra grams of condiments. It also works for two people sharing a pouch alongside a tortilla and cheese. Thru-hikers on long stretches and gram counters should look elsewhere: oatmeal with nuts and powdered milk gives more calories per ounce for far less money, and a pouch this size works as an occasional treat, not a daily staple.

## Verdict

The Breakfast Skillet is the best-reviewed freeze-dried scramble in its class, thanks to its meat content, ingredient quality and protein. It isn't an efficient calorie carrier, and it works best with a small flavor upgrade from your kitchen. Buy it as a trail luxury, not a base ration.
