## Overview

Real Turmat Pasta Bolognese is a freeze-dried, add-water dinner from Norway: durum wheat pasta, beef meatloaf crumbles and a mild tomato sauce, sealed in a single-serving pouch. It is lactose-free and milk-free, and it sits near the top of the brand's popularity list. The trade-off is the usual one for premium freeze-dried food: good flavor and low weight, paid for with a high price per calorie and a portion that some hikers find stingy.

## Performance

**Calories per ounce.** At 525 kcal for 139 g with the pouch, the meal delivers roughly 3.8 kcal per gram as carried, and about 4.3 kcal per gram of food alone. That is respectable for a pasta dish, which tends to trail rice- and potato-based freeze-dried meals on calorie density. The ingredient list explains the profile: pasta makes up 36 percent of the recipe, with rapeseed oil adding fat and a beef meatloaf component making up 13 percent. The 22 g of fat does a fair amount of the caloric work.

**Taste and texture.** The flavor target is "mild and familiar," and reviewers say it hits it. Verified buyers on a large Nordic outdoor retailer's site, where the meal has nearly 200 reviews, include one who called it the best pasta bolognese on the market and another who said a restaurant could serve it without anyone noticing. Skeptics are in the minority, but they exist: one reviewer called it good for freeze-dried but far from a real bolognese, and another noted that the minced-beef character does not always come through in the bite. The beef is finely chopped, which is part of the reason it has a reputation as a kid-friendly dinner. A Belgian head-to-head of five freeze-dried bolognese meals gave it 6.2 out of 10 overall, with taste scoring well (7.3) and aroma and texture middling (5.3 each). Reviewers there also noted the pouch design looks clinical.

**Preparation.** The routine is standard: tear the pouch, add boiling water, seal, wait. The brand says it is ready in 10 minutes at most. One owner's advice is worth repeating: respect the full rehydration time, or the pasta stays crunchy. The 500 g prepared weight implies a bit under 400 ml of water, so it is worth measuring rather than eyeballing. An insulated pouch cozy helps on cold evenings, since pasta rehydrates slowly once the water cools.

**Portion size.** The recurring complaint is volume. One buyer who rates the flavor highly warns that big eaters and expedition hikers should be cautious. At 525 kcal, the pouch is a modest dinner for someone burning 3,500 calories a day on a thru-hike, and it usually needs a side, such as olive oil, a tortilla or a drink mix, to feel like a full meal. Moderate-appetite hikers and shorter trips find it adequate.

**Price.** Retail prices vary by market: about 129 NOK direct in Norway, roughly €11–13 at European outdoor shops and about $15 in the US, where backorders have been reported. Buyers who otherwise like the brand call it a bit pricey. Reasonable for a few trail dinners; costly as a full-resupply staple.

**Dietary notes.** The lactose-free, milk-free label is helpful for dairy-sensitive hikers, but the meal still contains gluten from wheat pasta, plus celery and soy (the soy comes from soy sauce in the seasoning). It is not a gluten-free option.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Mild, familiar flavor that earns consistently high marks from owners
- 525 kcal in 139 g, a solid density for a pasta meal
- Lactose-free and milk-free without a flavor penalty
- Fine beef texture that works for kids and picky eaters
- Five-year shelf life suits food caching and emergency kits
- Flat 200 x 200 x 70 mm pouch packs easily

**Cons**

- A small portion for hungry thru-hikers
- High cost per calorie
- Pasta needs the full rehydration time or stays firm
- Meat character is subtle; some reviewers find it less "bolognese" than expected
- Contains gluten, celery and soy
- Stock can be inconsistent in some markets

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for section hikers, weekend backpackers and families who want a no-fuss, crowd-pleasing dinner with a mild profile. It also suits anyone avoiding lactose who still wants a hot pasta meal. Calorie-hungry thru-hikers and budget-minded resupply planners should look elsewhere or plan to supplement it. The same recipe is sold in a larger Field Meal format, which is the better choice for big appetites.

## Verdict

Real Turmat Pasta Bolognese earns its following by tasting like dinner rather than a compromise, and it weighs little for what it delivers. The price and the single-serving size keep it from being an everyday thru-hike staple, but for shorter trips and kid-friendly meals it is a reliable pick.
