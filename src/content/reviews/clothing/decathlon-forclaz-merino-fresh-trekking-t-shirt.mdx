## Overview

The Forclaz Merino Fresh is Decathlon's short-sleeved merino trekking tee, built for hikers who want wool's odour resistance and temperature regulation without paying Icebreaker or Smartwool prices. At 148 g in a men's size L and 155 g/m², it sits at the light end of Decathlon's merino range. The trade-off is a fiber a notch coarser than the brand's other merino tees, which matters for the sensitive-skinned.

## Performance

**Weight and warmth.** At 155 g/m², the Merino Fresh is a true summer-to-shoulder-season fabric. Decathlon's long-sleeved merino shirts run 190 g/m², a weight that reviewers describe as warm when it's cold and only reasonably breathable when it's hot. The Merino Fresh gives up that cool-weather bias for a thinner knit. At 148 g, it is about 2 g lighter than the 150 g MT500 short-sleeve in the same size, and roughly a third lighter than the 221–222 g long-sleeve versions.

**Odour control.** This is why anyone buys merino for a long trip. Forclaz markets its merino tees on multi-day wear, and the pitch is credible. Owners of Decathlon's merino tops consistently report no unpleasant smell after repeated wears. One Camino walker said the shirt dried far faster after a hand wash than a regular sports tee, and he has since converted to merino entirely. On a week-long route, that usually means one shirt on the body and one in the pack, rather than three.

**Durability.** Forclaz's merino line wraps the wool fibers in a thin polyamide sheath, a construction Decathlon says protects them from rubbing and cuts. That matters under pack straps, where lightweight merino tends to pill and thin first. Decathlon does not publish pack-strap abrasion data, so long-term wear is best judged by owner reports. Reviewers of the sibling long-sleeve note that pilling is possible over time.

**Softness.** The 19.5-micron fiber is a step coarser than the 18.5-micron wool in Decathlon's MT500 and Trek 500 shirts. Owners of those shirts report that they don't itch, and some who came from Icebreaker prefer the Forclaz for softness and fit. Side-by-side comparisons with premium merino are less kind, describing Decathlon's weave as looser and slightly more abrasive. Decathlon's own advice is to wash the shirt once before first wear for a softer hand. Expect a serviceable shirt rather than a luxurious one.

**Care.** The care label is strict. Wash at 30°C or below, and keep it out of the tumble dryer and away from the iron. Dry cleaning is also off the list. That's standard merino practice, but it rules out the careless laundromat routine.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 148 g in size L, light for a merino tee
- 155 g/m² fabric suits warm days and three-season hiking
- Merino odour resistance that allows several days of wear between washes
- Polyamide-sheathed wool construction aimed at abrasion resistance
- Costs far less than the big merino brands

**Cons**

- 19.5-micron fiber is coarser than Decathlon's 18.5-micron merino tees
- Less soft and refined than premium merino, according to side-by-side testers
- Pilling is a risk under pack straps over time
- Strict care limits: cool wash, no tumble dryer

## Who Should Buy This

This is the shirt for the budget-minded hiker or thru-hiker who wants a real merino tee for spring through early autumn. It also suits Camino walkers, travellers and anyone building a small wardrobe who'd rather own two merino shirts than one expensive one. Ultralight gram-counters can find lighter options, and anyone who wears a shirt against bare skin all day and values softness above all should spend more on a finer-micron tee from a premium brand. Hikers who sweat heavily in high heat may also prefer a synthetic.

## Verdict

The Forclaz Merino Fresh delivers the main thing merino is for, a shirt that stays fresh over several days, at a weight and price few competitors match. It isn't the softest or most refined wool tee on the market, but for three-season trekking it is hard to argue with. Buy it as a workhorse, and keep the premium merino for the shirt you wear next to the skin all day.
