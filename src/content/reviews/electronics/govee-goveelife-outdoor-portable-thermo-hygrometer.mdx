## Overview

The GoveeLife Outdoor Portable Thermo-Hygrometer (model H5171) is a pocket-sized temperature and humidity sensor with its own LCD, an IP65 rating and a lanyard for hanging off a pack strap or tent loop. It is built on a Sensirion SHT-40 sensor and logs to the Govee Home app over Bluetooth. At 23.5 g it costs little in weight and, on sale, little in dollars. The trade-off is that it tells you what the weather is doing without helping you do anything about it.

## Performance

**Accuracy and speed.** The SHT-40 is the same family of sensor found in far pricier weather instruments, and the ±0.3°C rating is tight for a gadget in this price class. The 2-second sampling rate means the display reacts quickly when the unit moves from a warm pack pocket to cold air, or when a stove is lit in a vestibule. Reviewers of Govee's related thermo-hygrometers consistently describe the readings as steady and consistent, including in hot and cold extremes. The usual caveat for any clip-on sensor applies: hung against a pack in direct sun or near a body, it will read warm. In shade, clear of the pack, it behaves.

**Standalone display.** Unlike many Bluetooth-only loggers, this one has a screen, so a phone is optional. The LCD shows temperature and humidity along with a comfort indicator, and owners describe it as clean and easy to read in daylight. There is no backlight, so checking the temperature at 2 a.m. in a bivy means reaching for a headlamp.

**App and logging.** Connecting through Govee Home is a short setup. The app shows live readings, min/max values and a 20-day curve, and it can send alerts when temperature or humidity crosses a preset threshold. Two years of cloud storage and export is more history than any hiker will use on a single trip, though it suits someone comparing seasonal conditions across a few years of overnights. Users of Govee's app on other sensors say the graph view can take a few taps to navigate.

**Range and battery.** Bluetooth range is listed at 197 ft in open air. For a camp, that means the unit can hang from a tree and still report to a phone in the tent, though trees, rock and bodies shorten the real number. Govee claims roughly a year of battery in Bluetooth mode. That is a manufacturer figure, but it suggests a fresh cell will outlast a thru-hike's worth of casual use.

**Durability.** The IP65 rating covers dust and water jets, so rain and spray are not a problem. Govee states plainly that the unit cannot be immersed, so a stream crossing with it dangling off a pack strap is a bad idea. A nylon lanyard with an adjustable buckle is included and clips to a pack, carabiner or loop.

**What it does for a hiker.** Here the case gets thinner. Knowing that it is 28°F and 85% humidity at dawn is useful for deciding whether to sweat-proof a quilt or how much condensation to expect in a single-wall shelter. Humidity data is the more interesting number: it explains why a tent is dripping and why a down quilt feels clammy by day three. But the information rarely changes a decision that a hiker's skin and a glance at the forecast wouldn't. Many hikers who carry weather-sensing gear already get temperature from a watch or phone, and neither of those reads humidity.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 23.5 g, light enough that it won't be questioned on a gram-counted list
- SHT-40 sensor with ±0.3°C and ±3% RH accuracy, and 2-second updates
- Works without a phone thanks to the built-in LCD
- IP65 body with lanyard for hanging from a pack, tent or carabiner
- App alerts, 20-day curves and 2-year export
- Typically discounted to around $18 against a $30 list price

**Cons**

- No backlight on the LCD
- Not submersible, despite the outdoor branding
- Adds weight and a battery for information that rarely changes decisions
- Bluetooth only, so no remote monitoring away from the unit without a Govee gateway
- Reading accuracy depends heavily on placement: sun and body heat skew results
- Phone-based logging needs the Govee Home app, with its account and cloud requirements

## Who Should Buy This

Gear-curious hikers, shelter-condensation obsessives and anyone building a log of overnight conditions for quilt and bag choices will find it a worthwhile oddity. It also suits car campers and section hikers who want a hang-anywhere sensor for a tent, greenhouse or trailhead cooler. Thru-hikers counting every gram and seeking tools that earn their place should skip it. A phone's weather app and a hiker's own sense of cold cover nearly every decision this device informs. Anyone who needs a dependable measurement for safety, such as avalanche or altitude work, should buy purpose-built instruments instead.

## Verdict

The GoveeLife Outdoor Portable Thermo-Hygrometer is a well-specified, honestly priced sensor that does exactly what it claims and no more. At 23.5 g it is easy to justify as a hobbyist's luxury, but it is a luxury all the same. Buy it for the data and the tinkering; leave it home if the pack weight matters more than the reading.
