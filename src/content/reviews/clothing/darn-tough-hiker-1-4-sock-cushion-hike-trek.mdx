## Overview

The Hiker 1/4 Sock Cushion (style 1959) is Darn Tough's midweight quarter-height hiking sock, a Vermont-knit merino blend that has become something of a default on long trails. It suits hikers in low-cut trail runners and light hikers who want real padding underfoot without the bulk or heat of a full boot sock. The headline trade-off is price: roughly $23 a pair, against a guarantee that is meant to make the purchase a one-time event.

## Performance

**Cushioning and comfort.** Darn Tough describes cushion as terry loops rather than a flat jersey knit, the carpet-versus-hardwood distinction, and in the Hike/Trek category the loops sit under the foot with padding carried up the Achilles. The result is a sock that owners describe as snug and padded without feeling bulky. One reviewer on REI who wore them for nine days on the Pacific Crest Trail called the weight about right: some cushion, but breathable and not excessively hot. Another reviewer who prefers very thin socks said the same thing in reverse, that thin wool never worked for them and this thickness did.

**Fit.** The fine-gauge knit is the product's signature. The brand's pitch is no bunching, no slipping, no blisters, and the review pool largely backs it: Darn Tough's own site shows an average of about 4.8 out of 5 across more than 2,000 reviews for this sock, and REI's men's listing sits at 4.7 across 552 reviews. Praise centers on a locked-in fit that stays put in the shoe. The knit runs snug, and a few buyers note that fit opinions vary, so sizing by the brand's chart rather than by habit matters.

**Moisture and temperature.** Merino does the heavy lifting here. Owners report that the socks wick well, dry quickly and stay free of odor through a full day, and several say their feet stay comfortable in summer heat as well as in cold. A women's-version reviewer on REI who used them on the PCT described stepping into an ankle-deep cold puddle and finding the socks dried fast and never turned unpleasant. A small number of buyers on Darn Tough's site mention cold feet, which is the predictable cost of a quarter-height sock with a vented upper in shoulder-season weather.

**Durability.** This is the reason the sock has its following. Owners report pairs lasting eight years or longer with careful laundering, and a CNN Underscored reviewer who has logged thousands of trail miles said a favorite pair held up over seven years of travel, including the John Muir Trail. The nylon content, at 38 percent, and the reinforced heel are the structural reasons. The record is not spotless. Some buyers have reported holes appearing sooner than expected, one runner reported pilling at the Achilles after the first wash, and a women's-sock reviewer saw wear on the top of the foot where the shoe rubbed. The lifetime guarantee is the answer to all of these: Darn Tough will replace a pair that fails, no conditions attached. Owners also say a tumble dryer shortens a sock's life, so hang-drying or a low setting is the better habit.

**Weight.** At about 75 g for a Large, this is not a gram-shaving sock. Thin lightweight merino models weigh less and dry a touch faster. What the 1959 offers instead is a middle path that does not need a second pair for padding, and the quarter cuff saves material and heat over a crew.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Terry-loop cushion underfoot with Achilles reinforcement, enough padding for high-mileage days
- Snug performance fit that owners say resists bunching and slipping
- True Seamless toe closes every toe seam, so there is no ridge to rub
- Merino blend resists odor over multi-day use and dries quickly
- Years of wear reported by many owners, backed by an unconditional lifetime guarantee
- Made in Vermont

**Cons**

- About $23 a pair, high for a sock if the guarantee goes unused
- Heavier and warmer than lightweight merino quarter socks
- Quarter height leaves the ankle exposed to debris, brush and cold
- Some owners report early holes, pilling or wear where the shoe rubs
- Tight fit that not every foot shape likes

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers, section hikers and weekend backpackers in trail runners or low-cut hikers, especially those who rotate two or three pairs and want them to last several seasons. It is also a strong pick for anyone who has lost patience with cheap socks that blister or wear through. Hikers who run hot, or who favor wafer-thin socks for a barely-there feel, should look at Darn Tough's lightweight quarter instead. Those hiking in gaiter-free brush, snow or tall boots will want a crew or micro-crew cut.

## Verdict

The Hiker 1/4 Cushion is the sock to buy if one midweight pair is going to cover most of a hiker's trail days. It carries a few more grams than the lightest options and costs more up front, but the fit, the durability record and the guarantee make that price easy to justify.
