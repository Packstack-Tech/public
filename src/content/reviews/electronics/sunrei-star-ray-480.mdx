## Overview

The Star Ray 480 is a compact, motion-sensor headlamp from Sunrei, a lighting brand that's much better known in Indonesian and broader Southeast Asian outdoor shops than it is on Western trails. It's aimed at hikers and campers who want a genuinely waterproof, hands-free light without committing to a single battery format — the 480 runs on either a proprietary rechargeable lithium cell or standard AAA batteries. Think of it as a budget alternative to lamps like the Black Diamond Spot or Nitecore NU25, with a couple of unusual tricks (motion sensor, hybrid power) thrown in.

## Performance

Let's be upfront about something: this is not a headlamp with a deep bench of independent reviews to draw from. I couldn't find any threads on r/Ultralight, Backpacking Light, or CleverHiker specifically testing the Star Ray 480 — the bulk of the coverage lives on Indonesian marketplaces like Tokopedia and Lazada and a handful of short unboxing videos on TikTok and Instagram. So take the following as an assessment based on spec-sheet analysis and category context rather than months of trail miles from a big pool of users.

**Waterproofing.** IPX8 is a genuinely strong rating for a headlamp in this price bracket — it implies protection against continuous submersion, not just splashes or heavy rain (IPX4-6 territory, where most budget headlamps top out). If Sunrei's rating holds up under real abuse, that's a meaningful edge for anyone hiking in monsoon conditions or doing creek crossings after dark.

**Battery flexibility.** The hybrid system — a <cite index="4-1">3.7v/1250mAh lithium battery or 3 AAA (alkaline) batteries</cite> — is the headline feature. On paper this is great for thru-hikers: charge the lithium cell via USB-C at town stops, but if you're stuck without power, you can raid a gas station for AAAs and keep moving. The catch is that a "3 AAA" bay means you're carrying more physical bulk and weight than a single 18650 or built-in cell would require, and hybrid systems can suffer from slightly weaker regulation across cell types compared to a dedicated build.

**Brightness and modes.** The sibling Star Ray 380 model documents specific beam patterns — <cite index="21-2">Focus Light at 19-250LM with 56m throw and 40-5H runtime, Floodlight at 7-200LM with 22m and 88-5.5H runtime, and Extremely Bright at 44-380LM with 50m throw and 16-3H runtime</cite>. The 480 uses a beefed-up "Cree + LED Beads" light engine to hit its higher 480-lumen ceiling, but Sunrei doesn't publish a full runtime table for this specific variant in the listings I found, so exact battery life at max output is something I'd verify before relying on it for a summit push. Expect the usual headlamp trade-off: high lumens for a short burst, then a big step down to a moderate, long-lasting mode for camp chores.

**Motion sensor.** This is a "love it or hate it" feature in the headlamp world. When it works, waving a hand to toggle the light on and off is genuinely convenient with cold or gloved hands. When it doesn't, it can trigger accidentally in a pack pocket (draining the battery) or fail to register in freezing temps. There's no independent long-term data yet on how reliably Sunrei's sensor performs versus, say, Black Diamond's implementation.

**Weight and fit.** At 110 g with battery, the Star Ray 480 is competitive with mid-tier rechargeable headlamps — heavier than ultralight minimalist lamps like the Nitecore NU20 (~55g) but well under bulkier multi-cell options. The compact 6.0 x 4.2 x 3.8 cm housing suggests a low-profile fit, though without hands-on time I can't speak to comfort over multi-hour wear or how the head strap holds up.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- IPX8 waterproofing is a genuine standout at this price point
- Hybrid battery system (lithium or AAA) offers real flexibility for long trips
- 480-lumen max output is competitive with headlamps costing much more
- USB-C charging on the lithium cell
- Reasonably light at 110g with battery installed

**Cons**

- Very little independent, English-language user testing available — most reviews are marketplace listings, not trail reports
- No published runtime table specific to the 480 variant's max-output mode
- Motion sensor headlamps are hit-or-miss for reliability; unverified here
- Primarily sold through Indonesian marketplaces (Tokopedia, Lazada), which may complicate warranty support or returns for buyers elsewhere
- Hybrid battery bay likely adds bulk versus a single dedicated cell

## Who Should Buy This

This headlamp makes the most sense for hikers in Southeast Asia (or anyone who can source it locally) who want a genuinely waterproof, flexible-power light without paying premium-brand prices. If you frequently hike in wet conditions, want the security of AAA backup batteries, and don't mind being an early adopter without a mountain of user reviews to lean on, the Star Ray 480 is worth a look. If you want a headlamp with a long track record, extensive third-party testing, and guaranteed warranty support in North America or Europe, established brands like Black Diamond, Petzl, or Nitecore remain the safer bet.

## Verdict

The Sunrei Star Ray 480 looks solid on paper — strong waterproofing, decent brightness, and unusual battery flexibility — but it's still an under-reviewed product outside its home market, so I'm rating it a cautious 6.5/10 pending more real-world trail reports. If you can get hands-on with one locally and the price is right, it's a reasonable gamble; if you need a proven performer for a big trip, I'd wait for more independent testing or stick with a more established brand.
