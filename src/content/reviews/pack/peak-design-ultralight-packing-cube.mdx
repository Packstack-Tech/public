## Overview

Peak Design's Ultralight Packing Cube strips the company's original cube down to a single compartment, a single zip and a weatherproof shell. In the two smallest sizes, XXS and XS, it is a 20–27 g pouch for the small stuff that otherwise migrates to the bottom of a pack: batteries, cables, a first-aid kit, a pair of socks. The trade-off is that it is an organizer, not a space-saver, and it costs more than the zip-top bag most hikers already carry.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 20 g for the XXS and 27 g for the XS, the cubes add almost nothing to a base weight. Empty, they fold flat and roll up, held by the elastic cord on the zipper pull, so a spare sits in a lid pocket without taking up room. The fabric is the other half of the weight story: Terra Shell is a recycled ripstop with coated faces and taped internal seams, which puts it closer to a dry bag than to the mesh-and-nylon cubes that dominate the category.

**Weather resistance.** Taped seams and a weatherproof zip are the reason to pick the Terra Shell version over a cheaper cube. Owners who pair the cubes with 25 L outdoor packs single out the waterproofing and the side pass-throughs as the features that matter. The cubes are not submersible dry bags, and the zip is the weak point of any pouch like this, but for keeping electronics and a spare layer dry in a rain-soaked pack, the shell does the job.

**Durability.** Pack Hacker's testers describe the fabric as reassuringly tough for its weight and a good fit for field work. Their caution is the zipper: forcing it closed over an overstuffed cube is where concern lies, and they questioned Peak Design's decision not to use YKK. Other long-term users report intact seams and no fraying after months of camping and travel. The practical rule is to load these cubes to the point where the zip closes without a fight.

**Packing behavior.** The same stiffness that makes Terra Shell tough also makes it hard to overstuff, and with no compression panel, the cube holds what it holds. Pack Hacker's testers note that the sides do not stay upright while loading, so filling an XS one-handed is a minor annoyance. A reviewer at SheClicks found the XXS suited to a power bank, cable and spare batteries, and the XS to balled-up socks, which is about the right scale of ambition. Neither size will keep clothes crease-free.

**Sling conversion.** Clip a flat-hook carry strap or a Peak Design camera strap to the Cord Hook loops and the cube becomes a minimal sling. It is a gimmick for most thru-hikers, but a useful one for a town day, a camera-and-snacks side trip, or a summit push when the pack stays at camp. The strap is sold separately and adds weight and cost.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes are almost the same shape, scaled for different jobs.

**XXS (20 g, 0.8 L, 18 x 8 x 6 cm).** Sized for a power bank and cables, a headlamp and spare batteries, a repair kit, or a small toiletry kit. It is the better choice for someone consolidating loose electronics.

**XS (27 g, 2.5 L, 23 x 11 x 9 cm).** Holds a few pairs of socks, a first-aid kit, a stove-and-lighter kit, or a camera accessory set. Owners report two pairs of average shoes fit in the next size up, the S, so the XS is not a shoe bag. The extra 7 g buys roughly three times the volume of the XXS, which makes it the better value per gram for hikers who want one cube.

Hikers who need a cube for damp socks or odd-shaped items should look at the mesh version, which weighs more (31 g and 45 g) but stretches and breathes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 20–27 g, light enough to ignore in a base weight
- Taped seams and a weatherproof zip, uncommon in cubes this light
- Folds flat and rolls up with an elastic cord
- External pass-throughs and sling option for Peak Design bag owners
- Lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- No compression, so it organizes but does not save space
- Stiff fabric and sides that collapse during loading make filling fiddly
- Zipper can be strained when overpacked
- Carry strap for the sling is a separate purchase
- Pricey for what a zip-top bag also does

## Who Should Buy This

The XXS and XS suit ultralight hikers and travelers who want dry, durable organization for electronics and small kits, and Peak Design bag owners who will use the external mounting points. They are a sensible upgrade over a flimsy mesh pouch that tears by mid-season. Thru-hikers counting every gram on a tight budget, or anyone hoping a cube will shrink their clothing volume, should look elsewhere: a freezer bag does the waterproofing for less, and a compression sack does the compressing.

## Verdict

The Ultralight Packing Cube in XXS and XS is a well-built, genuinely weatherproof organizer at a weight most hikers will not notice. It is not a space-saver and the zip rewards restraint, but for small gear that must stay dry and findable, it earns its place.
