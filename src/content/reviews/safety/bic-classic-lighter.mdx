## Overview

The BIC Classic is the full-size sibling of the lighter that lives in most thru-hikers' hip-belt pockets. It runs on pure isobutane, costs about a dollar when bought in a multipack, and can be replaced at nearly any gas station in North America. The trade-off is simple: a bigger fuel reserve and an easier grip in exchange for roughly double the weight of a Mini BIC.

## Performance

**Reliability.** The flint-wheel BIC has earned its place in trail culture through sheer repetition. In a structured test of backpacking lighters by 99Boulders, a BIC struck 200 times lit all 200. BIC says every lighter passes more than 50 automatic quality checks at the factory, and the company rates the Classic as safe to use at a 45-degree angle. That matters more than it sounds: lighting a canister stove burner often means tilting the lighter toward the burner head.

**Fuel life.** BIC claims up to 3,000 lights per lighter, but the real number depends on how long the flame stays on. Far Out Guides weighed a full-size BIC at 21.8 g full and 17.1 g empty, which leaves about 4.7 g of fuel. Its estimate is about 4,500 quick stove lights at 0.75 seconds each. A campfire-style 3-second burn cuts that to about 1,100. Total flame time comes to roughly 55 minutes. Even with campfire-length burns, one lighter can cover a long section hike or most of a through-hike with daily use. The manufacturer says the flame should not stay lit for more than 30 seconds.

**Wet and cold.** The flint mechanism's weakness is moisture. In a Backpacking Light forum test, a soaked classic-style BIC took more than 30 seconds of shaking, blowing and repeated flicks to light, and a lighter that had been wet and then frozen would not spark for 10 minutes. Wasting fuel on dead flicks was the tester's chief complaint. The usual fix is to dry the flint wheel by rubbing it on a pant leg before trying again. Keeping the lighter in a zip-top bag or an inner pocket avoids the problem.

**Wind.** The flame is a plain open one, and it is easily blown out. 99Boulders found a BIC difficult to light in even a slight breeze, and a windscreen or cupped hands are standard practice.

**Ergonomics.** This is where the Classic earns its extra grams. Hikers who prefer the regular-size body over the Mini often cite cold, numb fingers, when the smaller lighter becomes fiddly to spin. The longer body also leaves a bigger gap between thumb and flame.

**Weight.** At 22 g the Classic is about 10 g heavier than a Mini BIC, which weighs about 0.4 oz. That is the price of the larger fuel tank. Gram-counters will see the difference immediately, and for most trips the Mini's fuel is already more than enough.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Proven flint ignition, with strong consistency in testing
- Large fuel reserve: well past 1,000 lights even with long burns
- Easier to grip and spin with cold or gloved hands than a Mini
- Cheap and available almost everywhere, including trail towns and gas stations
- Child-resistant design and self-extinguishing when the lever is released
- Works at a 45-degree tilt, handy for lighting stoves

**Cons**

- About twice the weight of a Mini BIC
- Flint wheel is slow to recover after a soaking
- Not windproof
- Not refillable, so it goes in the trash when empty
- Fuel level is hard to judge by eye through the body

## Who Should Buy This

The Classic suits car campers, cold-season backpackers, and anyone who wants a backup fire-starter in a kitchen kit or emergency bag. It also suits hikers who find a Mini awkward with big hands or gloves, and groups that will pass one lighter around the campfire. Ultralight thru-hikers counting every gram should carry the Mini instead, since it delivers the same ignition character in half the weight. Anyone expecting to light a stove in a gale needs a windscreen, not a different BIC.

## Verdict

The BIC Classic is a dependable, nearly free fire starter that rarely fails and almost never needs replacing mid-trip. It is a poor fit for base-weight purists, who will do better with the Mini, but it is the right choice for cold-weather, group, and backup duty. Rating: 7 out of 10.
