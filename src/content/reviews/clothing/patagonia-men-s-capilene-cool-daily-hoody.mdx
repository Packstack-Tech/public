## Overview

The Capilene Cool Daily Hoody is Patagonia's all-purpose lightweight sun shirt: a thin recycled-polyester pullover with a fixed hood, built for trail days, water days and the long stretches in between. It weighs 179 g and costs roughly $65–69. Its strengths are comfort, breathability and an odor treatment that owners rate highly. Its weakness is sun protection, which is good but not the 50+ UPF that dedicated sun hoodies advertise.

## Performance

**Comfort and breathability.** The fabric is a smooth, thin jersey with some stretch, and nearly every owner mentions the feel first. One reviewer described it as silky and cool to the touch on a hot day. GearJunkie's tester scored breathability 8.6 out of 10 after six months of wear on ski days, desert hikes, beach trips and rainy walks in England and Wales. Blister's reviewer, who ran in it, found that the hood version breathes as well as Patagonia's standard Cool Daily tee despite covering more skin. The cut is slack, just short of baggy, which helps airflow and arm mobility. It is not a compression base layer, and it isn't meant to be.

**Drying and odor.** Washed in a sink and hung overnight, it is dry by morning, a point repeated across owner reviews. The HeiQ Mint treatment has earned the strongest praise. GearJunkie's tester called the odor control the best of any shirt he had tried. Several-day trips in a single shirt are the use case where that matters, and it is the main reason to choose this over an untreated synthetic tee.

**Sun protection.** Here the hoody is weaker than the marketing around sun hoodies suggests. Patagonia disclosed that its Cool Daily line, style 45310 included, tested at 17 to 45 UPF rather than the 50+ it once claimed, averaging 34. Even at 17 UPF, the fabric blocks about 92 percent of ultraviolet rays. In practice that is solid coverage for most trail days, and one owner reported no burn during a week of 95°F-plus days with the hood up. The hood adds the neck and ears. Hikers who spend long days above treeline or on open water with no shade will want a higher-rated fabric. GearJunkie's tester scored sun protection 6.5 out of 10, the lowest of his four categories.

**The hood.** It is deep, generous and sized to fit over a helmet or under a cap. Hikers who bunch it around the neck when the wind drops or the sun comes out find that it stays out of the way. Pack Hacker's long-term tester said the depth is a plus for sun coverage but that the hood can be floppy. In gusty conditions it will need a hat brim or a cord of some kind to stay put, and there is no drawcord to help.

**Temperature range.** It works best in warm conditions. In extreme heat, GearJunkie's tester found it a bit warm, which is the cost of full sleeves and a hood. It is thin enough to layer under a sweater, vest or shell when temperatures drop, so it works as a base layer in shoulder season. It is not a cold-weather piece by itself. A small touch: an internal sewn loop makes it easy to hang up to dry.

**Value and sourcing.** At $65–69 it sits at a modest price for Patagonia. The fabric is bluesign approved and the garment is sewn in a Fair Trade Certified factory. REI buyers give the men's version 4.8 out of 5 across 12 reviews, and the women's hoody on Patagonia's site holds a 4.7 across more than 400.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 179 g, light enough to stay in the pack all season
- HeiQ Mint odor control that owners rate among the best
- Soft, stretchy, quick-drying jersey that breathes well
- Deep hood that fits over a helmet or under a hat
- Bluesign-approved fabric, Fair Trade Certified sewing
- Priced under many competing sun hoodies

**Cons**

- UPF of 17–45 is lower than the 50+ typical of sun-focused hoodies
- Runs warm in extreme heat
- Hood can flop in wind
- No UPF-rated option for exposed alpine or open-water use

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want one shirt for hiking, camp and town, and who care about smell after a week without laundry, should buy this. It also suits trail runners, climbers and paddlers who want a thin hoody under a pack. Desert and high-altitude hikers who depend on the shirt as their main sun defense, or anyone who runs hot above 90°F, should look at a fabric with a verified 50+ UPF and vented construction.

## Verdict

The Capilene Cool Daily Hoody is a comfortable, honest piece of kit that does the everyday job well at 179 g. Buy it for the feel, the odor control and the price. Skip it if the highest sun protection is the main requirement.
