## Overview

The Power Stretch Stimulus is a thin, snug stretch-fleece glove that Mountain Hardwear pitches as both a liner and a standalone layer for cold mornings, winter trail runs and shoulder-season hiking. At 52 g it adds almost nothing to a pack, and it breathes better than most fleece gloves. The trade-off is that it is a cool-weather glove with a mixed record on touchscreens and seam durability.

## Performance

**Warmth and breathability.** This is a glove for hard effort in moderate cold, not for standing around. Backpacker's testers called it the most breathable glove in their roundup. One tester wore it on an after-work session in 40°F temperatures and found it wicked moisture off the palms. The same tester found it cut wind better than a standard fleece liner glove. The Power Stretch fabric also dries quickly and sheds snow and light rain, though it is not waterproof. Single Geared's liner roundup found it warmer on its own than The North Face's eTip glove. A reviewer in that roundup also noted it is warm enough for working with ski boots at altitude, if not for sitting in a snowstorm.

**Layering.** The smooth-knit outer face slips easily into a shell glove or mitten. A Backpacker tester said that when the sun set and temperatures dropped, the glove went on under thicker gloves with no fuss. For three-season hikers, this fits a long-standing Backpacking Light forum approach: a Power Stretch glove for walking, with a waterproof overmitt added for cold rain or camp.

**Fit and dexterity.** The cut is close and the elastane keeps it that way, with no loose fabric at the fingertips to snag on a zipper pull or a trekking-pole strap. Owners with larger hands have reported the glove still feels comfortable and does not cost them dexterity, and the cuff covers the wrist.

**Grip.** The palm print holds a phone, a pole grip or a stuff sack securely, even wet. The coverage is limited to the palm, though. One Single Geared tester found that slicker items, such as a water bottle or a tool, slipped out of the fingers because they lack any grip material.

**Touchscreen.** Stimulus conductive treatment sits on the index finger and thumb, which is enough for checking a map app or skipping a track. Results vary, though. One Backcountry reviewer gave one star specifically for poor touchscreen control. Another buyer said the current pair is not as good as an earlier one.

**Durability.** The synthetic overlay gets good marks for wear. The weak point is the stitching. Backpacker's tester developed a hole at the thumb seam after a winter of use. On Backcountry the glove averages 4 out of 5 stars across 14 reviews, and on REI it averages 3.6 out of 5 across 42 reviews. The split suggests this is a glove that works well for many people while others see seam or sizing problems.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 52 g (1.8 oz), small enough to carry as a spare or an emergency layer
- Four-way stretch fabric breathes well and dries quickly
- Works alone in cool weather or under a shell glove or mitten
- Palm overlay grips a phone, a pole handle or a pack strap
- Recycled fabric; materials are bluesign-approved

**Cons**

- Touchscreen response is inconsistent, with some users reporting poor results
- Grip is limited to the palm, with no grip material on the fingers
- Reports of seam wear at the thumb
- Not waterproof and not warm enough for sustained cold
- Mixed review averages, especially on REI

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers, trail runners and shoulder-season hikers who want a glove light enough to forget about, one they can wear all day on cold mornings and drop in a pocket by noon. It also suits anyone building a layered hand system, with a rain mitt or insulated shell over a snug liner. Winter hikers who need dependable warmth in sustained cold, and anyone who relies heavily on phone navigation with gloves on, should look at a thicker glove or a dedicated touchscreen-tested design.

## Verdict

The Power Stretch Stimulus does the simple job of a light stretch-fleece glove well: it is breathable, close-fitting and almost weightless. Treat the touchscreen function as a bonus rather than a feature to count on, and expect to inspect the thumb seam as the glove ages. It is a good value for cool-weather hiking and layering, and not a glove for anyone who needs serious warmth.
