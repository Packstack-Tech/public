## Overview

The LS-284CEX is a four-section carbon fiber travel tripod from Leofoto's Ranger line, and its party trick is a 55 mm leveling base fused into the apex. Twist a lever, tilt the platform up to 15° in any direction, and the bubble centers without anyone fiddling with leg lengths on a sloping trailside. In this kit, with a ball head included, it weighs 1,633 g. That is solid and compact, but it belongs to the photographer who hikes, not the gram-counter who photographs.

## Performance

**Leveling.** The base is the reason to buy this tripod over its non-leveling sibling, the LS-284CX. Fstoppers' reviewer, who used the kit for a year, singled out the captive locking lever as a real improvement over a fixed knob, since it can be repositioned around the head to dial in tension. The same reviewer notes that zeroing the platform before touching the head makes panoramas and motorized star-tracking far easier. On uneven ground the logic is simple. Plant the legs wherever they land, then level the top. A forum owner who shoots panoramas called the built-in leveler a standout feature of the model.

**Stability and load.** Leg tubes taper from 28 to 25, 22 and 19 mm, and the 10-layer carbon construction earns consistent praise for stiffness at this weight. Leofoto rates the tripod at 10 kg. One owner reports it holding a Sony A1 on a 600 mm f/4 with a fluid head, roughly 6 kg of gear, without complaint. Another forum user parked it in a running stream, halfway up the second leg section, with a 105 mm telephoto attached. Wind with a long lens is the territory where owners are most careful, and the usual advice is to keep the lower, thinner sections retracted when possible.

**Height.** There's no center column, which helps both weight and rigidity. Full extension reaches 1,462 mm (57.6 in) at the leveling platform. One owner says that's above eye level for him once the ball head is added. Taller shooters will find themselves bending a little. At the other end, the three leg angle stops (23°, 55°, 85°) let the legs splay nearly flat for a 100 mm minimum height, which suits low-angle macro and ground-level landscape compositions. One DPReview poster noted the narrowest stop is tighter than on a Gitzo he owned, and that legs can be opened partway between stops at a small cost in height.

**Packing and handling.** Folded at 533 mm (21 in), it fits a carry-on and many daypacks, though it is not as tiny as the folding-leg designs from Peak Design and similar brands. Fstoppers also points out that this larger format keeps compatibility with ordinary tripod heads, which the proprietary designs can't match. Twist locks release with a quarter turn, and the 6061 aluminum hardware is CNC-machined and hard anodized to resist scuffs. A carrying case is included, as are interchangeable 3/8" feet.

**The head.** Owners of Leofoto's ball heads praise machining and finish, and the heads tolerate telephotos up to about 70–200 mm class glass comfortably. The common complaint, voiced on DPReview about the LH-36 in particular, is uneven friction between pitch and roll, which owners say the weight of a mounted camera partly masks. Forum owners also note that heads with a 40–44 mm ball sit best on the compact 55 mm platform, since larger 52–55 mm heads look oversized and add weight the legs don't need.

**Price.** The tripod alone lists at $429, and Leofoto's USA store lists ball head sets from $529. That undercuts comparable leveling-base carbon travel tripods from premium brands by a wide margin, though a few reviewers have noted the loosening of leg-section shims on related Leofoto models over time.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Integrated ±15° leveling base removes the need to adjust leg lengths on slopes
- 10 kg load rating, and owners report stable support for heavy telephotos on a fluid head
- 21-inch folded length and a 100 mm minimum height
- Quarter-turn twist locks, three leg angle stops and a 1/4"-20 accessory socket
- Carrying case included
- Priced well below premium leveling travel tripods

**Cons**

- At 1,633 g with head, it is no ultralight option
- Leveling base cannot be removed or swapped
- 57.6 in maximum height is limiting for taller users, with no center column to add reach
- Leofoto ball heads have drawn complaints about uneven friction
- Larger, heavier ball heads look out of scale on the 55 mm platform

## Who Should Buy This

The ideal buyer is a landscape, astro or panorama photographer who hikes with a mirrorless body and one or two lenses up to the 70–200 mm class. They want a tripod that fits in or on a pack, and they're tired of re-extending a leg for the third time on a sloped campsite. It also suits videographers who want to level before panning. Thru-hikers who carry a camera only occasionally, or anyone for whom a kilogram-class tripod is out of the question, should look at lighter folding or compact designs. Shooters under a 6-foot frame who need eye-level height for long sessions can skip it as well, because it has no column to add height.

## Verdict

The LS-284CEX delivers a genuinely useful feature, quick leveling on uneven ground, in a stiff, well-built carbon package at a price that undercuts the premium names. The weight and the mixed reputation of Leofoto ball heads keep it from being a standout, but for the hiking photographer who prioritizes stability and fast setup, it's a strong buy.
