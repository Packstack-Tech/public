## Overview

The FLEXTAIL Zero Pillow is an inflatable camp pillow built around a "B-shaped" TPU bladder wrapped in a removable, washable peach-skin velvet cover. It's aimed at side and combination sleepers who find typical 2-3" inflatable pillows too thin, with an adjustable height range of 3.1-5.1 inches and a strap system to keep it from sliding off your pad. At 150g, it's noticeably heavier than minimalist inflatable pillows, positioning it more as a comfort-first option than a pure weight-weenie pick.

## Performance

The headline feature here is height. Most inflatable camp pillows top out around 3-4" fully inflated, which is fine for back sleepers but leaves a lot of side sleepers with their shoulder jammed into the pad. FLEXTAIL's B-shape design lets the Zero Pillow inflate up to 5.1", and in a hands-on review from The Radavist, the reviewer specifically sought out the Zero Pillow after struggling for years to find anything tall enough for his roughly 6" shoulder-to-neck gap, calling it <cite index="1-1,1-3">"his current favorite" and noting it's "the thickest he has been able to find."</cite> That's a meaningful data point — a dedicated side sleeper who'd tried the usual UL suspects landed on this one specifically for the loft.

The second big functional piece is the strap system. A lot of inflatable pillows migrate off your pad the second you roll over, and more than one owner review on FLEXTAIL's own product page flagged this as a pleasant surprise: <cite index="2-10">"I didn't realise it came with it's own soft pillow case and elastic strap to secure to my mat."</cite> In practice, a strap that pins the pillow to your sleeping pad solves the single most common complaint about inflatable pillows in general — waking up with it somewhere near your knees.

Shape-wise, owner feedback is consistently positive. One reviewer noted <cite index="2-12">"the pillow has a very good shape which helps to prevent pains on the neck,"</cite> and another simply called it <cite index="19-4">"very good shape which helps to prevent pains on the neck."</cite> The contoured B-profile is clearly doing what it's designed to do for neck alignment, which lines up with the broader trend of ergonomic-shaped pillows outperforming flat rectangular ones for side sleeping.

Not every note is glowing. At least one early user flagged the air chamber as feeling a bit thin before reserving final judgment: <cite index="2-13">"The impression for the air chamber is that it's a bit thin, but I need to try for a long time to give a solid review."</cite> That's a single data point and not corroborated elsewhere, but it's worth flagging if you're coming from a firmer foam pillow and expecting the TPU bladder to feel bombproof under pressure.

On the cover: a removable, washable peach-skin velvet shell is a nice touch that a lot of budget inflatable pillows skip entirely — TPU against your face gets clammy fast, and being able to toss the cover in a stuff sack wash on a resupply day is a real quality-of-life upgrade over pillows with sewn-in or non-removable covers.

The elephant in the room is weight. At 150g, the Zero Pillow is roughly double what you'd pay for something like a Sea to Summit Aeros or Nemo Fillo Elite, and it's in the same weight class as some 3-season sleeping pads' worth of pillow. For anyone counting grams on a thru-hike, that's going to be a hard sell unless the extra height genuinely solves a sleep-quality problem for you. I haven't seen independent long-term durability data (multi-month PCT/CDT-style testing) on this specific pillow yet, so treat claims about long-haul reliability as unproven either way — FLEXTAIL's broader inflatable-gear lineup (pumps, pads) has a decent reputation among reviewers, but the Zero Pillow itself is still relatively new to the market and hasn't accumulated the multi-year field record that, say, Sea to Summit's pillow line has.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely tall inflated height (up to 5.1") — a rarity in the inflatable pillow category and a real fix for side sleepers
- Integrated strap keeps it anchored to your pad through the night
- Removable, washable velvet cover feels better against skin than bare TPU and is easy to keep clean
- Contoured B-shape gets specific praise for neck support from actual owners

**Cons**

- At 150g, it's roughly 2x the weight of minimalist inflatable pillows aimed at the same use case
- At least one early report described the air chamber as feeling thin under pressure
- Limited long-term/multi-month durability data available at this point
- Packed size (7.8 x 4.1 in) is on the larger side for an inflatable pillow

## Who Should Buy This

This is the pillow for side sleepers who've bounced off every thin, 3"-tall inflatable pillow on the market and have resorted to stuffing a puffy jacket into a stuff sack instead. If neck and shoulder comfort matters more to you than shaving the last few ounces off your base weight, the extra height and strap system solve real, specific problems. Counting grams for a long thru-hike and mostly sleeping on your back? You can do lighter.

## Verdict

The FLEXTAIL Zero Pillow trades weight for genuine ergonomic gains — more height, a strap that actually keeps it in place, and a washable cover that most competitors skip. It's not a pillow for the ounce-counters, but for side sleepers chasing real comfort at camp, it's one of the few inflatable options built specifically around that problem. I'd put it at a 7.2/10: excellent at what it's designed to do, just not trying to be ultralight.
