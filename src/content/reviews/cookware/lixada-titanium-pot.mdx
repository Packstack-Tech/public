## Overview

The Lixada Titanium Pot is a 750ml single-wall cook pot built from 99.9% pure titanium, aimed at solo backpackers who want a boil-water-and-rehydrate setup without the TOAKS price tag. It comes with a lid, foldable wire handles, and a mesh stuff sack — the standard formula for this category of budget titanium cookware. It's a reasonable pick if you're outfitting a first ultralight kit and want titanium's durability and corrosion resistance without spending $30+ on a name-brand pot.

## Performance

The headline number here is weight, and it's worth pausing on before you buy. At 189g, this pot is noticeably heavier than most other 750ml titanium pots on the market. <cite index="6-1">The TOAKS 750ml pot weighs 93g alone and 110g with the lid.</cite> Other Lixada 750ml pots I found listed come in even lighter — one listing puts a similar 750ml/4.0"x4.3" Lixada pot at 128g with lid. That's roughly 60g (2 oz) lighter than the unit reviewed here for essentially the same footprint. If gram-counting is your main motivation for going titanium in the first place, this particular version undercuts the whole point somewhat — you're paying titanium prices without getting titanium's full weight payoff. It's possible this listing uses a thicker gauge of titanium for added durability, but I couldn't confirm that from the manufacturer's page, so treat it as a trade-off rather than a defect.

Where it does perform well is heat transfer and general usability, which lines up with what people report across the broader Lixada 750ml pot lineup (which shares the same shape, wall thickness, and construction as this one). One Amazon reviewer of the closely related Lixada 750ml pot noted it <cite index="12-6,12-7">had used it a lot since January and it "heats up fast and does the job very well."</cite> Another mentioned the <cite index="12-10">handles stay cool if you're using a mini canister stove</cite>, though <cite index="12-11">that changes if you're cooking over an open fire</cite> — expected for any titanium pot with wire handles, but worth knowing if car-camping over a fire is part of your plan. The <cite index="12-15">included stuff sack doubles as a wash cloth</cite> in daily use, and multiple owners reported it holding up fine over repeated trips with <cite index="12-9">no dings, dents, or other problems after many hikes in the bag</cite>.

One recurring gripe across Lixada pot reviews is the printed volume markings — <cite index="12-8">the etching wears off over time, though it's rarely needed for a precise reading anyway</cite> when you're just eyeballing water for a dehydrated meal. Also worth flagging: some buyers of the multi-piece Lixada sets note the <cite index="14-3">lid can sit a little loose</cite>, which tracks with what I'd expect from a stamped titanium lid at this price point — it won't seal like a French press lid, but it doesn't need to for boiling water.

For actual capacity, this size class handles what you'd expect: enough for one freeze-dried meal pouch rehydration or a couple of mugs of coffee, but tight if you're cooking for two or trying to simmer a real one-pot dinner. A separate review of a comparable Lixada set noted <cite index="15-8">water boiling in under 4 minutes on a canister stove</cite>, which is in line with typical titanium pot performance — thin walls transfer heat fast but also mean hot spots and easy scorching if you're not stirring.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Pure titanium construction — corrosion-resistant, won't impart metallic taste, and should outlast aluminum or steel equivalents
- Fast boil times typical of thin-wall titanium pots
- Foldable handles stay cool enough to handle with canister stoves
- Included mesh sack doubles as a light cleaning cloth
- Budget-friendly entry point into titanium cookware

**Cons**

- At 189g, it's meaningfully heavier than comparable 750ml titanium pots (TOAKS at 110g with lid, other Lixada 750ml variants around 128g) — undercuts the weight-savings case for going titanium
- Printed volume markings reportedly wear off with use
- Lid fit can be loose on this line of pots (not a dealbreaker for boiling, but don't expect a tight seal)
- Handles get hot over an open flame, as with most wire-handle titanium pots
- Single-wall titanium scorches easily if you're not paying attention while cooking anything beyond boiling water

## Who Should Buy This

This pot makes sense for a solo hiker who wants the corrosion resistance and longevity of titanium but doesn't want to pay TOAKS or Snow Peak prices, and who isn't chasing every last gram. If you're building an ultralight kit where every ounce is tracked against a base weight spreadsheet, the weight here is a real drawback — you can find lighter titanium 750ml pots, including from Lixada's own other listings, for similar or less money. But if "titanium and reasonably light" is good enough and you want a simple boil-and-eat setup with a lid and sack included, this covers the basics.

## Verdict

The Lixada Titanium Pot does the fundamentals right — fast boils, durable material, functional folding handles — but the 189g weight is hard to ignore next to competitors that shave off 60-80g in the same 750ml size. It's a fine budget cook pot for casual backpacking or car camping, but dedicated ultralight hikers should shop around within Lixada's own lineup or step up to a TOAKS before settling on this specific listing. I'd call it a 6.5/10: solid materials and function, dragged down by a weight spec that doesn't match the "ultralight titanium" pitch.
