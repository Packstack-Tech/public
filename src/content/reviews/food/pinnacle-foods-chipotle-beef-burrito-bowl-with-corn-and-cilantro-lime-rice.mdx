## Overview

Pinnacle Foods, a small-batch freeze-dried meal maker in High Point, North Carolina, built this burrito bowl from grass-fed ground beef, black beans, corn, roasted peppers and onions, and basmati rice cooked with garlic and lime zest, all folded into a creamy chipotle sauce and topped with cilantro. It is a single-serving dinner aimed at hikers who want real ingredients and a high protein count. The trade-off is price: at $17.99 a pouch, it sits at the premium end of the freeze-dried aisle.

## Performance

**Calorie density.** The macros add up to roughly 690 calories in 158.3 g, or about 4.4 calories per gram (around 123 per ounce). That is a strong figure for a dinner built around rice and beans, and it means a single pouch can replace the usual two-pouch "double serving" arithmetic that leaves many hikers hungry. Fat supplies about 43 percent of the calories, protein about a quarter, and carbohydrates the remaining third, a split that favors sustained energy and overnight recovery over a quick carb hit.

**Protein.** At 41 g, this is one of the more protein-heavy single-serve dinners a hiker can carry. Grass-fed beef does most of the work, with black beans adding to the total. For a multi-day trip where protein tends to slip, one pouch covers a large share of a day's target.

**Flavor and texture.** Buyers on Pinnacle's own site rate the bowl 4.93 out of 5 across 41 reviews, which should be read as a manufacturer-hosted score but is notable for its consistency. The recurring themes are specific: the heat is present but not overpowering, the corn, beans and beef hold their texture instead of dissolving into paste, and the cilantro-lime rice reads as a distinct layer rather than a generic base. One reviewer said it rehydrated perfectly when the instructions were followed and the pouch wasn't opened early.

**Rehydration.** The listed wait is 13 minutes with 1.25 cups of boiling water, a touch longer than some quick-soak competitors. Owners who let the full time elapse report an even result with no crunchy rice. The pouch rewards patience; a cozy and a second stir at the halfway mark are cheap insurance on a cold evening.

**Burrito mode.** Pinnacle itself suggests carrying hot sauce and tortillas, and reviewers have followed through: one reports stretching a single pouch into two or three burritos. That adds a few ounces for tortillas but turns the meal into something with more bulk and a better mouthfeel than a bowl of rice and beef.

**Sodium.** At 960 mg, the sodium is moderate for a 690-calorie dinner. Hikers on a sodium-restricted diet should note it, but on a sweaty multi-day trip it is more help than hindrance.

**Cost.** At $17.99, the cost works out to roughly 2.6 cents per calorie. That is more than a budget pouch from a big-name brand, and most of the premium goes to the grass-fed beef and the small-batch production.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 41 g of protein and about 690 calories in 158.3 g
- Ingredients that stay distinct after rehydration
- Moderate chipotle heat that doesn't overwhelm
- Holds up as a burrito filling with tortillas
- Grass-fed beef, with the whole meal prepared and packed in the U.S.

**Cons**

- $17.99 per single serving
- 13-minute rehydration is slower than some rivals
- High fat share means a heavier, richer meal than a rice-and-veggie pouch
- Single-serving only, so couples must buy two pouches

## Who Should Buy This

This is for backpackers who put protein and calorie density first and are willing to pay for a dinner that tastes like a cooked meal. It suits weekend trips, shoulder-season outings where a hot, hearty dinner matters, and long-trail resupply boxes where a few treat dinners break up the monotony. Thru-hikers on a tight food budget should look elsewhere, as should anyone who wants a meal in under 10 minutes or avoids spicy food.

## Verdict

Pinnacle's burrito bowl delivers real-food flavor, a high protein count, and excellent calorie density in a 158 g pouch. The price keeps it a dinner for planned splurges rather than a staple for every night on trail. Backpackers who can absorb the cost will rarely be disappointed.
