## Overview

The Trangia Billy is a 2.5-liter aluminum cook pot with a lid and a wire bail handle, built in Sweden to swallow an entire Trangia 27 Small stove. It's aimed at two to four people who cook real meals rather than rehydrate them. The trade-off is plain: a lot of capacity and a one-container cook kit, paid for in weight and bulk that most ultralight hikers won't tolerate.

## Performance

**Capacity and cooking.** At 2.5 liters, the Billy sits well above the 27-series system's own pots, which Trangia sizes for one or two people. That headroom is the point. A pot of pasta, a stew, or a double batch of rice fits without batching, and a wide, shallow-ish Ø200 mm footprint heats quickly on a spirit burner or over coals. Plain aluminum conducts heat well and cleans easily, though it has no non-stick surface, so oatmeal and cheese sauces reward attention and a spoon. Trangia sells a black non-stick version of the same pot at a higher price.

**The nesting trick.** The pot's reason for existing is that the whole 27 Small stove packs inside it. Owners of the 27 set describe it as a natural upgrade: the stove, burner and windscreen disappear into one container, and the lid closes over the lot. The manufacturer says to use it on the stove with the supports in the raised position, the same way the frypan is used, so the pot sits low and close to the flame rather than inside the standard pan arrangement. A Trangia pot sold for one system is not guaranteed to nest in another; retail Q&A notes the 2.5 L pot is taller than the 25-8 system and slightly narrower than it, so it is a 27 Small accessory and nothing else.

**Handle and fire use.** The bail handle is the other quiet strength. A long-time Trailspace reviewer praised its raised curve, which keeps the pot stable when hung from a chain or stick over a fire, and reported that it stayed cool even when the pot was nestled in embers. Trangia's guidance is blunt on one point: never leave the pot over an open fire empty. Used on gas or other heat sources, the pot works as any wide aluminum billy would. Note that the wire bail is for hanging and carrying, not for pouring; a pot grabber helps with a full 2.5 liters of boiling water.

**Weight and bulk.** The catalog weight is 395 g, or 13.9 oz, and that is before a stove or fuel. For a solo ultralight setup, that is several times the weight of a titanium mug-and-stove kit. Trailspace's long-time owner called it heavy at "almost half a kilo", and also noted it is a big pot that suits three or more people better than a solo hiker. A 200 mm diameter takes up real pack volume, though owners point out it doubles as a padded container for small items wrapped in spare clothes.

**Fit and finish.** Trangia's own shop shows five ratings averaging 4.4 out of 5. One recent verified owner called the build quality below Trangia's usual standard and said the lid didn't seat properly; another described it as a simple aluminum pot that does everything needed. Check lid fit on arrival.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 2.5 L capacity cooks one-pot meals for two to four without batching
- The entire Trangia 27 Small stove packs inside, giving a single-container kit
- Aluminum heats quickly and evenly, and is easy to clean
- Bail handle supports hanging over a fire and stays cool in use
- Inexpensive, at roughly $35 or €27
- Works on other heat sources, not just the Trangia burner

**Cons**

- Around 13.9 oz (395 g) and Ø200 mm wide, which is heavy and bulky for solo backpacking
- Plain aluminum with no non-stick coating, so sticky foods need care
- Lid fit has drawn at least one quality complaint
- Nesting only works with the 27 Small system
- Bail handle isn't designed for pouring

## Who Should Buy This

The Billy suits owners of a Trangia 27 who have outgrown its stock pots: couples, small families, scout groups and canoe or car campers who cook real meals. It also works for Trangia users who want a single container for the entire stove. Solo thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. A titanium pot of 0.7 to 1.3 liters weighs a fraction as much and doesn't need a 200 mm-wide stuff sack space.

## Verdict

The Billy is a cheap, honest, well-proportioned camp pot that does exactly what its Swedish maker says: it holds a lot and swallows a stove. It is a poor fit for ultralight trail use and a good one for groups and Trangia loyalists.
