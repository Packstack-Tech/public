## Overview

The K.120 Flipper is Tactica's entry-level EDC folder — a D2 steel, G10-handled flipper knife designed in Australia and sold at a price point well below most premium production knives. It's built more with everyday carry and car camping in mind than dedicated backpacking, and that distinction matters a lot once you look at the weight. If you're the type of hiker who's fine carrying a "real" knife instead of a 15-gram razor blade on a keychain, it's worth a look; if you're chasing base weight numbers, it's not.

## Performance

**Steel and edge holding.** D2 is the headline feature here, and it's a reasonable choice for the price bracket. It's a high-carbon, high-chromium tool steel, and Tactica's own product copy correctly points out that it offers <cite index="11-3">"excellent edge retention and durability – a step up from the stainless blades found on most knives in this price range."</cite> That's true as far as it goes — D2 will out-hold-an-edge most of the cheap stainless steels you'll find on knives in this price range. The trade-off, which the marketing conveniently skips, is that D2 is often called "semi-stainless": it has enough chromium to resist most surface rust but not enough to be truly stain-proof. On a backpacking trip where the knife is doing rope-cutting, food prep, and living in a damp pocket for days at a time, that means you'll want to wipe it down and put a drop of oil on the pivot and blade more often than you would with a true stainless like 14C28N or S30V.

**Deployment and lockup.** The flipper uses a ball-bearing pivot, and the one detailed owner account I could find on Tactica's own site described the action as smooth and confidently locked with no play after some hard use. Take that with the appropriate grain of salt since it's a single, brand-hosted review, but it lines up with what you'd expect from a well-tuned ball-bearing flipper at this price: light detent, positive flip-open, no wobble once it locks. There isn't yet a meaningful body of independent long-term reviews from ultralight or EDC forums (I couldn't find threads on r/EDC, r/Ultralight, or Blade Forums specifically dissecting the K.120), so treat any claims about long-term lock durability or wear as unverified.

**Ergonomics and grip.** The G10 scales are machined rather than stamped, which typically means crisper texture and tighter tolerances than injection-molded handles you see on sub-$30 knives. At 0.6 inches thick and 4.8 inches closed, it's a substantial handle — good for a secure grip with gloves on or wet hands, but it's also part of why this knife carries heavier and bulkier in the pocket than most dedicated backpacking knives.

**The weight problem.** This is the section that matters most for this audience. At 133 g (4.7 oz), the K.120 weighs roughly as much as some ultralight tent stake bags, or more than a full set of six titanium stakes. Compare that to a Swiss Army Classic (around 21 g) or even a mid-size folder like a CRKT Squid (around 60 g), and the K.120 is carrying a lot of extra mass for its 2.8-inch blade. None of that weight is wasted — it comes from the G10 handle scales, steel liners, and aluminum back spacer that give it its solid, "beefy" in-hand feel — but it's a knife built for daily carry durability, not for a summit pack.

**Carry.** The reversible pocket clip lets you set it up tip-up or tip-down for left- or right-hand carry, and the lanyard hole is a nice touch if you want to run a zipper pull or retention lanyard on a pack strap instead of pocket-carrying it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- D2 steel holds an edge notably longer than the mystery stainless found on knives at similar price points
- Ball-bearing flipper action feels smooth and deploys reliably by early accounts
- Machined G10 handles feel more premium than the price suggests
- Reversible clip and lanyard hole give flexible carry options
- Backed by what Tactica advertises as a lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- At 133 g, it's 3-6x heavier than dedicated ultralight EDC/backpacking knives
- D2 isn't fully stainless — needs more maintenance in wet, humid trail conditions than a true stainless blade
- Independent long-term reviews are scarce; most available feedback comes from the manufacturer's own site or subscription-box (BattlBox) inclusions
- 2.8-inch blade length is on the small side for camp chores like batoning kindling, despite the knife's overall bulk

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for someone who wants one knife to do double duty as an everyday carry piece and an occasional car-camping or day-hike companion, and who values a confident, chunky in-hand feel over gram-counting. It's not the right call for thru-hikers or anyone optimizing base weight — for that crowd, a sub-1-ounce keychain blade or a lightweight fixed blade will make far more sense.

## Verdict

The K.120 Flipper delivers legitimate value in steel quality and fit-and-finish for its price, but it's fundamentally an EDC knife that happens to be rugged enough for camp use, not a backpacking-specific tool. If weight isn't a concern, it's a reasonable buy; if it is, look elsewhere. I'd put it at a 6/10 for backpacking use specifically — solid materials and mechanism, undercut by a weight that doesn't match this audience's priorities and a thin track record of independent long-term reviews.
