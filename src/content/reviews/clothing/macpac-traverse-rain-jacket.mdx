## Overview

The Traverse is Macpac's answer to the three-season shell: a 3-layer Pertex Shield Pro jacket that uses a light fabric across the body and a heavier weave where a pack does its damage. It suits hikers who want a waterproof, windproof layer that stuffs small and doesn't feel like armor. The trade-offs are no pit zips, a durability ceiling set by that thin face fabric, and an Australian list price of A$530.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The Traverse relies on a fully sealed, three-layer shell, and owners report it does the job. One long-term Australian owner, who has used the jacket since 2020, says it kept him dry for many hours in the few times it rained hard, while allowing that no jacket stays dry forever in a sustained downpour. Wilderness Magazine's deputy editor made a similar point after a year of use: the 20,000 mm rating is a paper number, but the jacket has matched his heavier previous shell in wet New Zealand conditions at less than half the weight.

**Breathability without vents.** Macpac skipped pit zips to save weight and remove potential leak points, and that is the jacket's most debated choice. Trails-focused reviewers at Tracks Less Travelled say they lean on pit zips constantly to regulate temperature, and flag their absence as a minor mark against breathability. Wilderness's reviewer expected to miss them and didn't, crediting the thin, breathable fabric for keeping him from overheating while tramping. The practical read: the Pertex membrane copes well at a steady walking pace, but hikers who run hot on climbs will be managing heat with the front zip and hood alone.

**Durability.** The body, hood and sleeves use the 110 g/m² fabric. The shoulders and lower cuffs get a midweight Pertex Shield Pro weave (listed at 172 g/m² on Macpac's New Zealand site) to cope with pack straps. Wilderness reported no visible wear after a year of use, though the reviewer noted that so light a jacket could struggle over the long haul. Tracks Less Travelled ranks it below others in its roundup for toughness, which is the honest cost of a thin face fabric. Bushwhacking or scrambling in this jacket is asking for snags.

**Fit and features.** The hood is adjustable with a stiffened peak and zips away into the collar for a tidy look when the sky clears. A tricot-lined chin piece keeps the zipper off skin, and a full-length AquaGuard zip has an internal flap. Reflective branding sits on the chest and upper back. Hip pockets are pack-compatible in theory, but Wilderness's reviewer found them sitting at hipbelt height and awkward to reach when carrying a loaded pack. Tracks Less Travelled also describes the cut as a little short compared with other jackets. Macpac's own caveat applies too: AquaGuard zips are highly water resistant, not waterproof, so a phone or map belongs in a dry bag rather than a pocket.

**Packability.** Owners describe it as folding small and carrying like a luxury item for its weight, which is the main reason to choose a thin 3-layer over a heavier hardshell.

## Choosing a Version

The two listed versions differ by about 30 g, roughly an ounce. The Standard comes in at 300 g and the Men's at 330 g (size M). The construction, fabric and features are the same across both: the same Pertex Shield Pro shell, hood, pockets and 20K/20K ratings. Macpac's range runs Men's in S–3XL, and the lighter option suits hikers who count every gram and fit the cut. Anyone between sizes should prioritise fit over the 30 g saving, since a jacket that rides up under a pack straps negates the weight advantage. Macpac also lists a women's cut at 270 g in size 10.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 300–330 g for a fully seam-sealed 3-layer shell
- Body-mapped shoulders and cuffs add wear resistance where packs rub
- 20K/20K rating backed by owner reports of staying dry through hard, prolonged rain
- Hood stows into the collar; stiffened peak holds its shape
- Bluesign-approved main fabric

**Cons**

- No pit zips, so heat management is limited on steep climbs
- Thin face fabric is not built for abrasive terrain
- Hip pockets sit low under a hipbelt
- A$530 RRP is steep for the weight class (frequent sales have taken it much lower)
- AquaGuard zips are water resistant, not waterproof

## Who Should Buy This

The Traverse suits three-season hikers and trampers who carry a shell for the days it's needed, want it light and compact, and stay on maintained trails or open ground where branches and rock aren't constantly attacking the sleeves. It also works as a lightweight winter wind-and-rain layer over insulation. Hikers who sweat heavily, run hot on uphill grinds, or push through scrub should look at a jacket with pit zips and a tougher face fabric. Bargain hunters should wait for a sale rather than paying full price.

## Verdict

The Traverse is a well-built, light and legitimately waterproof shell that does what its spec sheet suggests, with durability and ventilation as the compromises. It is a strong buy at a discount and a considered one at full price. Hikers who value low weight over venting and ruggedness will be happy with it.
