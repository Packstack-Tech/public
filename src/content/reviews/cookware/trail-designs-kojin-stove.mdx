## Overview

The Trail Designs Kojin (named, as it happens, after the Japanese god of fire, the hearth, and the kitchen) is a 17g aluminum alcohol burner <cite index="4-1,4-2">designed to work inside the higher heat and lower oxygen environment of cone systems, and smaller than the older 12-10 stove — allowing it to pack inside smaller pots and eliminating the stakes the 12-10 requires for the Sidewinder.</cite> <cite index="4-3,4-4">It uses internal batting and a screw-top sealed metal lid to prevent spills and retain unburned fuel, regulating fuel flow and vaporization through the batting material rather than the chambers used in the 12-10.</cite> It's aimed squarely at existing Trail Designs Caldera/Ti-Tri cone users who want the fastest possible 1–2 cup boil from an alcohol stove they can seal and pack away mid-burn.

## Performance

### Boil Speed

The Kojin punches well above its weight class when it comes to raw speed. <cite index="11-22">In testing, 2 cups of water can be brought to a boil in under 5 minutes</cite> — fast for any alcohol stove. <cite index="12-28,12-29,12-30">Adventure Alan clocked just over 5 minutes to boil a pint, calling it "ripping fast for an alcohol stove."</cite> <cite index="7-1">In a head-to-head comparison conducted by Backpacking Light, the Kojin was the most "powerful" stove tested, boiling water the fastest — but also consuming the highest amount of fuel to do it.</cite>

### Fuel Efficiency

This is where the Kojin shows its one real trade-off. <cite index="5-9,5-10">In testing by Andrew Skurka, the Zelph Modified StarLyte used 18% less fuel than the Kojin to achieve a rolling boil — amounting to about 0.5 oz (15g) less fuel over a 5-day/4-night trip.</cite> That's not negligible. That said, <cite index="2-34">with optimal wide-bottom pots in a cone system, users report fuel efficiency of 15 ml or less to boil a pint</cite> — competitive for the category. And because <cite index="12-36,12-37,12-38,12-39,12-40">there's no need for precise fuel measurement or waiting for the stove to burn out, you simply pour in fuel, boil, snuff it out, cap it, and the unused fuel is saved for your next session.</cite> Over a trip, that recapture of leftover fuel partially closes the efficiency gap.

### Spill Resistance and Safety

This is genuinely where the Kojin earns its keep. <cite index="2-20,2-21,2-22">The aluminum screw-top housing contains a proprietary fibrous filler that acts as both a wick and a fuel stabilizer — you can turn the stove on its side and the fuel won't drain out.</cite> <cite index="1-16">The lid has a silicone seal that prevents leaking, resists heat, and threads tightly to the body of the stove.</cite> This means you can snuff the flame by capping the stove mid-burn without worrying about a leaking seal — a real advantage over the Zelph StarLyte's plastic press-fit lid. <cite index="11-1">One long-term user noted the threaded silicone-sealed lid on the Kojin had never leaked, while the StarLyte's plastic cap could let a little through.</cite>

One caution: <cite index="1-33,1-34">the stove is easier to fill than screened options due to its wide-open top, but you have to avoid overfilling — a dispenser bottle rather than a regular fuel bottle makes precise pours much easier.</cite>

### Cold Weather

Alcohol stoves and cold weather don't always mix, but the Kojin handles it reasonably well for the category. <cite index="12-46,12-47">One tester used the stove down to 12°F (-11°C) and reported it lit right up and boiled without difficulty</cite> when paired with a cone. <cite index="9-7,9-8,9-9,9-10">Another tester running prototypes in the field used it down to near single-digit temperatures at 11,000 feet in the Sierra Nevada with no ignition issues.</cite> The standard advice applies: <cite index="11-31">keep your fuel warm on cold trips by carrying a small dispenser bottle in a pocket, as cold alcohol will struggle to light.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- At 17g, among the lightest purpose-built alcohol burners available
- Spill-proof fiber filler means no fuel disasters when packing or tipping
- Silicone-sealed screw cap retains unused fuel and can snuff the flame mid-burn
- Compact enough to pack inside Evernew 600 and 900 ml pots with the cone
- No priming, no moving parts, no stakes needed in the Sidewinder setup
- Fast boil times for an alcohol stove — under 5 minutes for 2 cups in a cone system
- Proven in cold conditions down to near 10°F
- Inexpensive (~$12)

**Cons**

- Burns roughly 18% more fuel per boil than the Zelph Modified StarLyte in controlled tests
- Denatured alcohol only — no HEET, Everclear, or Esbit
- Needs a cone system (Caldera/Ti-Tri) to hit its performance ceiling; results with generic windscreens will vary
- Loses ground to canister stoves in raw efficiency and cold-weather reliability below about 15°F
- Overfilling is easy if you're not using a dispenser bottle
- Not ideal for large (4+ cup) boils — the 12-10 is a better pick there

## Who Should Buy This

The Kojin is a near-perfect fit for a solo ultralight or thru-hiker already committed to the Trail Designs Caldera or Ti-Tri cone ecosystem. <cite index="2-31">It works optimally with wide-bottom pots in the 0.9–1.3 liter range</cite> — Toaks 900, Evernew 900, and similar. If you're running a "boil and soak" meal plan (freeze-dried dinners, instant coffee, instant oatmeal), the Kojin is as dialed as an alcohol stove gets. It's also a compelling upgrade for anyone who's been hauling the 12-10 and wants to shave pack volume without sacrificing boil speed. If you're starting fresh without a cone system, the $12 stove cost is trivial — but you'll need to budget for the cone, which is where the real investment lies.

## Verdict

The Kojin is the best alcohol burner currently available from Trail Designs, and arguably the most practical option in its class for solo cone-system users. It trades a small amount of fuel efficiency for a noticeably faster boil and a genuinely spill-proof, re-sealable design that makes camp chores simpler and safer. It won't convert anyone who's already moved to canister or heat-exchanger setups, but within the alcohol stove world, it's a well-engineered, inexpensive, and durable tool. **Rating: 8/10.**
