## Overview

The Cocoon TravelSheet is a 100% silk rectangular sleep sack that sits somewhere between a sleeping bag liner and a bed sheet. It is aimed at hut-to-hut hikers, hostel travelers and anyone who wants a clean, breathable layer between skin and bag. The trade-off is the usual one for silk: superb feel and packability, modest warmth and a fabric that wants careful handling.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Cocoon rates the silk TravelSheet at up to 9.5°F of added warmth, and that figure deserves a discount. Users of Cocoon's silk liners on Backpacking Light's forums describe the real gain inside a sleeping bag as closer to half the claim, and a 3–5°F improvement is a reasonable expectation. That still counts for something on a 35°F night with a 40°F-rated bag, but nobody should plan a shoulder-season trip around it. Silk's strength is regulation rather than insulation. Travelers who have used Cocoon's silk sheet for years describe it as cool in warm conditions and a few degrees warmer in cool ones, which matches Cocoon's own pitch.

**Comfort and fit.** The rectangular cut is the main difference from Cocoon's tapered MummyLiner. It is about as roomy as a single mattress, so knees and elbows are not pinned the way they can be in a snug mummy liner. That makes it a better choice inside a wide quilt, under a hut blanket, or as the only layer on a hot night. The pillow pocket, a feature reviewers single out as useful, keeps a stuffed jacket or a borrowed pillow from migrating at 3 a.m. The silk is soft and doesn't cling the way many synthetic liners do.

**Entry and exit.** The VELCRO-secured side opening makes it easier to slide in and out of than a top-only sack, and it gives a little privacy in a crowded dorm. The catch is that the opening is only 38" long and stops well short of the foot, so getting in is still a shimmy rather than a step-through. Cocoon's lighter Thermolite version drops the side opening entirely to save weight, which says something about what the feature costs.

**Weight and packed size.** At 159 g, the TravelSheet is about 26 g heavier than Cocoon's own 133 g MummyLiner Silk, and noticeably heavier than the roughly 100 g Western Mountaineering Tioga Silk. For a gram-counting thru-hiker, that is the price of a roomier cut, a side opening and a pillow pocket. It still compresses to something not much larger than a fist.

**Durability.** This is silk, and it behaves like silk. Reviewers of Cocoon's silk liners at REI praise the comfort and the feather weight, but the most critical owners report fabric that tore or wore through after getting twisted up during use. Others get years of travel use from the same product. The double-stitched seams and reinforced gussets help at the stress points, but the fabric itself is thin. Rough camp-ground sleeping, sharp zipper teeth and a tossing sleeper are the usual causes of damage. Cocoon also sells a Ripstop Silk version for people who prioritize tear resistance.

**Care.** Washing is simple but fussy: a gentle cycle at 30°C, mild detergent, no bleach, and low heat or drip drying. A rinse with a splash of vinegar is Cocoon's tip for keeping colors bright. The liner earns its place here, since it takes the body oil and grime that would otherwise go into a down bag, and it is far easier to wash than the bag.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 159 g and packs small
- Roomy rectangular cut works inside a quilt, a wide bag or alone
- Side opening and pillow pocket add real usability
- Breathable and soft, with temperature regulation across a wide range
- Keeps a sleeping bag cleaner between washes
- Double-stitched seams and reinforced gussets

**Cons**

- Real-world warmth gain is well below the +9.5°F claim
- Heavier than tapered silk liners and the lightest ultralight options
- Thin fabric can tear if it snags or twists under stress
- Side opening is short and doesn't run the full length
- Pricey for a layer that adds few degrees, starting at $109.95
- Delicate-cycle care instructions

## Who Should Buy This

Hut-to-hut hikers, hostel travelers, and warm-weather campers who want a clean, breathable layer and a rectangular cut with a pillow pocket are the ideal buyers. It also suits side sleepers and restless sleepers who find mummy-shaped liners restrictive. Cocoon's XL version, which the company recommends for anyone over 6'2", is the better pick for tall users.

Thru-hikers shaving every gram, or anyone buying a liner mainly to extend a bag's temperature range, should look elsewhere. A tapered silk liner such as Cocoon's own MummyLiner or the Western Mountaineering Tioga weighs less, and a synthetic liner will add more warmth. Rough-use campers who sleep on abrasive surfaces should also consider Cocoon's Ripstop Silk model.

## Verdict

The TravelSheet is a well-featured, comfortable silk sheet that does the job it was designed for: keeping a sleeping bag clean and adding a degree or two of comfort, at a weight most hikers will barely notice. It isn't a warmth upgrade, and it isn't the lightest option. For hut trips and travel it earns a recommendation, but ultralight purists should choose a tapered liner and accept fewer features.
