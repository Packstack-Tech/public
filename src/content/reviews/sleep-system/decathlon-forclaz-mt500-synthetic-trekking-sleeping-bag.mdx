## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 5°C is Decathlon's entry-level trekking bag: a synthetic mummy with a wraparound hood, a 5°C comfort rating and a 0°C limit, priced at £59.99 on Decathlon's UK site. It is aimed at spring-to-early-autumn walkers who want warmth that survives damp and rough handling without paying down prices. The trade-off is plain: at 1,050 g in size Large, it is a budget bag, not an ultralight one.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Decathlon rates the bag for comfort at 5°C with a lower limit of 0°C, which puts it squarely in spring and early-autumn territory. Its insulation is three stacked layers of 60 g/m² polyester wadding on both the top and the bottom panels. That is a simple, proven construction, and it keeps working when damp, which is the main reason hikers in wet climates choose synthetic. One caveat: the underside layer compresses under body weight, so ground insulation comes from the pad, not the bag. Pairing it with a closed-cell foam pad rated around R 2.2 matches the bag's intended temperature range. Decathlon also suggests a liner to add warmth and cut down on washing.

**Weight and packed size.** The bag was redesigned to be lighter and more compact than earlier MT500 versions. Size Large now weighs 1,050 g, and size S for users up to 1.59 m weighs 900 g. It stuffs to 9.5 L, roughly a 35 × 19 cm cylinder, which fits in the bottom of a 40–50 L pack but eats noticeably more space than a down bag of the same rating. A well-made 40°F down quilt can come in at roughly half this weight, which is the real comparison for thru-hikers counting grams.

**Fabric and durability.** Both shell and lining are 20-denier polyamide, with a PFC-free water-repellent finish on the outside. That is thin enough to keep weight down and sturdy enough for ordinary tent-and-hut use. Decathlon says the bag was field-tested for five weeks in spring 2023 by two outside teams who trekked and bivouacked in the French Alps. The 5-year warranty is long for the price.

**Owner feedback.** Decathlon's UK listing shows an average of 4.62 out of 5 across 923 ratings, a strong score for a bag at this price. Reviewers on retail sites mostly praise the value and the compact pack size.

**Features.** A 3/4-length zip makes it easy to get in and out and to vent on warmer nights, and a compression stuff sack is included. There is also an internal security pocket for a phone or keys. Decathlon recommends storing it loose rather than in the stuff sack, so the wadding keeps its loft over seasons. Storage in a pack is fine; long-term compression is not.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low price, £59.99 at Decathlon UK
- Synthetic wadding that keeps insulating when damp
- Comfort 5°C and limit 0°C suit spring through early autumn
- Packs to 9.5 L with a compression sack included
- Machine washable at 30°C, with a 5-year warranty
- Wraparound hood and mummy cut to trim dead air space

**Cons**

- 1,050 g in Large is heavy for a 5°C bag by ultralight standards
- Bulkier in the pack than a comparable down bag
- Not a shoulder-season bag: the 0°C limit leaves little margin for frosty nights
- Requires a decent pad, since the compressed underside gives little insulation
- Synthetic wadding loses loft with long-term compression and repeated washing

## Who Should Buy This

This bag suits new backpackers, hut-to-hut and campsite trekkers, and anyone who wants a reliable summer-to-early-autumn bag without spending on down. It also suits hikers in rainy regions who accept a weight penalty for insulation that does not collapse when wet. Gram-counting thru-hikers, and anyone expecting regular nights near or below freezing, should look at a down quilt or a bag with a lower rating, such as Decathlon's own MT500 -5°C.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 5°C does what a budget three-season bag should: it keeps a sleeper warm in mild conditions, packs reasonably small, and takes abuse without complaint. It loses points for weight and bulk against ultralight options, but at around £60 few bags offer this much. A solid pick for casual and entry-level trekkers; thru-hikers will want to spend more and carry less.
