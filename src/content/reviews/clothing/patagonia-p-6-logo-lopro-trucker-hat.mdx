## Overview

The P-6 Logo LoPro is a six-panel trucker cap with a shallower crown than Patagonia's standard P-6, a recycled-polyester mesh back and a brim made from recycled fishing nets. It suits hikers and town-and-trail wearers who find regular ball caps sit too tall on their heads. Its main trade-off is that the cotton canvas front looks good out of the box but has a reputation for fading and holding sweat stains.

## Performance

**Fit.** The low crown is the reason this hat exists, and it is what owners praise most. Reviewers on REI and Patagonia's own site describe wearing a standard trucker or baseball cap and feeling like a kid in a borrowed hat. They say the LoPro sits closer to the head and doesn't tower off it. Several owners with larger heads say it fits them well too. A snap closure doesn't give the fine adjustment of a strap-and-buckle, but buyers say the range is enough to dial it in. A few report the back panel can look slightly wavy after use.

**Breathability.** The 3.2-oz polyester mesh across the back panels is what makes a trucker cap work in summer. Owners who hike in it call it cool and airy, and one REI reviewer calls the mesh ideal for hot days. The cotton headband is meant to take the edge off sweat against the forehead. Cotton canvas up front is not a performance fabric, though. It absorbs moisture and dries more slowly than an all-synthetic cap. It's fine for a day hike or a section hike with a rinse at the end, but a cap meant for daily long-distance mileage in humid weather would do better in a synthetic running-style fabric.

**Durability and fading.** This is where the reviews split. Many owners report years of regular wear for hiking, running and everyday use, with only modest fading. Others are blunter. One reviewer on Patagonia's site said a black hat taken on the Camino faded to brown within a week. Another REI owner noted noticeable fading after a few months. Runners and lifters report that sweat leaves stains and that they replace the hat every few months. The pattern is consistent: dark colors show sun and salt damage first, and heavy daily sweat shortens the cap's good-looking life. The hat is washable, but owners say a stain that sets in doesn't always lift out.

**Sun coverage.** The brim is short and stiff, and it keeps sun off the face and eyes. The mesh back and open sides leave the neck and ears exposed, as with any trucker cap. Anyone hiking above treeline or in desert sun will want sunscreen or a bucket or sun hat in the pack.

**Weight and packability.** At 85 g, it is not an ultralight piece by the standards of thru-hiker headwear, since a bare-bones running cap can weigh well under that. It's still light enough that nobody will notice it on their head. A travel reviewer calls it packable. The stiff brim and structured front mean it will tolerate being stuffed in a pack, but it takes a little reshaping afterward.

**Materials and ethics.** The brim uses Bureo's NetPlus, recycled fishing nets that Patagonia says are fully traceable. The cotton is organic, the mesh is recycled polyester, and the hat is Fair Trade Certified. Owners cite the recycled content as part of why they bought it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low crown solves the too-tall-cap problem for many wearers
- Recycled polyester mesh back vents well on hot hikes
- 85 g and easy to wear all day
- Recycled-net brim, organic cotton, Fair Trade Certified sewing
- Strong owner satisfaction, with review averages between roughly 4.4 and 4.8 out of 5 across retailers

**Cons**

- Dark colors fade noticeably with sun and washing
- Cotton canvas front stains with sweat and dries slowly
- Offers no sun protection for neck or ears
- Palette is narrow, and owners complain that the colorways have been cut back to a few basics
- About $39 for what is, functionally, a simple cap

## Who Should Buy This

This hat is for day hikers, weekend backpackers and travelers who want a cap that works on the trail and also looks fine in a trailhead brewery. It is a particularly good pick for anyone who finds standard caps too tall or too deep. Thru-hikers who sweat through headwear every day, or who work in intense sun for weeks, should look at an all-synthetic cap or a wide-brim hat instead. The same applies to anyone who wants a cap to stay unfaded for years.

## Verdict

The LoPro is a comfortable, well-ventilated, responsibly made trucker that fits a head shape many caps miss. It isn't built to withstand months of salt, sun and sweat without showing it. Buy it for the fit and the everyday versatility, and treat the fading as the cost of cotton canvas.
