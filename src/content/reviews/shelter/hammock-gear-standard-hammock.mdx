## Overview

The Standard is Hammock Gear's no-frills answer to the question of what a backpacking hammock actually needs: 11 feet of soft nylon, gathered ends, and a ridgeline. There is no bug net, no pockets and no fuss, just a sewn-in-Ohio bed that ships ready to hang. The trade-off is that it is a bare hammock. Bugs, rain and cold air from below are all problems the buyer has to solve separately.

## Performance

**Comfort and lay.** The Standard uses a symmetrical gathered-end cut, which is what allows a sleeper to lie diagonally and get close to flat. Length matters here. Hammock campers widely treat 11 feet as the benchmark for backpacking hammocks, and shorter models tend to bunch the sleeper into a banana curve. At 58 inches wide and 132 inches long, the Standard sits squarely in that benchmark class, and the structural ridgeline sets a repeatable 9 ft of functional length so the sag is the same every night. Hammock Gear describes the fabric as woven to feel soft rather than plasticky, and owners echo that, which matters when a hammock is the only thing between bare skin and the night air.

**Weight and packability.** At 383 g for the body and roughly 434 g once the suspension is counted, the Standard is lighter than the roughly 16 oz that forum veterans cite for a quality netted hammock. That gap is the bug net, and it is the whole point of this model. The double-ended stuff sack lets the hammock be stowed from either end and pulled out ready to hang, and the packed bundle measures about 10 by 5 inches, small enough to ride in a side pocket.

**Build quality.** Rolled hems finish all four edges, and the suspension channels are quadruple stitched. The 40D version is rated to 350 lb and the 70D to 500 lb, giving heavier sleepers or those who carry a lot of gear into the hammock a sturdier option. Owner feedback on the manufacturer's page is strongly positive, with the product holding a 4.86 out of 5 average, and one reviewer who used a pair on a National Forest weekend paired them with Hammock Gear's bottom-entry bug nets, a setup that shows how the Standard is intended to be used as a base layer for a modular system.

**Setup and system compatibility.** The hammock ships with a structural ridgeline, two continuous loops and two carabiners, so it hangs the moment it meets a pair of straps. Tree straps are not included, which means an additional purchase and a few extra grams. Four exterior pack hooks help stabilize an underquilt, and eight small D-rings provide internal attachment points for accessories or external tie-outs. In practice, that means the Standard is a platform. A net, an underquilt and a tarp with doors make it a complete shelter, and the hammock accepts all of them.

**Limitations.** A single layer of nylon provides no insulation. The sleeper's weight compresses any sleeping bag or quilt underneath, so cold-weather use requires an underquilt or a pad. And with no net, summer trips in mosquito country call for a separate bug net or a tarp with extremely good bug protection.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 383 g body, about 434 g with suspension, light for an 11-ft hammock
- Full 11-ft length with a 9-ft ridgeline-set functional length for a flatter lay
- Soft fabric with durable rolled hems and quadruple-stitched channels
- 500 lb rating available on the 70D fabric
- Ships hang-ready with ridgeline, loops and carabiners
- Pack hooks and D-rings support underquilts and accessories
- Made in the USA at $59.99

**Cons**

- No bug net; bug protection is an add-on
- Tree straps not included
- No insulation, so shoulder-season use needs an underquilt or pad
- Weight limit on the 40D fabric is 350 lb, lower than on the 70D

## Who Should Buy This

The Standard suits a hiker building a hammock system piece by piece: someone who already owns a bottom-entry net or a bug-proof tarp, or who camps in low-bug seasons and regions. It also suits first-time hammock campers who want a proper 11-ft gathered-end hammock at a modest price before committing to a full kit. Anyone who wants a single integrated shelter with a net sewn in should look at Hammock Gear's Circadian or a comparable netted model instead, as should hikers who dislike assembling a sleep system from separate parts.

## Verdict

The Standard Hammock covers the essentials of a good hang, with full length, soft fabric and light weight, at a price that undercuts most of the hammock market. It asks the buyer to bring a net, straps and insulation, but for those who can, it is among the most sensible starting points in hammock backpacking.
