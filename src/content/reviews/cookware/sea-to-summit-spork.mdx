## Overview

Sea to Summit's Alpha Light spork is a single piece of hard-anodized 7075-T6 aluminum with a spoon bowl at one end, fork tines at the other, and a mini carabiner threaded through a hole in the handle. It comes in two lengths, a 9 g standard and a 12 g Long, and it is aimed at hikers who want a durable metal utensil at titanium-beating weight and a fraction of titanium's price. The trade-off is a stamped, flat-edged handle that is functional rather than comfortable.

## Performance

Weight is the headline. At 9 g, the standard spork is lighter than most titanium sporks, which commonly land between 10 and 17 g. The Long version gives up only 3 g for an extra 5 cm of handle, and owners who have compared it with a 6.5-inch titanium spork note that it is two inches longer and still lighter, with the carabiner included in the figure. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers weighed the Long at 11.3 g, a hair under the claimed number.

Durability is the usual worry with aluminum at this weight, and the reports are reassuring. One long-term owner described nearly six years of use with the finish still looking close to new. A Trailspace reviewer reported no signs of wear after several seasons on the Long's sibling spoon. The hard anodizing forms a hardened shell around the alloy. That shell is what keeps the aluminum from leaching into food, and it is also why Sea to Summit says to keep the utensil out of the dishwasher, where detergent can attack the finish. Hand-washing on the trail takes seconds, and the smooth surface rinses clean.

Comfort is where reviewers split. Because the utensil is stamped from sheet aluminum, the handle has a ridge down its length and flat side edges. Several reviewers independently flagged that those edges are not the nicest thing to grip through a long dinner. Outdoor Gear Lab called the Long somewhat unnatural in the hand while still scoring it well for strength and ease of cleaning. Nobody calls it a dealbreaker, but it is a utensil that gets the job done and does not invite lingering over a meal.

The working end performs. The tines are sharp and long enough to spear noodles and potatoes, and the bowl is deep enough to be a real spoon. The tines also work as a pot-lid lifter, hooking wire bails and keeping fingers away from hot metal. Unlike a plastic spork, aluminum will not soften against a pot wall while stirring something on the stove.

The mini carabiner is more useful than it looks. Clipped to a pack strap, pot handle or bear canister, the spork stays out of the bottom of the food bag and is less likely to be left behind at a campsite. One Trailspace reviewer wondered whether the clip was big enough to matter, but others call it handy for keeping the utensil findable.

Made in China is the one recurring complaint about provenance, and it is a matter of buyer preference rather than performance.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions share the same material, finish, carabiner and basic shape. What changes is handle length, and with it the situations each one suits.

**Alpha Light (9 g, 16.5 cm).** The standard spork fits the typical solo cook kit: a pot or bowl of reasonable depth, with dinner eaten from it. It is the lighter option and stows easily in a small pot or pocket. It is the right choice for hikers who eat from a mug, bowl or wide pot, or who carry a cold-soak jar with a wide mouth.

**AlphaLight Long (12 g, 21.5 cm).** The extra five centimeters exist for freeze-dried meal pouches and tall, narrow pots such as those in Jetboil-style stove systems. With a standard-length spork in a deep pouch, the knuckles end up in the sauce; the Long keeps the hand clear of the bag. It costs 3 g, and it is the better pick for anyone whose dinners come in a bag. The stove-stirring reach is a bonus for hikers who cook in tall pots.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 9 g (standard) and 12 g (Long) are lighter than most titanium sporks of similar length
- Hard-anodized finish holds up to years of regular use, according to long-term owners
- Anodized shell keeps the aluminum from contacting food, and the utensil conforms to FDA food-safety standards
- Sharp tines double as a pot or lid lifter
- Included mini carabiner makes the spork easy to clip on and hard to lose
- Will not melt or soften against a hot pot, unlike plastic sporks
- Two lengths cover both bowl eaters and pouch eaters

**Cons**

- Flat, ridged handle from stamped construction is not comfortable through a long meal
- Not dishwasher safe, since detergent can damage the anodized finish
- The standard length does not reach the bottom of a tall freeze-dried pouch
- Made in China

## Who Should Buy This

This is the utensil for hikers building an ultralight kit who want a durable metal spork without paying titanium prices. Choose the standard length if dinner happens in a bowl or wide pot, and the Long if most of your meals come in a Mountain House-style pouch or a tall stove pot. Hikers who care most about hand feel through long, slow meals, or who want a spoon with a rounded, ergonomic handle, should look at a molded or machined spoon instead.

## Verdict

The Sea to Summit spork delivers what a 9 or 12 g utensil should: it is light, it lasts, and the carabiner keeps it from wandering. The handle keeps it from being a pleasure to hold, but at this weight and price that is a small complaint. Take the standard for bowls and pots, and the Long for pouches.
