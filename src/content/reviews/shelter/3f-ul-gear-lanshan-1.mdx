## Overview

The Lanshan 1 is a one-person, double-wall trekking pole tent from Chinese maker 3F UL Gear, built from 15D silnylon and sold for a fraction of what comparable shelters cost in the US or Europe. It has become the default answer on r/Ultralight when someone asks for a first trekking pole tent on a budget. The trade-off is a pitch that demands practice and a shelter that asks to be seam-sealed before it sees rain.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** The 760 g figure covers the fly and inner only. Retailers list the complete package, with nine stakes, guylines and stuff sacks, at about 930 g, so hikers who count a full shelter system should plan around the higher number. Even so, that is competitive with far pricier tents, and the packed bundle is roughly the size of a one-litre water bottle, according to UK reviewers.

**Space.** The 230 cm inner length is the tent's quiet advantage. Reviewers say it fits most solo hikers up to about 6'2" (188 cm) with room for a pack at the head, and the 100 cm head-end width is generous for a one-person shelter. The vestibule is large enough to store a pack and boots. Owners describe the interior as cramped only when a storm forces the fly to be pitched low and closed up.

**Weather.** The pyramid-style pitch sheds wind and rain well, and owners who have used it through storms rate it as reliable for the weight. The bathtub floor, listed at 6,000 mm, has kept ground moisture out for hikers on wet UK moorland. Silnylon sags when saturated, which is normal for the fabric. The guylines can be re-tensioned from inside the inner. The "4 season" label is generous. Owners describe it as a three-season shelter that handles wind and some shoulder-season cold, and one says plainly that cold weather is not what it was built for, so a good sleeping bag does the real work.

**Condensation.** Most of the condensation complaints about the Lanshan family come from the single-wall Pro version. A Reddit owner of the Pro recommends the double-wall Lanshan 1 for exactly that reason. The inner-and-fly design here handles moisture better, but pitching the fly edge close to the ground to block wind cuts ventilation and brings condensation back. Leave a gap when the weather allows.

**Pitching.** This is where the tent earns its mixed reviews. A pole height around 120 cm is recommended, and getting a taut, symmetrical pitch takes a few attempts. One owner finds it hard to pitch on rocky ground, where stakes won't bite, and calls the process fiddly even though they like the tent otherwise. The tent is only semi-freestanding, so it needs stakes or rocks. Practice in the backyard before the first trip.

**Prep work.** Seams are not factory-sealed, so a tube of silicone sealer and an afternoon are part of the purchase price. SectionHiker's review of the related Lanshan 1 Pro makes the same point about hand sealing before rainy use. A footprint is recommended to protect the thin floor, and 15D fabric rewards careful site selection.

**Value.** Owners have reported paying roughly $170 for the 1P, and Reddit sentiment is about 75 percent positive, with price and weight as the most-cited reasons. Some owners ended up buying the 2P version after using this one. Comparisons to the Durston X-Mid 1 come up often. Hikers who upgraded say the Western tent is better-finished, but few call the Lanshan a bad shelter.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 760 g for fly and inner, with a complete package around 930 g
- Double-wall design manages condensation better than the single-wall Pro
- 230 cm inner length fits taller hikers
- Roomy vestibule and a bathtub floor that stays dry in heavy rain
- Packs to about the size of a water bottle
- Price far below comparable trekking pole tents

**Cons**

- Seams need sealing before use in rain
- Pitching is fiddly, particularly on rocky or uneven ground
- Not freestanding; needs trekking poles and good stake placement
- Thin 15D fabric calls for a footprint and careful site choice
- Closing the fly low in a storm makes the interior tight and increases condensation
- "4 season" is optimistic for real winter use

## Who Should Buy This

The Lanshan 1 suits solo hikers and bikepackers who already carry trekking poles, want a double-wall tent under 1 kg, and are willing to spend an evening seam-sealing and learning the pitch. Taller hikers get particular value from the long inner. Anyone who wants to pitch in thirty seconds on any surface, who camps on rock more than soil, or who needs a shelter for sustained winter conditions should look at a pole-supported or purpose-built four-season tent.

## Verdict

The Lanshan 1 delivers sub-kilogram, double-wall shelter at a price that makes ultralight camping accessible, and owners report it holding up in real weather. It asks for more effort than premium rivals, and the finishing shows its price, but few tents offer this much room and protection per dollar and per gram.
