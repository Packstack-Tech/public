## Overview

The Inverse is the Polish cottage maker Cumulus's featherweight down jacket: 230 g, 900 fill-power goose down, and a 10-denier Pertex Quantum shell. It is built for spring and autumn hiking, cycling and fast-and-light trips, where a jacket spends most of the day at the bottom of the pack and comes out for camp and cold mornings. The trade-off is the usual one for this weight class: very good warmth per gram, paid for with a delicate shell and a fit that is hard to judge from overseas.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Down makes up roughly a third of the jacket's total weight (80 g of 230 g), which is a generous ratio for a garment this light. Retailers that stock it position it as a three-season layer that performs best above about 5°C. That matches the sewn-through construction, which leaves seams where cold can creep in once temperatures sink toward freezing. Owners of other Cumulus down pieces often say the brand's garments run warmer than similarly weighted jackets from Patagonia and Arc'teryx, and the Inverse's fill weight suggests the same generosity here. In practice, it is a camp-and-breaks jacket: worn under a shell on a cold dawn start, or over a base layer while dinner cooks.

**Weather resistance.** The Pertex Quantum shell is wind-resistant and carries a DWR finish, and the down itself is hydrophobic. Together they handle mist, condensation and a brief shower. They do not make the Inverse a rain jacket. Anyone expecting sustained wet weather should plan on a shell over it, as with any down garment.

**Packability.** This is where the Inverse earns its place. At 230 g with a thin face fabric, it compresses into the included bag to roughly the size of a large fruit, and it takes up little room in a pack where a fleece would not. A European retailer notes that because a down jacket sits stowed on warm-weather hikes, a lighter and less bulky model like this makes more sense than a heavier one.

**Durability and build.** A 28 g/m² face fabric is a fabric to treat gently. Brambles, rough rock and pack-strap rub are the usual enemies, and the DWR will need refreshing over time. Owner reports on Cumulus's workmanship are positive: one Inverse buyer described excellent quality but found a loose seam in a sleeve baffle, which the company collected, repaired and returned under warranty at no charge. The same reviewer praised the communication throughout. A Norwegian owner selling a lightly used Inverse compared the build quality to Norrøna and Arc'teryx. Reviewers of the brand's sibling jacket, the Incredilite Endurance, noted that zipper pull tabs tore after a season, an easy fix but a small sign of where weight was cut.

**Features.** The 2-way YKK main zipper lets the hem be opened for sitting or for hip-belt clearance, though one owner of a Cumulus down jacket with the same arrangement said they would rather not have the lower slider. Two hand pockets handle storage. The jacket has no chest pocket and no extras, which is consistent with its weight target.

**Fit and sizing.** This is the sore spot. Cumulus's customer service gives sizing advice, and the Inverse buyer who used it was happy with the fit. Others have been less lucky: a Trustpilot reviewer who normally wears an XL said the sizing ran differently from what they expected, and that returns require shipping to Poland. A Trailspace reviewer made the same complaint about another Cumulus jacket, saying return shipping would have cost over $200. A Norwegian seller describes the medium as running small. Buyers outside Europe should measure carefully and ask Cumulus for a size recommendation before ordering, because an exchange is expensive.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 230 g with 80 g of 900 fill-power down gives high warmth per gram
- Hydrophobic down plus a DWR Pertex Quantum shell handles damp mornings and light weather
- Packs small, and takes little pack space between uses
- 2-way YKK zipper adds ventilation and hip-belt access
- Cumulus handles warranty repairs promptly, according to owners
- Made in Poland; down is ethically sourced

**Cons**

- 10-denier shell needs careful handling around rough surfaces and brush
- Sewn-through baffles limit warmth near freezing
- Sizing runs small for some wearers, and returns from outside Europe are costly
- No chest pocket
- Not a rain jacket

## Who Should Buy This

The Inverse suits ultralight hikers, cyclists and fast packers who want a warm layer for spring and autumn trips and who are willing to handle their gear with some care. It works best for shoulder-season thru-hikes and summer alpine trips, where it spends most of its time in the pack. Buyers who need a sturdier shell, a jacket for sub-freezing camps, or an easy return policy should look elsewhere: a thicker, baffled jacket with a more robust face fabric will serve them better.

## Verdict

The Inverse delivers what its numbers promise: a warm, compact, well-made 900-fill jacket at 230 g, backed by a brand that repairs its work. The delicate shell and the difficulty of exchanging a wrong size keep it from a higher score. Buy it for three-season ultralight trips, and get the size right before checking out.
