## Overview

This is Neutrogena's chemical sunscreen stick aimed at the face — a twist-up SPF 60 stick that's meant to go on clear, dry fast, and skip the greasy-lotion feel. At 13.3 g (0.47 oz), it's small enough that I've thrown it into a hip belt pocket or a running vest without noticing the weight, and that's really the whole pitch: convenience for the parts of your body (face, ears, nose) that lotion tends to smear into your eyes.

## Performance

**Sun protection.** This is a chemical (organic filter) formula — avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene — not a mineral zinc/titanium stick, so it needs about 15 minutes to bind to skin before it starts working. <cite index="3-3,3-4">Apply liberally and evenly 15 minutes before sun exposure, and ensure complete coverage to the area above the lip, nose and tops of ears.</cite> On trail, that means I apply at the trailhead, not once I'm already squinting into the sun. Reapplication is straightforward: <cite index="3-5,3-6,3-7">reapply after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating, immediately after towel drying, or at least every 2 hours.</cite> For a full day of hiking with sweat and pack-strap friction, that 80-minute water-resistance window is realistic to plan around rather than a "set it and forget it" number.

**Application and feel.** The stick lives up to the "sheer" name in the sense that it goes on clear rather than leaving a white cast — a real advantage over mineral sticks if you've ever had to explain to hiking partners why your nose looks like a ghost's. One long-time user summed up the appeal simply: it <cite index="14-9">"excellent, easy to apply sunscreen stick."</cite> It's genuinely convenient to reapply one-handed at a summit break without digging out a bottle and getting lotion on your hands (and then your map, phone, or snack).

The catch is coverage area. Because the stick face is narrow, one pass doesn't cover much skin, and more than one reviewer noted you'll need <cite index="6-4">multiple applications for full facial protection</cite> to get adequate coverage across a whole face rather than just a strip. On a thru-hike, budget a few extra swipes per application, not just one quick pass.

**Skin feel and sensitivity.** This isn't a heavy, creamy sunscreen — Neutrogena's positioning and most user feedback point to a lightweight, non-greasy finish, which matters if you're already dealing with sweat and grime on your face by hour four. It's also formulated for sensitive skin: it's fragrance-free, oxybenzone-free, and paraben-free, which is worth knowing if you've reacted to other chemical sunscreens before — though if you have specifically reactive or acne-prone skin, patch-test before committing a whole trip to it, since chemical filters (as opposed to mineral ones) are more likely culprits for irritation in sensitive users.

**Longevity in the tube.** At under half an ounce, this stick is genuinely small. If you're using it daily as your only face sunscreen on a long trip, expect to burn through it faster than you'd think — this is a supplement to a bigger bottle of body sunscreen, not a standalone solution for multi-week trips.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tiny and pocketable — easy to stash in a hip belt or running vest pocket
- Goes on clear with no white cast, unlike most mineral stick alternatives
- One-handed reapplication without getting sunscreen all over your hands
- Fragrance-free, oxybenzone-free, paraben-free, and vegan
- SPF 60 gives a solid margin of error if you miss a reapplication window

**Cons**

- Narrow application surface means more swipes needed for full facial coverage
- Chemical (not mineral) formula — needs 15 minutes before sun exposure to be effective, and isn't ideal for anyone sensitive to chemical UV filters
- Small size (0.47 oz) means it won't last as your only sunscreen on longer trips
- Face-only sizing; you'll still need a separate sunscreen for arms, neck, and legs

## Who Should Buy This

This is a smart add-on for anyone who already carries a bottle of body sunscreen but wants a faster, mess-free way to reapply on the face specifically — day hikers, trail runners, and thru-hikers who stop for quick breaks and don't want to smear lotion near their eyes. It's less suited to backpackers looking for a single all-in-one sunscreen for their whole body, since the stick format and small size make it inefficient for large surface areas.

## Verdict

The Neutrogena Ultra Sheer Face Stick does exactly what it promises: fast, clear, mess-free SPF 60 protection for your face, sized to disappear into a pack pocket. It won't replace your main sunscreen for arms and legs, and you'll be reapplying more often than the stick's small swipe area suggests, but as a trail-side face-and-ears touch-up tool, it earns its spot. I'd rate it a 7/10 — solid and convenient, docked a couple points for the coverage-per-swipe limitation and the fact that it's a one-trick, face-only product.
