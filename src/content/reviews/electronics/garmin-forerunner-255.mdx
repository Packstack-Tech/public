## Overview

The Forerunner 255 is Garmin's mid-range running watch with multiband GPS, a 1.3-inch always-readable display and a battery that outlasts most weekend trips. It was built for runners and triathletes, but its 49 g weight and week-plus endurance make it a credible trail companion for hikers who want a watch rather than a phone for tracking. The trade-off: it has no onboard maps, so it records where you've been but can't show you where the trail goes.

## Performance

**GPS accuracy.** Multiband was the headline addition when the 255 launched in 2022, and it remains the watch's strongest argument. DC Rainmaker noted at launch that no other company sold a cheaper watch with a dual-frequency chipset. Tom's Guide ran the 255 on one wrist and the pricier Forerunner 955 on the other and found the recorded tracks almost identical. For hikers, the payoff comes in canyons, under dense canopy and along cliff bands, where single-band GPS tends to wander. Garmin defaults the watch to All Systems and lets users pick GPS-only or All + Multi-Band per activity profile, so the accuracy-versus-battery decision can be made hike by hike.

**Battery.** The numbers are generous for a watch this light. Garmin rates it at up to 14 days as a smartwatch and 30 hours in GPS-only mode, double the 245's smartwatch figure. Turning on multiband with all systems drops that to about 16 hours, which Android Central flagged as a real cost. Owners on Reddit mostly describe a week of battery with regular daily runs. The practical read for backpackers: on a weekend trip, record every hike in multiband and the watch shrugs it off. On a multi-week thru-hike, the watch needs a charging plan, and GPS-only mode is the setting that stretches a power bank furthest.

**Navigation, or the lack of it.** This is the sharpest limit for the trail crowd. The 255 has no map display, so a route loaded from Garmin Connect appears as a line to follow, not as terrain with trails, water sources or junctions. A barometric altimeter and compass help with elevation and heading, and the always-on MIP display is readable in direct sun. But hikers who want to glance at a wrist and see the trail will want a Fenix, Enduro or Instinct-class watch, or just a phone running a mapping app.

**Heart rate and training metrics.** The 255 brings HRV status, a morning report, training status, recovery time and daily suggested workouts, plus full triathlon and multisport profiles, all things the 245 lacked. Reddit's running communities rank the series near the top of fitness trackers on running performance and value. The recurring complaint is the Elevate wrist sensor, which owners say can read high or erratic during rides and other irregular-cadence efforts. Pairing a chest strap or HRM-Pro over ANT+ or Bluetooth solves that for anyone who cares about the number.

**Comfort and build.** At 49 g, the watch is light enough to sleep in, and the plastic case and 22 mm standard band make it easy to swap straps. 5 ATM water resistance covers rain, river crossings and swimming. TechRadar's verdict on the predecessor still applies: it is comfortable and unobtrusive, though it doesn't feel premium. That is the price of the weight.

**Price.** It launched at $349, with the Music edition at $399. Retailers in 2026 list it well below that, with new units from roughly €249 in Europe, which sharpens the value case against newer AMOLED models that sacrifice battery life.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Multiband GPS holds up against watches that cost much more
- 14-day smartwatch battery and 30 hours of GPS-only tracking
- 49 g and a slim 12.9 mm case that is easy to forget on the wrist
- Sunlight-readable, always-on display
- Full multisport profiles, HRV status and daily workout suggestions
- Works with ANT+ sensors, including chest straps and power meters

**Cons**

- No onboard maps, so navigation is breadcrumb-only
- Multiband GPS cuts battery to about 16 hours
- Wrist heart rate is erratic for some owners during non-running activity
- Plastic build feels budget next to metal-bezel Garmins
- Music storage costs extra, and the watch with music and GPS lasts only about 6.5 hours
- No solar charging, unlike the 955 Solar

## Who Should Buy This

The 255 suits runners, trail runners and fast-and-light hikers who want accurate tracks, long battery and training analytics in a watch that doesn't feel like jewelry on a long day. It is also a good fit for anyone who carries a phone or paper map for navigation and just wants a trustworthy record of distance, pace and elevation. Thru-hikers who want wrist-based maps, weeks of GPS tracking between charges, or solar top-ups should look at Garmin's Enduro or Fenix lines instead. Those who prefer an AMOLED screen should consider the Forerunner 265, which trades about three hours of multiband endurance for a brighter display.

## Verdict

The Forerunner 255 does the core job, tracking where you went with precision and for a long time, at 49 g and a modest price. It stops short of being a backcountry navigation tool, and its wrist heart rate is not its best feature. For runners and light-footed hikers, it remains one of the better-value GPS watches around.
