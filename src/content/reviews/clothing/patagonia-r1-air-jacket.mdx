## Overview

The R1 Air Jacket is Patagonia's full-zip, hoodless take on its best-known active fleece. The fabric is a waffled, zigzag-knit polyester built from hollow-core yarns. It's meant for people who move hard in the cold and don't want to cook. The trade-off is weight and wind: at 341 g it is no featherweight, and the open knit lets a breeze straight through.

## Performance

**Breathability and warmth.** The R1 Air's party trick is its fabric. The hollow yarns trap air for loft. The gaps between the zigzag ridges let heat and moisture escape once the pace picks up. Testers who have worn the hooded version, which uses the same cloth, are consistent on the result. Treeline Review called it one of the most breathable fleeces available that still insulates well in cold weather. Chris Townsend, who has logged decades of long-distance walking, found it very warm for its weight and more breathable than other fleeces he had tried. Owners describe a layer that spills heat when they're climbing, then holds enough warmth when they stop. A user on REI said it keeps a steady temperature on jogs and walks, with no shivering at the first break.

**Limits.** This is a mid-layer, and it behaves like one. The open weave is not windproof. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found that wind cuts through the thin zones of the knit, which makes the fabric better suited to bodies in motion than to standing around. Outdoor Gear Lab also noted that on high-heart-rate efforts, such as a steep climb or a run, the R1 Air can run warm. That makes sense for a 162 g/m² fleece, and a shell or wind layer over the top is the standard answer. Townsend wore the hooded version under waterproof, windproof and insulated jackets and reported that it worked under all of them.

**Comfort and fit.** The cut is slim and closer than the previous generation. Reviewers praise the stretch, which comes from the knit structure alone, with no elastane in the cloth. Fitted layers move well under a pack, and the offset shoulder seams keep seams off the strap contact points. The woven binding at the cuffs and back hem is quick-drying and has no drawcords to flap. Better Trail's testers found the latest hooded version a touch roomy near the hem, so sizing matters. Outdoor Gear Lab noted slight bunching in some sizes.

**Why no hood.** The hoodless full-zip fills a gap that owners had asked about. One Patagonia customer said they were disappointed when the R1 Air line first launched without a hoodless full-zip. The jacket is easier to layer under a shell with its own hood or a helmet, since there's no balaclava-style hood bunching at the neck. It also reads as normal clothing in town. The cost is less warmth at the head and neck: the hoody's close-fitting hood is one of the features reviewers like most.

**Pockets.** Pockets have been a recurring complaint across the R1 Air line. Hand pockets sit low enough that a pack hipbelt can cover them, and earlier chest pockets were too small for a phone. Patagonia enlarged the chest pocket on the latest generation, which Outdoor Gear Lab called a significant fix. Even so, expect to carry keys, a card and maybe a bar there, with the hand pockets as the fallback.

**Durability.** The fuzzy zigzag face softens and loses its loft-fresh feel with wear, according to Better Trail's testers. Some owners report pilling after a few months of light use, a criticism that shows up in critical REI reviews of the hooded women's version. Treat it as a layer for hiking, skiing and camp, not one to pair with abrasive pack straps for thousands of miles without expecting some fuzz.

**Weight and packability.** At 341 g, this is a midweight fleece. It is compressible for a fleece and the knit does stuff down, but it won't disappear into a pocket the way a synthetic puffy will. Outdoor Gear Lab called the hooded version a midweight layer and said weight-conscious hikers may want to look elsewhere. The same applies here.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Breathability that is hard to beat in a fleece
- Warm for its weight, and it vents quickly when effort rises
- Stretchy, slim cut with seams that stay clear of pack straps
- Hoodless full-zip makes layering under shells and helmets simple
- Fair Trade Certified construction, bluesign-approved recycled polyester fabric

**Cons**

- 341 g is heavy for ultralight kits
- Open knit offers little wind protection
- Hand pockets can sit under a hipbelt; chest pocket is modest
- Fuzzy face loses softness and can pill with wear
- No hood, so less warmth for the head and neck than the hoody

## Who Should Buy This

The R1 Air Jacket suits hikers, climbers and ski tourers who sweat through cold starts and want one layer to wear from the trailhead to the summit. It also suits shoulder-season thru-hikers who want a fleece they can wear all day and also use at camp or in town. It's slim enough to layer under a shell, and breathable enough that it doesn't need to come off every time the trail turns uphill. Gram-counters carrying a single warm layer, or anyone who wants wind resistance without a second jacket, should look at a synthetic puffy or a wind-resistant hybrid instead.

## Verdict

The R1 Air Jacket is among the best active fleeces available. It manages warmth, venting and stretch better than most of its competition, and the hoodless full-zip makes it easy to layer. It carries a weight penalty for ultralight kits, and the fabric trades wind protection for breathability. For cold-weather movement under a shell, it earns a strong recommendation.
