## Overview

The Fjällräven Rain Cover 40-55 is a conventional external pack cover designed to slip over any 40-55L backpack and shed rain on the trail. It's built from 70D PU-treated nylon with taped seams, comes exclusively in Fjällräven's signature UN Blue safety color, and ships without any PFAS/PFC chemical treatments. This is a mainstream hiking accessory — not an ultralight piece — aimed at trekkers and weekend hikers who want reliable protection for their pack exterior without fussing with an internal liner.

## Performance

### Weather Protection

The combination of PU-coated nylon and taped seams gives this cover solid resistance in moderate rain — you're not going to see water wicking through the fabric or leaking at stress points in a typical shower. That said, it's worth being honest about what any rain cover can and can't do: in a sustained downpour, water inevitably finds its way down the back panel, hip belt, and shoulder straps, which remain fully exposed. As one experienced hiker on Backpacking Light noted after a torrential multi-hour storm, a rain cover alone left gear soaked. For critical items — down quilts, electronics — an internal liner or dry bags remain the smart belt-and-suspenders approach regardless of which cover you use.

### Fit and Adjustment

The cover uses a dual compression system: elastic at the lower hem cinches it snugly around the base of the pack, while a drawcord at the top handles fine-tuning. Fjällräven describes the cut as "updated and spacious," which in practice means it accommodates the lumpy, gear-festooned silhouette of a pack carrying trekking pole tips, a rolled foam pad, or a wet shelter lashed to the outside. If you regularly pile on the external cargo, Fjällräven actually recommends sizing up to the 60-75L version — worth keeping in mind before you buy.

### The Guyline Hook

This is a genuinely useful detail that most budget covers skip: the drawcord includes a hook that lets you anchor it to a pack attachment point, effectively converting the drawcord into a guyline. In high winds, a loose rain cover flapping or peeling off is a real annoyance. This feature addresses that without adding meaningful weight or complexity.

### Storage and Accessibility

The cover packs into an integrated velcro pouch that lives inside your pack. That means it's accessible whenever you need it without digging through a stuff sack or hunting for it in a hip belt pocket. The velcro closure is quick and one-handed — a small thing, but convenient in the first drops of a fast-moving storm.

### Visibility

The UN Blue color is a deliberate safety choice — it's the same high-visibility blue used by UN operations and emergency services, and it stands out clearly in fog, rain, and low light. The reflective logo adds a bit more conspicuity after dark. If you're hiking near roads, in mountain terrain with vehicle traffic, or in conditions where search-and-rescue visibility matters, this is a meaningful feature. If you prefer to blend into the landscape, this cover isn't for you.

### Weight

At 135g (4.8 oz), this is a functional but not featherlight piece. A Sea to Summit Ultra-Sil Pack Cover in a comparable size runs around 60-65g; a Gossamer Gear clear pack liner checks in around 34g and arguably does a better job protecting your actual gear (though nothing for the pack shell itself). For a strict gram-counter, the weight penalty here is real. For a casual trekker or someone pairing this with a heavier traditional pack, it's unlikely to register.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Taped seams and PU coating provide solid rain resistance in typical hiking conditions
- Guyline hook is a smart detail for windy, stormy weather
- Spacious cut fits packs with external gear attached
- Integrated velcro storage pouch keeps it accessible without unpacking
- Dual compression (elastic + drawcord) works across the full 40-55L size range
- No PFAS/PFC chemical treatments — a genuine environmental improvement
- UN Blue + reflective logo offers real safety visibility in poor conditions

**Cons**

- 135g is on the heavier side — ultralight silnylon or DCF options exist at roughly half the weight
- Only available in UN Blue; no color choice
- Like all external covers, it leaves the back panel, shoulder straps, and hip belt exposed to water
- Offers zero protection from below — setting a wet pack on wet ground bypasses the cover entirely
- In very heavy sustained rain, water can work under the lower hem

## Who Should Buy This

This cover makes the most sense for hikers carrying traditional or midweight packs — a Fjällräven Abisko, a Gregory, an Osprey — who encounter intermittent alpine or European weather and want a reliable, ready-to-deploy option without overthinking it. It's also a good call for anyone prioritizing safety visibility or hiking in conditions where being seen matters. If you're building a true ultralight kit or hiking in genuinely sustained downpours, pair it with internal dry bags or replace it outright with a dedicated pack liner.

## Verdict

The Fjällräven Rain Cover 40-55 is a well-executed version of a well-understood product. The guyline hook, roomy cut, and PFAS-free construction show that real thought went into it. But 135g is a tangible weight cost for something that only protects the pack's exterior, and the ultralight community has largely moved on from external covers for good reason. At a 7/10, this is a dependable choice for most hikers — just go in with clear eyes about what a rain cover can and can't do.
