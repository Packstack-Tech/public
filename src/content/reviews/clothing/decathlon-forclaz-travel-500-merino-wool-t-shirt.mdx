## Overview

The Forclaz Travel 500 is Decathlon's short-sleeve merino tee for people who want wool comfort on the trail and in town without paying Icebreaker prices. It blends 70% merino with 30% acrylic in a 155 g/m² knit, which makes it a true warm-weather shirt rather than a shoulder-season layer. The trade-off is that the synthetic content dilutes the wool's famous stink resistance and leaves the fabric less hardy under pack straps.

## Performance

**Comfort.** At 19.5 microns, the merino sits at the fine end of what budget wool shirts usually offer, and owners describe the Travel 500 as very pleasant against the skin. One long-term comparison against an Icebreaker tee judged the Decathlon's hand softer. Itch is not a recurring complaint. Wearers also like the casual look: the plain colorways pass for an ordinary T-shirt, which matters on a trip that includes restaurants and trains.

**Weight and temperature.** At 148 g in a size L, the shirt is a reasonable middle-weight choice for a wool tee. The 155 g/m² fabric is lighter than the 190 g/m² cloth Decathlon uses across its MT500 and Trek 500 long-sleeve line, so it breathes better in warm weather. Owners report wearing it on a trek around Menorca and on a trip to Ireland, which covers roughly the temperature range it suits: cool mornings through warm afternoons. In real heat, a synthetic tee will still dry faster and feel less clingy.

**Odor control.** The shirt carries an ISO 17299-3 lab test claim for limiting perspiration odor, and many owners confirm that it stays fresh through a day or two of hard use. One owner who bought two shirts for a 14-day trek in 25–28 °C weather found each lasted about three days before the smell set in, after which it was simply a sweaty shirt. That is a respectable result for a blend, but it is not the week-long immunity of 100% merino. An older comparison of a long-sleeve Travel 500 against Decathlon's 100% merino Trek 500 found the Trek 500 clearly better on smell. Hikers planning to wear a single shirt for many days between washes should expect the 30% acrylic to show.

**Durability.** This is the weak point in the owner reports. One buyer said the fabric began to fray noticeably after a week of wear with little or no pack. Another long-term wearer reported no holes. The usual merino rules apply, with a heavier hand: keep the shirt away from abrasive pack straps and rough brush, and wash it gently.

**Fit.** The cut is snug. Owners advise sizing up, and one who normally wears a small found a medium fit better. The striped versions are reported to be somewhat see-through, so the solid colors are the safer pick.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft, fine 19.5-micron merino that owners say is comfortable all day
- 148 g in size L, with a fabric weight that suits three-season hiking
- Lab-tested odor control (ISO 17299-3) that holds up for a few days of wear
- Understated styling works on the trail and in town
- Acrylic blend lowers the cost of a merino shirt

**Cons**

- Odor resistance fades sooner than in 100% merino shirts
- Some owners report fraying early, especially with pack contact
- Runs snug; sizing up is common advice
- Light-colored and striped versions can be translucent
- Acrylic content means less natural-fiber purity than premium wool tees

## Who Should Buy This

The Travel 500 suits the hiker or traveler who wants a soft wool-feeling tee for weekend trips, hut-to-hut routes, and carry-on-only travel, and who washes every two to three days. It makes a good second shirt for camp and town. Thru-hikers who put heavy miles on a single shirt under a loaded pack, or anyone who wants a week of wear between washes, should look at Decathlon's 100% merino Trek 500 line or a premium wool tee with a stronger track record under pack straps.

## Verdict

The Forclaz Travel 500 delivers the comfort and short-term odor control that bring most people to merino, in a 148 g shirt that is easy to wear in a city or on a trail. Its acrylic blend and reports of early fraying keep it from being a long-haul workhorse. Buy it as a low-cost travel and weekend-hiking tee, and size up.
