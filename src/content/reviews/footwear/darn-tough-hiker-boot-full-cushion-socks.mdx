## Overview

The Darn Tough Hiker Boot Full Cushion (style 1405) is a midweight Merino boot sock with terry loops running from cuff to toe. It has been a fixture on long trails for years, with the maker pointing to its record on both the Pacific Crest Trail and the Appalachian Trail. It is built for hikers in boots under heavy loads, and the trade-off is plain: more padding and warmth than a minimalist trail-runner sock, at a cost of bulk, drying time and warm-weather comfort.

## Performance

**Cushioning.** This sock's whole identity is the terry loop. Unlike many hiking socks that pad only the heel, ball and shin, the 1405 carries the loops throughout the sock. Darn Tough describes the result as warmth, rebound and comfort, and owners' accounts line up with that. One buyer with arthritic feet credits the full-cushion hikers with real relief on long walks. Others use them in steel-toe work boots for the added padding. For a hiker carrying a heavy pack down a long descent, that continuous pad is the point of the sock.

**Fit and stay-put.** Darn Tough sells the fit as no slipping, no bunching and no blisters, and the toe seams are closed with its True Seamless Toe construction to leave nothing to rub. Owners report that the cuff holds its position and does not creep down the calf. The 2% spandex in the blend helps. A reinforced Achilles section sits where boot collars tend to chew through socks.

**Height.** At about 11 inches heel to cuff, the sock should rise roughly 5 inches above a standard hiking boot. That means mid-calf coverage, which helps against collar rub, trail debris and brush. In a low-cut trail runner, it is overkill. Owners who want a Darn Tough for shoes tend to move to the shorter micro crew or quarter-cushion versions, and one reviewer keeps the full-cushion boot sock for winter and wears a lower-profile sibling the rest of the year.

**Moisture and odor.** Merino does the work here. Darn Tough says the wool moves moisture to keep feet fresh and dry across conditions, and reviewers back up the odor claim: several-day stretches of hiking without the sock becoming unpleasant. Heavy terry will hold more water than a thin knit, so on a wet crossing or a soaked boot, a full-cushion sock is slower to dry than a lightweight one. That is the price of the padding, and it is worth knowing before a muddy week in the Cascades.

**Durability.** This is where Darn Tough earns its reputation. Owners report pairs lasting years, with one buyer describing a first pair still in rotation five years on. The brand also backs every pair with an unconditional lifetime guarantee, and one customer described returning a worn six-year-old pair and receiving full-cushion boot socks in exchange. For a thru-hiker, that can turn a $30 sock into a cheap one over time.

**Care.** Wash cold on a gentle cycle, inside out, and tumble dry low or hang dry. Owners say the socks survive the washer and dryer without noticeable shrinkage, which cannot be said of all wool.

**Weight and warmth.** At 65.2 g, the sock sits in the middle of the weight spectrum, well above a featherweight no-show but not a burden next to the boots it goes in. Midweight Merino is all-season in the manufacturer's framing, but the full terry makes it warmer than the label suggests. Hikers who run hot in summer, or who wear breathable trail runners, will find it more sock than they need.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Continuous terry cushioning from cuff to toe
- Strong durability record from long-distance hikers
- Lifetime guarantee, no questions asked
- Seamless toe and reinforced Achilles reduce hot spots
- Resists odor over multiday trips
- Holds its shape and stays up in the wash and on the trail
- Made in the USA

**Cons**

- About $30 a pair, a real outlay for a sock
- Thick terry dries slowly after soaking
- Warm for summer and for hikers in ventilated trail runners
- Boot height is excessive with low-cut shoes
- More bulk than a lightweight sock, which matters in snug footwear

## Who Should Buy This

This sock suits boot-wearing backpackers, shoulder-season and winter hikers, and anyone carrying a heavy pack over rough ground who wants padding underfoot at every point. It also suits those who prefer to buy once and rely on the guarantee. Hikers in trail runners, warm-weather hikers, and ultralighters counting every gram on low-volume shoes should look instead at the lighter, shorter cushion levels in Darn Tough's own range.

## Verdict

The 1405 is a sturdy, comfortable, long-lived boot sock that justifies its price over a few seasons rather than a few miles. It is not the choice for hot, fast, shoe-based hiking, but for boots and heavy loads it is among the safest bets on the trail. Recommended.
