## Overview

Jimmies began life in a Calgary gym, as an answer to the hair and bandages on locker-room shower floors. Hikers found them for a different reason. The EVA foam sandals fold in half, weigh 155–230 g per pair depending on size, and come with a mesh bag and carabiner for clipping to a pack. The trade-off is the usual one for foam camp shoes: low weight and low cost, with durability and support that are only adequate.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** The medium weighs 200 g, just over 7 oz, and that figure includes the mesh bag. Adventure Alan's 2026 round-up of ultralight backpacking sandals lists the Jimmies at 7.1 oz and calls them light but not ultralight. That is fair. Dedicated ultralight camp shoes from cottage makers weigh less, but they rarely fold into a bag and clip to a pack as neatly as these do. The sandals fold in half rather than rolling or compressing. Owners describe them as easy to toss in a suitcase or pack as a just-in-case item.

**Comfort and fit.** The sole is soft, cushy foam, held on by three straps: front, middle and heel. Buyers say the sizing runs true. One owner with narrow feet reported a good fit, and another said the sandals were comfortable even over long walks. Cushion is the strength here. The sole has no rigid shank, so arch support is minimal, as with any foam sandal. For an evening at camp, that is fine.

**Traction and water.** The foam is waterproof and textured for wet floors, which suits the original shower-room job. The sandals use no Vibram or other rubber outsole, a gap that a commenter on Adventure Alan's guide pointed out. Wet rock, muddy banks and loose scree are where foam tread gives out first. One buyer in Scotland planned to wear a pair for short river crossings. That is a sensible limit: wading through shallow water, yes, and anything rougher, no.

**Durability.** Owners say the sandals are robust for their weight. Another called them a bit flimsy for daily use. The accessories take more criticism. A reviewer on a retail listing said the carabiner came loose almost as soon as it was handled and the ribbon on the mesh bag began to fray. That reviewer then worried about the quality of the straps. Another buyer received a pair with a small factory flaw and had a replacement sent right away. Ratings on third-party listings are mixed, ranging from the mid-3s to about 4.5 stars. The foam itself draws more praise than the extras do.

**Camp use.** Thru-hikers who want something to change into after miles in trail runners have said that these are the best camp shoe they have tried, mostly on value, weight and packability. Pairing the sandals with waterproof socks for cold evenings is one buyer's plan. Because the shoes are cheap, light and compact, they work as a camp shoe, a hostel shower shoe and a gym-bag shoe all in one.

## Choosing a Version

Five sizes are offered, XS through XL, and the weight rises steadily with size: 155 g, 175 g, 200 g, 215 g and 230 g. The spread between the smallest and largest is 75 g, so size choice hardly affects pack weight. Fit is what matters. One commenter put the medium at men's 9–10.5. Buyers between sizes should think about the heel strap, which keeps the sandal on the foot and has to be snug enough to hold when walking downhill. XS and S suit small feet and smaller frames, M and L cover most adult feet, and XL is for large feet. The company ships to Canada, the US and abroad. One European buyer was charged a customs fee of about €9, so budget for that when ordering outside North America.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Folds in half and ships with a mesh bag and carabiner
- 155–230 g per pair, with the medium at 200 g including its bag
- Soft, cushioned foam sole with a heel strap
- Waterproof, so it works in showers and shallow crossings
- Low price, especially at the $34.49 sale price
- Owners report true-to-size fit

**Cons**

- Foam tread with no rubber outsole, so wet rock and mud are a gamble
- Carabiner and carry bag have drawn complaints about quality
- Heavier than the lightest dedicated camp shoes
- Some owners call them flimsy for daily wear
- Foam is cushioned but offers little structure for long walking
- Import fees can add to the price outside North America

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want a cheap camp shoe that folds flat should buy these, as should hostel and gym travelers who want a shower sandal that fits in a pocket. They suit warm-weather trips, section hikes and thru-hikers who treat camp shoes as a morale item. Hikers who plan to walk in their sandals, who cross rocky or fast water, or who count every ounce should look at a sandal with a rubber outsole or a stripped-down ultralight camp shoe.

## Verdict

Jimmies do what they are sold to do: fold up small, weigh about 7 oz in a medium, and keep feet off dirty floors and cold ground at camp. Treat the carabiner and bag as extras, and keep the sandals out of rough terrain. At the sale price, they are an easy choice for camp.
