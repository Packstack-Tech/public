## Overview

The Back Flush Syringe is a 60 ml plastic plunger syringe, made in the USA by BD (Becton Dickinson), that forces clean water backward through a Sawyer hollow-fiber filter to clear out trapped sediment. It is the replacement for the cleaning plunger that ships in Squeeze, Mini and Micro kits, and the part most likely to go missing between trips. The trade-off is plain: at 31 g it is dead weight on trail for most hikers, but it is the tool that keeps a Sawyer's flow rate from sliding into misery.

## Performance

Sawyer's hollow-fiber filters do not clog dramatically. They fade. Flow slows over weeks of silty water until squeezing a pouch feels like wringing out a wet towel, and the fix is a backflush: fill the syringe with clean water, press the tip against the filter's clean-water outlet, and push the water through in reverse. Sawyer's own guidance stresses force. Its hiker tips say a good backflush can restore up to 98 percent of the original flow rate, and that the key is to be forceful.

That is where a proper syringe earns its keep. Backflushing guides from Backcountry Banter and others make the same point: sustained pressure matters, because gentle flushes tend to carve paths of least resistance through the fibers and leave the rest of the filter dirty. A rigid 60 ml barrel with a medical-grade plunger delivers that pressure in a way a squeezed soda bottle does not. Hikers who have used both describe the syringe method as the more reliable way to bring a sluggish filter back.

The technique is not fussy. Owners and Backpacking Light forum members describe it as pressing the syringe onto the filter nipple and pushing, with a little water dribbling around the connection as the only complaint. Repeating the process several times, alternating with flow in the normal direction, until the water runs clear is standard advice. Pushing a vinegar solution through the filter is the usual fix for mineral buildup in hard-water areas, and a diluted bleach flush is Sawyer's recommended step before long-term storage. The syringe handles both. It is also useful for blowing air through a filter afterward to clear remaining droplets.

The BD pedigree is a quiet selling point. The part is a standard medical-style syringe rather than a molded accessory, and the barrel markings, plunger seal and tip are what a lab would expect. There is no moving part to fail beyond the rubber plunger tip.

Weight is where trail-minded buyers will hesitate. At 31 g, it is heavier than the Mini filter's 2 oz (about 57 g) sounds like it should be saved on. Plenty of thru-hikers leave it at home and carry the lighter Sawyer cleaning coupling instead, which screws a clean bottle onto the filter outlet for a no-syringe backflush. Others keep the syringe for town stops, where the home-style flush is the one that counts. Either habit is sound. Backflushing after each trip or at resupply stops, rather than letting debris dry in the fibers, is the consistent advice from experienced users.

One caution applies to the syringe and to the filter it serves: backflush with clean water. Using still-contaminated water risks pushing particulates back into the element, and very hard tap water can leave mineral deposits that cause problems later.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Delivers the forceful, sustained pressure that Sawyer says a backflush needs
- Fits the Squeeze, Mini and Micro
- BD-made and produced in the USA
- 60 ml holds enough water to flush efficiently in fewer strokes
- Doubles as a tool for vinegar and bleach maintenance flushes and for blowing the filter dry

**Cons**

- 31 g is a real penalty for gram-counters who only backflush at home
- Plastic barrel and rubber seal are not a field-repair item if cracked or crushed in a pack
- Does nothing on its own: it is a maintenance tool, not a filter
- Easy to lose, which is presumably why a replacement exists

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who has lost the plunger that came with a Squeeze, Mini or Micro, or who owns a Sawyer filter bought without a kit. Home-flush types who want a dependable syringe in the gear closet will get years out of it. Ultralight hikers who already carry a cleaning coupling and flush at resupply with a spare bottle can skip it. Those running other filter brands, such as the HydroBlu, which includes no backflush equipment, should check that the nozzle fits before buying, since nozzle sizes differ.

## Verdict

A simple part that does one job well, and the right way to keep a Sawyer flowing at the pace it was bought for. Buy it as a replacement or a home-maintenance tool; leave it off the trail packing list if the grams count more than the convenience.
