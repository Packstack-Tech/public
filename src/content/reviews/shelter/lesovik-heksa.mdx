## Overview

The Heksa is a hexagonal silnylon tarp from Polish cottage-industry-turned-brand Lesovik, cut at 360 x 300 cm and built around hammock camping. Its party trick is a pre-rigged suspension and guyline system that pitches without knots and tensions with one hand. The trade-off: at roughly 500 g it is not the lightest hex on the market, but the rigging is already on the tarp, which is where much of the weight of a typical setup hides.

## Performance

**Coverage.** The geometry does most of the work. A 360 cm ridge runs well past the length of a gathered-end hammock, and the raised ends and catenary-cut edges let the fabric pitch taut with few guylines. Lesovik's own pitch is that the tarp is intended to shelter a hammock and the sleeper's gear. A German hammock-forum user who set it beside a OneWind tarp found the Heksa lighter, smaller in the stuff sack and, to his surprise, larger in coverage. He was also pleasantly surprised by how tight it pulls.

**Rigging.** This is the feature that separates the Heksa from a bare sheet of nylon. Built-in guyline tensioners adjust with one hand, and laser-cut mini-hooks replace knots. In practice, a wet-fingered pitch at dusk becomes a hook-and-slide job rather than a knot-tying exercise. The six anchor points sit on laser-cut reinforcements. Owners have noted that the roof panel has no tie-outs, so there is no way to pull the ridgeline's fabric up and out in the middle. That is typical for tarps in this class, but it does limit pitch variety compared with tarps that carry mid-panel loops.

**Weather protection.** Silnylon rated to 6,000 mm is comfortably above what is needed to shed sustained rain; the fabric is not the limiting factor. Setup is. A hex tarp protects as well as it is pitched, and the Heksa's side width, reported at about 150 cm per side by an early tester, gives enough drop for a low storm pitch while still leaving room for a porch-style setup. As with any silnylon, expect some sag when the fabric is wet, which the tensioners make easy to fix without leaving the hammock.

**Weight and pack size.** The listed weight is 500 g, and early testers measured 480 g. That figure includes the suspension and tensioning hardware, so the comparison with a bare 300 g tarp that still needs cord, tensioners and hooks is closer than it looks. Packed, it measures 35 x 13 x 13 cm, which is long for the weight rather than fat. One Czech buyer called it extremely compact, and a US-based hammock user wrote that the Heksa is simply lighter and more convenient than a hardware-store tarp, which he carries even when not hanging.

**Ground use.** Lesovik pitches the Heksa as a ground shelter too, and the size supports that: several people can sit out weather under it. With a bivy or open sleeping bag, it works as a low-slung tarp, but a hexagon is a hammock-first design and gives up floor-plan efficiency compared with a rectangle or a catenary flat tarp.

**Build.** Reviewers describe the finish as clean and the look as a plus. One early tester found it useful but unremarkable and suggested elastic tensioners on the side guylines for shock absorption in wind.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roughly 500 g with the suspension and tensioners already attached
- 360 cm ridge gives generous end coverage for long hammocks
- Knotless, one-hand adjustment is quick in rain and cold
- Compact for its size; fits in a 35 x 13 x 13 cm bag
- Made in Poland with clean finishing

**Cons**

- No tie-outs on the roof panel
- Stakes are not included
- Silnylon stretches and sags when wet, so retensioning is part of the routine
- Heavier than the lightest cuben/DCF or thinner-silpoly hex tarps
- Hex geometry is less efficient than a rectangle for ground use

## Who Should Buy This

Hammock campers who want a large, ready-to-hang hex tarp without assembling their own rigging will find the Heksa an easy fit, and it pairs naturally with Lesovik's Draka hammock. It also suits anyone who prefers quick pitching in foul weather to a fiddly cord setup. Weight-obsessed hikers chasing sub-300 g shelters, and ground sleepers who want a floor-plan-efficient flat tarp, should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Heksa is a well-sized, well-rigged hammock tarp that earns its 500 g by arriving ready to pitch. It does not break weight records, and the lack of roof tie-outs limits its pitch options, but for three-season hammock use the convenience and coverage are hard to fault. Recommended to hammock campers who value speed and space over the last few ounces.
