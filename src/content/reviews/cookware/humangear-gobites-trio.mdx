## Overview

The humangear GoBites Trio is a three-piece nylon utensil set: a full-size fork, spoon and serrated knife that snap into a flat polypropylene case. It's built for people who want to eat a real meal with real tools, not wrestle a spork through a steak. The trade-off is weight and bulk. Three separate utensils plus a case will never compete with a single titanium spoon.

## Performance

The pitch is simple: each tool is sized for eating, not for fitting in a pocket. The fork tines are long enough to twirl spaghetti, and the knife blade is sharp enough to cut a piece of chicken, according to humangear. Reviewers back this up. One REI reviewer said they didn't expect the knife to work on meat, and it did. Another noted the spoon was bigger than expected, though a third said it isn't especially deep. Owners also warn that the fork tines are sharp.

Having a fork and a knife in hand at once is the main appeal. Top Notch Outdoor named the Trio its best spoon-fork-knife combo and scored it 87 out of 100. It rated ergonomics highest at 9/10, with weight lowest at 7/10. Its testers described the set as the most usable three-piece option for the weight.

Durability is a strength of the material. The high-temp nylon is rated to 400°F, which covers hot freeze-dried meals and a pot just off the stove. Testers expect it to last for years. The usual caveat applies. Nylon is not titanium, and it won't stand up to being used as a pry bar or left against a flame. Cleanup is easy, since the surfaces are smooth and the set is top-rack dishwasher safe. A Map Happy reviewer noted the handles are wider and shorter than a typical full-size utensil. They also found the Trio noticeably bulkier than the two-piece GoBites Duo. The same reviewer reported the knife-equipped set caused no problems at TSA checkpoints.

The case is the set's most debated feature. It keeps the utensils clean and together in a pack, and it clips to a carabiner or pack loop. Owners say that's useful mainly for keeping the set in one place. It also creates a single point of failure. Top Notch's tester noted that the set scatters in a bag if the case goes missing.

The weight is the hard number to ignore for ultralight hikers. At 92 g, the Trio weighs more than a typical titanium spoon-and-spork pairing by a wide margin. It's also more than most solo thru-hikers want to carry for a diet of cold soaks and rehydrated bags. The knife and fork are better suited to cooked meals, fresh produce and cheese.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-size fork, spoon and knife, with a knife that cuts meat and produce
- Case keeps the set clean and together, and clips to a pack
- Rated to 400°F, with BPA-, PC- and phthalate-free materials
- Top-rack dishwasher safe, lifetime warranty
- Inexpensive at roughly $13
- Included toothpick and bottle opener

**Cons**

- At 92 g, heavy by ultralight standards
- The case adds bulk compared with the two-piece Duo
- Nylon is less rigid than metal and not suited to hard prying or open flame
- Losing the case means the set loses its organization
- Spoon bowl is on the shallow side, according to some owners

## Who Should Buy This

The Trio suits car campers, paddlers, section hikers on short trips with real cooking, and anyone who wants one set that moves from the trailhead to the office lunch room. It also suits travelers who want a knife that gets through airport security and cuts something more than a sandwich. Solo thru-hikers and gram counters who eat mostly from a bag or a mug should look elsewhere. A long-handled spoon or a titanium spork will do that job at a fraction of the weight.

## Verdict

The GoBites Trio does what its name promises: three real utensils, tidily packaged, at a low price. It's a good choice for anyone who values eating well over shaving grams. Ultralight hikers will find it heavier than they need.
