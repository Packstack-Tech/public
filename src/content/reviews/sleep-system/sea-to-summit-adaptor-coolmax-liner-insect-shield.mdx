## Overview

The Adaptor COOLMAX Liner is Sea to Summit's warm-weather sleep liner, built from a stretchy polyester knit and treated with Insect Shield permethrin for built-in bug protection. It's aimed at travelers, thru-hikers, and hostel/hut users who want a cool, breathable layer that doubles as a standalone summer "sleeping bag" or a clean, bug-resistant liner inside a proper bag.

## Performance

**Temperature regulation and comfort.** This is where the Adaptor earns its keep. Multiple owners describe it as <cite index="3-6">the Coolmax material transferring moisture out away from your body and stretching as you dream about the next day's travels</cite>. One long-term reviewer who used it on <cite index="7-1">a weeklong hut trek in Norway's Jotunheimen National Park</cite> noted that it <cite index="7-1">feels like cotton and wicks moisture effectively, making it versatile in mild or cooler temperatures</cite>. On the hot end, a Grand Canyon rafter <cite index="3-13,3-14">used it while rafting the Grand Canyon in July and felt that it actively wicked moisture and definitely kept the insect world at bay</cite>. If you run warm at night or camp in humid climates, this liner is genuinely more comfortable against skin than most silk or cotton alternatives — it has real stretch and doesn't feel clingy.

**Bug protection.** The Insect Shield treatment is the liner's main differentiator from Sea to Summit's non-treated Adaptor and Reactor lines, and it's effective at <cite index="4-13">withstanding up to 25 washes and not dissipating, unlike permethrin sprays</cite>. Field reports are generally positive but not universal — one Amazon reviewer called it <cite index="2-21">"excellent for abroad travel in hot and chilled conditions"</cite> but only gave 4 stars <cite index="2-22">because there is a smell associated with the insect repellent</cite>, a smell that reportedly airs out within a week or so of hanging it up. A hammock forum poster referenced a secondhand account of someone <cite index="8-3">backpacking through jungles in Cambodia who said he stayed bug free with this and no net</cite>, though that's an anecdote worth taking with a grain of salt rather than a controlled test.

**Durability and packed size.** The knit fabric holds up to regular washing without falling apart the way silk liners can — one reviewer who'd previously destroyed a silk liner from heavy use called the Coolmax replacement's durability a clear improvement, noting Sea to Summit's <cite index="2-14">CoolMax fabric Traveler version worked out great, with enough room to stretch out and move throughout the night</cite>. It also <cite index="3-15">packs small and washes well</cite> in day-to-day use. That said, it's not a featherweight: reviewers comparing it against Sea to Summit's own silk and silk-cotton liners found it <cite index="7-12,7-13">the heaviest and bulkiest of the three, at 10 oz. and 3×5 inches in its stuff sack</cite> — consistent with the 248 g spec here. If you're chasing the lightest possible base weight, this isn't the liner for that; you're paying a weight premium for the bug protection and stretch comfort.

**Fit.** At 210 cm long, most users find room to spare. One reviewer at 5'8" said the roomy dimensions meant it <cite index="7-11">may be the best choice for bigger people, but is not oversized for me</cite> — so taller hikers shouldn't feel cramped, and average-height users won't be swimming in it either.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely cooling and moisture-wicking — good for hot, humid nights or as a standalone summer bag
- Insect Shield permethrin treatment adds real bug protection without spraying gear yourself, rated for up to 25 washes
- Stretchy knit fabric is more comfortable than typical liner materials and machine washes easily
- Generous 210 cm length works well for taller users
- Doubles as a clean layer inside hostel beds or rental sleeping bags

**Cons**

- Heaviest and bulkiest option in Sea to Summit's own liner lineup — not an ultralight pick
- Some users report a noticeable chemical smell from the Insect Shield treatment that takes time to air out
- Insect Shield protection isn't permanent — degrades after roughly 25 washes or six months of exposure
- Not available for sale in Canada due to the permethrin treatment
- Premium price relative to basic cotton or silk-blend liners

## Who Should Buy This

This liner makes the most sense for travelers and hikers heading somewhere hot, humid, and buggy — think Southeast Asia, the Gulf Coast, or summer thru-hikes in mosquito country — where you want a washable, cooling layer that also cuts down on bites without a separate permethrin treatment step. It's also a solid choice for hostel-heavy trips like the Camino de Santiago, where a clean sleep layer matters as much as bug protection. If your priority is shaving grams for an alpine ultralight kit, look at Sea to Summit's lighter silk or silk-cotton liners instead.

## Verdict

The Adaptor COOLMAX Liner delivers on its core promise: a cool, stretchy, washable sleep layer with genuinely useful built-in bug protection. It's not the lightest or cheapest liner on the market, and the initial insect-repellent smell is a minor but real complaint, but for hot-climate and bug-heavy trips it's a dependable piece of kit. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — excellent for its intended use case, less compelling if ultralight weight is your only metric.
