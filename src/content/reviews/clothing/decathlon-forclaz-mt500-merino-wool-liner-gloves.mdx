## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is a thin, stretchy merino liner glove built to sit under a shell glove or mitt and take the edge off cold mornings. At 46 g and roughly £13 in the UK, it undercuts most name-brand merino liners by a wide margin. The trade-off is durability: the knit is soft and fine, and some owners report it wearing faster than the price suggests it should.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Decathlon rates the glove at up to 3°C of added warmth when worn under a regular glove. That is a modest figure, and an honest one. A 190 g/m² merino knit is a layering piece, not insulation. Owners on Decathlon's UK site score warmth at or near 5/5, and several describe the gloves as warm enough to wear alone on cool days. One buyer who wears them under mitts says they still keep hands warm after getting wet, which fits merino's usual behavior. Several others use them solo for spring and autumn walks and winter running.

**Comfort.** The 18.5-micron fiber is at the fine end for budget wool, and owners back that up. A reviewer who normally finds merino itchy says these are not. Others call them soft and thin. The 4% elastane keeps the fit snug, so the gloves don't bunch under an outer glove.

**Touchscreen use.** This is where a liner earns its keep. Reviewers report that phone screens respond without removing the gloves, and tactile scores on Decathlon's site are mostly 4/5 to 5/5. One ski tourer praises how well the gloves work on a phone in cold weather. A buyer who wears them under a warmer pair describes using the liner alone for phone use or fiddly tasks, then pulling the outer glove back on.

**Moisture.** Wool knit moves vapor, and buyers who use the liner under waterproof gloves say it helps with clamminess. That is the practical case for a liner on a wet-weather hike: the shell blocks rain while the merino sits next to the skin.

**Durability.** This is the weak point. Abrasion-resistance ratings on Decathlon's site range from 2/5 to 5/5, which is a wide spread for such a simple product. One cyclist reported seams coming apart after a few months of use. Liners worn under another glove see less abrasion than standalone gloves, so the risk is lower for the intended use. Hikers who wear them bare while handling poles all day should expect faster wear.

**Rating overall.** Decathlon's UK listing shows 4.5 out of 5 across roughly 1,400 reviews, and the Indian listing shows the same score from about 1,500.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 46 g and thin enough to disappear under any outer glove
- Fine 18.5-micron merino that owners describe as non-itchy
- Touchscreen-compatible fingertips work for phone use
- Cost is a fraction of premium merino liners
- Warm when damp, and moves moisture under waterproof outer gloves
- Machine washable at 30°C

**Cons**

- Added warmth is limited to about 3°C by Decathlon's own claim
- Mixed durability reports, including seam failures on a cycling pair
- Elastane and polyamide blend means it is not pure merino
- Thin knit offers little wind protection alone

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season hikers and thru-hikers who want a cheap, light layer under their shell gloves or mitts will get the most from the MT500. It also suits anyone who wants to answer a phone or adjust a camera without exposing bare skin to cold. Day hikers, ski tourers and winter runners can use it as a stand-alone glove in mild conditions.

Hikers expecting a single glove to handle sub-freezing wind should look at a real insulated glove instead. Anyone who plans to wear liners as their only handwear through a long trip of pole-gripping and rock scrambling should expect to replace them, or pick a sturdier knit.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 is a well-priced, soft and light merino liner that does what a liner should: adds a few degrees, handles a phone, and stays out of the way. Durability is the only real caveat, and at £13 it is easy to carry a spare pair. Recommended for most three-season backpackers.
