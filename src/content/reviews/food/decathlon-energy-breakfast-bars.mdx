## Overview

Decathlon's Energy Breakfast Bar is a soft oat-and-chocolate bar sold in individually wrapped 40 g portions, usually in packs of six. It is built for the first hour of a hiking day, when appetite is low and a stove is optional. The trade-off is energy density: at roughly 150 calories a bar, it is a light, easy-to-eat snack rather than a calorie-dense trail ration.

## Performance

**Nutrition.** Decathlon's design brief explains the numbers. Its engineers pared the bar down to 40 g, half the size of a standard 80 g breakfast bar, on the logic that two bars make a full breakfast. One bar is therefore a modest top-up, not a meal. The macros reflect that: 24 g of carbohydrate, 6.4 g of protein and only 2.8 g of fat. Half the carbohydrate is sugar, 12 g per bar, with 2.2 g of fiber.

**Ingredients.** The Decathlon listing for the chocolate version puts oat flakes first at about a quarter of the recipe. Extruded pea protein accounts for about a fifth, and honey and wheat syrup supply the sugars. Low-fat cocoa powder and dark chocolate chips handle the flavor, and the bar is fortified with magnesium and vitamins C, B1, B3 and B6. Its pea-protein base explains the protein figure, which is higher than most oat bars in this weight class. The chocolate version is labeled gluten-free, which is useful for celiac hikers who struggle to find resupply-friendly bars.

**Calorie density.** The bar works out to about 374 kcal per 100 g, or roughly 106 calories per ounce. Ultralight food planners typically target 125 or more calories per ounce, so this bar sits below the line. The low fat content is the cause. A nut-and-seed bar of the same weight carries noticeably more energy because fat delivers nine calories per gram against four for carbohydrate. Over a five-day carry, the difference adds up to extra grams of food for the same calories.

**On trail.** The soft texture suits cold mornings and low-appetite stretches, and the 40 g size fits in a hip-belt pocket without crushing into crumbs the way a brittle bar can. The sugar-forward profile makes it more of a quick fuel than a slow-release one. The 6.4 g of protein and 2.2 g of fiber will take the edge off hunger for a short while, though not through a long climb. Open Food Facts rates the bar Nutri-Score D, a reflection of its sugar load.

**Packaging.** Each bar is individually wrapped, which keeps the rest of the pack clean and makes portioning a day's food simple. Six bars total 240 g before wrappers, and the single-serve film adds small but real trash weight.

**Availability.** The bars are sold through Decathlon stores and its website, which makes them an easy add for European hikers who already shop there for gear. Anyone resupplying in a trail town without a Decathlon nearby will need to buy ahead.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft, easy-to-eat texture suited to early starts
- 6.4 g of protein per 40 g, plus magnesium and B vitamins
- Labeled gluten-free in the chocolate version
- Individually wrapped 40 g portions simplify day-by-day planning
- Oat-first recipe with real honey and dark chocolate chips

**Cons**

- About 106 calories per ounce, below the usual ultralight target
- Half of the carbohydrate is sugar, which makes for quick but short-lived energy
- One bar is a snack-sized 40 g, so a full breakfast takes two
- Only 2.8 g of fat limits its usefulness as a calorie-dense food
- Single-serve wrappers add trash

## Who Should Buy This

This bar suits day hikers, section hikers and weekend backpackers who want a light, soft morning snack and already shop at Decathlon. It also works for gluten-free hikers looking for a packaged, ready-to-eat option. Thru-hikers counting every calorie per ounce, and anyone who wants a long-lasting, high-fat bar for the afternoon slump, should look at nut-based or oil-rich options instead.

## Verdict

The Energy Breakfast Bar does what its name promises: a gentle, carb-led start to the day with a respectable amount of protein, in a size that is easy to portion. It is a good supporting player, not a foundation for a long-distance food bag. Buy it for short trips and morning fuel; skip it if calorie density is the priority.
