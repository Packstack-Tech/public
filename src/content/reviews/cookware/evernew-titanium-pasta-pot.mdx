## Overview

The Evernew Titanium Pasta Pot (ECA548) is a 1-liter, hand-pressed Japanese titanium pot with a perforated strainer lid, a pour spout and rubber-coated folding handles. It's built for solo hikers who cook real food rather than only rehydrating freeze-dried bags. The trade-off is the usual one for ultralight titanium: very little weight, a high price, and walls thin enough to need some care.

## Performance

**Weight and wall thickness.** The pot is made from 0.3 mm titanium. Backpacker's testers put that at roughly 20 percent lighter than standard titanium cookware. CleverHiker weighed the pot, lid and all, at 4.4 ounces. That makes it one of the lightest cooksets in its guide, and one of the most feature-rich. Backpacker's testers also reported boil times up to 15 percent quicker than with a thicker-walled titanium pot. That's a small but real fuel saving over a long trip.

**The lid is the point.** Most ultralight pots stop at "holds water." The Pasta Pot's lid has drain holes, and testers describe it as tight and secure. CleverHiker calls the fit a little finicky and hard to pull off, but says the same tightness gives good stability when draining and storing food. Draining noodles through the lid, aided by the pour spout, means no separate strainer and no fumbling with a flimsy lid held on by a thumb. Owners also like the engraved volume markings, which make measuring water for a dehydrated meal a matter of glancing inside the pot.

**Cooking surface.** This is bare titanium with no nonstick coating, and it behaves like it. The thin walls heat fast and will scorch food if the flame is high and no one is stirring. Backpacker's testers scraped stuck linguine off the bottom with a rock and the pot survived. CleverHiker scored the cooking performance as middling, and that's fair. The wide, short shape helps: at 12.5 cm across, there's room to stir and less hot-spotting than on a tall, narrow mug-style pot. Boiling water, simmering noodles, and one-pot dinners are what it does best. Sautéing is not.

**Durability.** Titanium this thin tends to warp over time. Backpacker flagged that the pot can lose its shape, and suggested packing a fuel canister inside to hold it round. CleverHiker found it one of the more durable titanium cooksets it tested, crediting the tight lid for helping the rim keep its form. The practical advice is the same from both: don't pack it loose under a heavy load, and don't pry it back into shape with force.

**Packing.** The pot is sized to swallow a 230 g fuel canister, plus a stove in the gaps for many setups. The included mesh stuff sack keeps the lid and pot together and keeps soot off everything else in the pack. The insulated folding handles are large enough to grip and stay cool enough to lift a full boil.

**Capacity.** A liter suits one hungry hiker. It holds a large single serving of pasta or enough water for two freeze-dried meals. It isn't a two-person pot for anything beyond boiling water. Evernew also makes a smaller 750 ml version for those who only boil water.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 125 g with lid, handles and strainer built in
- Strainer lid and pour spout make draining pasta, rice or ramen simple
- Engraved volume markings
- Wide 12.5 cm base is stable on a canister stove and easy to stir in
- Nests a 230 g fuel canister, and ships with a stuff sack
- Insulated handles stay comfortable

**Cons**

- Costs well over $80, more than comparable pots without the lid
- 0.3 mm walls can warp if packed carelessly
- Bare titanium scorches food and takes some scrubbing
- The tight lid can be fiddly to remove
- Overkill for hikers who only boil water

## Who Should Buy This

Solo hikers and thru-hikers who cook actual meals (pasta, instant mashed potatoes, couscous, ramen) and want the strainer built into the lid rather than carried separately. It suits canister-stove users best, since the pot nests around the fuel. Hikers who only boil water for freeze-dried bags are better off with a cheaper, narrower mug pot, which saves money and some weight. Groups of two or more should look at something larger.

## Verdict

The Evernew Pasta Pot is the rare piece of ultralight cookware that adds function rather than just removing grams. It's expensive and needs gentle handling. For a solo hiker who eats pasta on trail, it's the best 1-liter titanium pot available.
