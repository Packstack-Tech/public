## Overview

The Igneous Bottle Cap Bidet is a 4-gram nylon cap with a single pinhole nozzle. It swaps onto a standard 28 mm water bottle, such as a SmartWater, and turns a squeeze of the bottle into a directed rinse. It is aimed at hikers who want to cut back on toilet paper without adding a dedicated bidet bottle to the kit. The trade-off is a narrow, single-jet stream that rewards a little practice.

## Performance

The bidet's main selling point is how little it asks of a pack. At 4 g it ties the Holey Hiker as the lightest backcountry bidet in Treeline Review's 2026 roundup. That is roughly the weight of a few sheets of toilet paper. Its 1.3-inch footprint means it can ride in a first-aid pouch or the pocket of a hip belt without being noticed. Igneous founder Ray Garcia has said the design was whittled down over many late nights, down to hundredths of a gram. The product was originally billed as a 3 g "3 Gram Bidet," and some retailers still list it at 3 g.

On the trail, the mechanics are simple. The cap screws onto the outside of the bottle's threads, so it does not pop off under squeeze pressure the way push-in bidet nozzles can. One owner who has used push-in styles said that is the main reason the Igneous earned a permanent place in her kit. The silicone gasket handles sealing, and flow is controlled by how hard the bottle is squeezed. Treeline's testers measured a spray of about 14 feet. That is a bit shorter than the CuloClean's 16.5 feet and ahead of the Pika Outdoors Backwoods Bidet, which reached only 4.5 feet.

Cleaning power is where the single-hole design shows. Treeline had testers clean a standardized dishwashing mess, and the Igneous needed an average of 4.5 oz of water, between 3.5 and 5.5 oz across two runs. For comparison, the Happy Bottom, a dedicated squeeze bottle, averaged 2.75 oz. The stream is concentrated, which owners describe as effective, but it covers a small area. Buyers who have tried it say it takes a few uses to find the right angle and pressure, and that this differs from a home bidet. One Australian buyer reported a direct hit with good force on the first squirt.

Bottle fit is the one place where reports diverge. Retailer listings say the cap fits most narrow-mouth bottles in the Americas, Europe, Australia, New Zealand, China and Japan. That includes SmartWater and other disposable PET bottles, as well as Igneous's own NOBO bottle. One Australian reviewer, however, found the cap would thread onto rigid HDPE 28 mm bottles, such as a shampoo bottle, but not onto a PET bottle. Anyone with an unusual bottle should check the thread before relying on it in the field. Treeline also notes that the cap covers some of the bottle's threads, which helps limit splashback and contact with the bottle's mouth.

Owner sentiment is strong. On Garage Grown Gear, the bidet holds a 4.3 average across 32 reviews, with 72 percent giving five stars and 9 percent giving one. European retailers show scores near 4.8 out of 5 on smaller samples. Treeline gave it the "best bottle cap bidet" award and named it the best affordable pick in its roundup. The price is about $9 to $10 in the US.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 4 g weight, tied for the lightest bidet in Treeline's roundup
- Threads on the outside of the bottle, so it will not pop off under squeeze
- Integrated silicone gasket gives a leak-free seal
- Works with SmartWater and other common 28-410 bottles, plus Igneous's NOBO bottle
- About 14 ft of measured spray distance, enough for a controlled stream
- Made in the USA, priced around $10

**Cons**

- Single-hole stream needs practice to aim and covers a small area
- Used about 4.5 oz of water in Treeline's cleaning test, more than dedicated squeeze-bottle bidets
- At least one buyer could not thread it onto a 28 mm PET bottle, so bottle fit is not guaranteed
- Has to be paired with a bottle, so the carried weight includes the bottle's water

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right pick for thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who already carry a SmartWater or similar bottle and want a hygiene upgrade for a few grams and a few dollars. It also suits Leave No Trace-minded hikers on long trips who want to cut paper out of their kit. Those who want a gentler, wider spray, or who dislike practicing their aim, should look at a dedicated squeeze bottle such as the Happy Bottom. Travelers who rely on an odd-sized bottle should confirm the thread first.

## Verdict

The Igneous Bottle Cap Bidet delivers on its premise: 4 g, a secure external thread, and a focused stream that works once the technique is learned. It uses somewhat more water than bulkier rivals, but few pieces of gear add this little weight for this much comfort on a long trail. It earns a place in most ultralight bathroom kits.
