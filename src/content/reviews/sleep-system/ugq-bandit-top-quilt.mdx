## Overview

The Bandit is the quilt that put Jackson, Michigan's UGQ Outdoor on the map: a down top quilt sewn to order, with an order form long enough to double as a personality test. Ground sleepers, hammock campers and thru-hikers who want a quilt fitted to their body and their temperature range get cottage-shop customization at a price that sits well below many competitors. The trade-off is patience. Every Bandit is built after it is ordered, and the queue can run for weeks.

## Performance

**Warmth and drape.** The Bandit's defining feature is its baffle layout. Vertical chambers sit alongside horizontal ones, which lets the quilt settle over the body instead of standing off it, and UGQ concentrates fill in the torso and footbox rather than spreading it evenly. In practice, the quilt's cold spots tend to be the gaps along the sides and neck, and the drape is meant to close those. Owners report that the design holds up in real cold: one seller of a 20°F, 950fp model said it kept them warm through a snowstorm and was simply too much quilt for summer camping.

**Weight in practice.** Weight depends heavily on what is ordered. A 20°F, regular-length, regular-width 950fp Bandit with a draft collar and fully insulated footbox came in at 1 lb 3 oz, with 14.51 oz of down, according to its owner. Territory Supply's tested 20°F quilt, an 850fp, 55-by-78-inch model, weighed 13.75 oz. A 0°F build such as the 711 g listing here carries substantially more down, and adding an overstuffed footbox, a draft collar or a longer, wider cut pushes the number up further. Choosing the 20d ripstop outer shell over 10d taffeta buys durability at a small weight cost.

**Ground and hammock use.** The Bandit is one of the few quilts that is equally at home in a hammock and on a pad. SectionHiker's review focused on the newer wafer-based pad attachment system and the Dynamic Tension Control, a cord arrangement that adjusts how snugly the quilt wraps around the sleeper. The site described the result as one of the more sophisticated top quilts available for ground sleepers. Territory Supply's reviewer, who tested the quilt in hammocks, awarded it Editor's Choice and rated its workmanship above what most small makers produce.

**Build quality.** Reviewers consistently describe the construction as clean and durable. The 10d M10T liner is soft and breathable, and the down is UltimaDOWN, which UGQ sells in 850 and 950 fill power. A lifetime warranty backs the quilt. UGQ's own product page shows a 4.94 out of 5 average from roughly 700 customer ratings, a figure worth treating as manufacturer-reported, though it matches the tone of independent reviews.

**Value.** Starting at $204.95 and topping out at $594.95, the Bandit is cheaper than many cottage-brand quilts with similar specs. An Outdoor Survival Nation reviewer, whose quilt shipped at $297.95, noted that other quilts he owns for milder temperatures cost almost double.

**Lead times.** Custom means waiting. UGQ currently lists shipping at about five to six weeks, and earlier in the year the same page quoted eight. A $200 priority-scheduling option moves an order up by four weeks, which is a steep premium on a quilt this affordable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Vertical-plus-horizontal baffling drapes well and limits side drafts
- Wide menu of temperature ratings (0°F to 40°F), widths, lengths and footbox styles
- 850 or 950 fill power down, with 10d or 20d outer shell choices
- Pad attachment system and stuff sack included
- Made in Michigan, with a lifetime warranty
- Price undercuts many comparable custom quilts

**Cons**

- Made to order, with weeks-long lead times
- The option list is long, and weight and price climb quickly with upgrades
- A 0°F build is a heavier, more specialized quilt than a typical 20°F three-season model
- Rush production costs an extra $200

## Who Should Buy This

The Bandit suits backpackers and hammock campers who know their sleep temperature and body dimensions, and who want a quilt built around them without paying boutique prices. Cold sleepers and shoulder-season hikers will appreciate the 0°F and 10°F options, and side sleepers who struggle with drafts should benefit from the drape. Anyone who needs a quilt next week, or who wants a stock item with no decisions to make, should look at off-the-shelf alternatives instead. Summer-only hikers should also order a warmer rating only if they plan to use it, since a 0°F quilt is more insulation than most warm-weather trips call for.

## Verdict

The Bandit is among the best-value custom down quilts available, with sound construction, a thoughtful baffle layout and enough configuration options to fit nearly any sleeper. The wait and the complexity of the order form are its only real costs. Hikers who can plan ahead should buy it.
