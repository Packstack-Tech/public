## Overview

The Actik Core is Petzl's do-it-all headlamp for people who move fast in the dark: trail runners, alpine-start mountaineers and backpackers who night-hike. It throws up to 625 lumens from a USB-C rechargeable battery, and it still runs on three AAAs when the battery dies far from an outlet. The trade-off is mass and moisture protection. At 88 g with the battery installed, it is a full-featured lamp rather than a minimalist one, and its IPX4 rating covers splashes, not immersion.

## Performance

**Beam.** The Actik Core uses two white LEDs, one for a broad, even flood and one for a tighter spot. The flood lights the ground at your feet. The spot reaches out to the rated 115 m. Reviewers at Outoria singled out the focused long-distance beam as the lamp's strongest suit. The 625-lumen peak is more than most backpackers will use for camp chores and trail walking. It earns its place on glacier approaches, scree and fast descents, where seeing the next 50 m matters.

**Output steps.** The lamp has no continuous dimming. It offers 7, 100 or 625 lumens, with nothing in between. Live For The Outdoors flagged that as a minor annoyance. In practice, 100 lumens for 7 hours handles most night hiking, and 7 lumens for 100 hours covers camp life and tent-bound reading. The 625-lumen setting lasts only 2 hours, so it works as a burst mode for route-finding rather than a default.

**Hybrid power.** The Hybrid Concept design is the headline feature. The lamp ships with the CORE rechargeable battery, which charges over USB-C, and it detects the power source automatically. Drop in three AAAs and it keeps working, but output falls to 450 lumens and range to about 100 m. Petzl's own numbers and Outoria's testers agree that the rechargeable pack outperforms alkalines at the top end. For a multi-day trip, the pairing is sensible. Charge the CORE from a power bank, and carry three AAAs as insurance. That adds weight, so many thru-hikers will skip the spares on trips with a reliable charging plan.

**Red light and signaling.** The red mode is a steady 2 lumens that runs for 60 hours. It preserves night vision and keeps a tentmate from being dazzled at 4 a.m. A red strobe is visible at up to 700 m and runs for a claimed 400 hours. Red is the one place the lamp falls short of runner-specific designs: there is no rear red light.

**Handling.** A single button cycles the modes, and testers at Whittaker Mountaineering, which gave it a Guide Pick designation, note that it is easy to operate with cold hands. A lock mode stops the lamp switching on in a pack. The headband is reflective, washable and made from recycled material. A phosphorescent reflector glows after dark so the lamp can be found in a tent. The included storage sack diffuses light, so the lamp can serve as a lantern. Helmet Adapt and Bike Adapt 2 mounts are compatible if the lamp needs to move off the head.

**Weather.** IPX4 means it handles rain and sweat. Reviewers at Live For The Outdoors pointed out that many rivals carry higher ratings. For a hiker who sets a lamp down in puddles or expects stream dunks, that is a real consideration. For standard three-season backpacking, a rain shell and a pack lid cover the gap.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 625 lumens and 115 m of throw from an 88 g lamp
- Hybrid power: USB-C rechargeable CORE battery with AAA fallback
- Flood and spot beams cover both close-up and distance work
- Simple single-button interface that works with gloves or cold fingers
- Lock mode, reflective headband and glow-in-the-dark reflector
- Red strobe and steady red light for camp and emergencies

**Cons**

- Heavy by ultralight standards, with a battery that cannot be separated for a shorter trip
- Only three white steps, with no dimming
- IPX4 is the minimum for a headlamp in this class
- High mode lasts just 2 hours, and AAA power drops it to 450 lumens
- No rear red light for runners and cyclists
- Priced around $88 in the US

## Who Should Buy This

The Actik Core fits backpackers, mountaineers and trail runners who regularly move at night and want one lamp that does everything. It also suits people who like the rechargeable convenience but hate being stranded by a dead battery on a multi-day trip. A gram-counting thru-hiker who only needs a lamp for camp chores should look at a smaller, lighter option. So should anyone who wants a submersion-rated lamp or smoother brightness control.

## Verdict

The Actik Core is a mature, well-sorted headlamp whose flexible power and bright, versatile beam justify its mass and price for night-focused users. It falls short of ultralight minimalism and offers fewer steps than some rivals, but its reliability and ergonomics are hard to fault. Buy it if night movement matters. Skip it if the lamp rarely leaves your pack.
