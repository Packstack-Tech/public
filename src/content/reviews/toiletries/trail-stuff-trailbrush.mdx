## Overview

The Trailbrush is a handleless toothbrush head that locks into the carabiner hole of a long-handled titanium spoon or spork. The spoon becomes the handle, so the usual ritual of snapping a toothbrush in half is skipped entirely. It suits gram-counters who already carry a compatible utensil, and the trade-off is a fit that works well for some owners and badly for others.

## Performance

**The idea.** Trail Stuff founder Andrew Vargo, an architect who developed the design after thru-hiking, built the Trailbrush around a simple rule: don't carry a second handle when a spoon already has one. The head clicks into the spoon's lanyard hole and is held by a silicone gasket. A cap covers the bristles in transit and doubles as a grip on the spoon handle while eating. Head and cap together weigh 4.4 g, which is lighter than any trimmed plastic toothbrush and a fraction of a full-size one.

**Brushing.** A long, rigid titanium handle changes the experience. Hikers get leverage and reach, and their fingers stay away from the mouth, a point the brand makes and one that anyone who has brushed with a stub of a toothbrush will appreciate. One reviewer at Trails magazine found the fit on a Sea to Summit AlphaLite snug enough that the head never popped off or wobbled mid-brush. The bristles are described as medium, though owners tend to say they feel soft. One retailer review calls them a touch too soft. A Finnish reviewer compared them to a child's brush.

**Fit.** This is where opinions split. At Garage Grown Gear, the product averages 3.3 stars across 95 reviews. Roughly half of those are 4 or 5 stars, and a bit over a third are 1 or 2 stars. The complaints are consistent: the head won't stay seated on certain spoons, and the cap is loose enough to fall off in a pack or while eating, which leaves the bristles exposed to grit. Other retailers show much friendlier scores. A shop that lists it at 4.8 out of 5 across 111 reviews is one example. Buyers there report that installation is fiddly but durable once done properly. The Trails magazine tester ran into the opposite problem from the loose-fit crowd: the head sat so snugly that it was hard to remove and effectively stayed on the spork, which meant eating dinner with the toothbrush attached.

**Hygiene.** The same tester noted that on a spork with a contoured handle, toothpaste foam ran down the handle during brushing and onto the hands, and that having one tool serve both mouth and meal felt less clean than a dedicated brush. Stripping the head off after each use and storing it in the cap is the workaround, though that is one more step in the morning.

**Weight in context.** Even the maker concedes the Trailbrush won't make a significant difference in base weight. The benefit is a few grams and a little less clutter in the toiletry bag. That only works if the spoon is already in the pack. Anyone who would otherwise carry a plastic spork or no long spoon at all gains nothing, since a titanium spoon outweighs a trimmed toothbrush.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 2 g head, 4.4 g with the cap
- Reuses a spoon already in the kit
- Long handle gives reach and keeps fingers clear of the mouth
- Medium nylon bristles on a silicone head
- Works with the most common titanium long-handle spoons

**Cons**

- Fit is inconsistent. Some owners report the head falling off, others find it hard to remove
- The cap can be loose and drop off in a pack
- Won't fit Snow Peak utensils, and compatibility depends on the hole size
- Shared mouth-and-meal tool is a hygiene ick for some hikers
- Bristles run soft
- Saves only a few grams, and only if a compatible spoon is already carried

## Who Should Buy This

The Trailbrush fits hikers who already carry a Toaks, Vargo or Sea to Summit long-handled spoon and want to shave the last few grams off a toiletry kit. Thru-hikers who like novelty and don't mind a bit of fiddling will enjoy it. Anyone who eats with a plastic spork, a short spoon or a Snow Peak utensil should skip it, as should hikers who dislike sharing a utensil with their toothbrush or want a firm brush. A trimmed standard toothbrush is the less finicky choice.

## Verdict

The Trailbrush is a clever answer to a problem that mostly exists in gear lists, and the 2 g head delivers on weight when it fits. Owner results swing between "works fine" and "falls off," so buyers should confirm that the hole in their spoon matches the ones on the compatibility list before ordering. It's a fun, cheap experiment for the right spoon owner, and a poor substitute for a regular brush for everyone else.
