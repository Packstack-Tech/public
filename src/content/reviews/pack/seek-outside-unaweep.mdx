## Overview

The Unaweep is Seek Outside's high-capacity external-frame pack: a 4,800-cubic-inch (79 L) roll-top bag in waterproof X-Pac on an articulated aluminum frame. It was built for hunters, packrafters and expedition hikers who need to move 40 to 75 pounds without the pack becoming the heaviest thing they own. The trade-off is that it is a load hauler first. On a 20-pound weekend it is more pack than the job needs.

## Performance

**Carrying comfort.** The frame is the point of the whole system, and it is where owners are most enthusiastic. SectionHiker's reviewer called the hip belt comfort and the ease of going heavy "a revelation" when loaded with more than 40 pounds of winter gear, water and food. The pack was rated 4.8 out of 5 there. Backpacking Light members describe a similar experience: one carried a 50-pound starting load on an 11-day Brooks Range packrafting trip and said the pack "carried like a dream." Another reports that the hip belt resists sliding down, a common complaint on lighter packs.

**Load ceiling.** Seek Outside lists a 300-pound maximum vertical load for the frame, a number no backpacker will approach. In practice, SectionHiker describes the pack as optimized for loads well beyond 75 pounds despite weighing under 4 pounds. Backpacking Light posters cite a year of expedition use at around 75 pounds. Rokslide hunters report hauling meat-season loads and 30-day archery elk trips without complaint. The Unaweep has a track record on the heavy end that most sub-three-pound packs can't match.

**Durability and weather.** X-Pac VX21 is a waterproof laminate, and a roll-top closure removes the zipper as a leak point. Owners treat the pack as close to indestructible. One Backpacking Light member says it feels like it will last forever and that, unlike with some ultralight packs, he doesn't worry about snagging it. SectionHiker's reviewer bought it specifically to survive off-trail New England bushwhacking, where thin Dyneema-style fabrics get shredded. The UHMWPE laminate option is aimed at hikers who want to trade some abrasion resistance for less weight.

**Organization and versatility.** Owners praise the side pockets, which hold Nalgene bottles, and the hip belt pockets, which are big enough to be useful. They also mention plentiful lash points on the outside of the pack. The optional Talon pocket adds external stowage against the back of the bag and can be mixed with daypack-style add-ons. Reviewers have used the Unaweep for thru-hiking, packrafting, ski mountaineering, hunting and winter camping, and SectionHiker's testers concluded it handles heavy and awkward loads well across all of them. As a roll-top, it has no full-length zipper access; hunters who want to reach gear mid-pack tend to look at Seek Outside's zippered bags.

**Where it doesn't fit.** An external-frame pack sits off the back differently from an internal-frame design, and the 79-liter bag is oversized for short trips. A Backpacking Light owner who usually carries 20 to 25 pounds called it overkill for most of his week-long trips, though he valued it for longer, heavier outings. Seek Outside sells the harness separately from the pack bag, so the total cost and weight of a complete setup depends on what's chosen.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Carries 40 to 75+ pounds comfortably, with a 300-lb vertical load rating
- About 3 pounds at 79 L of capacity
- Waterproof X-Pac bag with a roll-top closure
- Tough enough for off-trail travel and brush
- Large side and hip belt pockets, with many external lash points
- Flexible platform: Talon and daypack add-ons expand its uses

**Cons**

- Overbuilt for 20-pound three-season loads
- Roll-top offers no zippered access to the main compartment
- Harness is sold separately from the bag
- External-frame design is bulkier than a typical ultralight pack and won't suit everyone's preferences

## Who Should Buy This

The Unaweep suits backcountry hunters, packrafters, winter campers and long-range hikers who regularly carry food for 10 days or more, plus anyone hauling bulky gear such as a packraft, ski equipment or a meat load. It also appeals to hikers who go off-trail and want a pack that survives it. Thru-hikers who keep their base weights low and rarely leave town with more than 25 pounds should look at a smaller frameless or light internal-frame pack instead.

## Verdict

The Unaweep earns its reputation as a load hauler that stays light and survives abuse. Owners say it carries 50-pound and heavier loads comfortably, and its fabric and design hold up to rough off-trail use. Buy it if heavy and awkward loads are routine; skip it if they aren't.
