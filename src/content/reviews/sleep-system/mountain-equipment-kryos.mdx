## Overview

The Kryos is the warmest bag in Mountain Equipment's Extreme Alpine range, built for winter mountaineering, glacier camps and ski expeditions where every gram counts and tent condensation is a given. It pairs 743 g of 800+ fill goose down with a 10-denier DRILITE LOFT shell that shrugs off frost and drip. The trade-off is plain: at 1,150 g in Regular and a list price of £660, it is far more bag than most three-season thru-hikers will ever need.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Mountain Equipment rates the Kryos at -9°C comfort and -16°C limit, with an extreme figure of -37°C. The brand also quotes a "Good Night's Sleep" number of -20°C, which is its own metric rather than a standardized one; the comfort and limit figures are the ones to plan around. Construction does as much work as fill. Trapezoid baffles run through the body, and an elastic EXL system pulls the inner toward the sleeper to cut dead air and drafts. Ground-level side seams with V-baffles keep the top from gapping at the hip. An owner who stepped up from an earlier Mountain Equipment expedition bag called the Kryos very warm for its weight and noted how small it packs for a sub-zero bag.

**Weight and packability.** Roughly 65 percent of the Regular's weight is down, which is how 1,150 g reaches a -16°C limit. Packed, it is a dense 34 x 25 x 24 cm cylinder that sits comfortably in an alpine pack. Inside the winter-bag class, that ratio is a strong one. Against a 3-season quilt or a 0°C bag, it is several hundred grams of insurance against cold that never shows up.

**Moisture handling.** This is where the Kryos separates itself from most down bags. The DRILITE LOFT shell is breathable and water-resistant, and the fabric continues into the hood, collar and footbox, the places that collect breath moisture and tent condensation. Winter campers in the Cairngorms report that the bag copes well with tent condensation. The down itself is not hydrophobic, so a multi-week trip in persistent damp still calls for care. The shell buys time, not immunity.

**Fit and comfort.** Alpine Fit 2.0 adds a little length and room over earlier versions. Owners describe the EXL stretch as snug without feeling confining, and the six-baffle offset footbox leaves toes uncrushed. A 75 cm shoulder girth is trim even by expedition standards. That helps the weight figure, but larger sleepers wearing a down jacket inside will feel it.

**Details.** The zip sits low so the neck collar stays put, and a cross-baffle above it keeps down from sagging off the chest. A magnetic Lode Lock closure at the top of the zip draws the collar in around the neck and keeps the zip from creeping open. The hood is anatomically shaped from seven baffles. The one complaint in owner reviews is the lack of an internal pocket for a phone, radio or GPS communicator, a real nuisance when batteries need to stay warm.

## Choosing a Version

The Kryos comes in Regular and Long, both with a left-hand zip. The difference is length, not warmth. Regular weighs 1,150 g and fits sleepers up to 185 cm. Long is a 205 cm bag that weighs 1,200 g and fits up to 200 cm; the extra 50 g is the entire penalty for the extra room.

Anyone over 185 cm should buy the Long. Feet pressed against the end of a footbox compress the down exactly where circulation is poorest. Sleepers between roughly 180 and 185 cm who want space for boot liners or a water bottle can also justify the Long. Everyone else should take the Regular, which costs less (£660 list against £700) and is the length retailers stock most widely.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low weight for the temperature rating: 743 g of 800+ fill down in a 1,150 g bag
- DRILITE LOFT extends into the hood, collar and footbox, where moisture collects
- Trapezoid baffles and EXL elastic pull the bag close and limit cold spots
- Draft collar, Lode Lock closure and zip baffle hold heat in
- Compresses small for a bag rated to -16°C
- Waterproof roll-top stuff sack included

**Cons**

- Overbuilt and heavy for three-season thru-hiking
- Expensive: £660 list for Regular, £700 for Long
- Down is not hydrophobic, so prolonged wet conditions still demand care
- Trim 75 cm shoulder girth
- No internal pocket for electronics

## Who Should Buy This

Winter mountaineers, ski tourers and anyone camping on snow or glaciers at -10°C and below will get what they pay for, particularly in humid maritime ranges like the Scottish Highlands, where tent condensation soaks bags by morning. Cold-sleeping hikers heading into the high Sierra in shoulder season or onto a winter traverse could justify it. Hikers who mostly see nights above freezing should look at a quilt or a 0°C to -5°C bag that saves several hundred grams and a few hundred dollars.

## Verdict

The Kryos is a purpose-built cold-weather bag that pairs quality down with a shell that handles moisture, and it delivers a -16°C limit at 1,150 g. It is not a thru-hiker's bag, but for alpine and expedition use it earns its price. Buy it for real winter; skip it for everything else.
