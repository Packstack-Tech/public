## Overview

The Vasak is Petzl's general-purpose 12-point steel crampon, built for the middle of the mountaineering spectrum: glacier approaches, snow slopes and couloirs, and the occasional guided summit day. It trades weight for durability and versatility, with a modular design that lets a single frame work across very different boots. Ultralight hikers who want the lightest possible spikes should look at aluminum or 10-point steel options, but anyone who needs one crampon to do several jobs will find a lot to like.

## Performance

The Vasak's reputation rests on being unfussy. Two long, wide front points bite into hard snow, and Petzl says the point length is tuned for precise placements while still giving traction on the flat. The 12-point layout includes two mid-foot points that a 10-point crampon lacks, which adds stability when walking and flat-footing across firm slopes. A Mountain Project thread from December 2025 sums up the consensus in a line: a good all-around general mountaineering crampon.

Professional opinion has been favorable for years. UK mountain guide Chris Ensoll ranked the Vasak LeverLock ahead of the Black Diamond Serac Clip and Grivel G12 New Matic in a three-way comparison, and later revisited the design after Petzl added a quick-adjust length system to the linking bars. His read on that update was that it matters most to guides refitting crampons for clients, and less to owners who set them once and forget them. Both the original fine-adjust holes and the bi-position bars are still there, and together they cover boots from EU 36 to 50 without tools.

Snow management is handled by the ANTISNOW plates, which are meant to limit balling in sticky conditions near freezing. They are also the part owners have flagged for wear: a long-time Trailspace reviewer noted that the plates get damaged fairly easily when the crampons are used for mixed climbing. For glacier and snow use, which is what the Vasak is sold for, that's less of a concern. The plates are replaceable through the ALPEN ADAPT system, so a chewed-up plate doesn't end the crampon's life.

That modularity is the Vasak's real distinguishing feature. Front sections, linking bars, anti-bott plates and bindings are all sold as spare parts, and Petzl's system accepts different toe bails to fit stiff, flexible, telemark and snowboard boots. The Vasak is also compatible with Petzl's SARKEN front sections, which converts it into a technical mountaineering crampon. A buyer can start with a snow-and-glacier setup and move toward steeper terrain without replacing the whole frame.

The main criticism is weight and pack volume. A ski-mountaineering buyer who returned a Vasak for Petzl's Irvis noted the Irvis is 736 g against 842 g for the Vasak and packs smaller. A Mountain Project commenter made the same point from another angle: because the Irvis shares the Vasak's front and secondary point geometry, anyone who doesn't need the mid-foot points, the extra length for large feet, or a 12-point crampon for a guided climb may be carrying unneeded steel.

## Choosing a Version

The frame, points and fit range are identical between versions. The difference is the binding.

**LEVERLOCK UNIVERSEL (842 g with the rigid toe bail)** is the step-in-style option for boots with heel welts. A flexible front attachment is also supplied, which brings the weight to about 872 g and extends the crampon's fit range to a wider variety of mountaineering boots. This version suits stiff and semi-stiff alpine boots, and it is the lighter choice by roughly 50 g.

**FLEXLOCK (892 g)** is for footwear without toe and heel welts, such as flexible approach shoes and softer mountaineering boots. It is simpler to put on, but it is the heavier setup and doesn't offer the same positive lock as a lever on a welted boot. Choose it only if the boots don't have welts. Otherwise, the LEVERLOCK is the better crampon.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Steel construction built to last through years of glacier and snow use
- 12 points and two wide front points give stable, confident traction on hard snow
- Tool-free length adjustment covers EU 36–50
- Modular ALPEN ADAPT system allows individual parts to be replaced or upgraded
- Convertible to a more technical setup with SARKEN front sections
- CE, UKCA and UIAA certified, with a three-year guarantee

**Cons**

- 842–892 g is heavy for ultralight-minded climbers, and the 10-point Irvis saves about 100 g
- ANTISNOW plates can get damaged in mixed terrain
- Bulkier to pack than lighter 10-point or aluminum designs
- List price of $209.95 is steep for a crampon aimed at the beginner-to-intermediate range

## Who Should Buy This

The Vasak suits climbers who want one durable crampon for glacier travel, snow couloirs and guided objectives such as Rainier, Mont Blanc or Denali's approaches, particularly those who own more than one type of boot or expect to upgrade to steeper terrain later. It also fits people who need a crampon that meets the 12-point requirement of guided courses. Ski mountaineers and weight-focused alpinists who mostly walk on snow should look at the Irvis or an aluminum crampon instead. Anyone planning sustained mixed or dry-tooling routes will want something purpose-built with a more technical front section.

## Verdict

The Vasak is a proven, repairable and adaptable steel crampon that does the core mountaineering jobs reliably. It is heavier and pricier than the minimalist options, so ultralight buyers will be carrying more than they need. For a do-it-all classic mountaineering crampon, buy the LEVERLOCK UNIVERSEL version if your boots have welts.
