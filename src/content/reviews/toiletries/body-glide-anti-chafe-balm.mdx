## Overview

Body Glide Original is a twist-up stick of plant-based wax that lays down a dry, invisible barrier wherever skin rubs on skin, fabric or gear. It has been a runner's staple since 1996 and a quiet fixture in thru-hiker hip belt pockets for just as long. The trade-off is endurance: it prevents chafing cleanly, but in heavy sweat it asks for a midday touch-up.

## Performance

The formula is short and unfussy: plant-derived waxes and emollients, with allantoin and vitamin E rounding it out. The same base runs through the whole Body Glide line. On skin it behaves like a very light wax, something closer to lip balm than to a salve, and it dries almost at once with no visible residue. Reviewers consistently note that it has a neutral, nearly absent smell, which matters to anyone who dislikes scented products in a food-sharing environment like a tent.

**Friction control** is the reason to carry it. Applied before dressing, it forms a breathable layer that cuts down on skin-on-skin rubbing, fabric abrasion and pinching from gear. It does not transfer to clothing, stain, or leave greasy smears on a pack's hip belt, and it washes off with mild soap. Users on Body Glide's own review pages describe long days of walking, including a 24.6-mile Grand Canyon rim-to-rim hike with a single reapplication at the halfway point, without chafing or discomfort.

**Longevity** is where expectations need setting. The company's own guidance puts protection at roughly six to eight hours, and sweat, humidity and heavy friction shorten that. One owner reported it wearing off in about two hours in hot, humid conditions. On a muggy summer day in the Southeast or a long desert stretch, a midday reapplication is part of the routine. In cooler, drier weather, one morning swipe generally holds.

**Versatility** is the quiet advantage for backpackers. Thighs and underarms are the obvious targets, but hikers also run it over hip belt and shoulder strap contact points, along waistband lines, and on feet where shoe friction builds toward hot spots. It is a preventative, not a treatment: it will not heal skin that is already raw.

**Against the alternatives**, Vaseline is cheaper and easy to resupply but is petroleum-based, greasy and better at soothing than preventing. Squirrel's Nut Butter and similar salves are favored by some in extreme sweat but go on by hand and feel messier. Body Glide applies dry, cleanly and without washing up afterward. On long routes, resupply is the catch. It sits mainly in outfitters and running shops rather than every small-town grocery, so thru-hikers out west tend to send a spare stick in a mail drop.

Two smaller complaints recur. The plastic cap can crack or break, leaving the balm exposed, so a stick riding loose in a pocket does better in a small zip bag. And the wax softens in high heat, so a sun-baked car or exposed pack pocket is a poor place to leave it. A small minority of users also find the film slightly tacky, which seems to vary with skin type.

## Choosing a Version

All four sticks use the same Original formula; only the amount of balm changes.

- **0.35 oz pocket ($5.49):** The pick for first-aid kits, day hikes and weekend trips. It carries almost no weight, but a daily user on a longer trip will run through it.
- **0.8 oz travel ($8.99):** A stick of about 22 g, and the right size for most one- to two-week trips or section hikes where chafing is occasional.
- **1.5 oz regular ($10.99):** The 40 g stick and the most common choice among backpackers. It covers a long section or a heavy-chafe hiker on a multi-week stretch without becoming a burden.
- **2.5 oz large ($16.99):** Better for home, a shared basecamp or a mail-drop resupply box than for the trail itself, since it adds weight most hikers won't use. Decanting is not an option with a twist-up stick.

Hikers who chafe daily and plan to reapply should skip the pocket size. Those who carry it just in case should skip the large one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Effective at preventing chafing across a wide range of conditions
- Dry, clean application with no greasy hands or stained clothing
- Plant-derived, vegan, fragrance-free, free of petroleum and lanolin
- Works on thighs, feet, underarms and pack contact points
- Four sizes, from a pocket stick to a large one
- Widely sold at REI, running stores and online

**Cons**

- Protection fades faster in extreme heat and humidity, so reapplication is needed on big days
- Some users find the film slightly tacky
- Wax softens in high temperatures
- The cap is fragile and prone to cracking
- Harder to find on the trail than Vaseline, particularly on western long routes
- Prevents chafing but does not heal damaged skin

## Who Should Buy This

Body Glide suits any hiker with a recurring chafing problem: inner thighs on high-mileage days, shoulders and hips under a loaded pack, or feet before a hot spot turns into a blister. It's especially good for anyone who wants a no-mess, clothing-safe stick that can be applied without a hand-washing station. Hikers who want a salve to treat existing raw skin, or who expect a single application to survive a full day of drenching sweat, should look elsewhere or pair it with a healing ointment.

## Verdict

Body Glide Original solves a common trail problem cleanly, without petroleum or fragrance, in a form that fits a hip belt pocket. Its limit is staying power in the worst heat and humidity, which means heavy sweaters should carry it for reapplication. At these weights and prices, it earns a place in most kits.
