## Overview

The Deuter Exosphere -11° is the warmest bag in the German company's synthetic trekking line: a mummy-cut, Thermo ProLoft-filled bag with elasticated chamber seams that let the whole bag stretch up to 25% in width. It is built for cold-weather trekking, ski touring and mountain use, where damp conditions make synthetic fill a sensible bet. The trade-off is plain: at 1,945 g, it gives up a lot of pack weight in exchange for forgiving fit, low maintenance and a price well below a comparable down bag.

## Performance

**Stretch and fit.** The ThermoStretch seams are the reason to look at this bag. Reviewers describe a snug, close fit without the restricted feeling common to slim mummy cuts, which keeps dead air to a minimum so the bag warms up quickly. One reviewer at Advnture noted the body-hugging sensation feels strange at first but that the bag proves roomier than its slim profile suggests. Trek & Mountain's tester pointed out that Deuter's stretch runs through the shell, not just the inner, and found the bag accommodated restless sleepers well. A Cape Wrath Trail hiker who sleeps with one leg bent called it nearly impossible to match in any other mummy bag. Side sleepers and knee-benders are the people this bag was designed for.

**Warmth.** Deuter fills the bag evenly top and bottom, a 360° layout that the company says holds its temperature rating in any sleeping position. The shingle construction, with a 3x overlap here, is meant to eliminate cold spots at the seams. The -4 °C comfort rating is the figure that matters for the typical user, and the -11 °C limit is the number for a cold-sleeping man in a curled position. The -30 °C extreme figure is a survival rating and shouldn't be treated as a planning number. One owner of the earlier -4 model said they would hesitate to push it much below -8 °C despite a far lower official extreme, which is a sensible instinct for any bag rated this way. Deuter's own ratings follow ISO 23537 testing.

**Wet-weather behavior.** This is where synthetic fill earns its place. Thermo ProLoft's fibers absorb little moisture, and the bag keeps insulating when condensation builds up over multi-day trips. Dryzone panels at the head and foot resist the damp that collects where a bag touches tent walls or a bivy. Testers across the Exosphere range report no fill leakage and a bag that doesn't demand delicate handling, a contrast with down.

**Details.** The footbox gets extra insulation and room, the hood is contoured, and the zipper has an anti-snag guard. A draft collar and inner valuables pocket round out the list. The zip-together function lets two compatible bags join into a double, and the bag can be opened flat as a quilt, which suits van and camp use more than thru-hiking.

**Weight and packability.** This is the weak point. Deuter's lighter Exosphere -6° came in at 1,370 g, so the -11° carries roughly 575 g more for 5 degrees of limit rating. Hollow-fiber synthetic fill packs smaller than it used to, but it will still take up considerably more pack volume than a down bag of equal warmth. Ultralight hikers counting grams will notice.

**Value.** Deuter lists the -11° line at around €240 in Europe. UK reviewers consistently flag the price as a strong point against down bags of equal warmth, and Bergfreunde customers regularly cite price-to-performance as the main advantage.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- ThermoStretch construction suits side sleepers and restless sleepers, with up to 25% extra width
- Synthetic fill keeps insulating in damp conditions and tolerates rough handling
- Dryzone panels at head and foot guard against tent-wall condensation
- Comfort and limit ratings (-4 °C / -11 °C) cover most three-season and mild winter use
- Priced well below comparable down bags
- Recycled, bluesign-certified, PFAS-free materials

**Cons**

- 1,945 g is heavy by ultralight standards, and well beyond what a down bag of this rating weighs
- Bulkier packed than down
- Snug cut suits some sleepers less than others, and buyers on the women's version have called it tight
- Zip-together and quilt features matter little for solo backpackers

## Who Should Buy This

This bag suits trekkers, mountaineers and ski tourists who sleep in damp, cold places, side sleepers who find standard mummy bags confining, and anyone who wants a cold-rated bag without paying for down. It also works as a hardwearing winter bag for hut trips or car camping. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers chasing a low base weight should look at down quilts or bags, which save close to a kilogram at this temperature rating. Hikers who mostly camp above 0 °C will do better with the lighter Exosphere 0° or +4° models.

## Verdict

The Exosphere -11° is a comfortable, forgiving and fairly priced cold-weather synthetic bag, and the stretch construction does what Deuter claims. It is held back by weight and bulk that put it outside the ultralight conversation. Buy it for damp winter trekking and mountain use; skip it if every gram counts.
