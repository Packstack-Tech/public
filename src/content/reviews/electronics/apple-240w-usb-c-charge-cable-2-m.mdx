## Overview

This isn't a piece of gear you'll find in many ultralight packing lists, but it's earned a spot in mine as the one charging cable that can handle everything I bring on a trip — phone, power bank, and a USB-C laptop — without swapping cables. Apple's 240W USB-C Charge Cable is a 2-meter, braided USB-C to USB-C cable built to handle the higher-wattage charging protocols found in newer MacBook Pros and other high-draw devices. If you're the type who carries a MacBook or iPad along with your phone on resupply stops, it consolidates your cable bag into one cord instead of three.

## Performance

The headline number here is the 240W rating, and in practice it delivers on the charging side of things. <cite index="3-6">One Best Buy reviewer confirmed it can easily handle the 140W that a modern MacBook Pro charger can output</cite>, which matches my own experience running a MacBook Pro off this cable at hostels and trail towns — it charges just as fast as the cable that came in the box. If you're carrying a GaN charger capable of higher wattages for a laptop, this cable won't be the bottleneck.

Where it gets more nuanced is data transfer. Despite the beefy power rating, <cite index="4-4">it supports charging of up to 240 watts and transfers data at USB 2 rates</cite> — meaning if you're using it to dump photos off a phone or offload a full SD card from a camera at a resupply stop, you're capped at old-school USB 2 speeds, not the faster USB 3/Thunderbolt speeds you'd get from other USB-C cables. One user testing it with a Thunderbolt SSD enclosure found <cite index="3-9">a USB-4 M.2 SSD enclosure connected to a MacBook Pro M3 Max with this cable resulted in nothing</cite> — confirming it's strictly a charging/basic-data cable, not a high-speed data cable. If you need fast file transfers in the field, look elsewhere.

Durability is the trickiest part to nail down because user experiences diverge sharply. On the positive side, several buyers describe it as <cite index="6-6,6-7">feeling a little thicker than the cord that came with my iPad, with a braided cord that seems more flexible</cite> than Apple's older cables, and note it holding up over months of daily use. But there's a real minority report of failures — one aggregator review noted <cite index="1-1,1-2">multiple reports of the cable breaking or failing after very short use, sometimes as quickly as one month, with power output reportedly closer to 60W rather than the advertised 240W in some cases</cite>. It's worth noting some of those complaints trace back to third-party or open-box sellers rather than cables bought new and direct, so authenticity may be a factor. Best Buy's aggregated review summary is more favorable, describing customers as <cite index="2-4,2-5">enthusiastic about the cable's length, which allows for convenient phone use while charging, and its durability</cite>, though <cite index="2-6">a small number of users noted that the cable may generate heat during use</cite> — something I've also felt when pushing higher wattages through it on a warm day.

The 2-meter length is genuinely useful in hostels and shared bunk rooms where outlets are never where you want them, and it's long enough to charge a phone from a wall outlet while it sits on a top bunk. That said, at 93g it's noticeably heavier than a basic 1-foot or 3-foot USB-C cable — if all you're charging is a phone and you're counting grams, this is overkill.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Confirmed to handle 140W MacBook Pro charging without issue
- 2m length is genuinely convenient in hostels, hotel rooms, and shared spaces
- Braided jacket feels more durable than Apple's older rubber cables
- One cable covers phone, tablet, and laptop charging needs

**Cons**

- USB 2 data speeds only — not for fast file transfers
- 93g is heavy for a "just charge my phone" use case
- Durability reports are mixed; some users report failures within weeks
- Higher price than generic 240W cables with similar specs
- Some users report the cable running warm under heavier loads

## Who Should Buy This

This cable makes the most sense for backpackers already carrying a USB-C laptop (MacBook Pro/Air) alongside a phone and want to consolidate to a single charging cable capable of covering both, especially if you're doing long resupply stops with laptop work involved. If you're only charging a phone or small power bank, a shorter, lighter USB-C cable will save you weight and money with no real downside.

## Verdict

The Apple 240W cable does what it promises on the charging side — it comfortably handles high-wattage laptop charging and has held up well for most users, even if a vocal minority report early failures. For backpackers who need one cable to rule their phone, tablet, and laptop, it's a reasonable pick, but the USB 2 data cap and above-average weight mean it's not the default choice for everyone.
