## Overview

The LIFA Merino Midweight Crew is a fitted, two-layer base layer: a merino wool exterior bonded over a Helly Hansen LIFA polypropylene interior that sits against the skin. It is built for cold-weather work, such as ski touring, winter hiking and mountaineering. It is not built for shaving ounces. The headline trade-off is warmth and moisture handling in exchange for weight that sits well above a typical ultralight merino tee.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The 225 g/m² fabric is a true midweight, and testers treat it that way. A reviewer at Better Trail, who normally wears 100% synthetic light and midweight tops, said it felt noticeably heavier and closer to an expedition-weight synthetic. The review gave warmth 3.5 out of 5, which it described as slightly above average for the category. Retailer ratings from Helly Hansen's own spec sheet put insulation at 5 out of 6, breathability at 6 out of 6 and durability at 6 out of 6. For anyone planning to skip a midlayer on a cold morning, it does more of the work than a lightweight crew.

**Moisture management.** The two-layer build is the whole point. The LIFA interior is a mesh-style knit that pulls sweat off the skin, and the merino outside holds heat and breathes. Users who ski the backcountry in it describe the result as staying dry and warm without the clammy feel of single-layer wool after a hard climb. Polypropylene absorbs almost no water and dries fast, which is why this construction suits stop-and-go effort in the cold.

**Comfort.** Owner reports split on the skin feel. One long-term user called it non-itch and almost silky, with no tight spots and sleeves that reach the thumbs on a medium. Better Trail's tester was less charmed. That review said it isn't as soft against the skin as a full-merino top, and that the polypropylene liner isn't especially cozy. Flatlock seams keep chafe down under a pack's shoulder straps and hip belt. The consistent pattern is that people used to fine-gauge merino notice the synthetic liner, while people coming from polyester find it pleasant.

**Odor and durability.** Merino on the outside helps, but the liner is polypropylene, and Better Trail flagged that polypro retains odors. On a multi-day trip without a wash, that is a concern. Durability is solid rather than exceptional. The same review said it lacks the extra toughness of a true merino-synthetic blend. The line carries a two-year warranty.

**Weight.** This is the weak point for a thru-hiker. At 221 to 350 g, it weighs several times what a 150 g/m² merino tee does, and it earns its place only when temperatures justify that mass. In shoulder-season heat it is overkill, and the thick knit will feel it.

## Choosing a Version

The Men's version is the lighter of the two at 221 g. The Standard version is 350 g. Fabric, construction and the 57/43 merino-polypropylene blend are the same across the line, so the choice comes down to fit and how much weight you are willing to carry. Because the cut is fitted, anyone who plans to layer a thick fleece over it, or who sits between sizes, should size to the body rather than to the weight. Gram-counters should lean toward the Men's version, and anyone who wants the most material between skin and shell should take the Standard.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Two-layer design moves sweat off the skin while merino holds heat
- Midweight 225 g/m² fabric can replace a midlayer in cold, dry conditions
- Flatlock seams and a fitted cut layer cleanly under packs and shells
- Priced at $105, below many merino-synthetic blends
- Machine washable and dryer-safe

**Cons**

- Heavy for an ultralight kit, up to 350 g
- Polypropylene liner can hold odor over multi-day use
- Less soft against bare skin than full-merino designs, according to some testers
- Durability trails the best synthetic and nylon-core blends
- Too warm for three-season hiking

## Who Should Buy This

Winter hikers, ski tourers, mountaineers and cold-camp backpackers who sweat hard on climbs and chill fast at stops will get the most from it. It also suits anyone who wants one warm top that doubles as camp insulation under a shell. Warm-weather thru-hikers, and anyone who counts every ounce or wears a base layer for days without washing, should look at a lighter all-merino tee or a fine-gauge synthetic.

## Verdict

The LIFA Merino Midweight Crew does what Helly Hansen says: it keeps a sweating body dry and warm in the cold, at a fair price. It is a winter workhorse rather than a thru-hiking staple, and its weight and polypropylene liner keep it out of the ultralight core kit. Buy it for cold-season trips, and pass if the trail is warm.
