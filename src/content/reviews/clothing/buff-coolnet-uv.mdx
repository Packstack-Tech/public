## Overview

The Buff CoolNet UV is a seamless polyester tube that does the work of a bandana, sun hood, headband and neck shade for 34 g and about $25. Buff builds it from recycled REPREVE microfiber and treats it with HeiQ cooling and Polygiene odor control. It's aimed at hot-weather hikers, runners and anglers. The trade-off is simple: a fabric layer around the neck is still a fabric layer, so the cooling is a modest assist rather than air conditioning.

## Performance

**Sun protection.** This is the CoolNet UV's strongest argument. The fabric is rated UPF 50+, which Buff describes as blocking about 98 percent of UV. The rating comes from testing to the AS/NZS 4399:2017 standard, and the gaiter carries a CE classification as personal protective equipment against UV. The Skin Cancer Foundation also recommends it. Coverage depends on how it's worn. As a neck gaiter it shades the neck and jaw. Pulled up over the nose or worn as a hood, it also covers the cheeks, ears and scalp. Buff also notes that protection can weaken with wear, so a gaiter that's years old and stretched thin is a weaker sun layer than a new one.

**Cooling.** HeiQ Smart Temp is a treatment that responds to body heat. Buff describes it as triggered by rising body temperature to help regulate comfort, and the company markets the CoolNet UV as its pick for natural cooling. On the trail, that translates to a fabric that wicks and dries quickly. Dunk it in a stream, wring it out and wear it wet, and evaporation does the rest. Worn dry at a steady pace, the effect is subtle. A tube of polyester around the neck is warmer than bare skin, and the case for wearing one is the UV protection, not the thermodynamics. On exposed desert or alpine miles that trade is usually worth making.

**Odor.** The Polygiene treatment uses a low concentration of silver salt to inhibit odor-causing microorganisms. For multi-day trips, that means a gaiter that stays tolerable between washes, which matters for a piece of kit worn against the neck and face every day. Buff's care instructions rule out the dryer, and air-drying is the safer way to preserve the treatments. A tube this light dries in an hour or two on a pack strap.

**Versatility and fit.** Buff counts 12-plus ways to wear it, from neck gaiter and headband to balaclava, hair tie and sun cap. In practice, hikers lean on three or four: neck shade in the morning, headband on climbs, hood under a brimmed hat when the sun gets serious. The seamless construction avoids the rubbing that stitched alternatives can cause. The four-way stretch is generous, though the fit comes up in reviews on both sides. Some wearers with larger heads find it snug, while REI buyers with small heads point to Buff's junior size. Buyers on REI rate it 4.6 out of 5 across 303 reviews, with 88 percent recommending it. Complaints tend to be about value and finishing. Some owners find $25 steep for a tube of fabric, and a few note that the cut edges are left unhemmed, which raises questions about long-term durability. One buyer reported using it as a balaclava in bitter cold and found it warm enough for the job.

**Sustainability.** The fabric is 95 percent recycled polyester made from post-consumer plastic bottles, and Buff makes its gaiters at its Barcelona facility.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 34 g, light enough to ignore on a gear list
- UPF 50+ with third-party testing and Skin Cancer Foundation backing
- Seamless, stretchy construction with no chafing seams
- HeiQ cooling and quick-dry fabric work well when worn damp
- Polygiene odor control suits multi-day trips
- Handles neck, head, face and hair duties
- Recycled fabric; about $25

**Cons**

- Warmer than bare skin; the cooling effect is modest worn dry
- Dryer use is off the table, and care is more fussy than the price suggests
- Cut edges are unhemmed, and some owners worry about durability
- Fit can be snug on large heads and loose on small ones
- UV protection can weaken with wear, so it's worth replacing when it thins
- Not a substitute for a brimmed hat on its own

## Who Should Buy This

The CoolNet UV suits hikers and thru-hikers heading into sun-exposed terrain such as desert, alpine and open ridgeline, plus trail runners and anglers who want sun coverage without committing to a hat. It also earns a slot as a sub-two-ounce multi-tool on shoulder-season trips. Hikers who spend most of their miles under tree cover, or who just need a cheap headband, can skip the cooling and UV treatments and buy Buff's simpler Original EcoStretch. Anyone expecting a noticeably cooler neck at a steady walk should adjust that expectation.

## Verdict

The CoolNet UV is a cheap, light and well-built answer to sun exposure, with a UPF rating backed by testing and an odor treatment that suits long trips. The cooling claim is the weakest part of the pitch, but the quick-dry fabric and 12 wear styles make up for it. Hikers heading into hot, exposed terrain should pack one.
