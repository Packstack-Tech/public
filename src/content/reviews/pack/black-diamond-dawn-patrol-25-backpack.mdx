## Overview

The Dawn Patrol 25 is Black Diamond's do-it-all ski touring daypack, built for dawn-patrol laps and short backcountry missions where you need to carry an avy kit, skins, water, and a few extra layers without hauling around a 40L monster. It's aimed at backcountry and sidecountry skiers/splitboarders who want a dedicated, organized pack rather than a generic daypack pressed into ski duty.

## Performance

The headline feature is the zippered back-panel access, and it's the detail that shows up most in reviewer feedback. Instead of digging through a top-loader while your pack sits in the snow, you unzip the panel and lay the whole thing open on a flat surface — one reviewer specifically called out that this lets you <cite index="6-11">set the bag down in snow without getting the back panel covered with snow and ice</cite>. On a windy transition at a col, that's the difference between a 90-second layer swap and a five-minute one spent shaking snow out of your pack.

The front snow-safety pocket is another well-liked feature — it keeps your shovel, probe, and beacon separated from food and layers, and multiple testers noted the pack's <cite index="2-13">convenient front snow-safety pocket for avalanche gear and an oversized, insulated shoulder pocket to keep a hydration tube or radio dry and warm</cite>. That insulated hose routing matters more than it sounds — I've had hydration lines freeze solid on cold tours in packs without any insulation on the tube, and having that pocket dedicated to keeping water flowing is a real quality-of-life upgrade on sub-freezing days.

Skin storage is handled well too. One GearJunkie tester described transitioning at the top of a run and noting he <cite index="11-10">removed my skins, stuck them easily into the pack's interior, and then carved early-season turns down the slope</cite> — and the hip belt pockets are large enough to stash skins or snacks, with reviewers confirming <cite index="2-14">large hip-belt pockets can hold snacks or skins for downhill use</cite>, though one review flagged that the <cite index="2-17">waist-strap pockets are difficult to access while wearing the pack</cite> — worth knowing before you count on reaching them one-handed mid-skin-track.

On the fit side, the suspension gets consistent praise for how it disappears on your back. Black Diamond's own product page quotes a user saying the pack has <cite index="6-2,6-3">"all the storage you need, exactly where you need it, without any of the bulk. Materials down to the straps and padding are high quality and durable."</cite> That said, fit isn't universal — a shorter user on the same page noted <cite index="6-18,6-19">"I'm 5'1 and use ski packs quite frequently. I have a hard time with the size of this pack."</cite> If you're on the smaller end of the size spectrum, try it on loaded before you commit.

Carry versatility is solid for the category: it handles <cite index="2-15">skis, snowboards, a helmet, and ice axes</cite>, and one Freeskier tester who ran it across three Banff resorts found <cite index="3-8,3-9">the compact nature of the Dawn Patrol was impressive — no matter how much I stuffed in there, it zipped up nicely and without much added bulk</cite>. That same reviewer also liked the oversized front pouch, noting <cite index="3-11,3-12">the pack also comes with an oversized goggle pouch — while great for stowing goggles, it's also large enough to stow smaller items like a beanie, sunglasses, facemask</cite>.

The main tradeoff seems to be capacity ceiling. A separate review summary noted that <cite index="8-5">despite some users wanting more compartments and a little more space, the high-quality materials, small profile, and functional pockets make this a good option for those who don't need to carry too much gear</cite>. In other words: this is a day-tour pack, not a hut-trip or multi-day pack, and it's not trying to be.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Back-panel access makes transitions fast and keeps snow out of the main compartment
- Dedicated, insulated avy-tool and hydration organization — genuinely useful in cold, wet conditions
- Versatile ski/snowboard/ice-axe carry options for one pack
- Recycled main-body fabric without sacrificing durability, per long-term users
- Compact profile that doesn't feel bulky on lifts or in bounds

**Cons**

- Hip-belt pockets are hard to reach while the pack is on your back
- 25L is tight if you're carrying much beyond a standard day-tour kit
- Fit runs less forgiving for shorter/smaller-framed skiers
- At 1,040 g it's not the lightest 25L option if pure weight-saving is your priority

## Who Should Buy This

This pack is built for backcountry and sidecountry skiers and splitboarders doing single-day tours — dawn patrol laps, short skin-track missions, or resort days where you still want avy gear organized and accessible. If your trips regularly stretch beyond a single day or you're hauling group gear, look at something in BD's larger Dawn Patrol siblings; this one's sized and organized specifically for the shorter mission.

## Verdict

The Dawn Patrol 25 earns its reputation as a go-to day-touring pack: the back-panel access and avy-kit organization solve real problems on cold, snowy transitions, and the build quality holds up over repeated seasons. It's not the lightest 25L on the market and the hip pockets could be more accessible, but for a dedicated one-day backcountry pack, it's a well-executed 8/10.
