## Overview

The Yamatomichi Cap is the Japanese cottage brand's Stretch Mesh Cap with a different face: the same ultralight polyester construction, now carrying a logo embroidery designed by the artist Naomi Kazama. It weighs 33 to 38 g depending on size, shields the head with UPF50+ fabric, and can be adjusted with one hand in a gust. The trade-off is straightforward. Buyers get a cap that is nearly weightless and well made, and they pay slightly more than the plain version for the embroidery.

## Performance

**Weight and feel.** At 33 g in the smallest size, this cap is about 1 g heavier than the standard Stretch Mesh Cap (32 g). Yamatomichi's founder, Akira Natsume, says he designed it so that every panel stretches. The idea is that the cap stays comfortable on the move and even when worn to sleep. The brand's pitch is that it feels like barely anything is on your head, and the weight supports that claim. Merino alternatives in the same line run 37 to 43 g, and Yamatomichi's own Merino Cap was developed because the mesh cap ran cold in winter.

**Sun protection.** The main fabric is UPF50+ polyester. The mesh panels are the weak point for sun exposure. They exist to move air, not to block UV, so the crown of the cap is the most breathable part and the least shielding. For desert or alpine exposure, a wide brim covers more skin, and Yamatomichi sells a Stretch Mesh Hat for that job. For forest and ridge hiking, the cap's brim and UPF fabric handle the forehead, eyes and scalp.

**Brim.** The shape-memory brim is the cap's best trick. It can be bent into a curve or a flat peak, and it returns to flat after being stuffed into a pack. Retailers highlight that it holds its shape after being stashed in a backpack, which matters for a cap that will spend a lot of time compressed in a lid pocket. Yamatomichi also says the brim is thin and flexible enough to resist bending back in strong wind. In practice this means a light brim that does not flip or crease permanently.

**Wind and adjustment.** A bungee adjuster at the back can be tightened or loosened with one hand, so a cap can be cinched down on an exposed ridge without taking off a glove or a trekking pole strap. The elastic panels do most of the fit work; the adjuster handles the rest. Yamatomichi's pitch is that it stays on in strong winds. A bungee adjuster is a lower-profile solution than a plastic snap or a hook-and-loop strap, and it lies flat against a pack's hip belt or a head rest.

**Wet and dry.** The polyester and mesh blend dries quickly after rain or a river rinse, according to the manufacturer. Care is simple: machine wash gently in a laundry net with neutral detergent, skip fabric softener and bleach, shape the brim with your fingers, and line dry in shade. Tumble drying risks shrinkage.

**Durability.** This is a 33 g cap made of thin stretch fabric, so it is not built to be stepped on or jammed under straps all day. Yamatomichi offers repair services for its products, which helps with a cap that will take sweat and sun for seasons.

## Choosing a Version

Sizing is the only real variable, and it matters because the cap relies on stretch fabric and an adjuster rather than a snapback. Yamatomichi lists three sizes: S at 33 g, M at 35 g and L at 38 g. The weight difference is trivial, so choose by head size.

- **Standard (S), 33 g:** For smaller heads. Yamatomichi's fit guide for the matching Stretch Mesh Cap shows size S on a model with a 53 cm head circumference.
- **Medium, 35 g:** The right pick for most adult heads. The same guide shows size M on a 57 cm head.
- **Large, 38 g:** For larger heads. The guide shows L on a 57.5 cm head, so the step up from M is small, and anyone between M and L should check the size chart before buying.

Because the bungee tightens the fit, going up a size is safer than going down for people who run hot or wear a cap over a buff or a thin beanie.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 33–38 g, and the stretch construction makes it feel lighter than that
- UPF50+ main fabric
- Shape-memory brim recovers after being packed
- One-hand bungee adjuster works with gloves
- Quick-drying polyester and mesh
- Machine washable
- Embroidered design

**Cons**

- Costs more than the otherwise identical Stretch Mesh Cap (TW$1,397 versus TW$1,182 on Yamatomichi's Taiwan site)
- Mesh panels give less UV coverage than solid fabric
- Not warm; Yamatomichi itself made the Merino Cap for winter
- Narrow brim offers no neck or ear coverage
- Thin fabric will not survive rough treatment
- Limited retail availability outside Japan and Taiwan, with restocks selling out

## Who Should Buy This

This cap suits hikers who count grams and want a cap they can wear all day without noticing it: PCT and JMT walkers in hot, dry summers, Japan Alps trekkers in humid weather, and runners and fastpackers who sweat heavily. It also suits anyone who likes Yamatomichi's gear and wants the logo. Hikers who need full neck and ear coverage should buy the Stretch Mesh Hat. Those who want a plain look and a lower price should take the Stretch Mesh Cap, which is the same cap. Cold-weather hikers should look at the Merino Cap or a knit hat.

## Verdict

The Yamatomichi Cap does what a hiking cap should: it covers the sun, dries fast, stays on and disappears on the head. The shape-memory brim and one-hand adjuster are small design choices that pay off daily on trail. Buy it for the embroidery if the logo appeals, or buy the Stretch Mesh Cap for the same performance at a lower price.
