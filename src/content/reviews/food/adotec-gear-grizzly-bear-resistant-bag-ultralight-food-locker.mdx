## Overview

The Adotec Grizzly Bear Resistant Bag, sold by Adotec as the Ultralight Bear Locker and carried by Zpacks, is a 14-liter soft-sided food sack built from thick woven Dyneema. It carries an Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee certification (#245493), a credential that until recently belonged almost exclusively to the Ursack line among soft bags. At 199 g, it undercuts a hard canister by well over a kilogram. The trade-off is price and a break-in period: the fabric starts out stiff, and the bag costs more than most food storage a hiker will ever buy.

## Performance

**Protection.** The bag's credibility rests on its fabric. Backpacker's testers noted that the 6 oz/sqyd Dyneema is roughly twice as thick as the cloth used in the burliest Dyneema packs, and that it is backed by triple-stitched, sealed seams and reinforced panels at the top and bottom. The IGBC certification is the independent check: the committee's protocol is aimed at grizzlies, and containers that satisfy black bears alone may not hold up against them. Backpacker concluded the Adotec is just as secure as the Ursack, the soft-bag default for years, while being lighter and easier to use.

One caveat applies to every soft bag. It resists teeth and claws, but it doesn't make a rigid shell, so a determined bear can still crush what's inside. Hikers heading to places that mandate hard-sided canisters, such as Yosemite, still need one.

**Weight.** Adotec lists the 14L at 199 g (7.0 oz). Reviewers who weighed their own samples report slightly lower figures, between roughly 6.6 and 6.8 oz. Either way, it is the lightest IGBC-certified food bag available, and a gap of more than a kilogram separates it from the 2–4 lb typical of a canister.

**Capacity.** Backpacker's testers found the 14-liter size holds about as much food as a BearVault BV500 canister and handles five days of food for one hiker. Other reviewers put it at four to five days, so hikers resupplying every week should plan on compact, calorie-dense food and a tight pack job. The push-in corners create a flat bottom that helps the bag sit upright in camp and pack cleanly. At 9 in x 13 in packed, it rides horizontally in 50–70 L packs and vertically in smaller ones.

**Weather and odor.** The waterproof coating and taped seams keep out most moisture, which is something the Ursack Major cannot claim. For wet stretches, Zpacks suggests adding a "Big" Nylopro liner bag, which also adds odor resistance. The Adotec itself isn't odor-proof, so hikers who care about scent control should plan on that liner.

**Break-in.** New bags arrive stiff. Zpacks warns that the cinch closure may need working in before it closes fully the first time, and says the fabric becomes more flexible and easier to pack with use. Reviewers echo this: the first trip is the fussy one, and the bag gets easier from there.

**Use in camp.** The system is simple. Cinch the top, tie a figure-8 knot around a tree, and walk away. One backcountry reviewer described a bag that campers hauled back out of the snow for breakfast after a night outside, a small scene that sums up the appeal: no canister to wrestle, no hanging rig, no hunting for the right branch.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- IGBC grizzly-certified at about 7 oz, the lightest in its class
- Waterproof Dyneema with sealed, triple-stitched seams
- Holds roughly a canister's worth of food, about five days for one person
- Packs down and fits horizontally in 50–70 L packs
- Rodent resistant, with no hanging rope or bear-bag technique needed

**Cons**

- Expensive: Adotec lists the 14L at $163
- Stiff out of the box, with a break-in period for the closure
- Not odor-proof without a separate liner
- Doesn't prevent crushed food, and doesn't satisfy hard-canister regulations
- Only the 14L carries IGBC certification

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and section hikers in grizzly country, or anywhere a bear-resistant bag is accepted, who want to cut canister weight and can accept the cost. That includes much of the Northern Rockies, the Pacific Northwest, and Alaska-bound itineraries where a soft bag is allowed. It suits solo hikers on resupply cycles of about five days. Anyone hiking in an area that requires a hard-sided canister should skip it, and so should hikers on strict budgets, those carrying food for a week or more, and anyone who wants scent protection built in.

## Verdict

The Adotec is the lightest IGBC-certified food bag on the market, and its waterproofing and Dyneema build set it apart from the Ursack. Expect a stiff first trip and a steep price. For ultralight hikers in grizzly country where bags are allowed, it's the one to buy.
