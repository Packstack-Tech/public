## Overview

The Helium 55 is Bergans of Norway's long-running answer to the question of how little pack a 55-liter load actually needs. It is a top-loader with a single aluminum stay, an adjustable-length back, a removable lid and a full-length front zipper, built for fast multi-day trekking and ski touring. The trade-off is familiar: it carries heavier loads better than its weight suggests, but the padding and the zipper are where Bergans saved grams.

## Performance

**Carry.** The Helium's reputation rests on how much load it moves for the weight. Backpacker testers took it on big-load dayhikes and multiday trips with up to 35 pounds, and two of them chose it for a seven-day, 168-mile self-supported stage race in Arizona and Utah. They reported that the stiff framesheet passed weight efficiently to a lightly padded hipbelt and shoulder straps, even on back-to-back 30-mile days. SectionHiker's reviewer called the load-to-hip transfer responsive. The single aluminum stay can be pulled out and bent to match the wearer's back, which owners with non-standard builds cite as the pack's best feature. One owner took an earlier-generation Helium on a two-week High Sierra trip at roughly 45 pounds at the start and said it handled the weight.

The limits show up at the contact points. A UK reviewer found the hipbelt material and padding less comfortable under a 15 kg load, and described the shoulder straps as slightly abrasive at times. A retailer review of the current V5 generation also criticized the comfort and adjustment range. The pack suits a base weight that stays light and a total load in the low-to-mid teens of kilograms. Above that, the padding becomes the limiting factor.

**Access and storage.** The top opening is wide, and the full-length front zipper means a sleeping bag or a buried stove doesn't require unpacking the whole bag from the top. One owner called the zipper the feature he'd never give up on a pack again. The lid floats on adjustable straps, so extra gear can be wedged between lid and bag. The two stretch-mesh side pockets are large enough to swallow a sit pad or a small tent, according to Active-Traveller. The pack has no hipbelt pockets, so anything needed on the move goes in the lid or the side pockets.

**Weather.** The front zipper is the weak point. SectionHiker reported that the long zip leaks in heavy rain, which makes a pack liner or dry bags a requirement in wet climates rather than an option. Cold, dry conditions suit the pack better.

**Fabric and durability.** Earlier Helium generations used 100- and 210-denier Cordura, and owners called the fabric rugged where it counts. The current fifth-generation design uses a proprietary Nylon 66 and Dyneema ripstop that Bergans describes as very tough. Owners of earlier versions reported no visible damage after trips with strapped-on skis and rough rock.

**Ski and alpine use.** Attachment points for ice axes, poles and skis give the pack credibility on winter and alpine trips, and its compression system lets the volume shrink for shorter outings. The cord compression can be set up to run down the sides or across the front. Reviewers found it a little fiddly to rig and prone to tangling, though they judged it worthwhile.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 930 g without the lid is very light for 55 liters of internal-frame capacity
- Carries loads of about 15 kg well for its weight, with an adjustable torso length and a bendable stay
- Full-length front zipper gives access to the whole bag
- Removable lid saves 70 g
- Large stretch side pockets and ski, axe and pole attachment points
- Compression system lets the pack shrink for shorter trips

**Cons**

- Hipbelt and shoulder-strap padding is thin and grows uncomfortable under heavier loads
- Front zipper leaks in sustained heavy rain
- No hipbelt pockets
- Compression cord is fiddly to set up and can tangle
- Not a good fit for long carries with bulky, heavy gear

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want one pack for summer trekking and winter or ski-touring trips will get the most from the Helium. So will anyone who keeps the total load around 12–15 kg and wants more capacity and more attachment points than a typical frameless ultralight pack offers. Hikers who routinely carry heavier loads, or who need a padded hipbelt for big food carries, should look at a sturdier suspension. Wet-climate thru-hikers who don't want to rely on a liner should choose a roll-top pack with no long zipper.

## Verdict

The Helium 55 delivers a structured, adjustable-fit 55-liter pack at about a kilogram, and it does so with more carrying ability than its weight suggests. Buy it for cold, dry or alpine trips with disciplined loads. Skip it if you carry heavy or hike mostly in the rain.
