## Overview

The AVABAG LiTRIC Base is Ortovox's electronic avalanche airbag platform — co-developed with Arc'teryx — that you buy once and then zip onto whatever pack shell fits your day, whether that's a lean 18L sidecountry lap pack or a 40L multi-day touring hauler. It's built for backcountry skiers, splitboarders, and snowmobilers who want a reusable, rechargeable airbag system without the hassle of compressed air canisters, airline refill headaches, or a completely new pack every time their trip length changes.

## Performance

The headline number here is 1,100 grams, and it holds up as one of the lightest electronic airbag systems you can buy. <cite index="6-13">The LiTRIC system weighs 1100g, while the E2 arrives at 1190g, which includes two AA batteries</cite> — not a massive gap on paper, but it's noticeable when you're comparing loaded pack weights side by side at the trailhead. Reviewers consistently point to this as one of the system's biggest wins: <cite index="11-4">both testers agreed that the Litric was the first airbag pack they'd tested that wasn't infuriatingly clunky, difficult to pack, bulky, or heavy thanks to the reduced battery and airbag size</cite>.

Deployment reliability is where an airbag system either earns your trust or doesn't, and the LiTRIC seems to have nailed the basics. One tester described the process bluntly: <cite index="13-9">after a quick charge with the included USB-C cable, the charge indicator turned green and I was able to power on the inflation device, rotate the trigger to arm the system, and deploy the airbag</cite>. That same reviewer noted <cite index="13-10">figuring all this out without glancing at the owner's manual</cite> — a good sign for a piece of gear you need to operate correctly under stress. Ortovox also backs the multi-deploy claim with real specs: the system is <cite index="1-1">lightweight, technically sophisticated and reliable and enables at least two releases with just one charge</cite>.

Charging is a mixed bag. The USB-C port is a genuine convenience — <cite index="1-7">the advantage is that the LiTRIC can be charged with a USB-C cable and no specific cable is required</cite> — meaning you're not hunting for a proprietary brick at 5 a.m. before a dawn patrol. But full charges take longer than some competitors; one longtime tester noted <cite index="1-6">this took longer than I thought, at least when I compare it with my Arc'teryx Voltair</cite>. Budget for a full battery the night before, not a quick top-off in the parking lot. Once charged, the 60-hour runtime claim seems to hold up in practice for multi-day use, with one tester noting <cite index="1-9,1-10">a tour lasting several days should also be possible, and Ortovox claims 60 hours, which certainly wasn't maxed out with regular at-home charging</cite>.

The modular zip-on design is the other major selling point, and it works as advertised. <cite index="2-3">With the ability to deploy multiple times on a single charge and to switch out a zip-on pack from 18 liters to 40 liters in the normal frame size, and 16 liters to 36 liters in the small frame size, the Ortovox AVABAG LiTRIC TOUR is simply one of the most lightweight and versatile avalanche airbag packs out there</cite>. On the carry side, though, don't expect it to disappear the way a dedicated non-modular pack might — <cite index="1-3,1-4">the carrying properties and ski holder work very well, though there are slight compromises compared to large rucksacks without an interchangeable system that are also designed for heavier loads</cite>. OutdoorGearLab's testers were even more enthusiastic about comfort on the Freeride variant, praising it as <cite index="7-13">among the most comfortable options, primarily because of the wide and supportive hip belt and effective adjustment points</cite>.

One thing worth flagging honestly: this system had a rocky launch. <cite index="4-8,4-9">Ortovox issued a recall notice due to issues with the fan's airshield, and the airshield now arrives with two small screws to secure it in place</cite>. That's now resolved in current production units, but it's a reminder that electronic airbags are still a relatively young category, and even well-funded co-development between Ortovox and Arc'teryx wasn't immune to a hardware hiccup in year one. If you're buying secondhand, confirm the unit is post-recall.

Air travel is another common pain point for electronic airbags, and the LiTRIC handles it reasonably well despite the non-removable battery. <cite index="4-5,4-6">Ortovox instructs that the LiTRIC system be switched off, secured against accidental activation, and carried as hand luggage, noting that the fixed lithium-ion battery meets international safety standards for air travel</cite>.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**
- Genuinely light for an electronic airbag base — among the lightest supercapacitor systems on the market
- USB-C charging means no proprietary cable to lose or forget
- At least 2 deployments per charge, with a claimed 60-hour runtime
- Modular zip-on system lets one base serve multiple pack sizes and trip types
- TÜV certified and EN 16716 compliant, with straightforward air-travel documentation
- Intuitive arm/deploy interface — reviewers figured it out without a manual

**Cons:**
- Full charge time is noticeably slower than some competing systems
- Slight carry-comfort compromise versus non-modular, purpose-built packs, especially under heavier loads
- Early production units were subject to a recall over a fan airshield issue
- At roughly $1,400 for a complete pack, it's a serious investment
- Fixed, non-removable battery may still raise questions at airport security despite documentation

## Who Should Buy This

This system makes the most sense for backcountry skiers and riders who get into complex, avalanche-exposed terrain regularly enough to justify the cost, and who want one airbag investment that can flex between a light sidecountry lap pack and a bigger multi-day touring rig. If you're brand new to the backcountry and stick to mellow, low-consequence terrain a couple days a season, the case is less clear-cut — as one reviewer put it, <cite index="2-11">it might not be needed for someone who's new to backcountry skiing and going into backcountry one or two days a year on safe slopes</cite>.

## Verdict

The AVABAG LiTRIC Base delivers on its core promise: a genuinely light, reliable, multi-deploy electronic airbag system that doesn't force you into a single pack size. The recall history and slower charge times are worth knowing about going in, but current units address the hardware issue, and the modularity plus USB-C convenience make this one of the more practical electronic airbag platforms available today. I'd put it at an 8.5/10 — excellent for its intended audience, docked slightly for charge speed and the bumpy early rollout.
