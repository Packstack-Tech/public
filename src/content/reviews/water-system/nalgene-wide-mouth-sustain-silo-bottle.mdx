## Overview

The Wide Mouth Sustain Silo is Nalgene's 48-ounce bottle, molded in Tritan Renew, a copolyester the company says is derived from 50% plastic waste using ISCC-certified mass balance accounting. It's the same wide-mouth, loop-top design that has ridden in backpack side pockets for decades, stretched tall enough to hold about 1.4 to 1.5 liters. The trade-off is plain: it's tougher and easier to live with than a soft bottle, and at 209 g it's also nearly 75 g heavier than Nalgene's own HDPE Ultralite in the same size.

## Performance

**Capacity and carry.** At 285 mm tall and 89 mm across, the Silo slides into most pack side pockets, though it fills the pocket more completely than a 32-ounce bottle. One buyer on a deal forum noted the extra height has a practical side effect: the 32-ounce size tends to sink to the bottom of a pack, while the 48 stays within reach. The same commenter pointed out that the wide mouth makes it easy to top off a hydration bladder from a single bottle, which suits dry stretches where water is cached or carried in bulk.

**Filter and mouth compatibility.** The 63 mm wide-mouth neck is the de facto standard for backcountry water treatment. Retailer descriptions say it works with most major purifiers and filters, and the wide opening takes ice cubes, drink powder or a scoop of cold-soak oats without fuss. The molded mL and oz markings let it double as a measuring cup for dinner, and the bottle handles boiling water, so a hot-water bottle in a cold sleeping bag is a legitimate off-label use. Rounded interior corners make it easy to scrub, and it's dishwasher safe.

**Durability.** Tritan is a hard, clear copolyester, and owners describe the bottle as sturdy. One long-time Nalgene user reported carrying a bottle for more than twenty years before it finally cracked in a fall onto rocks. The record isn't spotless, though. On Nalgene's own product page, the Silo averages 3.33 out of 5 across nine ratings, dragged down by a reviewer whose clear 48-ounce bottle shattered when dropped from waist height, and another who reported a lingering chemical smell after a year of hand washing. Retail listings elsewhere run far higher: Walmart shows 4.8 out of 5 on the same nine ratings, and the older HDPE Ultralite holds a 4.7 on REI. The sample sizes are small, but the shattering complaint is worth knowing about if the bottle is headed for rocky terrain in freezing temperatures.

**Weight.** This is where the Silo loses ground with the ultralight crowd. Nalgene rates the HDPE Ultralite 48 at 134.75 g (4.75 oz), and REI reviewers measured their examples at about 5.3 oz versus roughly 7 oz for the Tritan version. A collapsible bottle or a recycled soda bottle carries the same water for a fraction of the weight. What the Silo offers in return is stiffness: it stands on its own, doesn't flop around while filling from a trickle, and won't be punctured by a stray tent stake. The HDPE version, by comparison, is described by owners as noticeably softer.

**Small details.** The loop-top cap keeps the lid tethered, though the cap is made in China while the bottle is American-made, and replacement caps run $4.41 on Nalgene's site. One Ultralite owner reported snapping the cap strap on day one when a child carried the 48-ounce bottle by it, a reminder that the loop is a lid tether, not a handle. There's no insulating sleeve available for this size, a gap noted by at least one buyer. Several owners pair the bottle with drink-through inserts to avoid dribbling; the wide mouth is a known weakness for sipping on the move.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Standard 63 mm mouth fits most filters and fills quickly
- Rigid, upright shape is easy to fill from shallow sources
- Withstands boiling water and subzero temperatures
- Molded mL and oz markings work for meal prep
- Leakproof threads; dishwasher safe and easy to clean
- Bottle made in the USA from material derived from 50% plastic waste

**Cons**

- 209 g, about 74 g more than Nalgene's own HDPE Ultralite
- Some owners report cracking or shattering on hard drops
- A few reports of lingering plastic odor
- Wide mouth is messy to drink from while walking
- Large footprint in a side pocket when full
- Costs more than the standard Tritan 48 ounce in the Nalgene lineup

## Who Should Buy This

The Silo suits section hikers, base-camp users, bikepackers and anyone who wants one rugged, filter-compatible vessel for dry carries or camp water storage. It also fits hikers who value a bottle that can take boiling water and be scrubbed clean, and who aren't chasing a sub-10-pound base weight. Thru-hikers counting every ounce, or anyone who sips on the move, should look at the HDPE Ultralite, a soft bottle or a repurposed commercial bottle. Buyers who simply want a 48-ounce Nalgene and don't care about recycled content can find the standard Tritan version for less.

## Verdict

The Sustain Silo is a well-made, familiar bottle that does exactly what a 48-ounce Nalgene always has, with a recycled-content story attached. For ultralight use, the weight penalty and the mixed durability reports keep it from being a first choice. Buy it for camp and big-volume carries; skip it for a gram-conscious thru-hike kit.
