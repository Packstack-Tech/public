## Overview

The JetSet is a three-piece nylon utensil kit: a spoon, a fork and a spatula, each with a telescoping handle that collapses to 5.2 inches and extends to 8.5. It was designed around the tall, narrow cups of Jetboil canister stoves and the deep corners of freeze-dried pouches, and it does that job for about $11. The trade-off is the mechanism. The handles collapse neatly, but owners say they don't always stay extended.

## Performance

**Reach.** This is the reason to buy the kit. At 8.5 inches, the spoon and fork get to the bottom of a dehydrated-meal pouch or a Jetboil cup without knuckles in the gravy. One Trailspace reviewer used the set with a tall non-Jetboil pot and found it reached and stirred well. Another liked that the long handles keep hands farther from a stove or fire. Collapsed, the 5.2-inch stack fits in a small zip-top bag, and the extended pieces slide into a meal pouch. A Dick's Sporting Goods buyer reported that it didn't scratch nonstick pots.

**Weight.** At 37 g for three pieces, the set is light, but not the lightest way to eat. A single titanium long-handle spoon weighs roughly the same as one of these nylon pieces. The JetSet's case rests on getting a fork and a spatula along with the spoon for about the weight of two ounces' worth of gear. It is not a case for gram-counting. Solo thru-hikers who eat cold-soaked meals or rehydrate in a pouch will find the spatula to be dead weight.

**Heat.** Jetboil describes the nylon as high-temperature, and most owners use it for stirring and eating without trouble. Not all of them. One CampSaver reviewer said the spatula melted the first time it was used to flip a pancake in a hot pan. Field & Stream, which named the kit its best set, noted that it feels flimsier than metal utensils and may not suit very high heat. The practical limit is stirring boiling water and sauces. A hot, dry pan is outside the set's range.

**The locking problem.** The most common complaint across a decade of reviews is that the sections slide apart too easily. A Trailspace reviewer in 2014 reported that a spatula handle came apart and ended up at the bottom of a bag. Another in 2023 gave the set 2.5 stars because the sections did not lock properly. Others call the set sturdy and durable, and Amazon buyers give it 4.4 stars across more than 1,300 ratings. A Dick's reviewer's tally was less flattering: 4.0 stars, with 74 percent recommending it. The sections hold in light use. A sharp tug while scraping a pouch can collapse them.

**Cleaning and durability.** Owners report that the pieces rinse easily and hold up better than expected for plastic, including through rough handling on short trips. Long-term durability reports are thin on the metal-versus-nylon question. One buyer wondered aloud how long non-metal pieces would last.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 8.5-inch reach for deep cups and meal pouches
- Collapses to 5.2 inches
- 37 g for three utensils
- Safe on nonstick cookware
- Easy to wash
- Inexpensive, with a one-year warranty

**Cons**

- Sections can slide apart under load
- Nylon is not suited to hot, dry pans, and a spatula has been reported to melt
- Feels less rigid than metal
- A titanium spoon is a lighter, sturdier choice if only one utensil is needed
- Too small for group or basecamp cooking, according to one buyer

## Who Should Buy This

Solo backpackers who cook in a Jetboil-style cup or eat from freeze-dried bags will get the most from it, particularly if they want a fork and spoon in one pack-friendly bundle. It also suits anyone cooking on nonstick pots who needs a stirring tool that won't scratch. Gram-counters who carry one spoon, hikers who cook in frying pans, and anyone who pulls hard on a utensil in a pouch should pick a titanium spoon or a one-piece long-handle spork instead.

## Verdict

The JetSet does what it was built for: it gets a spoon to the bottom of a meal bag and then disappears into a pack. The handles' tendency to slip, and nylon's limits with heat, keep it from being a set-and-forget piece of kit. It is a good cheap add-on for Jetboil owners, and an unnecessary one for everyone who already carries a long spoon.
