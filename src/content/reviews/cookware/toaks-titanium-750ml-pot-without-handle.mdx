## Overview

The TOAKS POT-750-NH is a single-wall, uncoated titanium pot with no handles at all. That puts it at 79 g with the lid, about 31 g less than the same pot with its bail handle. It's built for solo hikers who boil water and rehydrate meals, and who are willing to give up convenient gripping for a tighter, lighter kit.

## Performance

**Weight and capacity.** The pot alone weighs 60 g, and the lid brings it to 79 g. TOAKS' own handled 750 weighs 110 g with its lid, so removing the bail and hinges saves roughly 31 g. A 750 ml volume handles the standard solo job: a freeze-dried pouch with enough left over for a drink. Owners of the handled version describe the size as enough for more than one hot drink, or a drink plus a ramen or a Mountain House meal.

**Cooking.** The pot is single-walled, uncoated titanium. It heats quickly and doesn't hold heat the way a double-wall cup does. Titanium also concentrates heat at the contact point, so stirring matters for anything thicker than boiling water. The pot is best treated as a vessel for boiling, not simmering. Owners of the handled version praise the rounded bottom edge, which makes cleanup easy. The handleless pot has the same body, and the sides carry mL and oz graduations.

**The no-handle trade-off.** This is the pot's defining feature, and its main limit. With no handles or bail, the pot is gripped by its rim or body, so a pot gripper, bandana or glove is a practical necessity when it's full and hot. One buyer on a retail listing gave it three stars, saying they hadn't realized it had no handles. The product page does state it plainly. The design makes the most sense inside a wind-protected system. Backpacking Light forum posts show the NH pot used in custom Trail Designs Ti-Tri Caldera Cone kits, with a ridge added 1 inch from the bottom. In that setup the pot sits inside a cone and the handles would only get in the way.

**Lid.** The lid has a lockable grip. On the handled version, reviewers noted that the pull tab rarely got too hot to touch. The handleless pot uses the same lid design, but the lid is the only thing that stays cool on the pot, so it's the part hikers will grab.

**Nesting.** This is where the pot earns its place. TOAKS lists the pot as fitting inside the POT-1100, POT-1100-BH, CKW-1100 and BWL-550-D103. The CUP-375, CUP-450 and the STV-12 wood-burning stove slot inside it. A 110 g isobutane canister (with a Pocket Rocket 2) fits as well. A 32 oz Nalgene bottle also fits, though the lid won't close when the pot is stacked with bottles like that. On the handled pot, the bail and hinges cut into this nesting space. Removing them is the whole point of the NH.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 79 g with lid, about 31 g lighter than the bail-handle version
- 750 ml is enough for a solo meal plus a drink
- Nests cleanly with TOAKS cups, the STV-12 stove and a 110 g canister
- Uncoated titanium with no metallic taste, and corrosion-resistant
- Mesh sack included
- Works well in cone-style and wood-burning systems
- About $33

**Cons**

- No handles, so a full pot is awkward to lift and pour when hot
- Single-wall construction cools quickly and offers no insulation as a mug
- Thin titanium heats unevenly, so it's poor for simmering or sticky meals
- Buyers who don't read the listing may get a surprise
- Stock is spotty, with some retailers showing it sold out

## Who Should Buy This

This pot is for the solo backpacker who boils water for dehydrated meals and coffee. It suits a cook who is already counting grams and building a nested kit around a canister or a small wood stove. Cone-stove users and anyone who owns TOAKS cups and bowls will get the most out of it. Hikers who cook real meals, who pour from the pot often, or who don't want to carry a gripper should look at the bail-handle 750. They can pay the 31 g penalty and keep a pot that can be lifted safely off a fire. Two-person teams should step up to the 1100 ml.

## Verdict

The POT-750-NH is a well-made, no-frills titanium pot that does one thing well: it saves weight and pack space in a nested solo kit. It asks hikers to manage heat with their own gear, and for those who do, it's a good value at about $33.
