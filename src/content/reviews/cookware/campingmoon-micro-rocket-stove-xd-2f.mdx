## Overview

The Campingmoon XD-2F is a canister-top stove that weighs 92 g, carries a built-in pressure regulator and a piezo igniter, and costs a fraction of what the big-name regulated stoves do. Owners and reviewers consistently describe it as a close copy of the Soto WindMaster, with a few MSR Pocket Rocket Deluxe cues in the valve and wire control. The trade-off is pedigree: this is a clone, so the appeal is value and performance, not brand support or refinement.

## Performance

**Boil speed.** Owners agree that the XD-2F is quick. One user on the Classic Camp Stoves forum reported two cups of water boiling in under 2.5 minutes at full blast. A UK reviewer at Ride Roam clocked a boil of 2 minutes 47 seconds on a stopwatch. A 3.3 kW rating puts it in the same class as the stoves it imitates.

**Regulator.** The regulator is the reason to buy this stove instead of a bare-bones $15 burner. Unregulated stoves fade as the canister cools and empties, usually when the fuel drops to about a quarter. Owners say the XD-2F holds its flame much longer. One long-time user says the regulator pulls a canister nearly dry with no noticeable drop until the last moments. A Trailspace reviewer with a year of daily use at home and in camp said the regulator worked flawlessly from normal temperatures down to freezing. Others have tested cold starts by leaving stove and canister in the freezer overnight, including one owner at roughly 9,000 feet, and report the stove still lit and ran.

**Igniter.** The piezo sits vertically at the back of the burner. Owners report that it fires within one to three clicks. A forum user with several units said nothing had failed in the igniter, pot supports or regulator valve. Campingmoon's own marketing cites a user with three years on the igniter. The usual caveat applies: a piezo is a convenience, not a fire source. Carry a mini Bic.

**Wind and stability.** The stove borrows the WindMaster's recessed burner head and wide-spread pot supports. Owners say this gives a low center of gravity and a stable platform, and that it handles a stiff breeze much as the Soto does. It will not match a fully enclosed canopy system in a gale. On a wet ridge, a windscreen is still worth packing, with the usual care about heat reflecting back onto the canister.

**Packing.** At 5 x 5 x 9 cm folded, it tucks easily into a small pot with a canister. The four pot supports fold flat, and some owners say the stove fits inside their pots more neatly than the original WindMaster. The included canvas case is a nice touch at this price.

**Fuel economy.** The stated burn time is about 1.4 hours on a 250 g canister at full output. That is a maximum-power figure. Simmering and boiling only what is needed will stretch a canister much further on a multi-day trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 92 g with a regulator and an integrated igniter
- Quick boils, in the range of roughly 2.5 to 3 minutes for a couple of cups
- Regulator keeps output steady in cold and as the canister empties
- Low, stable, wide pot supports and a recessed burner that cope with breezes
- Folds to 5 x 5 x 9 cm and includes a carry case
- Street price commonly in the $22 to $38 range, well below the stoves it resembles

**Cons**

- Clone of the Soto WindMaster, so fit and finish and quality control vary more than with the original
- Piezo igniters of any brand are an unreliable sole ignition source
- Four detachable pot supports are a little more to manage than a fixed-arm design
- No windscreen, and the open burner is less wind-proof than a canopy system
- Warranty and support are less certain than with an established outdoor brand

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who wants a regulated, igniter-equipped stove at roughly 3 oz and doesn't want to pay WindMaster money. That includes weekend hikers, section hikers, and thru-hikers who want an inexpensive primary stove or a backup. Cold-weather three-season campers get most from the regulator. Anyone who needs a well-supported name brand, a guaranteed warranty, or an enclosed windproof system should look at the Soto original or a canopy stove instead.

## Verdict

The XD-2F does what a good canister stove should: it is light, folds small, boils quickly, and keeps its flame steady as the canister empties. Long-term owner reports on the igniter and regulator are good enough that the main risk is the usual one with a clone, not a known flaw. For ultralight cooks on a budget, it is an easy recommendation.
