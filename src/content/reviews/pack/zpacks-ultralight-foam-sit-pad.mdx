## Overview

The Zpacks Foam Sit Pad is a folding sheet of closed-cell foam that weighs 0.95 oz (27 g) and costs $9.95. It is built for ultralight hikers who want a dry, padded place to sit at lunch, and it moonlights as back padding in Zpacks packs or as a cushion for the feet inside a sleeping bag. The trade-off is the obvious one: it is thin, single-purpose foam that does one small job well and nothing heroic.

## Performance

The sit pad's appeal is arithmetic. At 27 g, it weighs less than most trail snacks, and it removes a recurring annoyance: wet logs, cold rocks and damp tundra at every rest stop. Because the foam is closed-cell, it does not soak up water. A sit pad that stays dry on a wet day is the entire point, and Zpacks buyers say it delivers. One five-star reviewer on the Zpacks site described a rainy first trip in which they kept finding new uses for it. Sitting was the main one. Kneeling to deal with wet gear and having a clean, dry surface to set things on were the others. Others in the reviewer's family wanted one afterward.

Packing is the second selling point. Zpacks designed it to ride in the base straps of its own packs or to slip into an exterior pocket. That makes it a grab-and-sit item. A pad stashed deep in the pack tends to stay there. One tucked under a strap gets used at every break.

The foam is also a useful side hustle in the sleep system. Slid into the footbox of a quilt or sleeping bag, it gives some padding under the feet at night. In Zpacks' Nero frameless packs, it can serve as a back panel, which is how many ultralight hikers get a bit of structure and comfort without adding a dedicated pad. Zpacks notes that the Gray 3-section version is the more rigid choice for this job. Both versions share the same overall dimensions. The difference is in the fold: the Blue 4-section pad folds smaller and suits storage inside the pack or in an external pocket, while the Gray 3-section pad folds into a longer, skinnier shape that stands up better as a back panel.

Thin foam has limits. This is not a cushion for a long camp lounge or a meaningful insulator on snow, and it will not substitute for a torso-length sleeping pad. It is a barrier and a bit of padding, not a chair.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Weighs 0.95 oz (27 g), a rounding error in a base weight
- Closed-cell foam keeps seat, knees and gear dry
- Folds small and rides in base straps or an exterior pocket
- Doubles as back padding in Zpacks packs or foot padding in a sleeping bag
- $9.95 is hard to argue with

**Cons**

- Thin, so it offers a dry seat rather than real cushion
- Choice of fold matters: the 4-section pad is compact but less rigid as a back panel, and the 3-section pad packs longer
- Not a sleeping-pad substitute for insulation or comfort

## Who Should Buy This

Ultralight and thru-hikers who take frequent short breaks, especially in wet or cold regions, will get the most from it. Zpacks pack owners get a bonus, since the pad fits their strap layout and doubles as a back panel in the Nero. Buyers who stash it in the pack should take the Blue 4-section. Those who want back padding in a Nero should take the Gray 3-section. Hikers who want a chair-like sitting experience or who prefer an inflatable or heavily insulated seat should look elsewhere. So should anyone who only camps with a full-length foam pad that already serves as a seat.

## Verdict

The Zpacks Foam Sit Pad is the sort of purchase that pays back within the first rainy lunch break. For under ten dollars and just under an ounce, it keeps hikers dry, protects knees and gear, and offers a few extra uses. It earns a place in most ultralight kits.
