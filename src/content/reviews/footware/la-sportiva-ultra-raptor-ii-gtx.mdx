## Overview

The Ultra Raptor II GTX is La Sportiva's low-top, technical hiking shoe built for hikers who want trail-runner agility with more structure underfoot for scrambling and off-trail travel. It's descended from a shoe that <cite index="8-6,8-7">was originally developed as a mountain trail running shoe and became a bit of a legend in the running world for its balance of cushioning, support, protection and traction</cite>. This version wraps that platform in a Gore-Tex Extended Comfort liner and pairs it with La Sportiva's sticky FriXion XF 2.0 rubber, making it a fit for hikers who spend serious time on rock, scree, and mixed alpine terrain rather than groomed gravel paths.

## Performance

**Traction:** This is where the Ultra Raptor II earns its keep. Reviewers consistently single out the outsole as a standout feature — RunRepeat's lab testing found <cite index="9-1,9-2,9-3">the shoe to be a mighty hiking shoe that can bite and hold up against many trail obstacles above the treeline, making testers feel surefooted and well-protected on both rock-heavy and slippery trails</cite>. On the Mid version (which shares the same outsole and last), OutdoorGearLab similarly praised the compound, noting <cite index="1-1,1-2">outstanding traction in mountain terrain and a Frixion XF 2.0 rubber compound that is hearty and durable</cite>. If your trip involves slabs, talus, or wet rock, this outsole is a genuine asset — it's designed for grip first, everything else second.

**Durability trade-off:** That aggressive rubber isn't free. RunRepeat flagged long-term outsole wear as <cite index="9-4,9-5">long-term outsole durability, the only characteristic that holds this La Sportiva back from being the ultimate shoe for technical terrain</cite>. In practice, this tracks with what you'd expect from any stickier, softer rubber compound — it grips better but wears faster than a harder-compound shoe like a Salomon X Ultra. Budget for resoling or replacement sooner if you're putting on serious mileage on abrasive terrain like granite or sandstone.

**Fit:** The Ultra Raptor II runs narrow, and this shows up again and again across reviews. Switchback Travel found <cite index="14-4">the boots run noticeably narrow at the forefoot and were prone to slippage in softer surfaces like mud</cite>. Better Trail's tester had a similarly blunt first impression, describing the shoe as feeling <cite index="15-2">narrow, stiff, and uncushioned—definitely not love at first step</cite>, though comfort improved with mileage. If you have a wide or splay-prone forefoot, La Sportiva does offer wide-width versions of related models, and it's worth trying those before committing to standard width.

**Break-in and comfort:** Don't expect all-day comfort straight out of the box the way you might with a Merrell Moab. Multiple testers describe a genuine adjustment period — Better Trail noted the shoe <cite index="15-3,15-4">wasn't thinking about the fit anymore by mile two, and by mile seven, it actually felt comfortable, with a bit of a break-in period</cite> before it softens up. The upper and midsole lean minimalist by design — as Better Trail put it, the shoe <cite index="15-1">leans minimalist in design, with thin padding in the collar and tongue and a firm, low-profile underfoot feel</cite>. That's consistent with the shoe's trail-running DNA: it's built for protection and precision, not plush cushioning.

**Cushioning and stack:** At 27mm/16mm (11mm drop), the stack heights sit in trail-runner territory rather than max-cushion hiking shoe territory. That's intentional — this is a shoe designed to keep you close to the ground for technical footing, not to soak up miles of hardpack fire road. If you're coming from a plush daily trainer, expect a firmer, more connected ride.

**Waterproofing:** The GORE-TEX Extended Comfort membrane performs as expected for a GTX liner — reliably waterproof with reasonable breathability, though as with any waterproof-breathable shoe, expect some clamminess on hot, high-output days. None of the reviews surfaced complaints specific to this shoe's membrane, which is itself a decent sign — no news is good news with GTX liners.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional grip on rock, scree, and mixed technical terrain thanks to FriXion XF 2.0 rubber
- Trail-runner agility and low weight relative to its protection level
- GORE-TEX Extended Comfort keeps feet dry without excessive bulk
- Structured, protective toe cap and heel counter for off-trail scrambling
- La Sportiva's lacing system distributes tension well and helps avoid hot spots

**Cons**

- Narrow last — a dealbreaker for wide or splay-prone feet
- Break-in period required; not immediately plush out of the box
- Sticky rubber compound sacrifices some long-term outsole durability
- Firm, minimal cushioning that some hikers will find under-padded for long hardpack miles
- Premium price point that's hard to justify if you're mostly hiking maintained trails

## Who Should Buy This

This shoe is for hikers and fastpackers who spend real time off-trail, on scrambles, or above treeline where foot placement and grip matter more than plush cushioning. If your typical hike is a well-graded state park loop, this is overkill — RunRepeat's own advice is blunt: <cite index="9-10,9-11">if you are in search of a shoe for easy hikes on hard-packed trails, skip the Ultra Raptor II GTX in favor of a less technical hiking shoe, which will save you money and unnecessary bulk</cite>. But if you're chasing summits, navigating talus fields, or want one shoe that can handle both trail running and technical hiking, it's a strong fit — provided your feet aren't wide.

## Verdict

The Ultra Raptor II GTX delivers exactly what it promises: best-in-class traction and trail-runner nimbleness in a package protective enough for technical, off-trail terrain. The narrow fit and firm, minimal-cushion ride won't suit everyone, and the outsole's grippy compound trades away some longevity — but for hikers who prioritize footing security over cushioned comfort, it's one of the more capable technical hiking shoes on the market. I'd rate it a 7.8/10: outstanding for its intended use case, but a poor match if you're not the target hiker.
