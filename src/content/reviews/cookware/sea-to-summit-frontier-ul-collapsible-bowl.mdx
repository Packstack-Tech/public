## Overview

The Frontier UL Collapsible Bowl is a silicone-walled camp bowl that squashes down to roughly the thickness of a stack of crackers. It suits backpackers who care more about pack volume and rigid, stable eating than about shaving the last few grams. The trade-off is weight: a plain plastic bowl or a cut-down freezer container will beat it on the scale.

## Performance

**Packability.** This is the bowl's reason to exist. The sidewalls fold down into the rigid base and rim ring, leaving a disc about 17 mm thick in the Medium. Owners describe it as frisbee-shaped. That slides flat against a pack's back panel or into a side pocket without the dead space a rigid bowl wastes. Sea to Summit also designed the Medium to nest inside its Frontier 2L collapsible pouring pot, and one buyer reported fitting four bowls inside the pot with room to spare. For anyone already invested in that cookware line, the kit packs very tightly.

**In the hand.** An earlier version of the Frontier line lacked a rim. This one has a stiff nylon rim that gives a secure grip, and the curve where the wall meets the base was shaped to match the radius of Sea to Summit's spoons. The goal is to scoop the last bite of dehydrated dinner without chasing it around a corner. Reviewers on retail sites call the bowl rigid yet light, stable on the ground, and easy to carry full. The silicone is rated to 150°C, so a freshly boiled rehydrated meal poses no problem. Sea to Summit says it cycles each bowl through a rig that extends and collapses it 5,000 times. Owner feedback on durability has been consistently positive.

**Cleaning and wear.** The fold grooves in the silicone take more attention than a smooth-sided bowl. One reviewer at The Adventure Gene flagged this as the main downside. A critical REI review reported lingering food smell after several washes, which is a familiar silicone habit with greasy or spiced meals. Metal cutlery is the other hazard: OVR's testers found that a metal utensil set left fairly deep cut marks in the large bowls after repeated meals. A titanium or plastic spoon avoids the problem. There is no lid, and Sea to Summit says the omission is deliberate, citing safety concerns about a press-fit lid on a collapsible bowl.

**Weight.** At 62 g for the Medium, the bowl isn't ultralight by the standards of the cottage-gear crowd. Reviewers note that lighter non-collapsible plastic bowls exist, and a mug-and-pot system can make a separate bowl unnecessary. Folks eating straight from a freezer-bag meal can skip it entirely. The bowl earns its place for hikers who want a real surface to eat from, or who are cooking for two and need a vessel that doesn't eat pack space.

## Choosing a Version

The sizes differ in more than capacity. The Medium (62 g, 680 ml) is the deeper of the two at 5.1 cm, and it's the one that nests in the Frontier 2L pouring pot. It holds a standard freeze-dried pouch's worth of food and suits solo hikers and anyone counting grams.

The Large (82 g, 890 ml) is wider at 17.8 cm and shallower at 4.5 cm. That makes it better for big appetites, cold-soak oatmeal with toppings, or couples sharing a one-bowl dinner, and it doubles as a plate when collapsed. One REI reviewer who uses the bowls as plates in their collapsed form found the Medium a tad small for that job. The extra 20 g is a fair price for the added room. Solo thru-hikers who eat once per sitting from a pouch or pot should take the Medium.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Collapses to under 2 cm, so it takes almost no pack volume
- Rigid base and rim make it stable on uneven ground and easy to hold
- Spoon-matched curve helps with scooping the last bite
- Medium nests in the Frontier 2L pouring pot
- Dishwasher safe, BPA/PFOA/PTFE-free, lifetime guarantee
- Retail ratings sit between 4.7 and 4.9 out of 5 across hundreds of buyers

**Cons**

- 62–82 g is heavy next to a simple plastic bowl
- Fold grooves are harder to clean than smooth walls
- Silicone can hold food odors
- Metal utensils can score the surface
- No lid available

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want a proper bowl but resent the volume a rigid one takes up. Couples and small groups, section hikers, and car-campers who also travel will get the most out of it, especially those already using Frontier pots or mugs. Gram-counting solo hikers who eat from pouches or a single pot should look elsewhere. The same goes for anyone who carries metal cutlery and won't switch.

## Verdict

The Frontier UL is a well-made bowl that solves the one problem collapsible bowls exist to solve: it disappears when packed and holds up when used. It doesn't win on weight, and it takes a little more scrubbing than a smooth bowl. For most backpackers who carry a bowl at all, the Medium is the better pick.
