## Overview

The INIU P63-E1 is a 25,000mAh power bank built around a simple pitch: give travelers and gear-heavy backpackers a genuine 100W laptop charger without the brick-sized shell most high-capacity banks come in. It's aimed at people who need to keep a laptop, phone, and a handheld or camera alive over multi-day trips, car camping, or long-haul flights — less so at gram-counting thru-hikers, since 397g is a real tax on a base weight.

## Performance

The headline feature is the size-to-capacity ratio. INIU uses what it calls TinyCell high-density battery cells paired with a "HyperStack" internal layout, and <cite index="4-1">the company says it has shrunk a 25,000mAh power bank by 30% compared to typical designs in that capacity class</cite>. That's not just marketing fluff — multiple independent reviewers picked up on the same thing. One reviewer who spent a month with the unit noted <cite index="1-12,1-13,1-14">the P63 packs a massive 25,000mAh battery, roughly 2.5 times the capacity of the smaller P50, yet weighs less than 1 lb and is manageable to carry in a backpack or pocket</cite>. A separate reviewer measured it at <cite index="10-1">around 392g and 11 × 7 × 3.6 cm, noticeably smaller and lighter than most power banks in its class</cite>.

On the charging side, the 100W USB-C port is the real differentiator over cheaper 25,000mAh banks that cap out at 18-20W. <cite index="8-12">According to INIU, the P63 can fast charge a MacBook Pro 14" M3 to 72% in 30 minutes from a 20% battery, and add over 17 hours of wireless web browsing to a MacBook Pro 13"</cite>. It's worth flagging, as one reviewer pointed out, that <cite index="8-11">manufacturer numbers don't mean a lot without real-world context</cite> — I'd treat those figures as best-case rather than gospel. Phone charging speeds held up in third-party testing too: <cite index="2-12">the power bank supports Samsung Super Fast Charging 2.0, charging a Samsung S24 Ultra to 75% in 30 minutes</cite>. Handheld gaming is also covered — <cite index="2-13">the P63 offers full-speed charging for the Steam Deck, allowing uninterrupted gaming sessions</cite>, and one field reviewer confirmed it <cite index="6-7">handled laptop charging, phone charging, and smaller electronics without issue during testing</cite>.

Recharge speed is genuinely useful for trip logistics. <cite index="3-11">The 1.4-hour recharge time and the inclusion of a detachable 100W cable both add meaningful practical value</cite> — topping off the bank during a lunch stop or a short layover is realistic rather than aspirational.

For travelers, the 25,000mAh figure isn't arbitrary. <cite index="1-17,1-18">The capacity hits a sweet spot because most airlines allow power banks under 100Wh in carry-on luggage without special approval, and the P63 stays within that limit</cite>. One outlet went as far as calling it an <cite index="3-7">"airline‑approved mobile studio," noting the 25,000mAh capacity sits comfortably within carry‑on limits for most airlines</cite>.

Build quality gets consistent praise. A longer-term reviewer who used it through hikes, gym sessions, and work travel called out <cite index="7-7">balanced weight distribution and a compact shape that nestles next to mechanical keyboards or controllers in a tech bag without shifting during movement</cite>, and pointed to <cite index="7-10">the combination of massive reserves, blistering speeds, thoughtful accessories like the lanyard cable, and rugged build quality</cite> as standout traits. That said, the same reviewer's summary flagged <cite index="7-3">"minor display quirks"</cite> as the one wrinkle in an otherwise strong showing — worth keeping expectations in check on the LCD readout's precision.

At least one real owner posting outside of a sponsored review context backed up the "smallest in class" claim, saying <cite index="11-6,11-7,11-8">they own it and it's very good — best bang for the dollar when it comes to size and power output, with 100W input and output, and it's definitely the smallest 25k mAh power bank</cite> they'd found.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- True 100W USB-C output — actually useful for charging modern USB-C laptops, not just a marketing number
- Genuinely compact for a 25,000mAh cell; noticeably smaller than most competitors in this capacity class
- Fast 1.4-hour recharge time via the 100W input port
- Three simultaneous ports (100W USB-C, 45W USB-C, 18W USB-A) cover phone + laptop + accessory charging at once
- Sits just under the 100Wh airline threshold, so no TSA hassle
- Detachable 100W cable instead of a fixed cable that can fray or fail
- 3-year warranty, longer than many budget competitors

**Cons**

- At 397g, it's a noticeable addition to a lightweight pack — this is a travel/basecamp tool, not an ultralight thru-hiking accessory
- LCD display has been flagged as slightly imprecise by at least one long-term reviewer
- Manufacturer charging-speed claims (MacBook to 72% in 30 min, etc.) are best treated as ceiling numbers rather than guaranteed results
- Second USB-C port caps at 45W, so charging two power-hungry devices simultaneously means neither gets full speed

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for travelers, digital nomads, photographers, and van/car campers who need to keep a laptop and a phone (or a camera, drone, or handheld console) charged for days without reliable wall access, and who care about carry-on weight and airline compliance more than pack weight. It's less suited to weekend backpackers or thru-hikers optimizing grams, where a smaller 10,000mAh bank or a solar setup makes more sense.

## Verdict

The INIU P63-E1 backs up its "smallest 100W, 25,000mAh" pitch with real-world results — fast laptop and phone charging, a quick recharge cycle, and a shell that's genuinely easier to live with than most bricks in this capacity class. It's not a pack for ultralight hikers, but for travelers and content creators who need serious charging capacity in carry-on-friendly form, it's an easy recommendation. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — docked slightly for weight relative to true ultralight needs and the minor display quibbles noted by long-term users.
