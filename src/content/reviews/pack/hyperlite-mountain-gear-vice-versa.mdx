## Overview

The Vice Versa is Hyperlite Mountain Gear's smallest fanny pack: a 1.3-liter Dyneema pouch that weighs 76 g (2.7 oz) and is meant for the things you want within reach without digging through a pack. It's the little sibling of the Versa, and it makes the same trade at a smaller scale: very low weight and a very dry interior, in exchange for a price that looks steep next to what the pouch holds.

## Performance

**Capacity.** Hyperlite pitches the 1.3 L volume at the daily-carry list: ID, phone, keys, a multitool, lip balm. Owners say that's about right. One buyer who clipped it to a kid-carrier pack's hip belt called it light but roomy enough for phone, keys and wallet. A phone in a case plus snacks fits. A second layer, a full-size battery bank and a headlamp together probably don't.

**Weather protection.** The DCF body is waterproof, and the zippers are the weak link in principle, as they are on every Dyneema bag. Hyperlite describes the pack as highly water-resistant rather than submersible. Owners of the larger Versa, which uses the same approach, report that the interior stayed dry even when the outside was soaked. CleverHiker's testers put the Versa through rain simulation and submersion tests and it passed. A long-term reviewer using the Versa's coated zippers reported minimal water ingress through monsoon downpours in Thailand. The Vice Versa's smaller zipper run and simpler construction suggest similar results, though the reports come from the Versa rather than this pack.

**Carry flexibility.** The removable belt is the point of the design. Worn around the waist, it keeps a phone and snacks off your shorts pockets. Detached, it clips onto a pack's hip belt or shoulder strap, where it fills the gap left by Hyperlite packs, which have no brain or lid. Buyers also use it as a camp-to-water-source pouch for quick trips to filter water or watch a sunset. Reviews of the Versa note that bulkier hip belts, such as the one on the 3 Day Assault CL, can be tight for the clip system, and that reaching hip-belt buckles with a pouch attached takes some fiddling. A pack this small leaves less in the way, but ultralight hikers with thick hip belts should expect some friction.

**Durability.** DCF is about as abrasion-tolerant as ultralight fabrics get. CleverHiker's abrasion test on the Versa left the fabric fuzzed but intact and fully functional. Owners report that Hyperlite's fanny packs hold up through snowshoeing, skiing and thru-hiking. The paper-thin feel and crinkle are normal for DCF.

**Owner satisfaction.** Hyperlite's own listing shows a 4.7 rating across 83 reviews, with 69 of them five-star and 93 percent of buyers saying they'd recommend it. The most common complaint is size. A thru-hiker who carried the Vice Versa on the Appalachian Trail called it outstanding, then bought the Versa for more room. The Versa weighs 4.3 oz and holds roughly twice as much.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 76 g (2.7 oz), light enough that wearers stop noticing it
- Waterproof DCF body with water-resistant zippers keeps phones and electronics dry
- Four ways to carry it: waist, chest, shoulder sling or clipped to a pack
- Removable belt adjusts to 52 in, so it fits over a layered torso
- Durable fabric holds up to abrasion and long-term use
- Fills the "no brain" gap on Hyperlite packs

**Cons**

- At $65, the cost per liter is high
- 1.3 L is tight for anything beyond the daily-carry essentials
- Not rated for submersion, so the zipper line is the limit
- Bulky hip belts can make the clip-on setup awkward
- DCF crinkles, and the white version will show trail dirt

## Who Should Buy This

The Vice Versa suits ultralight backpackers who already carry a Hyperlite pack, or any pack without a top pocket, and want phone, keys, ID and a snack in reach without taking off the pack. It also works for day hikers and travelers who dislike full pockets. Hikers who want to carry a headlamp, sunscreen, a battery bank and a snack bag in the same pouch should look at the Versa. At twice the capacity, it adds only about 2 oz. Budget shoppers can get a nylon hip pouch for a fraction of the cost, at the price of waterproofing and a few grams.

## Verdict

The Vice Versa does what it says: it keeps a small kit dry, within reach and almost weightless. The $65 price and 1.3 L volume limit its appeal, and anyone who wants room for more than essentials should step up to the Versa. For hikers who need only phone, keys and a snack in reach, it's one of the best small fanny packs available.
