## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is Decathlon's one-person stainless steel cook kit: a 0.95-liter graduated saucepan with a folding handle, a strainer lid, a 0.45-liter bowl, a folding spoon/fork and a carry bag, all nested into a package 12.5 cm across and 10 cm tall. It is aimed at hikers who want a complete, durable kit at a supermarket price. The trade-off is the metal: stainless steel makes the set tough and cheap to replace, but it is heavier than the titanium pots that ultralight regulars favor.

## Performance

**Weight and packing.** At 305 g for everything, the set is a full kitchen rather than just a pot. The pot alone accounts for 197 g, and that is where the weight penalty sits. Titanium pots of similar capacity weigh noticeably less, and a hiker who already carries a mug and a spork can skip the bowl, lid and cutlery to trim roughly 108 g from the kit. The carry bag adds only 16 g.

**Cooking.** The pot is tall and narrow, a shape that suits boiling water for freeze-dried meals, instant noodles and coffee and is less happy as a frying vessel. Stainless steel conducts heat poorly compared with aluminum, so on a canister stove with a hot flame it can scorch thick sauces and oatmeal unless stirred. The upside is that the surface shrugs off metal utensils, which Decathlon cites as one reason it chose 18/8 (304) steel, and it will not corrode or dent like a thin aluminum pot. The pot is also rated for wood fires, gas and induction, so it works on a campfire as well as a trail stove. Internal graduations let hikers measure water without a separate cup, a small feature that owners single out.

**Packing and handling.** The folding handle locks the lid in place for transport, and owners on Decathlon's site say the whole set swallows a gas canister, stove, stand and a cloth with room to spare, if the bowl stays home. That nesting is the set's chief practical appeal. The handle design has a catch: one owner notes that it can't be clipped to the outside of a pack, so the pot has to be dried before it goes in. Another reviewer, writing in French, reported that the kit's edges wore holes in a pack and its waterproof pockets, so a stuff-sack or cloth wrap around the pot is sensible. The spork's folding switch is described as stiff, especially in cold weather, though the spork itself gets a good mark.

**Owner satisfaction.** Decathlon's own product page shows an average of 4.76 out of 5 across 455 reviews, with robustness and ease of use among the categories rated most highly. Most of the praise centers on value and on the set being a complete, no-fuss kit for boiling water and eating from. The occasional three-star review points to handling quirks rather than failures.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Complete one-person kit at 305 g, including bowl, strainer lid, spork and bag
- 18/8 stainless steel is corrosion-resistant and safe with metal utensils
- Works on gas, wood fire and induction
- Graduated markings inside the pot
- Folding handle locks the lid for transport; nests a canister and stove
- Strong owner ratings (4.76/5 across 455 Decathlon reviews)

**Cons**

- The 197 g pot is heavy next to titanium alternatives
- Stainless steel heats unevenly; thick meals can stick or scorch
- Handle design prevents clipping the pot to the outside of a pack
- Spork switch is stiff in the cold
- Sharp edges can abrade pack fabric if the kit is not wrapped

## Who Should Buy This

This set suits first-time backpackers, weekend hikers, bikepackers and anyone who wants a single durable kit that tolerates campfires and rough handling without a titanium price tag. It also appeals to people who cook on a mix of gas and wood. Thru-hikers counting every ounce, or anyone who only boils water and already owns a mug and spork, should look to a titanium pot in the 600–900 ml range instead and leave the bowl and lid at home.

## Verdict

The MT500 is a sturdy, thoughtfully nested kit that does the basics well and costs little. It will not win any gram-counting contests, but for three-season trips where durability and completeness matter more than shaving ounces, it is a sound buy. Ultralight purists should treat it as a starter kit or a fire-friendly backup.
