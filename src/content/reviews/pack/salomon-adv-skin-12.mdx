## Overview

The Salomon ADV Skin 12 is a race-style running vest built for long trail days, ultras, and fastpacking missions where you need hydration, layers, and nutrition within arm's reach without a pack that bounces or chafes. It's been a fixture at ultramarathon start lines for over a decade, and the 2025 update refines the fit, materials, and pole/pocket details rather than reinventing the formula. This one's aimed at runners covering serious mileage — think 60km+ efforts — not casual walkers who just want a place to stash a phone.

## Performance

The headline feature of the ADV Skin 12 is its Sensifit construction, and every review I found agrees it's the standout element. Multiple testers noted that <cite index="9-1,9-2">the Sensifit construction hugs the body without feeling overly constrictive, and one of the standout features is that it doesn't feel like you need to have it overly tight through the chest area to keep it from bouncing.</cite> That's a real departure from older-school vest designs that rely on cinching straps hard against your sternum to kill bounce.

The adjustment system draws consistent praise too. CleverHiker's testers found <cite index="11-2">the cord adjustment was easy to operate with just one tug, allowing them to modify the fit throughout a run as the pack load decreased</cite>, and appreciated that <cite index="11-16">unlike some competitors with strap closures, the flexibility in this system meant that even when fully loaded, the closure wasn't at its maximum.</cite> Treeline Review echoed this, noting <cite index="13-1,13-2">the vest shines in its versatility precisely because of its easy fit adjustments, accommodating changes in load weight and even body shape.</cite>

On durability, GearJunkie's long-term test is the most compelling data point out there. Their reviewer wore an older version through the Leadville 100 and <cite index="10-8,10-9">finished without a single hot spot or hint of chafing — high praise for a pack worn through one of the toughest ultras in North America.</cite> Six years later, <cite index="10-10">that same pack is going strong, a rare claim for a piece of running gear that's seen that much punishment.</cite> That's about as strong an endorsement of long-term build quality as you'll find in this category.

Storage and organization also score well. Treeline Review called it <cite index="13-8">the most comfortable running hydration vest they've tested, and it won Best Overall Running Hydration Vest in their guide.</cite> My Outdoor Basecamp specifically liked the front bottle holders, noting <cite index="15-18,15-19">the soft flask holders are snug, easy to use, and don't bounce — they stay firmly in place even as the bottles empty.</cite>

It's not flawless, though. CleverHiker's one real complaint was fit-related: <cite index="11-7">their only caveat with this otherwise excellent pack was that the collar chafed at the base of the neck</cite> during a long training run. Team RunRun's reviewer had a similar early hiccup with the closure system, mentioning <cite index="14-11">the bungee closure system was adjustable, though at first, it hit awkwardly below the sports bra, causing a slight rope burn when running shirtless</cite> — though they found a fix by re-adjusting. The same reviewer also flagged that <cite index="14-19,14-20">heavier items, like a phone, tend to shift around in the stretchy pockets, though nothing that a little pocket rearranging can't solve.</cite> None of these are dealbreakers, but they're worth knowing about before your first long run in it — dial in the fit on a short shakeout before you commit to a 50-miler.

One nice touch on the 2025 refresh: the insulated hydration sleeve setup has changed. Treeline notes the vest is <cite index="13-17">compatible with a 2L bladder, though it no longer includes an insulation sleeve</cite> — so if you're running in heat and want your water to stay cool, you may need to source that separately or lean on the soft flasks instead, which CleverHiker's older testing found came with <cite index="11-17">a removable insulated hydration sleeve that kept water cool throughout a run</cite> (a feature specific to the version they tested).

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Sensifit construction delivers genuinely bounce-free carry without needing to crank the chest cords tight
- Single bungee-cord adjuster is fast to operate on the fly as your load lightens
- Proven long-term durability — one reviewer's copy survived Leadville 100 and years of subsequent use
- 12L is a sweet spot for long runs and ultras: enough room for mandatory gear without feeling like a backpack
- Compatible with both soft flasks and a 2L bladder, so you can choose your hydration setup

**Cons**

- Some runners report the collar chafing at the base of the neck on very long efforts
- Bungee closure can sit awkwardly depending on torso/chest shape until you find the right adjustment point
- No bladder or insulation sleeve included — budget for that separately if you want one
- Small, dense items (phone, in particular) can shift in the stretch pockets

## Who Should Buy This

This is a vest for people racing or training long — ultramarathoners, FKT chasers, and fastpackers who need 60km+ worth of capacity and organization without the weight or restriction of a traditional pack. If your runs top out at 90 minutes and you just want somewhere to clip a flask, the smaller 5L ADV Skin sibling (or a simple belt) will serve you better and cost less. But if you're gearing up for a mandatory-gear-list race or a multi-day fastpack through remote terrain, the ADV Skin 12 is built for exactly that.

## Verdict

The ADV Skin 12 earns its reputation as the vest to beat in the ultrarunning space — it fits close, carries a genuinely useful 12L without bouncing, and has the field-tested durability to back it up. Minor gripes about neck chafing and pocket item-shifting keep it from being perfect, but for long days on trail, I'd put my money here before almost anything else on the market. Call it an 8.5/10.
