## Overview

The Merino Resist is Forclaz's lightweight women's trekking tee, built from ultra-fine 18.5-micron merino with mesh panels under the arms. At 115 g/m², it is far thinner than the brand's 160 g/m² MT500 short-sleeve and the 190 g/m² long-sleeve tops that dominate owner discussion. That makes it a warm-weather and shoulder-season hiking shirt first, and a base layer second. The trade-off is the usual one for thin merino: comfort and odor control now, durability questions later.

## Performance

**Fabric and feel.** The headline number is the 115 g/m² fabric weight. Forclaz's heavier merino tops sit at 160 g/m² for the MT500 short-sleeve and 190 g/m² for the long-sleeve, and Decathlon's own MT900 climbs to 240 g/m². At 115, the Merino Resist is built for sweaty, warm-day mileage rather than for insulating on its own. The 18.5-micron fiber is fine for a budget shirt. Owners of Forclaz's merino tops, which use the same fineness, consistently describe the wool as soft and free of the itch that plagues coarser merino, and some who came over from Icebreaker said they preferred the Forclaz for feel.

**Breathability.** The mesh underarm panels target the two spots that overheat first under a pack. Decathlon's pitch for its merino range is that the fiber absorbs up to 35% of its weight in moisture before feeling damp, and that evaporation from the wool cools the skin slightly in hot, dry weather. On a long climb, a thin merino tee like this one will feel less clammy than a cotton blend. It will also dry more slowly than a polyester shirt, which is the standing compromise of any wool.

**Odor control.** This is the reason to buy merino, and Forclaz's merino line earns consistent praise for it. Owners report wearing the brand's merino tops several days in a row without trouble, which on a section hike means one shirt in the pack instead of two. A 145 g shirt that can be worn day after day is an easy trade for the weight-conscious.

**Durability.** The BETA-SPUN construction wraps each merino fiber in polyamide to resist abrasion, and owners of other Forclaz merino shirts with the same approach report a year or more of regular wear and washing. The caveat is the fabric weight. A 115 g/m² knit has less material to lose than the 190 g/m² shirts those reports describe, so expect pilling and wear where pack straps and hip belt rub the fabric. Washing at 30°C and drying flat, rather than tumble drying, is the care that the maker specifies and the one that protects thin merino.

**Ethics and sourcing.** The wool comes from non-mulesed South African flocks through Decathlon's Konsortium Merino partnership, which the company says covers animal welfare and environmental standards. For hikers who check the label for that, it is a point in the shirt's favor at this price tier.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Light fabric at 115 g/m², well suited to summer and high-output hiking
- 18.5-micron merino that owners of the Forclaz line describe as soft and itch-free
- Polyamide-wrapped yarn adds abrasion resistance
- Mesh underarm panels add ventilation where packs trap heat
- Multi-day odor resistance reduces the number of shirts in the pack
- Non-mulesed wool with a documented sourcing partnership
- Machine washable at 30°C

**Cons**

- Thin knit is likely to pill and wear faster than Forclaz's heavier merino tops
- 145 g in size M is not the lightest option among merino tees
- Dries more slowly than a synthetic tee
- Cannot be tumble dried
- Too light to serve as a cold-weather base layer without something over it

## Who Should Buy This

The best fit is a woman hiker who wants a merino tee for summer and shoulder-season trekking, hut-to-hut routes or long section hikes where laundry is rare, and who would rather spend on a budget brand than on Icebreaker or Smartwool prices. It also suits anyone who sleeps in the shirt they hike in and wants it to stay wearable. Hikers who are hard on gear, such as bushwhackers and those carrying heavy loads with chafing shoulder straps, should look at Forclaz's 160 g/m² MT500 short-sleeve for more fabric. Cold-weather hikers need a long-sleeved top at 190 g/m² or heavier. Anyone who prioritizes fast drying above all else is better served by a synthetic.

## Verdict

The Merino Resist delivers what a good merino tee should: soft 18.5-micron wool, mesh where heat builds, and odor resistance that holds over a multi-day trip, at a weight of 145 g in size M. Its thin 115 g/m² fabric trades some longevity for that lightness, so wash it gently and keep it away from abrasive straps. A solid, sensible pick for warm-weather trekking, but not one for rough use.
