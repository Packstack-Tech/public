## Overview

The 3F UL Gear Qidian Pro is a frameless, 46+10L UHMWPE-blend backpack aimed squarely at budget-conscious hikers looking to dip a toe into the ultralight world. At 880g (31 oz), it hits a respectable weight-to-volume ratio, and its headline trick — a dedicated back panel pocket that accepts your foam sleeping pad as a surrogate frame — is a genuinely clever idea. The target audience is weight-curious beginners and solo fast-and-light hikers who already keep their base weight lean, not expedition-minded folks expecting a full suspension system.

## Performance

### Weight & Volume

At 880g and a measured ~53L of usable space, the Qidian Pro lands at a weight-to-volume ratio of around 0.55 oz/L — genuinely competitive with packs that cost significantly more. The roll-top opening adds flexible volume control and a reasonable degree of weather protection. In practice, the advertised "46+10L" is a bit of marketing shorthand: the 10L comes from the external pockets, not the main compartment, and real-world usable volume sits closer to 53L total once you account for pack geometry.

### Fabric & Durability

The body is a UHMWPE-nylon ripstop blend — 3F UL doesn't publish the denier breakdown beyond "210D Nylon," and the specific UHMWPE thread weight isn't disclosed. That's mildly frustrating if you want to make precise comparisons. What reviewers have consistently found, though, is that the material feels as tough as or tougher than other budget UHMWPE packs in this price class. YKK zippers and DURAFLEX buckles are the right hardware choices at this price; they're unlikely to be your first point of failure.

### Pockets & Organization

This is where the Qidian Pro actually earns some praise. The large front mesh pocket is cavernous — capable of swallowing four 1L Smartwater bottles simultaneously — and easy to access on the go. The hip belt pockets are similarly oversized. Users who carry their sleeping pad in the dedicated back panel pocket report it being genuinely convenient for quick access to layers, a trowel, or a sit pad while on trail.

### Comfort & Fit

Here's where things get honest. The Qidian Pro is a frameless pack that relies entirely on your foam sleeping pad — inserted into the back panel pocket — as its only structural element. The shoulder straps have moderate padding (~12.5mm), and the hip belt is beefy-looking. But the hip belt is padded only at the outer ends, not across the lumbar region, which means load transfer to your hips is limited. The load lifters exist but provide minimal correction. Put plainly: with a true ultralight load (say, sub-6kg), you won't notice much, because you're mostly shoulder-loading anyway. Push past that and the inadequate lumbar wrap becomes noticeable fast.

Strap management is also a real-world annoyance. There are more straps on this pack than on most competitors at this size — compression straps, secondary pocket cinches, pad-retention straps — and loose ends flap and catch in the wind or brush against you while hiking. It's a minor thing, but cumulative over a long day.

The foam-pad-as-frame system is clever in concept but somewhat finicky in execution. Using a full-sized Z-Lite or similar pad can push the pack away from your back, reducing load stability. A thin sit pad or partial foam insert tends to work better in practice.

Fit is a single-size proposition: torso-length adjustment is not available beyond shoulder strap and hip belt buckle positioning. Hikers toward the extremes of the 160–190cm height range may find the back panel geometry less than ideal.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent price-to-weight-to-volume ratio for UHMWPE construction
- Durable fabric that punches above its price class
- YKK zippers and DURAFLEX buckles — right hardware choices
- Massive, accessible front mesh pocket and hip belt pockets
- Roll-top provides useful volume adjustment and weather resistance
- Clever sleeping pad integration keeps your pad internal and accessible

**Cons**

- Hip belt lacks lumbar padding, severely limiting real load transfer
- Load lifters are largely ineffective
- Too many straps — loose ends are a persistent nuisance on trail
- No torso-length adjustment; one-size fit is a gamble when ordering blind
- Specific UHMWPE fabric weight is not disclosed
- No in-person try-before-you-buy, and returns to China are logistically painful

## Who Should Buy This

The Qidian Pro makes most sense for the hiker who's already committed to a genuinely light kit — think sub-5kg base weight — and wants to explore the frameless category without committing to the price of a Hyperlite or a Zpacks. It also suits the tinkerer who doesn't mind some DIY strap management or minor modifications to dial in fit. It's a harder sell for anyone building a heavier load or who needs reliable hip-belt load transfer for consecutive long days.

## Verdict

The Qidian Pro is a legitimately useful pack at a genuinely low price, and the UHMWPE fabric is more durable than you'd expect for the money. But its comfort ceiling is real: the hip belt is mostly decorative above light loads, and the strap chaos adds friction to an otherwise clean concept. At this price, know exactly what you're getting — a capable minimalist hauler for light kits, not a refined suspension system. I'd rate it **6/10**: worth the buy if you hike light and your budget is tight, but experienced UL hikers should look at the Granite Gear Crown3 or stretch a bit further to the Durston Kakwa before settling here.
