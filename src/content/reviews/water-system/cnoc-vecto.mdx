## Overview

The CNOC Vecto is a collapsible TPU water container with two openings: a wide, slider-sealed mouth for scooping water, and a soda-bottle-style threaded neck for screwing on a filter. It is aimed at backpackers who squeeze or gravity-filter with a Sawyer or similar filter and are tired of the narrow-mouthed pouches those filters ship with. The trade-off is weight. The Vecto is roughly twice as heavy as the thinnest soft bottles, and it asks for a little more care than a rigid bottle.

## Performance

### Filling

Filling is the reason the Vecto has a following. The wide mouth takes a swipe or two through a stream to fill the bag, where a Sawyer pouch, which fills only through its 28 mm opening, takes many. In a shallow trickle or a silty puddle, that gap decides whether water collection takes thirty seconds or ten minutes. In still water such as a pond or lake, owners say it takes two hands: one to hold the mouth open, one to dunk the bag. Many hikers simply bail with a cook pot in those spots.

### Filtering

The threaded neck mates with any 28 mm filter. Users report a tight, leak-free seal with Sawyer filters when the threads are started carefully and not overtightened. A small number of Trailspace reviewers had trouble getting the filter to engage, and the fix is the same each time: seat the gasket squarely before tightening.

For gravity filtering, the loop on the upper closure lets the bag hang from a branch or a trekking-pole tripod. The slider seals watertight even when the bag is upside down, which is something the Platypus GravityWorks dirty bag cannot reliably do. Hang it, thread on the filter, and go pitch the tent.

### Carrying Water

The Vecto is a better water collector than a water carrier. It tends to squirm out of external straps unless it is full, and CNOC's own FAQ advises against packing a full bag inside a pack if leaks are a concern. Most owners carry it inside anyway without incident, but a hard bottle earns more trust in a pack with a laptop sleeve or a down quilt.

### Durability

The TPU rolls and flexes without the kinking that gives Sawyer's stock bags pinhole leaks. It also stands up to the pressure of hard squeeze-filtering. Over the long run, it wears. One longtime owner got close to two years from a Vecto before it sprang a leak after about 150 days on trail, a pattern that looks like fatigue rather than puncture. CNOC says pinholes from UV, cold and abrasion are not covered by warranty unless they appear on a new bag. A round patch of Tear-Aid Type A or a drop of superglue handles the repair and weighs next to nothing.

The slider and cap are tethered to the bag, so neither can be dropped in the dirt or lost in the dark. A lost cap can be replaced by any standard PET bottle cap. The slider is the weak point: if it breaks, it cannot be fixed in the field. CNOC sells replacements, which is little help on day nine of a trip.

### Cleaning and Taste

The Vecto turns inside out, dries in about twenty minutes after washing, and the wide mouth allows a hand inside for scrubbing. A new bag gives water a faint plastic taste that fades with use. TPU is porous and not completely odor-free from the factory, so CNOC recommends washing and airing it before the first trip.

## Choosing a Version

The three versions differ in capacity, thread size and weight, not in design.

**Water Container 2L (74 g)** is the lightest option and suits solo hikers who filter at the source and carry two liters or less. It packs to about 7 × 2 × 1.5 inches. It is the choice for hikers who count every ounce.

**28mm (79 g)** is the threaded configuration for the Sawyer Squeeze, Mini and Micro, the HydroBlu Versa Flow and the LifeStraw Flex. For anyone using one of those filters, it is the version to buy. Hikers running a 42 mm filter such as the BeFree should buy the 42 mm thread option instead, which is slightly heavier.

**3L (105 g)** adds 31 g over the 2L and packs to 7 × 2 × 2 inches. It suits dry camps, couples sharing a gravity setup, and anyone who wants to filter a full evening's cooking and drinking water in one pass. Its longer shape (about 17 inches when full) also hangs well from a tree. For hikers who regularly camp away from water, the 3L is worth the extra weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide mouth fills quickly from streams, seeps and shallow sources
- Threaded neck fits 28 mm filters, with a 42 mm option for BeFree users
- Built-in loop and watertight slider make gravity filtering easy
- Cap and slider are tethered
- Turns inside out for fast cleaning and drying
- TPU resists the kinking and pinholes that plague stock Sawyer pouches

**Cons**

- Heavier than minimalist soft bags: a 2L Platypus Platy weighs about 37 g against roughly 76 g for the Vecto 2L
- Pinholes can develop with age, UV and cold, and are not covered by warranty after purchase
- CNOC advises against carrying it full inside a pack
- A broken slider cannot be fixed on trail
- New bags taste of plastic until washed and broken in
- Not suited to drinking on the move, and it has no hose option

## Who Should Buy This

The Vecto fits backpackers who filter with a Sawyer, HydroBlu, LifeStraw Flex or BeFree and want a collection bag that does not fight them. The 3L suits dry-camp hikers and small groups, and the 2L suits solo hikers who carry little and filter at the source. Anyone who drinks from a hose on the move, or who wants the lightest possible bag for the least money, should look at a plain Platypus Platy or an Evernew bottle instead.

## Verdict

The Vecto does one job, collecting and holding untreated water for filtering, better than the pouches that come with most filters. It adds weight and demands a patch kit for long trips, but the fast fill and easy gravity setup justify both. At about $21, it is one of the better upgrades a Sawyer-based water system can get.
