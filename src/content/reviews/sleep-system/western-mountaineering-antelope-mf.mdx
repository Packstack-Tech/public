## Overview

The Antelope MF is Western Mountaineering's cold-shoulder-season workhorse: a 5°F (-15°C) mummy filled with 850+ goose down, wrapped in 20D MicroLite XP, and finished with a full down collar and draft tube. It is built for climbers, shoulder-season backpackers and anyone who sleeps cold. The trade-off is simple: roughly a pound and a half less than most 5°F bags of its era, paid for in dollars rather than features.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Seven inches of loft from roughly 26 ounces of down in the 6'0" is the whole story here. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers took it out on nights dipping into the low teens and found it warmer than the Kelty Cosmic Down, Rab Ascent 900, Nemo Sonic and Western Mountaineering's own lighter Versalite. The full down collar and draft tube do real work: a reviewer at ExpediTom singled out the snug collar as a feature many ultralight bags lack, and described the hood as lofty enough to make a beanie unnecessary. Owners who use it in alpine conditions tend to trust the rating, with one Trailspace reviewer saying he sticks to Western Mountaineering's conservative numbers after long, cold days of climbing. Cold sleepers should still pair it with a proper pad.

**Weight and packed size.** The manufacturer lists 2 lb 7 oz (1,105 g) for the 6'0". Scales tell a slightly different story. Both Outdoor Gear Lab (42 oz) and the Trailspace reviewer (2 lb 10 oz, 1,208 g) measured their bags noticeably heavier than the claim, so expect something closer to 1,200 g on a home scale. That is still excellent for a bag rated to 5°F. The bag stuffs to 8 x 17 inches, and ExpediTom's reviewer called the packed size astonishing for a -15°C bag, fitting it into a camera pack. The generous loft does make the bag a little fussy to stuff back into its sack, according to owners.

**Shell and weather resistance.** MicroLite XP is a tightly woven 20D microfiber that sheds light moisture, condensation and spindrift while staying breathable. Outdoor Gear Lab rated it strong and highly water resistant for its weight. It is not a waterproof shell. Down is still down, and a wet tent floor is still the enemy.

**Comfort and fit.** The 62-inch shoulder girth and 53-inch hips give more room than slim-cut ultralight mummies such as the Sea to Summit Spark II, per ExpediTom. Owners describe the fit as comfortable rather than coffin-tight, with room for side sleepers and a roomy footbox. Continuous baffles let the down be shifted toward the top in cold weather or toward the bottom when it is mild, which owners cite as the reason the bag stretches into warmer nights. The zipper earns repeated praise for not snagging, a rare thing in a down bag.

**Weak points.** Outdoor Gear Lab marked down the Velcro at the neck baffle as weak and the hood as shallow. ExpediTom's reviewer also reported a few feathers working through the fabric. Western Mountaineering sells overfill options (a footbox overfill for $40, or 3 oz of extra down throughout for $65) for those who want a bit more margin.

## Choosing a Version

The Antelope MF comes in three lengths with the same 8 x 17 inch packed size and the same loft.

- **5'6" (165 cm):** 1,050 g with 680 g of down. For shorter sleepers, and the lightest way into the bag.
- **6'0" (180 cm):** 1,105 g with 735 g of down. The standard size, and the right call for most average-height users.
- **6'6" (200 cm):** 1,160 g with roughly 790 g of down, plus an inch of extra girth at the shoulders and hips. For taller sleepers or those who want to stash boot liners and a water bottle inside.

Fitting the bag to height matters more than usual here. Extra length is dead space to heat, and a bag that is too short compresses the down at the feet.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional warmth for the weight, with real-world weight still under 1.3 kg
- 850+ goose down with a full collar and draft tube that seal out drafts
- Roomy cut that does not feel restrictive
- Smooth-running #5 YKK zipper, with left, right and clear options
- Packs to 8 x 17 inches
- Made in the USA with a long-lived, repairable build

**Cons**

- Expensive; one retailer lists it at $819
- Measured weights run above the claimed figure
- Shallow hood and weak neck-baffle Velcro in testers' notes
- Water-resistant shell, not waterproof, so it needs careful moisture management
- Overbuilt for summer; a lighter bag suits warm nights better

## Who Should Buy This

The Antelope MF suits alpine climbers, shoulder-season thru-hikers, and cold sleepers who want one bag to cover three seasons without hauling a two-pound-plus synthetic. It is also a good fit for people who spend long nights in cold camps and value a draft collar. Summer-only hikers and anyone who rarely sees temperatures below freezing will carry more bag than they need and should look at a 20°F or 30°F option. Those who want a snugger, lighter cut should check the narrower bags in the Western Mountaineering line.

## Verdict

The Antelope MF is a very good cold-weather down bag: warm, compressible, comfortable and built to last for years. The price is high and the hood and collar closure could be better, but few 5°F bags beat its warmth for the weight. Buyers who need the rating should buy it in the length that fits and not look back.
