## Overview

The StormLine Stretch is Black Diamond's do-it-all rain shell. It's a 2.5-layer jacket with a soft two-way stretch face, pit zips and a helmet-compatible hood, built to move between hiking, climbing and skiing. The trade-off is weight. At 320 g it carries features most ultralight shells leave off, and reviewers who tested it in sustained rain say the extras earn their place.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The StormLine's reputation rests on staying dry, and testers say it does. One reviewer in rainy Bellingham, Washington, wore it for two months of downpours, wet snow and high wind. The face fabric beaded water throughout and never soaked through. Even the wrists, where many shells wet out first, showed only minimal surface saturation after six hours in heavy rain. A Pennsylvania tester who wore it through a wet summer, autumn and early winter reached the same conclusion, and singled out the well-sealed zippers.

The 10,000 mm hydrostatic rating puts it in the middle of the field. UK outlet The Great Outdoors called that rating a good benchmark for a spring-summer hillwalking waterproof. In its view the jacket handles drizzle, showers and the odd sudden downpour. It is not storm-level protection on paper, though field reports suggest the taped seams and DWR make up for much of that on typical trips.

**Breathability.** Here the shell shows its limits. The membrane is rated at 10,000 g/m² per 24 hours, and Gear Institute's testers flagged it as not the most breathable jacket they used, recommending it for low-aerobic activity. The pit zips are the fix. Reviewers describe them as large and mesh-free, and say they provide plenty of venting for high-output hiking. On a steep, humid climb, hikers will end up unzipping them early and often.

**Comfort and fit.** The stretch face is the other selling point. Gear Institute's testers called it the most comfortable jacket they tested that year, with a soft feel against the skin. Women's-model reviewers describe easy movement with no sense of restriction. The cut is Black Diamond's "regular" fit. It is roomy enough over a couple of insulating layers without looking like a poncho over a base layer. A waist cinch, a rear hood cinch and hook-and-loop cuffs that adapt to glove thickness help seal out wind. Reviewers also credit the fabric with strong windchill protection in gusts.

**Hood and zippers.** Owners are less unanimous about the hood. Some Backcountry buyers praise it, but a Trailspace reviewer listed hood size and function among the jacket's weak points. The same reviewer also noted some resistance in the zippers. Both are the kind of fit-and-finish quibbles that show up on a budget-conscious shell, not deal-breakers.

**Weight and packability.** At 320 g, the StormLine is a three-season workhorse. It is not a gram-counter's jacket, and Gear Institute described it as a little heavy. It does stuff into its own pocket, which testers appreciated, and it carries features that lighter 2.5L shells usually skip, including pit zips, zippered handwarmer pockets and a stiffer, more structured hood.

**Durability and value.** SectionHiker characterized it as tough and functional for a moderately priced jacket, and judged it lightweight enough for year-round use. On Backcountry, the men's version averaged about 4.6 stars across 168 ratings.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Reliable waterproofing in multi-hour, multi-week field tests
- Soft two-way stretch face moves well and feels good against the skin
- Large pit zips offset the modest breathability
- Fully seam-taped, with a helmet-compatible hood and adjustable hood, cuffs and hem
- Packs into its own pocket
- Good price for a feature-rich shell

**Cons**

- 320 g is heavy by ultralight standards
- 10,000/10,000 ratings are mid-pack, so it runs warm in sustained aerobic effort with the pit zips closed
- Some owners find the hood and zippers fussy
- Regular-fit cut leaves it a bit bulky for those who want a trim, minimalist layer

## Who Should Buy This

The StormLine suits backpackers and hikers in rainy climates who want a durable, comfortable shell that can also handle a day on skis or a scramble with a helmet, without paying alpine-shell prices. It is a strong choice for section hikers, weekend backpackers and anyone who values pit zips and a stretch face over saving 100 grams. Thru-hikers building a sub-5-lb base weight, and anyone who sweats through every climb, should look at lighter, more breathable shells instead.

## Verdict

The StormLine Stretch is a practical, comfortable and well-sealed rain shell that keeps water out in sustained weather. The weight and middling breathability keep it out of the ultralight conversation, but its feature set and value make it an easy recommendation for general backpacking and all-around three-season use.
