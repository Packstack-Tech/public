## Overview

Tenacious Tape Patches are Gear Aid's pre-cut, outdoor-shaped take on its classic repair tape: a three-pack of peel-and-stick nylon patches in animal, mountain and geometric shapes, ready to press over a tear without scissors, thread or heat. They suit hikers who want a fast field fix for a puffy, tent, pack or stuff sack that doesn't look like a strip of duct tape. The trade-off is fabric compatibility. The adhesive grips technical nylon and vinyl very well and slips on silicone-treated cloth.

## Performance

The patches use the same backing-and-adhesive approach that has kept Tenacious Tape in repair kits for decades. Gear Aid describes the adhesive as ultra-aggressive and says it won't peel off technical fabrics. Reviewers who have compared it with duct tape say the bond is noticeably stickier.

Application takes under a minute. Peel the backing and press the patch onto a clean, smooth, dry surface. Gear Aid recommends giving it time to set. The patch is waterproof right away, but it is only machine washable after a 24-hour cure. A patch applied at camp should stay out of the washer, and the dryer, until the next resupply.

Durability reports are strong. A reviewer at Garage Grown Gear applied shaped patches to a down jacket, a tent and several stuff sacks, including a Dyneema one. The patches didn't shift despite friction, stuffing, washing and soaking. The same reviewer saw no fading in sunlight and no curling at the edges, which is where cheaper fabric tapes usually fail. Long-term owners of the standard roll tell a similar story. One REI reviewer left Tenacious Tape on a torn NeoAir pad and a ripped pack as a "temporary" fix. It held through many more trips with no leaks. Gear Aid's own page quotes a customer whose roll, bought in 2003, was still sticky when the last of it went onto a pair of hiking pants.

The shapes do more than decorate. Rounded outlines have no sharp corners to catch on a pack strap or a sleeping bag shell. That is the reason Adventure Alan gives for preferring hex-shaped patches over rectangles. The sheet's leftover border is usable too: the Garage Grown Gear reviewer trimmed it into smaller pieces for tiny punctures.

Limits matter here, and the biggest is silicone. Gear Aid says standard Tenacious Tape doesn't bond well to silicone-treated fabrics, so silnylon and silpoly shelters and packs need the company's dedicated silnylon patch. The patches also do poorly on textured or porous surfaces such as mesh and shoe uppers. They aren't intended for underwater use or very stretchy materials like neoprene. Retail feedback reflects the mismatches. The black pack has 3.8 stars across 77 Walmart ratings, with about a fifth of reviewers giving one star. Buyers who matched the patch to the fabric, such as a down jacket, an air mattress or a coat, mostly report clean fixes.

At 70 g per pack, as listed, the patches are a trivial addition to a repair kit. Most hikers would carry one or two from the pack. The sheet is flat, so it slips into a first-aid pouch or the back of a pad-repair kit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Waterproof bond that holds through washing, stuffing and sun exposure, according to owners
- Peel-and-stick application with no scissors, thread or heat
- Pre-cut shapes with rounded outlines that resist catching and peeling
- Works on ripstop nylon, vinyl, polyester and Gore-Tex
- Three patches per pack, plus usable border material for micro-repairs
- Adhesive is stronger than duct tape and doesn't leave sticky residue behind

**Cons**

- Poor grip on silnylon, silpoly and other silicone-treated fabrics
- Not suited to mesh, shoes, neoprene or other stretchy and textured surfaces
- Fixed shapes cover large or irregular tears less efficiently than a roll cut to size
- More expensive per square inch than a roll of tape
- Novelty shapes are a matter of taste; hikers who want an invisible repair should choose plain black or clear

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and weekend backpackers carrying down jackets, nylon rain shells, or tents and packs in standard nylon or Dyneema-faced fabrics will get the most from these patches. They also suit anyone who wants a repair that survives the laundry and looks deliberate. Hikers whose shelter or pack is silnylon or silpoly should buy Gear Aid's silnylon patch instead. Anyone patching large tears or mesh should carry a roll of tape and a needle.

## Verdict

Tenacious Tape Patches deliver the brand's proven, weatherproof bond in a shape that's easy to apply and easy to live with. Check the fabric before packing them, since silicone-treated gear is the one place they fail. For everything else in a typical kit they are a light, cheap and reliable fix.
