## Overview

The Marmot PreCip Eco Full-Zip Pants have been a staple of the mid-range rain gear market for good reason: they combine 10,000mm waterproofing, fully taped seams, and full-length side zippers into a package that won't break the bank. <cite index="3-10">Now made of recycled NanoPro nylon ripstop fabric, the classic PreCip pant has stepped up to meet the expectations of the more eco-conscious modern buyer.</cite> They're aimed squarely at three-season hikers and backpackers who want a capable, packable rain pant without paying Gore-Tex prices.

## Performance

### Waterproofing

The PreCip Eco holds up well in its primary job. <cite index="13-17,13-18">During extended periods of heavy rain, water consistently beads off the surface and no moisture makes it through.</cite> <cite index="6-14">The recycled nylon fabric, combined with fully taped seams and storm flaps over the zippers, ensures no water penetrates the critical seam points.</cite> The 10,000mm rating is honest mid-range territory — <cite index="7-18">these pants will keep you dry in light to moderate rain, but they may not be your best option in sustained heavy weather.</cite> That said, I've seen users report staying dry through solid downpours. Manage your expectations for multi-day deluges rather than a passing Pacific Northwest squall.

### Breathability

On paper, 17,000 g/m²/24h is a strong number for this price point — <cite index="7-19,7-20">that's above average for the category.</cite> In practice, the picture is more nuanced. <cite index="13-19">While no rain pant is entirely "sweat-proof" during intense aerobic activity, these perform admirably, managing moisture buildup better than many alternatives.</cite> However, some long-time users have been more skeptical: <cite index="2-12,2-13">in warmer rainy weather, you can't unzip the sides or rain will get in, and if you keep them fully zipped, perspiration can build up quickly.</cite> This is the fundamental tension in any non-vented rain pant. The good news is the full-length zippers do double duty: <cite index="7-21,7-22">the zip can be opened from the top or the bottom to provide ventilation, and the pockets can be unzipped to increase airflow further.</cite> Use those vents aggressively on uphills and you'll manage.

### Full-Zip Entry System

This is where the PreCip Eco Full-Zip earns its price premium over the standard ankle-zip model. <cite index="3-3">Each zippered leg has two sliders, allowing for full foot-to-hip and hip-to-foot zipping, which makes sliding the rain pants over hiking pants a cinch.</cite> <cite index="3-5">The waterproof boots already on your feet don't need to come off — the pants slip right over chunky boots without fuss.</cite> For anyone who's ever done the soggy one-legged shuffle trying to pull on rain pants in a downpour, this feature alone justifies the choice. <cite index="4-21">Full-zip is a rare feature at this price range.</cite>

### Weight in Context

At 312g (11 oz), the PreCip Eco Full-Zip isn't a gram-counter's choice. <cite index="11-4,11-5">The Outdoor Research Helium comes in under six ounces — nearly half the weight of many pants in the category — though it isn't as feature-rich.</cite> If you're building a strict UL kit, the Helium or a sub-6 oz cuben option will serve you better. But <cite index="1-15">the PreCip Eco provides an ideal weight-to-performance ratio, ensuring you stay dry without sacrificing comfort or mobility.</cite> The full-zip hardware does contribute meaningfully to that 312g number — <cite index="4-20">the full length zippers do add some bulk</cite> compared to simpler ankle-zip designs.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-length dual-slider side zippers are genuinely useful — on/off over boots in seconds
- Solid 10,000mm / 17,000 g/m²/24h specs for the price
- PFC-free DWR that holds up through multiple washes
- 100% recycled nylon is a real sustainability step, not just marketing language
- Available in Short, Regular, and Long inseams — rare at this price point
- Three zippered pockets, plus storm flaps over all zippers
- Lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- 312g is on the heavier end for three-season rain pants; not a UL option
- Breathability under sustained aerobic effort in warm rain is modest — you'll feel the humidity
- Thin, unreinforced fabric won't survive sustained bushwhacking or crampon use
- Velcro waist closure can wear out with repeated use
- Side zippers can occasionally catch on the thin face fabric
- Pockets become mostly useless when layered over hiking pants — waist drops with weight

## Who Should Buy This

The PreCip Eco Full-Zip is the right call for three-season hikers and backpackers who want a dependable rain pant with no hassle at the trailhead. <cite index="1-16,1-17">It's an excellent choice for eco-conscious adventurers who need reliable rain protection without breaking the bank — the NanoPro Eco technology and recycled construction make it a solid option for hiking, backpacking, and casual outdoor use.</cite> The full-zip feature specifically makes it worth the extra cost over the standard model if you hike in boots, wear gaiters, or just hate stopping to undress. It's not for weight-obsessed UL thru-hikers — look at the OR Helium or a similar minimalist option if every ounce counts. It's also not the right tool for sustained technical mountaineering, where a more robust hardshell is warranted.

## Verdict

The Marmot PreCip Eco Full-Zip Pants are a well-rounded, genuinely versatile rain pant that earns its place in the mid-range category. The full-zip entry system is the real differentiator, and the recycled NanoPro fabric backs up its spec sheet in real conditions. The main tradeoffs are weight and under-exertion breathability — if neither of those concern you, this is a hard pant to argue against at around $100.

**Rating: 7.5/10**
