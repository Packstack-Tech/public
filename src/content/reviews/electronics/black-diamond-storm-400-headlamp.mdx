## Overview

The Storm 400 is Black Diamond's weather-sealed workhorse: 400 lumens, four AAA cells, and a control scheme that survives gloves and cold fingers. It suits backpackers who night-hike, cook after dark, or run into foul weather and want a lamp that simply works. The trade-off is mass. At 120 g with batteries it weighs well over what most ultralight kits allow for a light.

## Performance

**Output and beam.** Reviewers consistently describe the Storm 400 as bright enough for fast trail travel. The 100 m maximum throw is generous for a lamp this size. One Backcountry buyer noted that the full 400 lumens is plenty for camp use, with the caveat that anyone who looks your way will be blinded. The spot and wide settings let hikers focus on the trail or flood the area for peripheral vision. A close-range setting handles tasks like sorting gear and cooking.

**Controls.** PowerTap is the signature feature. A swipe or tap on the main button jumps straight to full output and back to your dimmed level. Brightness memory means the lamp turns on at the level you last used instead of defaulting to maximum. Owners who do close-up work, such as hunters and anglers, name that memory as the reason they bought it. A secondary switch changes beam modes. Some Backcountry reviewers still found the controls confusing at first. Three small dots on the side show remaining battery.

**Night-vision modes.** Red, green, and blue modes are included, which is more than most lamps in this price range offer. Owners like the red for preserving night vision around camp. A recurring complaint is that the colored LEDs are dim, with at least one reviewer wishing the red were brighter.

**Battery life.** This is the lamp's weak point on high. Black Diamond rates it at 5 hours on max. Climbing's tester drained a set of cells in two nights of heavy use on the top setting. The low setting is a different story: 150 hours on paper, and one long-term owner who has logged hundreds of miles says disciplined use of the dimmer levels makes a set last for weeks of nightly use. Another reviewer was less impressed, calling the lamp a battery hog. The pattern is consistent: run it on medium or low and it is frugal, leave it on high and it eats cells.

**AAA power.** Four AAAs are included, and they're the reason for both the weight and the flexibility. Owners like being able to buy rechargeable AAAs, carry spares, or pick up cells at any gas station in a resupply town. Reviewers who wanted USB charging say they wish they had bought the rechargeable version.

**Weather and build.** The sealed housing is rated IP67, and Black Diamond says it withstands immersion over 1 m for 30 minutes. Anglers and hunters working in rain and dirt report no durability problems. One reviewer called the housing plastic a little cheap-feeling. One Amazon owner described the latch (the battery-door catch) as a weak link. Black Diamond backs its headlamps with a three-year warranty.

**Comfort.** The headband is adjustable and comfortable over long sessions according to Backcountry and Steep & Cheap reviewers, and one climber described it as barely there. The 120 g is noticeable, though. One Amazon reviewer found the lamp heavy enough to chafe when worn without a hat. For comparison, Black Diamond's Spot 400 uses three AAAs and weighs 86 g.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Bright output with a 100 m maximum beam
- Simple controls, with PowerTap and brightness memory
- IP67 sealing handles rain and dust
- Red, green, and blue night-vision modes
- Standard AAA cells are easy to resupply, and rechargeables work too
- Three-year warranty

**Cons**

- 120 g is heavy for an ultralight kit
- About 5 hours on high
- Not rechargeable
- Colored modes are dim
- Reports of a weak battery-door latch and cheap-feeling plastic

## Who Should Buy This

The Storm 400 fits backpackers who want a dependable, bright, weatherproof lamp and don't weigh every gram: shoulder-season hikers, climbers, hunters, and anyone who regularly hikes or cooks after dark. It also fits those who prefer swappable AAAs to a built-in battery. Thru-hikers counting grams and those who night-hike only occasionally should look at lighter three-AAA models or rechargeables. Anyone who needs a high-output beam for many hours a night will burn through cells quickly.

## Verdict

The Storm 400 is a dependable, well-priced lamp whose intuitive controls and weather sealing hold up in real use. Its 120 g weight and short high-mode runtime keep it out of the lightest kits. Buy it as a durable general-purpose headlamp, not as an ultralight pick.
