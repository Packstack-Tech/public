## Overview

The TOAKS Light Titanium 650 ml is a single-wall titanium pot built around one job: boiling water for one person at the lowest weight that still comes with a lid, folding handles and a stuff sack. At 80 g with the lid, it sits at the point where a pot starts to feel like a mug with ambitions. The trade-off is the 0.3 mm wall. That wall makes it feather-light, and it also makes it less tolerant of abuse than TOAKS's standard 0.4 mm pots.

## Performance

**Capacity.** Six hundred and fifty milliliters is the right amount for a solo hiker on a freeze-dried diet. It's enough to rehydrate a bag meal and leave a little water for tea or coffee. Retailers pitch it as exactly that, and owners agree. One buyer called it "just the right size for one" and noted it isn't too wide to sit steadily on a lightweight stove.

The ceiling is real, though. A forum poster comparing it with a 1-liter pot judged it fine if all you do is boil water and pour it into something else. A Backpacking Light member who owns both the 650 and TOAKS's 700 ml pot used the smaller one with an alcohol stove. He ended up preferring the 700 for a John Muir Trail trip and every trip since. Hikers who cook real meals, or boil for two, will feel the limit quickly.

**Weight.** TOAKS lists 60 g for the pot and 80 g with the lid. One retailer's listing puts the pot and lid at about 72 g, with the mesh sack adding roughly 14 g. The thicker TOAKS Titanium line adds weight and stiffness, but this pot sits at the light end of titanium cookware you can buy for under $40.

**Handles and lid.** The low-profile handles fold flat against the wall and have a small bend that keeps fingers back from the heat. The lid has a stand-up lifter with a locking grip and vent holes for steam. Owners note that the vent holes let the lid double as a strainer for pasta or ramen water. A reviewer on a related TOAKS pot mentioned a bit of lid rattle in some units. A common fix is to remove the bail, squeeze it slightly and refit it. The handle design here is different, so that fix won't carry over.

**Nesting.** This is where the 650 earns its place in a kit. It swallows a 110 g gas canister with a small stove, with room for a sponge or a lighter. It also fits a TOAKS 375 ml or 450 ml cup, and it slips over a 1-liter Nalgene without covering the bottle's lid. A Backpacking Light member reported that a small MiniBull Designs pot fits inside it, and the 650 itself nests inside TOAKS's 900 ml D115 and 1100 ml pots. TOAKS's retailers suggest wrapping a small towel or pot protector around the canister, which stops the rattle and prevents a rust ring from forming in the pot.

**Durability and heat.** The 0.3 mm wall is the point of the pot, and also its weak spot. UK retailer Ultralight Outdoor Gear said plainly that it won't be as durable as the standard-wall originals, and that hikers have to choose between weight and toughness. The pot has no coating, so nothing flakes off, and titanium gives no metallic aftertaste. It's best suited to boiling water. Thin titanium concentrates heat over a small flame, so anyone simmering sauces or cooking over a hot canister stove needs to stir and watch it. The rounded bottom edge helps with cleanup, and the interior graduations make measuring water easy without a separate cup.

**Owner satisfaction.** Ratings are consistently high. REI's page shows 4.7 out of 5 across 42 reviews, and every one of the 27 reviewers who answered the question would recommend it. Owners on other retailer pages echo the same themes: light, well made and sized right for one person.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 80 g with lid, 60 g without
- Fits a 110 g canister and small stove, plus a TOAKS cup or a 1 L Nalgene
- Folding handles, vented lid with locking grip, and an internal ounce/ml scale
- Uncoated titanium with no aftertaste
- Priced around $37, which is modest for titanium
- Includes a mesh stuff sack

**Cons**

- The 0.3 mm wall dents more easily than TOAKS's 0.4 mm pots
- 650 ml is tight for two people or for a meal cooked in the pot
- Thin walls can scorch food over a hot flame
- The lid and handles are light and basic, so some owners report a rattly lid or a hot grip

## Who Should Buy This

This is the pot for the solo hiker who boils water, rehydrates dinner in a bag, and wants a mug-sized vessel that nests a small stove and fuel can. It suits thru-hikers trimming their cook kit, alcohol-stove users, and anyone who wants a titanium cup that also works as a pot. Hikers who cook for two, simmer actual meals, or are hard on gear should look at TOAKS's 0.4 mm 750 or 900 ml pots, which cost a few grams more and hold more water.

## Verdict

The Light Titanium 650 ml delivers the weight, the nesting and the solo capacity that ultralight hikers want, at a price well below most titanium cookware. It rewards careful handling and a boil-and-pour cooking style. Solo hikers will find little to complain about.
