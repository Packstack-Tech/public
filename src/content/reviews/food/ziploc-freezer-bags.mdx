## Overview

Ziploc's gallon freezer bag is the unglamorous constant of long-distance hiking: a clear, double-zipper polyethylene bag that costs well under a dollar and shows up in resupply boxes, food bags and pack-liner systems from the PCT to the Pyrenees. It is built for freezers, not for trails, but its thicker film and sturdier closure make it a clear step up from the sandwich-bag tier. The trade-off is plain. It is cheap and reusable, but it is still thin plastic, and it will not take much abuse.

## Performance

**Closure.** The double zipper is the main reason hikers pick freezer bags over the bargain alternatives. Ziploc's current design pairs two zipper tracks with an extended pull tab and a textured grip, and the Smart Zip Plus seal gives a tactile and audible cue when it closes from edge to edge. Owners who have switched from slider-style bags report the zippers do not jump the track and ruin the bag. A recurring complaint is that the seal can be fiddly to start with cold, wet or gloved fingers, and some buyers say recent batches tear along the side seams when opened.

**Durability.** At 2.7 mil, the film is meant to resist freezer conditions and the odd poke. In a pack, that translates to a bag that survives portioned snacks, tortillas, dehydrated dinners and spare socks, and fails on sharp things: tent stakes, trekking pole tips, dried pasta shards, the corner of a bear canister. Thru-hikers describe Ziploc freezer bags as durable enough for a full trail when washed and dried at resupply, but not in the same league as Loksak OPsaks, which cost far more. Hikers who reuse every bag report mixed results: some get months out of a single bag, others lose them to rips within a couple of uses.

**Water and odor.** The seal is rated to keep air out, and Ziploc says the bags hold off freezer burn for up to 12 weeks in ground beef testing. That is a freezer claim, not a creek-crossing claim. On a trail, the bags shed splashes and keep a phone, ID or paper map dry in a rainy morning, but they are not a submersion-proof dry bag, and a bag with a slightly open corner will wick water. They also do not contain food odor; they are not a substitute for an odor-proof liner in bear country.

**Size.** The gallon format is the awkward middle child of the backpacking food world. It swallows a full resupply of bars, a day's food, a stuff-sack-worth of clothing or a cook kit, which makes it useful as a modular organizer. For freezer-bag-style meals, though, the backpacking tradition leans on the quart size, where hot water goes in, the bag is sealed, and a pot cozy handles the rest. Forum users who do this report no trouble with boiling water in quart freezer bags, but Ziploc's own rating covers microwave use, so that remains an off-label hack. A gallon bag is too wide to eat from comfortably.

**Weight and cost.** At 19 g per bag, one gallon bag adds little, and a week's worth of them is lighter than a single silnylon stuff sack. A 28-count box typically sells for around $6 to $8 at hardware and farm-supply retailers, so replacing bags is trivial.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Double zipper holds up better than single-track or slider bags
- Costs pennies per bag and is available in nearly every town
- Washable and reusable between resupplies
- Clear film makes it easy to find the right food or gear at a glance
- Large gallon capacity works as a resupply or organization bag

**Cons**

- Punctures and tears at the corners and side seams, especially with hard or sharp contents
- Not waterproof for submersion and not odor-proof
- Zipper takes some effort to start with wet or cold hands
- Single-use plastic that most hikers will replace regularly
- Gallon size is too big for most trail meals and snacks

## Who Should Buy This

Thru-hikers and section hikers who want a cheap, easy-to-replace container for resupply boxes, food organization, electronics and paperwork. It is also a reasonable choice for car campers and anyone building a DIY meal kit at home. Hikers who need guaranteed waterproofing, long-term abuse resistance or odor control should look at roll-top dry bags, OPsaks or purpose-made odor-proof liners. Those mainly packing trail meals should buy the quart size instead.

## Verdict

Ziploc's gallon freezer bag is a cheap, familiar, light and good-enough container that earns a place in most food bags. It will not last forever, and it will not hold up to rough use. For the price, no other bag does this much.
