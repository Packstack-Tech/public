## Overview

The Petzl NAO is the headlamp ultrarunners have long reached for when the sun goes down and the miles don't stop. The current NAO RL pairs a thin nine-LED front unit with a rear battery pack and Reactive Lighting, a sensor that adjusts brightness and beam pattern on its own. It suits night runners and fast-and-light hikers who want serious output without a heavy head unit. The trade-off is a premium price and an auto mode that some owners find fussy.

## Performance

**Weight and fit.** The NAO RL's selling point is how little it asks of your neck. Road Trail Run's reviewer clocked the battery pack at 74 g and the head unit with straps and cable at 71 g. That is about 45 g less than the model it replaced. The front plate is thin, flexible and vented, so heat from nine LEDs dissipates away from the forehead. Testers at Ultra Running Magazine and UKC both noted virtually no bounce on the run, thanks to a rear pack that counterweights the front. The headband borrows from Petzl's IKO, with a bungee tightening system instead of a full strap, and the pack rides securely against the back of the head.

Not everyone gets along with it. Some REI buyers called the split elastic strap fiddly and slow to dial in. A skier reported the lamp wouldn't stay put over a helmet.

**Reactive Lighting.** The sensor reads ambient light and reflected light off whatever you're looking at. The lamp dims when you stare at nearby objects or at dawn, then ramps up when you look down the trail. Petzl says the result is longer burn time and less button-fiddling. Ultra Running Magazine's testers found the low-to-high transitions smoother and less jumpy than on earlier NAOs.

Owner opinion is split. Several REI reviewers still describe the transitions as jumpy. One said the lamp wouldn't reach peak brightness in Reactive mode, even in dark woods. Another complained of a narrow central spot with a dim flood. Fog and breath condensation can also fool the sensor. A Skimo reviewer wished the mode could be disabled permanently, because the lamp cycles through it every time it's switched on. The manual fixed levels are the fallback, and plenty of experienced night runners treat Reactive as a situational tool rather than a default.

**Output and beam.** Petzl rates the NAO RL at 1,500 lumens on Max Power, with UKC's group test listing steps of 1,500, 800 and 300 lumens. Testers found the beam even, with a mixed flood-and-spot pattern that handles both footing at your feet and trail ahead. One REI reviewer measured fixed mode as much brighter than its roughly 900-lumen rating suggested. That kind of headroom is welcome on a fast technical descent.

**Battery.** Petzl's minimum burn time is 5 hours in Reactive mode at the Standard level. Dropping to the lowest level stretches that into the tens of hours, and a skier reported skinning on the low setting with long battery life. Higher levels drain faster, so anyone planning an all-night effort at high output should carry a second pack. The USB-C port charges the pack and can also push power out to a phone. One user reported going from 2 to 5 bars in about 90 minutes on a 20 W adapter.

**Weather.** IPX4 covers rain and sweat. It does not cover submersion, and Petzl's own pitch makes no claim beyond that.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions are separated by weight and by design generation. The **NAO RL (145 g)** is the current lamp: the 1,500-lumen output, thin head unit, USB-C pack and easy rear red light described above. The **NAO (185 g)** is the heavier earlier-generation design. That chassis topped out at 750 lumens, used a bulkier front unit and managed its rear light through an app. It remains well-regarded for durability and battery life, and UKC forum owners praise how bombproof it feels. Even so, Road Trail Run's reviewer said the RL was lighter, brighter and about $30 cheaper at launch, and recommended it as an upgrade even to current NAO+ owners.

For nearly everyone, the NAO RL is the right pick. The 185 g NAO only makes sense for someone who already owns the compatible batteries or finds one at a steep discount.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 145 g total, with a balanced rear battery that stays put at running pace
- 1,500-lumen peak with a smooth, wide-plus-spot beam
- Reactive Lighting can stretch battery life without button presses
- USB-C charging, with power-out for a phone
- Rear red light on its own button; reflective headband

**Cons**

- Reactive mode draws mixed reviews, with jumpy transitions and sensor confusion in fog
- Strap system takes time to adjust and is awkward over helmets
- IPX4 is modest for a premium lamp
- Peak output drains the pack quickly; long nights need a spare
- Top-tier price compared with simpler 500-lumen lamps

## Who Should Buy This

The NAO RL is built for ultrarunners, fastpackers and thru-hikers who log real mileage after dark and want a lightweight, high-output lamp that doesn't bounce. It also suits anyone running technical trail at pace, where throw and a wide, even flood both matter. Hikers who use a headlamp for camp chores and the odd pre-dawn start can skip it. A 300–500 lumen lamp costs far less and weighs less. Skiers who wear a helmet should look at lamps with a better mounting system.

## Verdict

The NAO RL is a fast, light, powerful night-running headlamp with comfort few rivals match, even if its signature auto-dimming mode takes some patience. Buy it if you need 1,500 lumens and a balanced fit for long nights. The older 185 g NAO is the one to pass over.
