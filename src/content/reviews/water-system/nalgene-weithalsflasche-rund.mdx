## Overview

Nalgene's wide-mouth round bottle is the plain HDPE workhorse of the line. It is a translucent, BPA-free container that comes in sizes from a 30 ml sauce vial to a 1.5 L water carrier. It suits backpackers who want one leakproof, easy-to-fill system for liquids and dry goods. The trade-off is weight: it is sturdier and more versatile than a disposable soda bottle, and it is also heavier.

## Performance

**Weight.** HDPE is the reason this bottle belongs in a pack at all. Nalgene's clear Tritan bottles are the ones most people know, and the HDPE versions weigh roughly half as much. One reviewer measured a 32 oz wide-mouth HDPE bottle at 3.75 oz, against about 6 oz for the clear equivalent. At the small end of the range, the listed 25 g is close to nothing, and a pair of condiment or soap bottles barely registers on a scale.

Water carriers are a harder sell for gram counters. A 1 L HDPE wide-mouth weighs about 100–125 g depending on the retailer's measurement. A narrow-mouth Smartwater bottle weighs around 28 g. That is a gap of roughly 70–95 g per liter, and a reviewer at Trailspace advised serious gram-counters to pass on the larger bottles for that reason.

**Sealing and durability.** Owners praise the leak resistance. The wide, straight-shouldered threads and screw cap have a decades-long record. Long-term buyers on REI and Nalgene's own site describe bottles that survived years of travel, bike camping and rough handling without cracking or leaking. The cap stays attached to the bottle on the larger water versions, so it can't be lost at a stream crossing. Nalgene's HDPE also tolerates freezing, a benefit when a bottle has to survive a cold night.

**Filling, cleaning and mixing.** The wide opening is the main practical advantage over a narrow-neck soda bottle. It accepts ice, drink powder, electrolyte tablets, and Aquamira drops without fuss, and it is easy to scrub out. A reviewer at PMags notes it handles boiling water without melting, which is why it shows up in cold-weather kits as a bed-warming hot water bottle and a spare mug. The mouth is too big for Sawyer-style threaded filters, so it doesn't work for people who thread a filter directly onto the bottle.

**Small sizes.** This is where the line stops being a water bottle. The 30 ml size carries hot sauce or soap, a 250 ml bottle holds alcohol stove fuel, and mid-size bottles hold cooking oil, spices, or a day's snacks. Because they are all one thread-and-cap design, owners trust them to stay shut inside a food bag. They are also translucent, so the contents and level are visible at a glance.

**Taste.** HDPE can impart a faint plastic taste, especially when new. SierraDescents flagged it in a long-running comparison with polycarbonate bottles. The same reviewer rates HDPE the safest choice among Nalgene's plastics for people wary of chemical leaching, and stays loyal to it for its weight and wide mouth.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Wide size range, from 30 ml to 1.5 L, covers water, fuel, food and toiletries with one system
- HDPE is about half the weight of the clear Tritan equivalent
- Leakproof screw closure with a long record of trail use
- Wide mouth makes filling, cleaning, and adding powders or ice easy
- BPA-free, dishwasher safe, and rugged enough to last for years

**Cons**

- Heavier per liter than a disposable soda bottle, by roughly 70–95 g at the 1 L size
- Mouth doesn't fit common threaded filters
- Can leave a faint plastic taste, particularly when new
- Rigid shape wastes pack space compared with a soft flask when empty

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers who want a durable water bottle that also takes hot drinks, and anyone who needs leakproof containers for stove fuel, oil, spices or toiletries, will get good service from it. The small sizes make the best case: they are cheap and light, and they replace the sandwich bags and travel containers that tend to fail in a pack. Thru-hikers who put a threaded filter on a Smartwater bottle, and gram-counters who only need to carry water, should stay with the disposable bottle.

## Verdict

The Nalgene wide-mouth round bottle is a dependable, adaptable piece of kit that holds up better than anything it's competing with. It loses on weight for pure water carrying, but wins on everything else, and the small sizes are a bargain for organizing a food and kit bag. Buy it for versatility, not for the scale.
