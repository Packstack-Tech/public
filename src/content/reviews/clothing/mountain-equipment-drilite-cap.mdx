## Overview

The Drilite Cap is Mountain Equipment's answer to a problem every hood-hater knows well: sometimes you just want your head dry without sacrificing peripheral vision and sound. It's a 5-panel running-style cap built from the same waterproof/breathable Drilite membrane technology ME has used in its shells for decades, making it one of the few caps on the market that's genuinely rated waterproof rather than just water-resistant. This one's aimed at hikers, trekkers, and mountain runners who want a low-profile alternative to a rain hood for showers and drizzle, not sustained deluges where a full hood and brim would serve better.

## Performance

The headline feature is right there in the name — this cap is actually waterproof, not just DWR-treated cotton twill that wets out after twenty minutes. Mountain Equipment's Drilite membrane technology <cite index="13-3,13-4">carries everything the brand has learned about comfort and protection in the mountains, with a waterproof membrane thin enough to keep jackets packable while still tough enough for serious routes</cite>. That same membrane in cap form means you get real rain shedding rather than the "resists a light mist" performance most softshell caps offer.

Reviewers consistently point to two things: it works, and it doesn't cook your head doing it. One buyer noted after a wet Ben Nevis climb that the cap <cite index="4-15">fits well, kept my head dry and the rain off my face</cite>. Another long-term reviewer summed up the core value proposition well, saying it <cite index="16-1">does exactly what I need it to in foul weather: keeps my head dry without turning into a sauna</cite>. That balance matters — a fully waterproof membrane on your head could easily become a swamp on a climb, but the breathability of Drilite seems to keep pace reasonably well with cap-level exertion (not the same as a hardshell hood, where venting is harder).

The brim gets specific praise from glasses wearers. One user noted the <cite index="16-7">structured brim does a great job of shedding water and keeping my lenses clearer for longer, which makes a real difference when visibility matters</cite> — a detail that matters a lot if you've ever tried to navigate a switchback trail with rain-speckled lenses. The curved brim shape seems to be doing real work here, not just cosmetic.

Comfort-wise, the tricot-lined headband earns consistent mentions for sweat management. As one review put it, <cite index="16-6">the tricot headband adds a bit of comfort and helps manage sweat, a nice touch during high-output climbs</cite>.

The one recurring gripe is the rear adjustment strap. A verified buyer review flagged it as <cite index="4-3">their only minor gripe</cite> with an otherwise comfortable, breathable, and waterproof cap. Another reviewer likewise suggested <cite index="16-7,8-7">checking the fit if you're on the larger end of the sizing scale</cite>, since it's one-size and the hook-and-loop strap has finite adjustment range. If you've got a larger head, try it on or check a sizing chart before buying — this isn't the cap to blind-buy if you're already at the top end of typical cap sizing.

Durability reports are thin since this is a fairly niche item without a large user base on forums like r/Ultralight or BPL, but the reviews that do exist mention the fabric holding up over repeated wet outings without obvious wear. I'd treat that as encouraging but not exhaustive — nobody's put this through a 2,000-mile thru-hike and reported back yet, as far as I could find.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Genuinely waterproof, not just water-resistant — a rarity in cap form
- Breathable enough to avoid the "sauna head" problem common with waterproof headwear
- Structured brim noticeably helps keep glasses/sunglasses lenses clear in rain
- Tricot-lined headband manages sweat and adds comfort
- At 45g, negligible weight penalty for the rain protection you get
- PFAS-free DWR — a plus if you're trying to reduce "forever chemicals" in your kit
- Fully taped seams back up the waterproof claim rather than leaving it half-finished

**Cons**
- One-size-only fit with a hook-and-loop strap that has limited range — runs tight for larger heads
- Not a substitute for a full rain hood in sustained heavy rain or wind-driven rain (it protects the head/face, not the neck or ears)
- Sparse independent long-term durability data — most available reviews are early-impression rather than multi-season
- Premium price for what is, mechanically, a running cap (roughly $45–50 depending on region)

## Who Should Buy This

This cap makes the most sense for hikers and trail runners who spend a lot of time in showery, changeable weather but don't want to live under a jacket hood — think UK hillwalking, Pacific Northwest drizzle, or spring/fall trekking where rain comes and goes rather than settling in for a full storm. It's also a solid pick for glasses wearers who are tired of rain-speckled lenses ruining visibility on descents. If you're dealing with sustained heavy rain, high wind, or cold enough temps that you need ear/neck coverage, you'll still want a proper hood or a warmer waterproof beanie — this cap is a specialist tool for a specific kind of wet weather, not an all-conditions solution.

## Verdict

The Drilite Cap delivers on its core promise — real waterproofing in a genuinely lightweight, breathable package — and the brim design earns its keep for anyone who wears glasses on trail. The one-size fit and limited strap adjustment are worth checking before you buy, and durability data is still thin, but for showery-weather hiking and running, this is a smart, low-weight addition to a rain kit. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: excellent at the specific job it's built for, with a fit caveat that keeps it from being a universal recommendation.
