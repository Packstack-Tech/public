## Overview

The Foray 3L is Outdoor Research's workhorse rain jacket—now updated with a PFAS-free DWR and a 3-layer AscentShell Dry fabric instead of the old Gore-Tex Paclite. It's aimed at hikers, backpackers, and commuters who want serious, durable weather protection rather than the lightest possible packable shell. If you're chasing grams for a thru-hike, this isn't your jacket; if you want something that'll shrug off a cold, multi-day soaking in the Cascades or the Whites, it's worth a look.

## Performance

**Waterproofing:** This is where the Foray earns its keep. <cite index="15-6,15-7">The updated 3-layer AscentShell Dry fabric held up admirably in sustained rain and gusty alpine winds, with water beading and rolling off the face fabric rather than soaking through, aided by the PFAS-free Nikwax Direct.Dry durable water-repellent finish.</cite> That's consistent with what I've read across multiple long-term tests: this jacket sheds sustained downpours as well as its Gore-Tex predecessor did, not just a light drizzle.

**Breathability:** This is the trade-off. AscentShell as a fabric family is genuinely air-permeable and can be impressively breathable in lighter-weight applications like the Motive or Helium jackets. But the Foray uses a heavier, burlier build of that fabric, and reviewers consistently note it doesn't carry over the same breathability. One in-depth tester put it plainly: <cite index="1-3">it's an excellent wind shell and sheds moisture as effectively as its Gore-Tex predecessor but is not as breathable—making it better suited to cool and colder weather conditions</cite> than warm summer showers. In practice, expect to run pit zips often if you're working hard uphill in anything above 50°F.

**Weight and packability:** At around 13.7 oz for the men's version (some size-dependent listings put it closer to 15.3 oz), this sits solidly in the "durable 3L shell" category rather than ultralight territory. <cite index="13-1">The burly nature of this three-layer jacket means it is not ultralight, not particularly breathable, nor small when packed down.</cite> If your priority is minimizing pack weight for a summer thru-hike, this isn't the pick.

**Comfort and mobility:** The mechanical stretch in the 40D nylon face fabric makes a real difference on trail. Testers of the closely related Aspire 3L (the women's version, sharing the same AscentShell Dry fabric) found <cite index="15-9,15-10,15-11">the comfort a notable upgrade over many traditional rain jackets thanks to its 3-layer construction and integrated stretch, with a soft fabric backer much gentler against bare skin and added mobility that made it easy to move freely while setting up a tent in the rain or hiking with a loaded pack.</cite> That stretch also means the jacket moves with you reaching overhead or scrambling, rather than fighting every motion like a stiffer Paclite shell.

**Fit under a pack:** This is a mixed bag. The cut is generous enough for layering, but that roominess has a cost. <cite index="15-12,15-13">It's not a perfect experience under a backpacking pack—the generous cut, while helpful for layering, leads to some bunching, and the long hem-to-bicep pit zips can create pressure points beneath a hipbelt.</cite> If you run a hipbelt cinched tight, be aware the TorsoFlo venting zips can dig in a bit.

**Build quality:** YKK Aquaguard zippers throughout are a genuine step up from the fabric-flap closures found on cheaper rain shells, and reviewers of the sibling Aspire model noted the <cite index="15-1">coated front zipper as opposed to a fabric flap gives the jacket a higher-end look</cite>. On the flip side, some found the finishing details on lower-cost touchpoints—like hem adjusters—less impressive: <cite index="15-3">the hem adjustment feels cheap and poorly executed; fabric flaps over the hand pockets are less premium than coated zippers</cite>.

**User sentiment:** On REI, the Foray 3L carries a strong <cite index="10-1">4.5 out of 5 stars across 169 reviews</cite>, which lines up with the general consensus that this is a well-liked, dependable jacket for its intended use case—just not a do-everything ultralight shell.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely durable, weather-resistant 3L construction that holds up in sustained rain and wind
- PFAS-free DWR treatment without a noticeable drop in water-shedding performance
- Mechanical stretch fabric adds real comfort and mobility versus stiffer traditional shells
- YKK Aquaguard zippers and TorsoFlo venting are functional, well-executed features
- Strong sustainability story: recycled nylon, bluesign approval, carbon-neutral certification
- Solidly rated by real owners (4.5/5 on REI across 169 reviews)

**Cons**

- Not particularly breathable for its price point—better suited to cool/cold conditions than warm, humid rain
- At ~13.7 oz, it's heavier than dedicated ultralight rain shells (roughly double the weight of OR's own Helium)
- Generous cut can bunch under a loaded pack; pit zips can create pressure points under a hipbelt
- Some lower-cost finishing details (hem adjuster, pocket flaps) feel like a step down from the premium zippers
- At $249, you're paying hardshell-adjacent money for a jacket that isn't the most packable option

## Who Should Buy This

The Foray 3L makes sense for backpackers, hikers, and commuters who spend real time in cold, wet, or windy conditions and want a shell that can take a beating without babying it—think shoulder-season backpacking, PNW drizzle, or daily commuting through unpredictable weather. If you're an ultralight or thru-hiker counting ounces and mostly dealing with warm-weather cloudbursts, look at OR's own Helium instead; it's roughly half the weight, though you'll sacrifice some durability and venting.

## Verdict

The Foray 3L is a genuinely durable, well-built rain jacket that nails the fundamentals—waterproofing, wind resistance, and now a PFAS-free DWR—without sacrificing much in the trade to sustainability. It's just not the breathable, packable all-rounder some buyers might expect from the AscentShell name; that honor goes to lighter siblings like the Motive or Helium. For cold, wet trips where durability matters more than grams, I'd rate it a 7.5/10.
