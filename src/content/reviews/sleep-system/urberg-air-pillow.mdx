## Overview

The Urberg Air Pillow is a plain inflatable camping pillow with a soft microfiber top, built for hiking, travel and campsite use. It weighs 90 g and lists at about €19, which puts it among the cheapest ways to get a proper pillow into a pack. The trade-off is that it's a simple, one-size air bladder with no shaping, no integrated pad attachment and no insulation, and nothing about it is designed to win a gram-counting contest.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 90 g, the pillow adds about as much to a base weight as a full-size smartphone with a thin case. Packed, it measures 12 x 8 cm, roughly the footprint of a deck of cards stood on its side, so it tucks into a quilt stuff sack, a shoe or a pot without claiming real estate. Anyone who currently sleeps on a bundled fleece and a rain jacket will find the swap costs very little weight and frees up the clothes for actually being worn.

**Comfort.** The 45 x 28 cm top is wide enough to stay under the head through a normal amount of rolling, and the microfiber face is the pillow's main comfort feature. Against bare skin on a cold night, a brushed fabric surface feels less clammy than the slick nylon or TPU found on many ultralight inflatables, and it doesn't stick to a cheek or an ear. Because the fill is air alone, loft is set by how much breath goes in. Inflatable pillows in general reward a slightly underfilled bladder, since a fully pumped one pushes back against the ear for side sleepers. Testers of other inflatable pillows, including CleverHiker's, have flagged exactly that pressure problem at full inflation, and the same logic applies here: let some air out and the head sinks in rather than perching on top.

**Stability and sleep system fit.** Nothing on the spec sheet suggests anchoring features such as straps or a pad-locking base, so the pillow can wander on a slick pad or a sloped tent floor. Pairing it with a pad that has a textured top helps. Urberg's own pad lineup, such as the 549 g Insulated Airmat, is the obvious companion, and both sit at a similar budget price tier.

**Insulation.** There is no fill beyond air, so the pillow offers no warmth under the head. In shoulder-season conditions, the heat loss through a head-sized air chamber is minor compared with what a pad's R-value controls, but anyone in sub-freezing weather may want a layer of clothing between the pillow and the ground.

**Value.** The €19 price (199 SEK in Sweden, 149 DKK in Denmark) is the pillow's strongest card. It makes the Air Pillow a low-stakes purchase for a weekend trip, a hostel bag or a second pillow for a partner. Urberg, a Swedish brand that donates 1% of revenue to environmental causes, positions its gear as quality at a sensible price, and this pillow fits that pitch.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 90 g and 12 x 8 cm packed, small enough to forget about in the pack
- Soft microfiber top is gentler on skin than slick-fabric pillows
- Generous 45 x 28 cm surface for restless sleepers
- Around €19, one of the cheapest ways to add real head support
- Works as well for travel and car camping as for the trail

**Cons**

- No contoured or shaped design to cradle the neck
- No pad-attachment system, so it may slide around
- Air-only, with no insulation or foam to take the edge off a firm fill
- One size, so loft is controlled only by inflation
- Heavier and bulkier than minimalist stuff-sack or tiny-bladder alternatives

## Who Should Buy This

This is a fit for budget-minded backpackers, weekend campers and travelers who want a real pillow without spending premium money, and for hikers on three-season trips who are tired of sleeping on a lumpy clothes bag. It also suits anyone who finds slick-fabric inflatables unpleasant against the face. Side sleepers who need a precise, contoured loft, and gram-focused thru-hikers who already use a stuff sack as a pillow, are better served by a shaped pillow or the gear they already carry.

## Verdict

The Urberg Air Pillow is an honest, inexpensive inflatable that delivers a soft surface, a small packed size and a 90 g weight without pretension. It won't impress the ultralight crowd with features, but at about €19 it's an easy recommendation for casual three-season campers and travelers.
