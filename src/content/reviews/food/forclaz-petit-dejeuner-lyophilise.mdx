## Overview

Forclaz's chocolate muesli is a single-serving freeze-dried breakfast from Decathlon's trekking line: oat, wheat and rye flakes with chocolate, sealed in a flat pouch that doubles as the bowl. It suits hikers who want a no-fuss morning meal without lighting a stove. The trade-off is energy density: at roughly 397 kcal per 100 g of dry food, it is a pleasant breakfast but not a calorie-per-ounce champion.

## Performance

**Preparation.** The pitch is simplicity: add 100 ml of water, wait 3–5 minutes, eat from the pouch. Hot water is the default, but Decathlon says the muesli also rehydrates cold, which means a breakfast with no stove, no fuel and no boil time. Cold-soaked mornings take longer than the hot-water window, so the practical move is to add water as the tent comes down and eat once the pack is loaded.

**Water use.** The 100 ml requirement is on the low side for a freeze-dried meal. One French reviewer at Tibison singled it out as a plus on treks where water is a carry-in-and-ration affair, and it is a small saving over many mornings in dry country.

**Energy and weight.** The 100 g of dry food delivers 397 kcal, with one reviewer listing 61 g of carbohydrate per 100 g. Counting the 20 g pouch, that works out to about 3.3 kcal per gram of total carried weight. That is respectable for a breakfast, but denser options exist: MX3's chocolate muesli, for instance, lists 492 kcal in a 110 g net portion. Hikers counting every calorie-per-gram will notice. Hikers who treat breakfast as a pleasant start rather than a fuel dump will not.

**Taste and texture.** Owner feedback is warm. The pouch carries a 4.5/5 average from 44 reviews on a Decathlon storefront, with buyers calling it easy to eat and good. One reviewer would like a few almonds or walnuts in the mix. A long-term French trail reviewer at Le Banquier Randonneur found the flavor and texture close to ordinary muesli, though they ranked the red-fruit version of the line above the chocolate. Chocolate muesli of this type is sweet and carb-forward by design, a sensible match for a morning of walking, though it is lighter on protein and fat than a hiker doing big days may want.

**Packaging and handling.** The flat 15 x 15 x 2.5 cm pouch slides into a pack without eating much volume. The food is packed under protective atmosphere and, per the maker, should be eaten in full once opened. That means a half-portion is not really an option, and it is a poor fit for hikers who like to split a breakfast over two days.

**Provenance.** The cereal flakes come only from France or Germany, and the range is made in the Landes region of France by a supplier Decathlon works with. Reviewers also note the absence of preservatives.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rehydrates with hot or cold water, so no stove is needed
- Only 100 ml of water required
- 397 kcal in a 120 g pouch; flat, packable shape
- Flake-style muesli texture that owners say resembles the regular thing
- Strong owner ratings (4.5/5 across 44 reviews on one Decathlon storefront)
- Made in France with no added preservatives, according to reviewers

**Cons**

- Less calorie-dense than some rival muesli pouches
- Sweet, carb-heavy profile with little protein
- Must be finished once opened
- Sold mainly through Decathlon, so availability outside Europe is limited
- Some buyers want nuts or more inclusions in the mix

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend backpackers, section hikers and European thru-hikers who want a quick, cheap-to-run breakfast that works with or without a stove will find it a good fit. It also suits anyone who wants to skip the morning boil on cold-soak trips. Hikers chasing the highest possible calorie density, or those who need a protein-forward start for 30-mile days, should look at denser muesli or porridge pouches, or add a scoop of nuts or milk powder.

## Verdict

Forclaz's chocolate muesli does one job well: it turns 100 ml of water into a familiar, filling breakfast in a few minutes, hot or cold. It gives up some calorie density to competitors, but the convenience and owner satisfaction make up for most of that. A solid, unfussy trail breakfast.
