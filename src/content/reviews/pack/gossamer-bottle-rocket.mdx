## Overview

The Bottle Rocket is a shoulder-strap pocket that puts a standard sport bottle within reach of one hand, no pack removal required. It's built for Gossamer Gear's own Mariposa and Gorilla packs, but it gets used on plenty of others. The trade-off is simple: it carries a bottle better than most strap pockets, but its attachment system doesn't suit every set of shoulder straps.

## Performance

The Bottle Rocket's main selling point is structure. Most strap-mounted bottle holders are floppy mesh sleeves that sag when empty and swing when full. Gossamer Gear stiffens this one with EVA foam at the top, bottom and spine, so the mouth stays open for one-handed stowing. That matters at mile 18, when a hiker is trying to slide a bottle back in without looking.

Retention is the other half of the design. An elastic loop at the top locks the bottle in. Owners cite it as the reason bottles stay put on rocky ground and scrambles, where open-top pockets tend to spit them out. One owner reported eight days of scrambling, climbing and bushwhacking in New Zealand's Westland with the pocket staying secure. Another liked the bungee enough to say they'd copy the idea for handlebar bottle bags on a mountain bike.

Fit on the pack is where reviews diverge. Owners using Gossamer Gear's Mariposa and Gorilla report a clean, easy install, and some report the same on a Minimalist 19 day pack, an Osprey Aura and a Deuter AC Lite 26. The attachment system is unusual, though. Outdoor reviewers who rank strap holders advise checking how it will mount before buying. One Exos 58 owner found the Velcro straps too weak to hold a full 1 L Smartwater bottle. Another has a pack with no good attachment points and can't use the pocket at all. A third called it "just OK" on a non-Gossamer pack and wanted other strapping options.

Capacity is a smaller wrinkle. The pocket takes bottles up to 1 L, and users have run it with a 750 ml cycling bottle, a 950 ml bottle and a 1 L CNOC Vesica. A 1 L bottle is slightly wider than a 700 ml one, though, and one owner added a section of cut-down bottle as a stiffener to keep the sleeve rigid. Another reported that the pocket sits higher on the shoulder than they like. Others note that an empty pocket can get in the way when shrugging on the pack.

Durability gets little criticism. Owners describe sturdy construction and quality materials, with one calling the workmanship five-star while docking points for design. The 100D Robic nylon and mesh are light, and one buyer worried about how long such thin fabric will last. Customer ratings sit around 4.3 to 4.6 out of 5 across Gossamer Gear and retailer pages.

At 48 g, it adds about the weight of a few sheets of paper to the front of the pack. It costs $23, which makes it the cheapest holder in at least one round-up of strap bottle pockets.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Foam-reinforced opening allows true one-handed stowing and retrieval
- Top tether and elastic loop keep bottles in place on rough terrain
- 48 g, with recycled, PFAS-free fabric
- Takes bottles up to 1 L, including Smartwater and CNOC bags
- Inexpensive at $23

**Cons**

- Attachment system doesn't fit every pack's shoulder straps
- Velcro mounting can slip under a full 1 L bottle on some packs
- Empty pocket can snag when donning the pack
- Sits high on the shoulder for some users

## Who Should Buy This

Owners of a Gossamer Gear Mariposa or Gorilla should buy it, since the pocket was designed around those straps. Hikers who carry Smartwater-style bottles on a pack with plain, webbing-style shoulder straps will likely also find it works. Anyone with heavily contoured or padded straps, unusual attachment points, or a habit of carrying a full 1 L bottle on a pack with flimsy straps should confirm the fit first or look at a holder with a different mounting method.

## Verdict

The Bottle Rocket is among the better-built and cheaper strap-mounted bottle pockets, and its tether and stiffened opening do the job they promise. The caveat is compatibility: check that it will mount securely to your shoulder straps before ordering. Where it fits, it's an easy 48 g upgrade.
