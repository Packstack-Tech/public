## Overview

The Trangia Stove 27-2 UL is a self-contained, alcohol-burning cook system designed for 1-2 people. It ships as a complete nested package: two 1L saucepans, a 0.6L kettle, an 18cm frypan, a spirit burner with simmer ring, upper and lower windshields, pot grip, and strap — all packed into a cylinder roughly the size of a large water bottle. It's the go-to system for campers who prioritize reliability, versatility, and actual cooking over chasing grams.

## Performance

**The burner itself** is the core strength of this system. <cite index="13-4,13-5">It's easy to use due to its simple construction, with no loose parts that can break and is completely maintenance-free.</cite> <cite index="19-52,19-53,19-54">The Trangia burner can simmer when the simmer ring is used — and relatively few alcohol stoves can really simmer, which means you can cook real food, not just "boil in a bag" type meals.</cite> That's a meaningful distinction if your camp menu goes beyond ramen and instant coffee.

<cite index="12-1,12-2,12-3,12-4">
  One filling of 100ml (max 2/3 of maximum volume) will burn for approximately
  25 minutes, and boils 1 litre of water in around 10 minutes — though this
  varies according to weather and quality of fuel.
</cite>
That's noticeably slower than a canister stove in calm, warm conditions, so if
you're in a hurry in the morning, manage expectations accordingly.

**Wind resistance** is where the Trangia earns its "storm cooker" reputation. The two-part windshield — upper and lower — wraps the burner in an integrated cocoon rather than relying on a separate piece of kit you forget at home. <cite index="28-6">The integrated two-part windshield protects the burner from the wind, ensuring efficient heat transfer even in harsh weather conditions.</cite> <cite index="6-2,6-3">Alcohol performance is technically reduced in colder weather, but the system stands up to cold better than LPG gas canister stoves.</cite> In practice, this means the gap between a Trangia and a canister stove narrows considerably on a breezy ridge in October — conditions that would kill a canister stove's output but leave the Trangia largely unfazed.

**Cold weather** is the one real soft spot. <cite index="13-30,13-31">The spirit burner works in all weather conditions, however in slightly colder climates from about +5°C, the Winter Attachment with Preheater is recommended for better effect.</cite> Below freezing, Trangia recommends warming the burner against your body before use. That's workable, but it's an extra step you won't have with canister or white-gas systems.

**The UL aluminium alloy** (designated 5005) is meaningfully better than the old standard. <cite index="29-15,29-16">The UL alloy is 50% stronger than regular aluminum (alloy 1050), which allows the cookware to be made thinner and the stoves to be lighter without compromising durability and functionality.</cite> That said, seasoned forum members on Backpacking Light have consistently pointed out that <cite index="11-5,11-6">"calling a Trangia 27 ultralight is more marketing than fact — even with the new alloy they're using."</cite> At 860g, they're not wrong.

**Fuel efficiency** deserves an honest look. Alcohol contains roughly two-thirds the energy per gram of white gas or LPG. On a weekend trip you'll barely notice. <cite index="17-25">A week-long trip might require two or more litres of alcohol.</cite> That adds up in your pack. If you're going long, budget for the extra fuel weight.

**Soot** is a minor but real annoyance. The spirit burner does leave a layer of carbon on pot bottoms over time. A tip that's circulated among Trangia owners: <cite index="8-39,8-40,8-41">adding about 10% water to the methylated spirits makes it burn cleaner — almost no soot — but still plenty hot enough.</cite>

**The kettle (27-2 specific)** is what distinguishes this model from the 27-1. It's a handy addition for two people running different things simultaneously — hot drinks while dinner cooks — though at 0.6L it's not enormous. <cite index="3-8,3-9">Sets with a 0.6L kettle are only available with the spirit burner, because there isn't enough space for both the kettle and gas burner when the stove is packed away.</cite> Worth knowing if you're planning to swap in the optional gas burner later.

**Longevity** is legitimately impressive. <cite index="3-1,3-2,3-3">One long-term owner reported having their T27 for 30 years with it still as good as the day they bought it — strong, light, and unbreakable, with the spirit burner and fuel bottle easier to monitor than gas.</cite> You always know how much fuel you have. That's not something you can say about an opaque isobutane canister.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Complete nested cook system — pots, pan, kettle, windshield, burner all in one package
- Exceptional wind resistance thanks to the integrated two-part windshield
- Dead-simple burner with no moving parts and no maintenance required
- Simmer ring enables genuine cooking, not just boiling
- Leftover fuel caps and stores — no waste between meals
- Alcohol fuel is widely available globally (denatured alcohol, methylated spirits, bio-ethanol)
- Optional gas burner accessory adds flexibility without replacing the whole system
- Decades-proven durability; individual parts are replaceable
- Completely silent — a genuinely underrated quality in camp

**Cons**

- 860g is heavy by modern ultralight standards; the "UL" branding is relative
- ~10 min/L boil time is slow compared to canister stoves in still, warm conditions
- Alcohol's lower energy density means carrying more fuel weight on longer trips
- Performance drops below +5°C without the optional Winter Attachment
- Alcohol flame can be nearly invisible in bright sunlight — a real safety concern
- Spirit burner soots up pots over time (mitigated with diluted fuel)
- Pot clips are sized for Trangia's own cookware; third-party pots require modification
- No lid included with saucepans; the frypan doubles as a lid, which is functional but not ideal

## Who Should Buy This

This system is built for the 1-2 person camper who wants to actually cook — scrambled eggs, sautéed veg, rehydrated meals, hot drinks simultaneously — and who values a reliable, weatherproof, virtually indestructible setup over an obsession with base weight. <cite index="6-8">The 27-2 is great for one person who likes to do some fancier cooking, is set on an alcohol stove but going to colder and windier areas, or for two people.</cite> It's equally at home on a weekend in the mountains, a cycle tour across Europe, or wherever fuel canisters are hard to find. <cite index="18-22,18-23">Out of the box the stove burns alcohol and derivatives, but attachments are available to allow it to operate with isobutane canisters or petroleum fuels including white gas, gasoline, kerosene, and diesel — making it a versatile system compatible with a wide range of fuels globally.</cite> If you're counting every gram for a sub-10-lb base weight build, look elsewhere. If you want something you'll still be using in 30 years and trust completely in bad weather, this is it.

## Verdict

The Trangia 27-2 UL is not an ultralight stove in the modern sense of the word, and you'd be doing yourself a disservice to buy it expecting one. What it is, is one of the most complete, reliable, and genuinely weather-resistant cook systems available at any weight class — a reputation it has earned across decades of real-world use. If the ~860g total weight fits within your kit and you want to cook actual food without fussing over moving parts or empty canisters, this remains a hard system to beat. **7/10**
