## Overview

Decathlon's three-pack of breathable microfibre boxers is a running-underwear staple that has quietly become a walking and hiking one too. The fabric is an 84% polyester, 16% elastane blend that wicks sweat and dries fast, in a long-leg cut with a mid-height waistband. The headline trade-off is simple: at £11.99 for three, it gives up brand cachet, merino's odor resistance and fancy construction, and keeps the part that matters on a long day, which is not holding moisture against skin.

## Performance

**Moisture and drying.** The case for synthetic underwear on a trail is the same as for a running kit: cotton soaks up sweat and then sits there, and wet fabric rubbing on skin over distance is how chafing starts. These boxers take the opposite approach. Decathlon describes the polyester knit as a wicking, breathable fabric that dries quickly, and says the garment passed an independent laboratory's perspiration-wicking test. For backpackers, the practical payoff is a pair that can be rinsed in a stream or sink in the evening and be dry by morning, and a pair that doesn't feel clammy through a humid climb.

**Stretch and chafe control.** Sixteen percent elastane is a generous dose for underwear, and it explains the stretch Decathlon leans on in its marketing: the fabric follows the leg through high steps and scrambling. The cut is long, intended to avoid restriction at the groin, and Decathlon says the crotch area has been designed with seams removed or offset to limit friction. A long leg that stays put under shorts or a kilt is what keeps thigh-to-thigh rubbing at bay on a hot, multi-day walk, though the standard fit is a boxer rather than a compression brief, so there is some looseness compared with a true boxer brief.

**Waistband.** The mid-height elastic band is designed to give support without being tight. Under a hipbelt, that sits at a sensible height: the band is thin enough not to add bulk beneath a loaded pack's belt, though anyone who finds a hipbelt pressing on seams should check the band against their own pack.

**Durability and care.** Decathlon positions these for intensive use and repeated washing, and says the garment was tested for shape and colour retention after washing and drying. The care label bans tumble drying, which hardly matters to anyone hang-drying on a trail. Owner scores are strong: the UK listing sits at 4.5 stars across roughly 4,600 ratings, with a near-identical single-pair Kalenji boxer rated even higher across more than 17,000. Walkers on UKC forums have long recommended Decathlon's Kalenji boxers for distance walking and running, noting that they have come out on top in earlier underwear threads.

**Weak spots.** This is a plain synthetic garment. Polyester holds onto odor more readily than merino does, so on a week-long stretch without laundry, a merino boxer will smell better for longer, at roughly three to five times the price per pair. There is no fly, pouch engineering or anti-microbial finish. And the three-pack is sold in pre-selected colourways, with no option to mix sizes.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About £4 per pair, so replacing a worn pair doesn't hurt.
- 84/16 polyester-elastane fabric wicks sweat and dries quickly.
- Long leg and offset seam layout aimed at reducing thigh chafe.
- Mid waistband sits comfortably under a hipbelt.
- Sizes from S to 3XL (UK 30" to 44").
- Consistently strong ratings from thousands of buyers.

**Cons**

- Polyester holds odor longer than merino on multi-day trips.
- No fly or specialized pouch.
- Can't be tumble dried; lower-temperature care recommended.
- Colour combinations are fixed within each pack.

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who hikes, runs or thru-hikes and wants a dependable synthetic boxer without spending merino money: section hikers, weekend backpackers, trail runners and people building a rotation of two or three pairs for a long walk. The three-pack is also a good way to try the synthetic approach if cotton has given you chafing problems. Those who want the lowest odor over a week or more without washing, or who prefer a snug, supportive boxer brief, should look at merino or a compression-style trunk instead.

## Verdict

These are cheap, quick-drying and comfortable enough that reviewers and owners keep buying them in bulk. For a cost-conscious hiker they're the sensible default, and merino is a luxury upgrade rather than a necessity. A solid 8 out of 10.
