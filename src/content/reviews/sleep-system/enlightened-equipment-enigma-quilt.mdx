## Overview

The Enigma is Enlightened Equipment's lightest and most minimalist quilt. It takes the template of the company's Revelation and swaps the partly open footbox for a permanently sewn-closed one, which saves weight and removes a place for drafts to creep in. The 20°F version tested here weighs about 601 g. Its trade-off is the one every quilt makes: low weight and a small packed size in exchange for a learning curve at night.

## Performance

**Weight and packed size.** Weight depends on how the quilt is built. The Trek's testers cite a range of roughly 10 to 37 oz across the line, with about 20 oz typical for a 20°F model. CleverHiker's tested 20°F weighed 1 lb 5.6 oz with 15.46 oz of 850-fill duck down. A Regular/Wide 20°F listed by one retailer comes in at 22.23 oz, including 16.3 oz of fill. Choosing 950-fill goose down trims the fill weight further. CleverHiker's 950 option uses 13.83 oz for the same warmth. Packed volume is small either way. The regular 20°F compresses to about 6 liters, and a long-term user described it as about the size of a netball once cinched down.

**Warmth and comfort.** Reviewers describe the Enigma as closer to a blanket than a bag. The back is open, so it can be draped loosely on a warm night or tucked in when it turns cold. CleverHiker called the 54-inch shoulder width generous for a quilt. It reaches the edges of a standard sleeping pad, and that helps keep drafts out. The 40-inch footbox is snug, and it suits cold-footed sleepers. A Backwoods Pursuit tester compared a 30°F Enigma with a Zpacks Solo quilt of the same rating. The Enigma held more down (11.07 oz against 8.9 oz). It also gave a touch more warmth, but at 16.77 oz in Regular/Wide with a draft collar it ran heavier than the Zpacks at 14.1 oz.

**Draft control.** The elastic pad straps and the snap-and-drawcord neck closure are the system's main defense against drafts. The U-shaped baffles let a sleeper shift down toward cold spots. The quilt also needs some practice. CleverHiker's testers noted that it takes time to use effectively. The straps do most of the work in cold weather, so side sleepers who thrash around may have to experiment with the fit before it works.

**Durability and moisture.** The DWR-treated shell and treated down help it shed condensation and dry faster. Long-haul reports are good. A PCT thru-hiker logged more than 2,600 miles in one and called the warmth-to-weight ratio excellent. A separate reviewer covered 2,000 km over more than five years in a Regular/Regular and described a product that compresses well and holds up. As with any down quilt in a wet tent, dry storage matters more than the DWR.

**Customization.** This is the Enigma's other selling point. Buyers choose length, width, fill, shell denier, color, draft collar and temperature rating. The Trek's reviewer listed a price range of $270 to $630 depending on the build. CleverHiker listed the tested 20°F at $320. The cost of that freedom is that the right choices have to be made up front. The Trek's reviewer ordered Regular width and received Wide, which is a reminder to check the measurements carefully.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low weight for a 20°F quilt, especially with 950-fill and 7D fabric
- Compresses to about the size of a Nalgene bottle or a netball, depending on build
- Sewn-closed footbox removes zipper weight and a draft path
- Wide range of sizes, ratings and fabrics so the quilt fits the sleeper
- Pad straps and neck closure are included
- Hand-built in Minnesota, with a long thru-hiking track record

**Cons**

- Takes practice to seal out drafts, especially for restless sleepers
- A sewn-closed footbox means it cannot be opened into a flat blanket
- Option costs add up; lighter fabrics and 950 fill push the price well above the base
- Weight varies widely with options, so the headline figure is not guaranteed

## Who Should Buy This

The Enigma is for thru-hikers and weekend ultralighters who already sleep on a pad, want a three-season quilt and don't mind learning how to tuck it in. It also suits hikers of unusual height or build who struggle to find a bag that fits. Cold sleepers should choose a rating below the temperatures they expect. People who toss and turn a lot, want a hood, or prefer a zip-and-forget bag should look at a mummy bag instead.

## Verdict

The Enigma is one of the best-regarded ultralight quilts for good reason. It is light, packs small, and can be built to fit almost any sleeper. The 20°F version is the best all-round choice for most three-season trips. Buy it if you're willing to spend a few nights learning the strap system.
