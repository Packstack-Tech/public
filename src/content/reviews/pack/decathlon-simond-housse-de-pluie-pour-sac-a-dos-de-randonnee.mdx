## Overview

Simond's 20–40 L rain cover is Decathlon's entry-level answer to a wet pack: a seamless yellow polyester shell with a polyurethane coating, held on by two elastic straps. It costs about €7.99 in France, and it is aimed at day hikers and weekend walkers rather than gram-counters. The trade-off is simple. It is cheap, visible and dependable for ordinary showers, but at 128 g it is heavier than the lightest covers, and like every cover, it leaves the harness side of the pack exposed.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The cover does what a cover does: it sheds rain off the top, back and sides of the pack, and keeps outer pockets and the pack fabric from soaking up water. Seamless construction removes the stitch lines where cheaper covers tend to weep. Decathlon's own pitch is modest. It describes a basic cover meant to protect a pack from rain, not to waterproof it.

That distinction matters. Backpacking Light forum veterans and gear writers have long made the same point about every pack cover on the market: the side facing the hiker stays open, and in sustained rain, water runs down the shoulders and into the channel behind the straps. Sea to Summit warns that wind-driven rain can enter through the same gap. Anything that must stay dry, such as a down quilt or insulating layer, belongs in a liner or dry bag, with the cover serving as a second layer for the pack itself.

**Fit and attachment.** The two elastic straps hook around the shoulder straps, which is quick to do with cold fingers and needs no buckles. Decathlon says the cover suits all hiking packs from 20 to 40 L, and its own product page advises checking compatibility for a precise fit. At the top of that range, a bulging 40 L pack with a lashed sleeping pad is a stretch. At the bottom, a 20 L daypack sits in a cover with spare room that can flap in a breeze. Hikers on wind-exposed ridgelines are better served by a snug cover or none at all, since a loose one catches gusts.

**Visibility.** The yellow is a deliberate feature. On a gray fell or a road shoulder at dusk, a pack wearing this cover is easy to spot, which is useful for group hikes, hunting season, and walking along lanes.

**Weight and packability.** At 128 g, the cover is not a gram-saver. Forum posters who favor a 35-gallon liner note that such a bag weighs only about 2.5 oz (roughly 70 g) and also protects contents from the inside. Dropping a trash-bag liner and carrying this cover instead saves nothing. Carrying both, the setup experienced hikers favor in heavy rain, adds weight to a lightweight kit. The integrated storage pouch keeps the cover compact and means it won't get separated from the pack.

**Durability and value.** Owners rate it 4.7 out of 5 across roughly 2,800 reviews on Decathlon's French and Belgian sites, a strong number for a €7.99 accessory. The 2-year warranty is generous at this price. Care is straightforward: hand wash, and never tumble dry.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low price, with a 4.7/5 owner rating across roughly 2,800 reviews
- Seamless coated polyester, with no stitched seams to leak
- Yellow makes the hiker more visible in poor light
- Two-strap attachment is fast and buckle-free
- Integrated pouch that can't be lost
- 2-year warranty

**Cons**

- 128 g is heavy next to silnylon covers and plain liner bags
- Does not protect the back panel or shoulder-strap channels
- Loose on small packs, and may be tight on loaded 40 L packs
- Can flap in strong wind
- Hand-wash care only
- Covers only a single size range, so it is no use for larger trekking packs (Simond sells 40–60 L and 70–100 L versions separately)

## Who Should Buy This

Day hikers, weekenders and school-trip walkers with a 20–40 L pack will get what they need: a cheap, visible, set-and-forget shield against rain showers. It also suits anyone who wants to keep a pack's outer fabric and pockets from getting waterlogged on a wet week. Ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers should look elsewhere. A pack liner protects gear more reliably and weighs about half as much, and the lightest silnylon covers cost less in grams. Hikers in sustained storms or heavy wind should treat this as the outer layer of a system, never the only one.

## Verdict

A competent, bargain-priced cover that does a modest job well: keeping a 20–40 L pack's shell dry and its owner easy to see. It doesn't earn a place in a base-weight-conscious kit, where a liner does the real work. Buy it for day hikes and casual trips, and pair it with a liner for anything wetter.
