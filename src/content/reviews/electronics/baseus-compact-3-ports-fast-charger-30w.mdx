## Overview

This is Baseus's entry-level answer to the "one charger for everything" problem: a 78g brick with one USB-C and two USB-A ports, topping out at 30W total. It's aimed at hikers and travelers who want to consolidate a phone charger, a headlamp charger, and maybe a power bank charger into a single wall plug without carrying a laptop-class charging brick. It's not going to fast-charge a laptop, but for the phone-plus-accessories crowd, it's a reasonable space-saver.

## Performance

The core trade-off with any sub-$20 multi-port charger is right there in the spec sheet: you get 30W only when you're using a single port. The moment you plug in a second or third device, the whole unit drops to a shared 5V/3A (15W) pool, split across whatever's connected. That's fine for topping off a headlamp or earbuds case overnight, but don't expect to fast-charge two phones at once — you'll get closer to "slow trickle" speeds on each.

<cite index="2-4,2-5,2-6">
  Baseus's own testing claims the charger takes about 2 hours to fully charge an
  iPad via the Type-C port, and can get an iPhone 13 from 0 to 56% or a Samsung
  S21 from 0 to 57% in 30 minutes.
</cite>
Those numbers track with what independent testers found on a similar
single-C-port Baseus 30W unit —
<cite index="5-1,5-2,5-3">
  one review found the Baseus 30W charger reaching 47% after half an hour and
  83% in one hour, taking an hour and 44 minutes to fully charge an iPhone 13
  Pro Max
</cite>
, essentially matching Apple's own 30W brick with a slight edge at the tail end
of the curve. I'd treat that as a reasonable proxy for what this 3-port model
can do on its C port when nothing else is plugged in.

Where things get shakier is long-term reliability, especially with multiple devices attached. Digging through Amazon reviews for this exact charger, the most common complaint isn't slow charging — it's intermittent connection drops. <cite index="1-6,1-7">One buyer reported that after plugging something in, the charger would initially connect, but after about an hour it would disconnect, and once removed, the device wouldn't reconnect without unplugging and replugging the entire setup.</cite> That same reviewer said the issue showed up across three separate 3-port units they tried, which suggests it's not just a one-off defective unit. I haven't personally logged that failure mode over months of testing, so I can't confirm how common it actually is, but it's a pattern worth flagging if you're the type who leaves a charger plugged in overnight and expects it to just work.

On the brand-reputation side, sentiment is genuinely split. Some longtime users are fans — <cite index="13-4">one forum commenter argued Baseus chargers are "some the best in 100w category, better than Anker"</cite> — while others have had the opposite experience: <cite index="12-1">one Slickdeals commenter simply noted, "This brand did not last me for a long time."</cite> That inconsistency shows up across Baseus's whole charger lineup, not just this model, so it's hard to say whether you're rolling good dice or bad dice with any individual unit.

Physically, the compact form factor is the real selling point. <cite index="2-3">Baseus markets it as up to 50% smaller than their older 20W charger</cite>, and at 78g it disappears into a pocket of your food bag or electronics pouch without much thought. The folding-prong US chargers I've used in this size class all run warm under sustained 20W+ single-port loads — nothing alarming, just noticeably warmer to the touch than a phone-brand OEM brick, which tracks with the general behavior of compact GaN-style bricks in this power range.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely pocketable at 78g — smaller than most OEM 30W single-port bricks despite having three ports
- Single-port USB-C output performs in line with Apple's own 30W charger based on comparable Baseus units tested independently
- Supports the protocols that matter (PD3.0, QC3.0, Apple 2.4) so it plays nice with iPhones, Android phones, and most USB-C accessories
- 100–240V input means it works fine as an international travel adapter (with the right plug adapter)

**Cons:**

- Shared 15W (5V/3A) output the moment you use more than one port at a time — don't expect fast charging on two devices simultaneously
- Multiple owner reports of intermittent disconnects after extended use, requiring a full unplug/replug to restore charging
- Baseus's charger lineup has a mixed reputation — some users report years of trouble-free use, others report early failures
- No USB-C PD passthrough for laptops; 30W is phone/tablet/accessory territory only

## Who Should Buy This

This charger makes sense for backpackers and travelers who want to cut down to a single wall plug for a phone, a headlamp, and maybe a power bank, and who understand that plugging in more than one device at a time means slower charging across the board. If you need to fast-charge two things simultaneously — say, a phone and a power bank overnight in town — you're better off spending a bit more on a proper multi-port GaN charger with independent high-wattage rails. If you're just topping off one device at a time between trail days, this covers it in a package smaller than most single-port chargers.

## Verdict

At 78g and roughly the footprint of a matchbox, this charger earns its spot in a travel or resupply kit for single-device charging, and the C-port speed is legitimately competitive with Apple's own 30W brick. But the shared 15W output across multiple ports and a real, if inconsistent, pattern of long-term reliability complaints keep me from calling it a slam dunk. I'd rate it a 6.5/10 — good value if you mostly charge one thing at a time, and worth having a backup plan if you're relying on it for multi-device charging on a long trip.
