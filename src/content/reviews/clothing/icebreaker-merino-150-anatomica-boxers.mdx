## Overview

The Merino 150 Anatomica Boxers are Icebreaker's lightest men's underwear: a slim, no-fly boxer brief cut from a 150gsm corespun merino, nylon and elastane blend. They suit hikers who want a pair that works as daily wear and also goes on a warm-weather thru-hike. The trade-off is durability. The thin fabric is the reason they pack down and breathe so well, and it is also the reason some owners wear through the seat in a year or so.

## Performance

**Comfort.** Comfort is the reason people buy a second and third pair. Icebreaker's own customer reviews describe the fabric as smooth and soft, with a supportive, slim cut and no chafing. A long-term owner who ran three pairs hard for two years reported no chafing or rubbing in that time, and credited the waistband, seams and fabric alike. Another buyer, who wore them as part of a multi-day hiking kit, called them very comfortable. The one recurring nit is the center seam at the rear. One reviewer found it mildly irritating, though not enough to put them off the underwear.

**Odor and moisture.** Merino's main advantage over synthetic underwear is that it doesn't smell, and these boxers keep that advantage at 150gsm. Owners who cycle pairs on week-long trips with occasional handwashing say a night of airing out, or an hour in the sun, resets a pair well enough to wear it for a third day. Reviewers also describe the fabric as breathable in summer heat and as handling sweat well. Merino dries more slowly than nylon or polyester, so a camp-sink wash takes longer to turn around than it would with a synthetic brief. Stuffing a damp pair in a pack side pocket is a common compromise.

**Durability.** Opinions split here. The 150gsm fabric is thin by design, and thin merino wears faster than heavier weights. One Icebreaker customer who bought six pairs reported that the wool had nearly vanished from the crotch area after a little over a year, leaving only the elastic. Another owner with a dozen pairs reported fast wear at the seat. The two-year user described far better results, noting only some thinning at the back and bottom. The pattern is consistent enough to plan around: daily wearers should expect to replace pairs sooner than occasional-trip wearers, and rotating three or four pairs spreads the wear.

**Fit and sizing.** Reviewers generally call the fit true to size, though some on Icebreaker's European site found it on the small side. A few owners of related Anatomica boxers said the fabric could bunch or ride up during activity. A related complaint is length: one buyer wished the legs were longer, and Icebreaker sells a separate Long Boxer in the same fabric for that reason.

**The tag.** Several buyers flagged a small but irritating flaw. The internal tag is meant to be torn out near the seam, and one buyer ended up with small holes in two of six pairs. Cutting the tag out carefully with scissors avoids the problem.

**Weight and packability.** At 70 g in Medium, a spare pair is a rounding error in a base weight. An owner reported fitting ten pairs into a small packing cube, which is more than any trip requires.

**Temperature range.** This is a warm-to-moderate-weather brief. Owners describe it as cool in summer, and the thin fabric adds little insulation in cold conditions.

## Choosing a Version

The two listed versions, Standard and Men's Large, are the same boxer in the same fabric and cut. The catalog lists both at 70 g, and Icebreaker's published weight is for a Medium. Large uses more fabric, so expect a slightly higher figure on a scale. What changes between versions is the size, not the garment.

- **Standard:** The baseline listing, and the one that matches the 70 g Medium weight Icebreaker publishes. It suits hikers who fall in the middle of the size range.
- **Men's Large:** For larger frames. Because the cut is slim and has no fly, there is little structure to hold a pair in place, and reviewers who found the fit loose reported bunching and migration. Reviews of sizing are split, with some calling it true to size and others calling it small, so try a pair on or buy from a retailer with easy returns before stocking up on a full rotation.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft, smooth fabric with no chafing reported, even over long wear
- Strong odor resistance, with two to three days between washes reported
- 70 g (Medium) and packs very small, so a spare pair costs almost nothing
- Slim cut sits flat under hiking shorts and pants
- Breathable enough for summer miles

**Cons**

- Thin 150gsm fabric can wear through in the seat and crotch within a year or two
- Slower to dry than synthetic underwear after a trail wash
- Sizing reports are mixed, and some owners get bunching or ride-up
- The care tag is awkward to remove and can tear small holes in the fabric
- Expensive for underwear

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who sweat through a long summer or shoulder-season trip and want to go several days between washes will get the most out of these. They also suit anyone who wants one pair that works at the trailhead, on the trail and at the office. Hard-wearing daily users, and anyone who has already worn through merino in the seat, should look at Icebreaker's heavier Anatomica weights or accept a faster replacement cycle. Hikers who want underwear that dries in an hour at a creek are better served by a synthetic brief.

## Verdict

The Merino 150 Anatomica Boxers are among the most comfortable and odor-resistant hiking underwear available, and they weigh almost nothing. Durability keeps them from a higher score, because a minority of owners wear through them in about a year. Buy them for summer hiking and travel, buy three or four to share the wear, and cut the tag out rather than tearing it.
