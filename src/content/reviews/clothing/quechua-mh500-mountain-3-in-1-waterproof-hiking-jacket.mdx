## Overview

The MH500 Mountain is Decathlon's answer to the cold-weather hiker who wants one jacket purchase to cover rain, snow and chilly camps. A zip-in padded jacket nests inside a fully taped waterproof shell, and the inner layer also works alone as a mid-layer. The trade-off is weight: at roughly 1.2 kg for the full set in size L, this is a budget all-rounder, not an ultralight system.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The inner jacket is the warm half of the system. It uses 170 g/m² polyester padding through the body and 150 g/m² in the sleeves, which is a conventional synthetic build. Decathlon rates the combined jacket as comfortable to -10°C. That figure assumes the shell is zipped over the padding, and it's best read as a stationary or low-output number rather than a promise for a hard uphill push. Stretch inserts on the padded jacket keep the sleeves from binding when arms are raised or poles are planted.

**Weather protection.** The shell uses a 10,000 mm membrane with fully taped seams. That is a mid-range figure. Decathlon's own premium MH500 shell is rated at 20,000 mm or higher. In practice it has been enough for the conditions it was designed for. An Indiahikes trek leader who used the jacket on Gaumukh Tapovan, Kuari Pass, Deoria Tal–Chandrashila and Brahmatal reported that the shell stayed dry through more than an hour of sustained snowfall on Kuari Pass. He also praised the woven, reinforced outer fabric for handling rough terrain. Anyone expecting multi-day Scottish or Pacific Northwest downpours should treat 10,000 mm as the limit, not the ceiling.

**Versatility.** This is the real selling point. The shell can go out alone in warm rain, the padded jacket can go on alone for a cold evening at camp, and the two can be zipped together for winter days. The same trek leader described the arrangement as three use cases with no redundant layer in the pack. Compared with a down jacket, owners also point to the easy care: the synthetic padding doesn't demand special washing or careful storage between trips.

**Ventilation and fit.** Zipped underarm openings on the shell let heat out on climbs. The standard cut leaves room for a technical layer such as a microfleece under the combined jacket, though stacking all three layers will feel snug on a hard climb.

**Weight and packability.** At about 1.2 kg for the complete set in size L, the MH500 Mountain weighs more than a typical rain shell and a standalone synthetic puffy carried separately, and it gives up the compressibility of a modern down jacket. Hikers counting grams will notice it. The zip-in design also locks the two layers together in a way that a separate shell and mid-layer don't. When it's raining and mild, the inner jacket either stays at home or rides in the pack as dead weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Three configurations from one purchase: shell only, padded jacket only, or both together
- Warmth rated to -10°C for the combined system
- Fully taped seams and a reinforced woven shell fabric
- Underarm zips for dumping heat
- Synthetic padding is easy to care for, and a wet-weather mishap is less costly than with down
- Well under £100, with a Decathlon rating of 4.7 from more than 3,500 votes

**Cons**

- About 1.2 kg in size L is heavy by ultralight standards
- 10,000 mm waterproofing trails Quechua's own higher-end MH500 shell
- Polyester padding packs larger than down for the same warmth
- Paired layers are fine for cold camps but too much for active climbing in the cold
- Standard cut can feel snug with a chunky layer underneath

## Who Should Buy This

The MH500 Mountain suits budget-conscious trekkers, cold-season day hikers and anyone who walks in changeable mountain weather and wants a single jacket to cover it. It also works for hikers outfitting a trip to cold, wet places without buying a shell and an insulated jacket separately. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers should look elsewhere. A dedicated lightweight rain shell and a down or synthetic puffy will weigh less, pack smaller and let each layer be left behind independently.

## Verdict

The MH500 Mountain 3-in-1 is a sensible, inexpensive jacket for cold, wet hiking where weight isn't the first concern. The warmth, the taped shell and the three-way flexibility are real, and so is the weight penalty. For general mountain walking it's a good buy at the price. For ultralight use, a separate shell and puffy are the better route.
