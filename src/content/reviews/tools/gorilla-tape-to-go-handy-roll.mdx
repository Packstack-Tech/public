## Overview

The To-Go Handy Roll is Gorilla's pocket-sized version of its black duct tape: 1 inch wide, 10 yards long, and wound on a full-size 3-inch core. It is aimed at campers, travelers and anyone who wants a stronger-than-hardware-store-basic tape in a bag. For ultralight hikers the trade-off is simple: the tape is excellent, but the roll holds far more of it than a week on trail requires.

## Performance

**Adhesion and strength.** This is a cloth-reinforced duct tape with a thick rubber adhesive, and it behaves like one. Gorilla's technical data lists 40 ounces per inch of adhesion to a steel test panel and 50 pounds per inch of tensile strength. The company says the adhesive grips smooth, rough and uneven surfaces, and that the tape resists moisture, UV and temperature swings. Gorilla's own comparison puts the line at three times the strength of a leading value-brand duct tape, a claim based on 2016 data and worth reading as marketing rather than a lab result.

**Field repairs.** The use case is the standard trail kit list: a split tent-pole sleeve, a cracked bottle or tent stake, a torn pack panel, a flapping shoe sole held on until the next town. Owners on retail listings describe it sticking firmly to plastics and fabrics and holding through rain and weeks of use. The 1-inch width is the right size for narrow jobs like pole splints, zipper-garage tears and strap repairs. It is too narrow to cover a large gash in a rain jacket without overlapping several strips, and duct tape in general is a stopgap for waterproof shells rather than a lasting fix.

**Handling.** The tight weave lets the tape tear by hand despite its heft, so no scissors are needed with cold fingers. The black backing is stiffer and more opaque than silver gaffer-style tape, which helps it bridge holes but makes it less conformable on curved or heavily flexing surfaces.

**Temperature and age.** The adhesive is rated for application between 40°F and 120°F, so cold-morning repairs on a frosty shell work best if the tape is warmed in a pocket first. Backpackers on the Backpacking Light forums note that the tape works well as a utility fix but can turn sticky and gummy as it ages. That is a good reason to leave the factory roll in a drawer and carry a fresh wrap each season.

**Weight and bulk.** This is where the product loses ground with the gram-counting crowd. At roughly 4 oz, the roll weighs more than many hikers' entire repair kits, and the 3-inch core takes up real room in a hip-belt pocket. Most ultralight hikers wind a few feet of tape around a trekking pole, lighter or water bottle instead. The Handy Roll works best as the supply from which those wraps are cut, not as the item that goes in the pack. Its own manufacturer pitch, clipping it to a carabiner or tossing it in a pack, suits car camping and travel better than a thru-hike.

**Value.** Retail prices are modest. Two-packs have sold for under $8 at big-box retailers, and the listing carries about a 4.6-star average across nearly 400 reviews, which makes it an easy way to stock several wraps for the price of a single specialty tape.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Reinforced cloth backing and thick rubber adhesive handle tough, uneven surfaces
- 50 lb/in tensile strength and 40 oz/in adhesion on steel
- Tears by hand; no scissors required
- UV- and weather-resistant shell, with a rating up to 150°F
- Inexpensive and sold at hardware and big-box stores nationwide
- Made in the United States

**Cons**

- About 4 oz and a 3-inch core: heavy and bulky for what a trip actually uses
- 1-inch width is limiting on larger tears
- Adhesive can turn gummy with age, according to long-time users
- Cold-weather application is limited below 40°F
- Black backing is stiffer and less conformable than thinner repair tapes
- Not a durable fix for waterproof fabrics

## Who Should Buy This

Weekend hikers, car campers, travelers and anyone building a home repair stash will get good use from it, as will ultralight hikers who want a cheap source for pre-cut wraps. Buyers who plan to carry the whole roll on a thru-hike, or who need to patch silnylon and Dyneema, should look at purpose-made flat patch tapes and wrap their own few feet of duct tape instead.

## Verdict

The Handy Roll is strong, tear-by-hand duct tape in a convenient size at a low price, and it does exactly what a duct tape should. It is a poor fit as a whole-roll carry for weight-conscious backpackers, but a very good bulk supply for the strips they do carry.
