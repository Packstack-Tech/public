## Overview

The Plasma 1000 is a hoodless, pocketless down jacket that weighs about as much as a pair of hiking socks. Montbell built it from 1000-fill-power EX Down and a 7-denier Ballistic Airlight ripstop shell, and the result sits at the extreme end of the ultralight spectrum. The trade-off is plain: very little down, almost no features, and warmth best described as sweater-plus rather than belay-parka.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** The Plasma 1000 is the reason sub-5-ounce down jackets exist as a category. At 136 g it is lighter than most ultralight down jackets with a hood and pockets, and a fraction of Mountain Hardwear's Ghost Whisperer UL, which The Trek lists at 6.7 oz. The Trek's reviewer compared it to little more than an extra pair of socks. It compresses to roughly the size of a large grapefruit, and Montbell includes a stuff sack that reviewers say it fits into without a fight.

**Warmth.** Forty-five grams of 1000-fill is about a third of the jacket's weight. Montbell's own Plasma 1000 Alpine Parka carries 96 g of fill, so the jacket is a different garment from the parka and not simply a hoodless version of it. Reviewers consistently place the jacket in the three-season layer category. Trailspace's tester described it as a good lightweight extra layer for three-season hikers and said plainly that it isn't a winter coat. A reviewer at Ridgeline Images reached a similar verdict and suggested that a higher fill ratio would add warmth for very little extra weight. The skip-stitch quilting is meant to cut down on cold spots, and The Trek's reviewer vouched for the warmth at this weight. Not every owner agrees. One Rokslide poster called the jacket a waste of money and said it wasn't warm even at 60°F, while praising the hooded parka. Both camps have a point: this is a camp-and-sleep-layer-weight piece that works best under a shell or inside a quilt, not as a standalone garment on cold ridgelines.

**Fit and layering.** The cut is trim. Trailspace's reviewer said it wears closer to a sweater than a jacket, with a body length about on par with a pullover, and found it easy to layer a rain jacket over it. The lack of a hem drawcord means warmth can leak out the bottom if the fit isn't snug, a point Ridgeline Images flagged as well. There are also no cuff adjusters beyond the elastic wrists, and no pit zips.

**Shell fabric.** The 7-denier ballistic nylon is soft against the skin, and testers describe the jacket as comfortable enough to wear around town. The fabric is also a little crinkly and noisy. The DWR finish handles light moisture, but the shell is gossamer thin: it asks for careful handling around brambles, pack-strap abrasion and campfire embers. Thru-hikers who take this jacket should plan to carry a repair patch.

**Features.** There is little to talk about, which is the point. A full-length YKK zipper with a chin guard, elastic cuffs and a sewn-through body. Reviewers list the absent pockets, hood and adjustment points as the cost of the weight, and The Trek's reviewer called the no-pockets decision the trade that gets the jacket under the five-ounce mark.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 136 g, among the lightest full-zip down jackets made
- 1000-fill EX Down and a skip-stitch baffle layout for good loft per gram
- Compresses to about a grapefruit
- Soft, comfortable shell; trim cut layers cleanly under a rain jacket
- Includes a stuff sack

**Cons**

- Only 45 g of fill, so warmth stops at three-season camp use
- No hood, pockets or hem drawcord
- 7-denier shell is fragile and a bit noisy
- Premium price for a limited-use garment

## Who Should Buy This

Gram-counting thru-hikers and fastpackers who want a camp or sleep layer that disappears in a pack, and who already carry a hardshell and a hat to cover the missing hood. It also suits hikers who run warm and want a puffy as a safety margin in summer mountains. Anyone who needs real warmth for shoulder-season nights, who wants pockets for their hands, or who is hard on gear should look at a hooded jacket with more fill. Montbell's own Plasma 1000 Alpine Parka, at 237 g with 96 g of fill, is the obvious step up, and CDT hikers have taken it the full length of the trail.

## Verdict

The Plasma 1000 is a specialist tool that does exactly what it claims: very little weight, very good loft, and a layer that stays in the pack until it's needed. It's a poor fit as a hiker's only insulation in cold conditions, but for three-season ultralight kits it's hard to beat on grams alone.
