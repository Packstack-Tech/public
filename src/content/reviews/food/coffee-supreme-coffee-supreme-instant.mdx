## Overview

Coffee Supreme is a well-regarded specialty roaster out of New Zealand, and their Instant is exactly what it sounds like: single-serve sachets of freeze-dried coffee made from their standard Supreme Blend rather than a mystery instant-grade bean. It's aimed at people who like good coffee but don't want to haul a pour-over setup into the backcountry — think weekend trampers and car campers more than gram-counting thru-hikers, given the price tag.

## Performance

At 4 g a sachet and zero packaging to speak of beyond the foil pouch, this disappears into a pack liner without a second thought — it's about as close to a non-decision on weight as backpacking food gets. The pitch from Coffee Supreme is straightforward: <cite index="21-1,21-2,21-3,21-4">they source coffee from the Yacuanquer region in Colombia, take the same quality coffee, roast it, brew it, and freeze dry it, with no additives or fillers</cite>. That's a real point of difference from the Nescafé/Moccona end of the shelf, and it shows up in the cup — this tastes like coffee that used to be a proper brew, not a chemistry-set approximation of one.

That's borne out by outside tasters, too. Metro's write-up on NZ instant coffee had judges rank <cite index="20-2,20-3">Coffee Supreme reigns supreme, describing it as dark and bouncy and bright, courtesy of some very nice citrusy notes</cite>, and one judge noted it <cite index="20-17,20-18">"tastes like filter," because that's exactly what it once was before it was freeze-dried</cite>. On trail, that translates to a cup with actual acidity and some fruit-forward character rather than the flat, burnt-toast flavor you get from bulk instant — a genuine upgrade if you're used to grocery-store granules at 6 a.m. on a cold summit.

That said, not everyone's palate agrees, and it's worth flagging the other side of the ledger. A Consumer NZ blind taste test of instant coffees found Coffee Supreme <cite index="17-3,17-4,17-5,17-6">comes with hipster pedigree and an eye-watering price tag at $76.14 per 100 grams, and after coming in sixth place in the taste test, you're probably better off buying a bag of cheaper instant and decanting it into a Ziploc bag</cite>. That panel <cite index="17-15,17-16,17-17">scored it 42 out of 100, with participants feeling the offering had hints of coffee in its aroma that were lacking in its flavour — just 20% of panellists said they'd buy it for home</cite>. So there's a real split here: coffee-forward tasters who value acidity and brightness tend to rate it highly, while a broader panel drinking it black found it underwhelming for the price. Worth noting that instant coffee generally scores better with milk added in blind tests, so if you're a black-coffee purist on trail, temper expectations accordingly.

Practically speaking, the sachets are genuinely convenient — tear, dump, stir, done, no measuring spoon or waste sachet fumbling in the cold. The catch is the cost per cup. At New Zealand specialty-coffee pricing, you're paying multiples of what a jar of Mount Hagen or even Starbucks VIA costs per serving, for a product that's polarizing even among people who like coffee. If you're doing the math on a long thru-hike where you're brewing daily for weeks, that adds up fast.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Made from an actual specialty blend rather than commodity instant-grade beans — no additives or fillers
- Distinct, bright, citrusy flavor profile that stands out from typical bulk instant coffee
- Pre-dosed single-serve sachets are genuinely convenient — no scooping, no waste
- Negligible pack weight per serving
- Well-regarded outside the outdoor world for its cup quality

**Cons**

- Expensive relative to other instant coffee options, even other specialty brands
- Blind taste-test results are mixed — polarizing rather than universally loved
- Limited availability outside New Zealand/Australia, so restocking mid-trip abroad isn't realistic
- Only one blend/roast option, so no variety if you want to mix things up

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for New Zealand and Australia-based trampers who already appreciate good coffee and don't mind paying a premium to bring café-quality flavor into the backcountry for weekend or week-long trips. It's a harder sell for budget-conscious thru-hikers doing months on trail, where the per-cup cost and reliance on a niche retailer for restocking become real logistical and financial friction points.

## Verdict

Coffee Supreme Instant delivers a noticeably brighter, more complex cup than typical bulk instant coffee, and the pre-dosed sachets are about as frictionless as trail coffee gets. But it's priced like the specialty product it is, and not every taster agrees it's worth the premium — so treat this as a treat for shorter trips rather than a thru-hike staple. I'd put it at a 6.5/10: excellent for what it is, but the value proposition only makes sense if you're already sold on Coffee Supreme's flavor at home.
