## Overview

The Unbound is Hyperlite Mountain Gear's thru-hiking pack, and it marks a clean break from the brand's older tube-shaped Dyneema bags. The new design is paneled and boxier, with a single aluminum stay, a replaceable hip belt and pockets large enough to hold real gear. It comes in 40 L and 55 L versions that weigh almost the same. The trade-off is price and a no-frills back panel in exchange for waterproof, durable construction and easy on-trail access.

## Performance

**Carrying comfort.** The Unbound is the first Hyperlite pack that treats load transfer as a design problem rather than an afterthought. CleverHiker's testers carried up to 40 pounds in the Rocky Mountains without complaint. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers credited a thin layer of foam in the back panel and the contoured stay, which stiffens the pack and moves weight onto the hip belt. A UK long-distance walker who used the 55 L in a large for two weeks in the Scottish Highlands, at up to roughly 13 kg, reported no back or hip trouble. Switchback Travel found it surprisingly comfortable for an ultralight pack.

It is not a plush carry. Shoulder strap padding is thin, the back panel is slick and not ventilated, and there are no load lifters. Hikers who run hot or like to overload a pack will feel all three on long climbs.

**Weather resistance and durability.** Dyneema Composite Hybrid fabric and taped seams kept gear dry through CleverHiker's extended water-exposure tests. The bottom panel uses heavier DCH150 because that is where packs wear first. A Section Hiker tester who has used Hyperlite packs since 2013 put about 150 miles on the 40 L through a New Hampshire winter and into spring without problems.

**Organization.** This is the biggest upgrade over Hyperlite's older packs. The oversized side pockets are the feature reviewers praise most, and they are easy to reach on the move for bottles. The dual-entry mesh front pocket holds wet shell layers and tent stakes. A bottom stash pocket with a trash port gives snacks, a rain jacket or garbage a place of their own. Four vertical daisy chains and a horizontal one add lash points for trip-specific gear. AdventureAlan's one complaint is that the rear pockets lose their give when the main bag is stuffed to capacity.

**Fit and adjustability.** Torso length sets the size: Small covers 15–17 in., Medium 17–19 in., Large 19–21 in., and Tall is anything over 21 in. The back is fixed, so sizing matters. The hip belt swaps out, so a hiker who drops weight over a few months can change belts instead of buying a new pack. It can also come off entirely on lighter trips.

## Choosing a Version

The 40 L and 55 L share the same suspension, fabrics, pocket layout and 40 lb load rating. The 55 L weighs 3 g more than the 40 L at size M. What changes is the main compartment, which gives 49 L total on the 40 and 64 L on the 55.

The 40 L suits hikers with compact, dialed-in kits who resupply every few days, mainly in warm-weather conditions. The 55 L suits long food carries, bulkier shoulder-season insulation and shared gear. The roll-top lets a half-full 55 L shrink toward the 40's size, and AdventureAlan recommends the larger pack to most buyers for that reason. The one caution is that both packs carry the same 40 lb rating, and a bigger bag makes it easier to exceed it.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Handles loads up to 40 lbs more comfortably than older Hyperlite designs
- Waterproof DCH construction with a tougher DCH150 bottom panel
- Oversized side pockets, dual-entry front pocket and bottom stash pocket with trash port
- Removable, replaceable hip belt for weight savings or mid-trail swaps
- Four torso sizes, with the same weight in both volumes

**Cons**

- Expensive, at roughly $400 for the 55 L
- Thin shoulder straps, no load lifters and a non-breathable back panel
- Rear pockets lose slack when the main bag is packed full
- Fixed back length means sizing needs care
- Heavier than the barest frameless packs

## Who Should Buy This

The Unbound is for thru-hikers and section hikers who want Dyneema's waterproofing and durability and also want to find a snack without unpacking. It suits wet climates, multi-day carries and hikers who want a single pack for everything from shakedown weekends to a 2,000-mile trail. Gram-counters who want a frameless bag at minimum weight and price should look at Hyperlite's own Southwest, which Switchback Travel preferred over the Unbound 55. Hikers who want a cushioned, ventilated carry should look at a conventional suspension pack.

## Verdict

The Unbound gives up a little of Hyperlite's old weight obsession for comfort and organization, and the independent testing suggests the trade is worth it for long-distance hikers. It is expensive and the back is spartan. For most thru-hikers the 55 L is the better choice, since it weighs the same as the 40 L and rolls down when it isn't full.
