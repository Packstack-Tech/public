## Overview

The Bumster is Gossamer Gear's answer to a small problem: the phone, snacks, and sunscreen that need to be within reach without stopping to dig through a pack. At 88 g and roughly $25–29, it is a 1.5-liter fanny pack in 70D recycled Robic nylon that can be worn at the waist, on the back, or across the chest as a sling. The trade-off is room. It holds a day's worth of essentials, not much more, and the front-facing zipper is not the fastest to work on the move.

## Performance

**Capacity.** Reviewers land in the same place: 1.5 liters is enough for the quick-access kit and not much beyond it. One tester at Garage Grown Gear could fit sunglasses, keys, a couple of snacks, and a phone, and judged that it carried almost everything needed for hiking and biking. A member review on Backpacking Light, from a hiker who used it on the Tahoe Rim Trail, the Collegiate Loop, and Arizona Trail overnights, describes the limit more bluntly. Fill the main compartment with a sunglasses case, snacks, sunscreen, hand sanitizer, lip balm, a phone in a zip bag, and headphones, and the rear pocket has no room left for anything else. Treat the Bumster as a snack-and-phone pouch and it works. Treat it as a mini daypack and it won't.

**Durability and weather.** The 70D Robic nylon draws consistent praise. The same Backpacking Light reviewer reports that it held up across those thru-hikes and overnights. Treeline Review's testers, who named it the best lightweight fanny pack in their 2026 roundup, point to the water-resistant fabric and waterproof zippers. Neither is a substitute for a dry bag. A phone that worries its owner still goes in a zip bag.

**Carry.** Versatility is where the Bumster stands out from most fanny packs. Worn at the waist, it sits comfortably under or alongside a backpack hipbelt, and owners mention that the side-mounted buckle makes it easy to wear over a pack. Rotated to the back, it stays out of the way of a swinging arm. As a sling, it becomes a chest or crossbody bag, which one reviewer favored for walking around cities. CleverHiker's testers found it stayed secure during dynamic movement. With no padding, the pack stays flat and unobtrusive when it's lightly loaded, and it gets floppier as it fills.

**Access.** The main compartment opens with a front-facing zipper. Garage Grown Gear's reviewer found that this makes it harder to rifle through contents on the move than a top-opening design like the Westbound Gear fanny pack. Owners who like it say the zipper runs smoothly and can be worked one-handed. Exterior loops allow a few light items to be clipped on.

**Limits.** The nylon webbing belt is permanently attached, so it can't be removed and the pack can't be clipped to a pack's hipbelt in place of the strap. The belt maxes out at 114 cm (45 in.), so anyone with a larger waist, or wanting to wear it over bulky layers, may run out of adjustment.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 88 g for 1.5 L, a strong weight-to-capacity ratio
- Rugged 70D recycled Robic nylon that has survived thru-hike mileage
- Wears at the waist, on the back, or as a sling
- Concealed rear pocket for cash, cards, or a key
- Two internal mesh pockets and a key clip keep small items from migrating
- Low price makes it a cheap experiment for anyone new to fanny packs

**Cons**

- Fills quickly; a full front compartment leaves the rear pocket unusable
- Front zipper is slower to access than a top-opening design
- Permanently attached belt, maxing out at 114 cm
- Unpadded and unstructured, so it flops when overloaded
- Water-resistant, not waterproof

## Who Should Buy This

The Bumster suits ultralight hikers who carry a pack without large hipbelt pockets, and who want snacks, a phone, lip balm, and sunscreen at hand. It also suits day hikers, travelers, and anyone who wants one bag that moves from trail to town. Hikers whose packs already have roomy hipbelt and shoulder-strap pockets will probably find it redundant, as Garage Grown Gear's reviewer notes, and may use it more for short hikes, bike rides, and walks than for backpacking. Anyone who wants a camera, a full layer, or a wide-opening top-loading pouch should look at a larger or differently designed pack.

## Verdict

The Bumster does one job well: it holds a day's trail essentials for 88 g and about $25–29, in fabric that has held up on long trails. It earns a place on the packing list of hikers who need the extra pocket and can live with a front zipper and a modest volume. Those who need more room or quicker access should look elsewhere.
