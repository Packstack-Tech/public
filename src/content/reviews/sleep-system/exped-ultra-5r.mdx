## Overview

The Exped Ultra 5R is a 585 g insulated air pad built for cold-season backpacking, bikepacking and alpine trips. Its trick is synthetic insulation, not the crinkly reflective film in many rival pads. That makes it warm and unusually quiet. The cost is a bulkier packed size than the lightest competitors, plus a thin 20D shell.

## Performance

**Warmth.** A 4.8 R-value puts the Ultra 5R at the top of the three-season range and into winter use. SectionHiker rates it good down to about 0°F (-18°C), and notes that Exped helped push the industry toward the standardized R-value test. That makes the number comparable with other brands. Backwoods Pursuit tested the Ultra line from 1R to 7R and found Exped's temperature ratings accurate. Its testers would take the 5R down to roughly 0°F and move up to the 7R below that. Owners on Reddit report sleeping comfortably around -10°C, and one cold sleeper said the side of the body on the pad stayed plenty warm at -2°C. The caveat is the other direction. A reviewer at The Trek found it almost too warm on mid-summer nights, so this is not an ideal pad for hikers who only camp in July.

**Noise.** This is the pad's calling card. Reviewers at SectionHiker contrast it with NeoAir pads that sound like a bag of potato chips when a tentmate rolls over. Outdoorguru found it nearly silent, with turning noise but none of the rustle of insulation film. Owners who switched from a Nemo Tensor say a partner finally slept through the night. It isn't universally silent. Backwoods Pursuit's testers thought the current Ultra series was slightly louder than earlier Exped pads, and a few owners describe some crinkle when moving. Even so, most comparisons put it ahead of other insulated ultralight pads.

**Comfort.** At 7 cm, the pad is thick enough to isolate a sleeper from roots and rocks. The lengthwise chambers form a shallow cradle, and the outer tubes are a bit fatter than the middle ones. That helps keep restless sleepers from sliding off. Side sleepers split on it. Some call it one of the most comfortable pads they have used. Others find that the pad stays soft even when fully inflated, and that a hip can sit close to the ground. Bettertrail, which scored comfort 3.5 out of 5, called it supportive for a pad of this thickness. Inflation is easy to adjust, and letting out air is quick.

**Weight and packed size.** At 585 g, the Ultra 5R is not a gram-counter's pad. Reddit owners who also carry a NeoAir XLite say it packs larger and weighs more, and some always choose the lighter pad for that reason. The synthetic fill adds bulk, so the pad stuffs to ø 12.5 x 23 cm. Outdoors Magic summed it up as lighter and more compact mats existing, but not with this level of winter warmth.

**Durability and inflation.** Reviewers disagree here. SectionHiker calls Exped's insulated pads bombproof, while Bettertrail rated durability 2 out of 5 and said the 20D fabric trails the competition. Experienced backpackers will treat it like any 20D pad: clear the ground and keep it away from thorns. The included Schnozzel Pumpbag inflates the pad in about 20 seconds according to one owner. It also doubles as a waterproof pack liner. A repair kit comes in the box. The separate inflation and deflation valves are a minor annoyance for some testers, who find them clunky.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 4.8 R-value is enough for winter camping at 585 g
- Among the quietest insulated air pads, with no crinkly film
- 7 cm thickness and lengthwise chambers cradle the sleeper
- Schnozzel Pumpbag inflates fast and doubles as a pack liner
- Reasonably priced for the warmth, listed at $160 by Bettertrail

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than the lightest insulated pads
- 20D shell is a durability worry on rough ground
- Some side sleepers find it soft, with hip contact
- Overkill for warm-weather-only campers
- Separate inflate and deflate valves feel clunky

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season hikers who sleep cold, shoulder-season campers, alpine and winter backpackers, and bikepackers who want a warm pad that won't wake a tentmate will get the most from the Ultra 5R. Quilt users, who lose insulation under them, benefit most from the high R-value. Summer-only hikers and ounce-counters who sleep warm should look at a lower-R pad such as Exped's own Ultra 3R. Hikers who weigh every gram, or who need the smallest possible packed size, should look at a lighter pad with film insulation and accept the noise.

## Verdict

The Exped Ultra 5R is one of the best answers to a common problem: a warm pad that is also quiet and thick enough to sleep on. It gives up some weight, packed size and fabric toughness to get there. Cold-season hikers and light sleepers should buy it. Warm-weather weight-weenies should not.
