## Overview

The second-generation Elemnt Bolt is a 68.4 g GPS bike computer for riders who want turn-by-turn navigation without a touchscreen or a head unit the size of a phone. It's built for road cyclists, gravel riders and bikepackers who run long days on a light handlebar setup. The trade-off is a 2.2-inch screen that keeps the package small but limits how much map and data it can show.

## Performance

**Navigation.** The Bolt's main upgrade over the original 2017 model is on-device smart navigation. It gives turn-by-turn directions, reroutes automatically when a rider leaves the course, and can route back to the start. Riders can also drop a pin on the map page and have the unit build a route without the phone. The 16 GB of storage is four times the original's, and Cycling Weekly's long-term testers rode in Mallorca without downloading extra maps. The same testers saw the position marker lag slightly behind the rider's real location, mostly on fast descents.

**Screen.** The 64-color display is a clear step up from the monochrome unit it replaced. Testers at FeedTheHabit and Road Bike Rider describe it as easy to read, and fields shift color as power or heart rate moves between zones. An ambient light sensor adjusts brightness automatically, which helps in tree shade and flat light. The shortcoming is size. Road Bike Rider's reviewer found the screen cramped for navigation, and TechRadar lists the small screen area among its cons. Zoom buttons on the right edge enlarge the data fields, but a page full of fields gets dense quickly.

**Controls.** There's no touchscreen, and reviewers at Pocket-lint call that the right choice on a display this size, especially for riders in gloves. Three large buttons sit below the screen, one on the left edge handles power, and two on the right zoom. Road Bike Rider found the raised buttons easy to press in full-finger gloves, and one owner reports easy button access in heavy rain. TechRadar's tester found them still stiff. Pairing new sensors and adjusting settings is done mainly through the Elemnt companion app, which owners describe as quick to set up. Routes sync automatically from Strava, Ride with GPS, Komoot and similar services, and finished rides upload without a cable.

**Battery.** Wahoo claims 15 hours, and Cycling Weekly's long-term testers found that figure accurate. Cyclists Hub measured closer to 11 hours in its own use, so a full-day push with a bright screen and live sensors deserves a margin. USB-C makes topping up from a power bank on the move simple, which matters on multi-day bikepacking trips. The charge port sits under a cover at the bottom of the unit.

**Durability.** The graphite polycarbonate body and Gorilla Glass held up through a wet British summer in Pocket-lint's testing, with no issues from storms or road spray. The IPX7 rating covers immersion to about 1 m.

**Ecosystem.** The Bolt pairs over ANT+ and Bluetooth with power meters, heart rate straps, electronic shifters and Wahoo's Kickr trainers. Strava Live segments and structured workout files from apps such as TrainerRoad are supported, though following a workout needs a power meter. A row of customizable LEDs above the screen can flash speed, power, heart rate or turn cues. The Bolt 3 omits that strip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 68.4 g head unit with a semi-integrated out-front mount that keeps the bars clean
- Full on-device navigation with automatic rerouting
- 16 GB of storage holds large map regions
- Color display with ambient light sensor is readable in most conditions
- Physical buttons work in rain and with gloves
- USB-C charging, including mid-ride
- Simple app-based setup and automatic route and ride syncing

**Cons**

- 2.2-inch screen is small for map reading
- Real-world battery life can fall short of the 15-hour claim
- Single-band GNSS only
- No touchscreen, so on-device sensor pairing and edits are limited
- Some riders still find the buttons stiff
- The mount is specific to the Bolt

## Who Should Buy This

The Bolt suits road, gravel and bikepacking riders who value a small, light, fast-to-configure computer and mostly follow pre-loaded routes. It is easy to live with and rarely demands attention mid-ride. Riders who read detailed maps on the fly, run data-heavy pages, or want a touchscreen should look at the larger Elemnt Roam or a Garmin Edge.

Buyers should also know the Bolt 3 is on the market. It has a 2.3-inch 16-million-color display, 32 GB of storage, multi-band GNSS and a 20-hour claimed battery. It weighs 84 to 86 g and costs more. Early reviews were mixed: Cyclists Hub preferred the second-generation unit for its compact size and simpler interface, while others favor the newer model for its battery and display. A discounted second-generation Bolt is still a sound buy for anyone who prioritizes weight and price.

## Verdict

The Elemnt Bolt does the core job of a navigation computer, and does it in under 70 g. Its small screen is the real compromise, and riders who can live with it get a reliable, easy-to-use unit. It is an easy recommendation for weight-conscious cyclists, especially at a discount.
