## Overview

The NU33 is Nitecore's mid-weight, triple-emitter headlamp: a 700-lumen white spot, a warmer auxiliary flood and a red LED, all fed by a built-in 2,000 mAh battery that charges over USB-C. It was designed as the smaller, longer-running successor to the NU32, and Nitecore says it's 15 percent smaller. The trade-off is plain. It gives backpackers more beam and more modes than a 45 g minimalist lamp, and it charges that difference to the pack weight.

## Performance

**Output and runtime.** The primary white LED has three levels: Turbo at 700 lumens (rated 4 hours), High at 200 lumens (9 hours) and Mid at 60 lumens (38 hours). Independent runtime testing by LiquidRetro found that Turbo holds its full 700 lumens for only about five minutes before stepping down. After that the output stays flat for roughly 3.5 hours, with a few more step-downs before the lamp quits at the four-hour mark. High gave about 10.5 hours in the same test, at an output between 100 and 200 lumens. Turbo is a tool for finding a trail junction or a lost tent stake, not for sustained use. On a real night hike, High and Mid do the work.

**Beam.** The main emitter throws a diffused spot with a generous halo of spill, and Nitecore quotes 135 m at peak. The unibody optic is meant to blend the hotspot into the surrounding light. Testers describe the transition as soft rather than ringed, which is easier on the eyes when the beam sweeps across nearby ground. The main LED is cool white. LiquidRetro measured it at about 5,870 K with a modest CRI of 66 and a slight green lean. The test unit also showed no PWM flicker, because the driver is constant current.

**The auxiliary flood.** Camp tasks are where the NU33 earns its extra grams. The second white emitter is a warmer, wide flood for reading, cooking and sorting gear within arm's reach. It has two levels: 50 lumens for about 47 hours, and a 6-lumen Ultralow for up to 147 hours. Nitecore rates its CRI at 90 or higher. LiquidRetro's meter read 85 CRI at about 4,575 K, which is still a clear step up from the main emitter and pleasant at close range. Six lumens will last through an entire section hike of tent-bound evenings without a recharge.

**Red light.** The red LED puts out 13 lumens constant for about 26 hours, or a slow flash for about 50 hours. That's enough to read a map or get into a bag without wrecking anyone's night vision in a shared shelter.

**Controls and comfort.** Two buttons sit on top. The power button switches on and off and cycles brightness, and the mode button changes emitters. A short press of the mode button shows battery status. Reviewers find the two-button layout quicker to learn than single-button lamps that bury the red LED behind a long press. Owners and reviewers describe the lamp as comfortable for its output. The back of the bracket is contoured, the elastic band is adjustable, and the band carries reflective strips. At 95.5 g, though, it sits noticeably heavier on the forehead than the sub-50 g lamps.

**Durability and battery.** The aluminum front shell helps pull heat off the LED, and Nitecore's temperature regulation steps the output down to protect the emitter. IP66 covers rain and hose-down, but not submersion. The 2,000 mAh cell is sealed in, so a dead battery in the backcountry means a power bank, not a spare pair of AAAs. Nitecore equates the capacity to three AA cells, which is a fair way to think about the weight comparison with a disposable-battery lamp plus spares. A full charge takes about 1.5 hours.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Three emitters cover distance, camp tasks and night vision from a single lamp
- Warm auxiliary flood runs up to 147 hours on its lowest level
- No PWM in testing, and a soft-edged beam
- USB-C charging in about 1.5 hours
- IP66 and aluminum front shell hold up to trail abuse
- Clear dual-button interface with a battery indicator

**Cons**

- 95.5 g is about double the weight of true ultralight lamps such as the 44 g NU21
- Turbo drops from 700 lumens within about five minutes
- Battery is not user-replaceable
- Main emitter is a cool, low-CRI white
- Measured CRI on the auxiliary LED came in below Nitecore's claim in at least one independent test

## Who Should Buy This

The NU33 suits backpackers who do real camp work after dark, such as cooking, reading, map study and gear sorting. It also suits those who want one lamp that can do night hiking, trail running and shelter chores. Thru-hikers who charge daily from a power bank will like how little care the battery needs. Gram-counters should look at the NU21 or similar 45 g lamps, which give up beam and runtime to save about 50 g. Anyone heading out for weeks without a charging opportunity, or wanting swappable cells, should pick an AAA-powered lamp instead.

## Verdict

The NU33 does what it says: a bright, comfortable, well-sealed lamp with a useful warm flood and a proper red mode. It's not an ultralight lamp. At 95.5 g it's a capable all-rounder for those who value flexibility over the last 50 g.
