## Overview

Urberg's Packing Cube is a plain rectangular zippered bag in 100 percent polyester, sold by the Swedish budget outdoor brand mainly in a four-piece set. It is aimed at people who want to keep clothes and small gear sorted in a backpack, duffel or suitcase without paying Osprey or Eagle Creek prices. The trade-off is weight: at 57.5 g for the Standard and 70 g for the Large, these cubes sit well above what a gram-counting thru-hiker would carry for the same job.

## Performance

**Organization.** The job here is simple, and the design does it without fuss. A 6 L cube measuring 34 × 21 × 10 cm is roughly the size of a folded base layer, a pair of socks and a beanie stacked together, or a day's worth of food. The four-piece set is graduated, so the 1 L cube can swallow a first-aid kit or charging cables while the two 6 L cubes split clothing into sleep and camp layers. Because the zipper runs around the lid, the bag opens wide enough to load a rolled puffy without wrestling it.

**Zipper and fabric.** Urberg specifies YKK zippers, the component most likely to fail on a cheap organizer, and that choice is the main sign of intent in an otherwise basic bag. The polyester body is treated with a fluorocarbon-free water-repellent finish, which sheds surface moisture. That is a light-shower buffer, not weather protection: the cubes are not waterproof, the seams are not sealed and there is no roll-top. Anything that has to stay dry still needs a liner or a proper dry bag, and on a wet trail a cube full of down will be damp by the end of the day if the pack leaks.

**Compression.** There is none. No second zipper, no straps. The cubes hold their shape, which keeps a pack interior tidy, but they will not squeeze a fleece to half its volume. Hikers who want to reduce pack bulk will do better with a compression sack or a single large stuff sack for sleeping insulation.

**Weight in context.** The 6 L cube's 61 g in the set is the number to weigh against alternatives. A full four-cube set comes to 213 g, which is more than many ultralight shelters' stakes, cord and stuff sacks combined. A zipper and boxy panels cost weight that a drawstring bag or a plain zip-top bag does not carry. Where Urberg earns its keep is price and durability: the set lists at €39 and is routinely discounted to under €30 in Outnorth's European stores, which undercuts name-brand cube sets by a wide margin.

**Handle and visibility.** The solid-panel 4-pack has no mesh window, so contents are hidden until opened. Urberg also sells a Mesh version of the set, listed with a narrow top handle on each cube for carrying or hanging and 4–6 g heavier per cube. Anyone who wants to see what is inside before unzipping should look at that model instead.

## Choosing a Version

The Standard weighs 57.5 g and the Large weighs 70 g, a gap of 12.5 g, or about 22 percent. That extra weight buys more interior room and little else, since the fabric, zipper and construction are the same across both.

- **Standard:** The better fit for a 30–45 L pack, where a cube has to share space with a sleeping bag and shelter. It handles socks, base layers, electronics or a day of food, and costs less weight per use.
- **Large:** Better for weekend trips with bulkier clothing, for travel days, or for keeping one big "clothes" cube in a 50 L-plus pack. Hikers who already carry more than they would like should skip it, since larger cubes invite overpacking.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- YKK zippers on a budget organizer
- Graduated four-piece set covers everything from cables to a full change of clothes
- Rectangular shape stacks cleanly in a pack
- Water-repellent finish is fluorocarbon-free
- Low price, with regular discounts in Outnorth's European stores

**Cons**

- 57.5–70 g per cube is heavy by ultralight standards
- Not waterproof and not sealed, so it is no substitute for a dry bag
- No compression
- Solid panels hide contents (mesh version sold separately)
- Sold mostly as a set, which means paying for sizes a hiker may not use

## Who Should Buy This

Section hikers, travelers and gear-organizing types who pack for trips of a few nights and want a tidy pack at a low price will find these cubes do the job. They also suit car campers, families sharing a duffel and anyone moving between a suitcase and a pack on the same trip. Thru-hikers and anyone chasing a sub-10-pound base weight should look elsewhere: a single dry bag, a zip-top bag or a few plastic stuff sacks cover the same ground at a fraction of the weight.

## Verdict

Urberg's Packing Cube is a competent, inexpensive organizer with better zippers than its price suggests. It is a poor fit for ultralight kits, where 60-odd grams per pouch is hard to justify, but a sound buy for travel and casual backpacking.
