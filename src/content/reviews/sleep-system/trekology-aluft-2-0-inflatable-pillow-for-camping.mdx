## Overview

The Trekology ALUFT 2.0 is a budget-friendly inflatable camp pillow built for backpackers who want something more supportive than a stuffed puffy jacket but aren't ready to spend $50 on a Sea to Summit or Exped pillow. It's a scalloped, mummy-hood-shaped pillow made from TPU-coated fabric, and it's become something of a cult favorite in budget ultralight circles precisely because it does most of what the premium brands do at a third of the cost.

## Performance

**Comfort and shape.** The scalloped top and raised edges are the pillow's best feature. <cite index="1-17">The Aluft's larger size and ergonomic shape allow it to cradle the head and comfortably fit around the neck and shoulders.</cite> Multiple owner reviews echo this — one Walmart reviewer said the pillow <cite index="13-14">the top is rounded and will fit easily in my mummy sleeping bag without moving around at night and the seams in the middle make it comfortable for side sleepers</cite>. That center baffle/seam detail isn't just cosmetic — it's doing real work keeping your head from sliding off the sides during the night.

Fully topped off, though, this pillow gets firm — like, "camping pillow trying to be a yoga block" firm. One Walmart review flagged this directly, saying the pillow's <cite index="13-21,13-22">only flaw is that it is very rigid. Could use some tweaks in the comfort department.</cite> The fix that most users land on is under-inflating it to somewhere around 80% capacity, which softens it up considerably. The click-button valve actually makes this easy to dial in — you can crack it open and let out a puff of air without losing the whole load of air like you would with a traditional twist valve, which is a genuinely useful design touch for 2 a.m. adjustments.

**Valve.** The proprietary click valve is the pillow's headline feature, and it mostly lives up to the hype. It's a one-way valve, so <cite index="12-22,12-23,12-24,12-25">it allows you to just blow and not worry about air leaking as you go — with past inflatable camping items, it's always tricky to finish blowing it up without losing air, but this valve doesn't leak air as you go. It only allows air to pass through in one direction.</cite> Inflation takes just a handful of breaths — reviewers commonly report 3-5 breaths to full loft.

**Weight discrepancy.** Worth flagging up front: Trekology lists 120 g on the official spec sheet, but independent scale checks from reviewers and retailers land closer to 105-110 g (3.7-3.9 oz) for the pillow by itself, with the strap and stuff sack adding a bit more. It's a small gap, but if you're weighing your kit to the gram, don't take the manufacturer number as gospel.

**Anti-slip strap.** The detachable pad strap and rubber dots are meant to stop the pillow migrating off your sleeping pad. Reception here is mixed — some users like the added grip, but at least one long-term Trailspace reviewer was unconvinced, noting flatly that <cite index="2-2,2-3">the pillow, despite the manufacturer's claim, does not grip — yes, there are rubber/silicone dots on the rear of the pillow, but in their experience they are wasted weight.</cite> Your mileage will vary depending on your pad's fabric.

**Durability and cleaning.** The TPU-fused construction holds up well to normal backcountry abuse, but it's not bombproof. A multi-season Trailspace user reported that <cite index="15-9,15-10,15-11">the pillow, over time, will start to stain from everything from sunscreen, bug dope, sweat, and hair products, and it is not as easy to clean as they would have liked.</cite> Don't expect to toss it in the wash — spot-cleaning is about the extent of your options.

**Sizing.** The 4-inch loft is substantial by camp-pillow standards, which is great if you're a side sleeper who needs the extra height, but it can be overkill for smaller-framed people or stomach sleepers. One reviewer's camping partner stopped using hers specifically because <cite index="15-13,15-14,15-15">the pillow is a bit large — her camping partner no longer uses it because of that, with overall height around four inches, which was too tall for her.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely comfortable ergonomic shape that cradles the head and stays put in a mummy hood
- One-way click valve makes inflation fast and mid-night firmness adjustments painless
- Packs down to soda-can size at a real-world ~106-120 g
- Roughly a third of the price of premium competitors like Sea to Summit or Exped
- TPU-fused construction is quieter than crinkly nylon pillows

**Cons**

- Fully inflated, it's noticeably firm — plan on under-inflating for comfort
- Anti-slip dots and strap don't reliably prevent sliding for everyone
- Fabric stains over time and isn't easy to spot-clean
- 4" loft may be too tall for stomach sleepers or smaller frames
- Actual weight runs a bit lighter than advertised, but no in-between inflation "sweet spot" is marked, so dialing it in takes some trial and error each night

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for budget-conscious backpackers and thru-hikers who want a real pillow experience without spending $40-50 on a premium inflatable, especially side sleepers who'll benefit from the taller loft and scalloped shape. Stomach sleepers, minimalists chasing every last gram, and anyone who wants a pillow that holds a consistent shade of firmness without fiddling should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The ALUFT 2.0 delivers real ergonomic comfort and a genuinely clever valve system at a fraction of what the big sleep-system brands charge, and that alone makes it worth a look for most backpackers. It's not perfect — the firmness curve takes some fiddling, the anti-slip features are hit-or-miss, and the fabric will show its age — but for the price, it's hard to find a better night's sleep per gram. I'd put it at a 7.5/10: a smart buy for value-focused hikers, with a few rough edges that keep it just shy of premium-tier polish.
