## Overview

Mountain House's Beef Stew is one of the brand's original just-add-water pouch meals — diced beef, potatoes, carrots, and peas in a brown gravy, freeze-dried and packed for the trail, the pantry, or the emergency bin. It's aimed at backpackers who want a fast, no-cleanup dinner, and at anyone stocking emergency food thanks to the 30-year shelf life.

## Performance

**Prep and texture.** This is about as low-effort as backpacking dinners get: <cite index="1-13">just add water directly to the pouch and enjoy a tasty dish in the backcountry in less than 10 minutes</cite>. One 72Hours.com reviewer suggested going off-script for better results, noting they <cite index="3-8">get best results by putting quite a lot of water in the package, stirring thoroughly and letting it soak for longer than they recommend</cite>. That tracks with my own experience with Mountain House pouches generally — the printed water line is a starting point, not gospel, and stew in particular benefits from a slightly wetter mix and a few extra minutes of steeping to avoid crunchy bits at the bottom corners of the pouch.

**Taste and portion size.** Opinions are split, which is normal for freeze-dried food but worth flagging here specifically. On the positive side, buyers on 72Hours.com called it <cite index="3-2,3-3">"nice and tasty," with a "good portion" where "everything reconstitutes nicely."</cite> Another reviewer specifically appreciated that it's <cite index="3-4">"dairy free"</cite> and one of their favorite Mountain House flavors despite not usually liking stew.

On the critical side, a Trailspace reviewer who tested it as a family meal was less impressed with the cut of the meat, writing that <cite index="2-9">the 1/4 inch cubes reminded them of caned soup and not a hearty dinner</cite>, and describing <cite index="2-10">a thin brown gravy that didn't stick to the noodles they served with it</cite>. Portion size was the bigger complaint — that same reviewer was clear that they <cite index="2-14">would NOT serve this alone as a meal for two</cite>, ending up supplementing it with ramen for their family. If you're a hungry adult hiker eating solo, one pouch works as a solid single dinner; if you're trying to stretch it across two people after a big mileage day, budget for a side.

**Calories and nutrition.** At roughly 210 calories per 1-cup serving (420 per pouch), this isn't a calorie-dense option by ultralight standards — Peak Refuel and some other competitors pack more calories per ounce into a similar pouch. Sodium is the other thing to watch: forum regulars on Backpacking Light and High Sierra Topix flag Mountain House's sodium content routinely, with one poster comparing meals directly and noting <cite index="16-16">Beef Stroganoff sodium was lesser in Peak Refuel — 950 mg — compared to 1,530 mg in Mountain House</cite>. The Beef Stew's ~850 mg per serving (1,700 mg per pouch) isn't the worst offender in the lineup, but if you're sensitive to sodium or already stacking salty snacks on trail, it adds up fast over a multi-day trip. That said, several longtime backpackers on those same forums argue the extra sodium is a feature rather than a bug for anyone sweating heavily on trail, not a bug.

**Shelf life and packability.** The 30-year shelf-life claim is standard across the Mountain House lineup and is genuinely useful if you're rotating meals through an emergency kit rather than eating them all in one season. The pouch itself is compact and flat enough to disappear into a food bag without complaint — at 122 g it's not the lightest two-serving option out there, but it's not a burden either.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely fast and foolproof — hot water, stir, wait, eat straight from the pouch, no dishes
- Gluten-free formulation opens it up to a wider range of dietary needs
- 30-year shelf life makes it a reasonable dual-purpose pick for both backpacking and emergency storage
- No dairy, which some reviewers specifically called out as a plus among freeze-dried meals

**Cons**

- Thin gravy and small, uniform beef cubes read more like canned soup than a hearty stew to some testers
- Sodium content (~850 mg per serving) is on the higher end even for freeze-dried meals
- Calorie density per ounce lags behind newer competitors like Peak Refuel
- "2 servings" on the label is optimistic if you're hiking hard — one pouch is more realistically a single hearty dinner

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good fit for solo backpackers who want a reliable, low-effort dinner and don't mind a fairly classic, no-frills stew flavor — and for anyone building or rotating an emergency food supply where a long shelf life matters more than gourmet execution. If you're feeding two hungry hikers off one pouch, or you're watching sodium closely, look elsewhere or plan to supplement.

## Verdict

Mountain House Beef Stew does exactly what it says on the label — hot water, ten minutes, dinner — but it's a comfort-food classic rather than a standout in either flavor or nutrition density. I'd rate it a 6.5/10: reliable and shelf-stable, but not the pouch I'd reach for if calories-per-ounce or bold flavor are priorities.
