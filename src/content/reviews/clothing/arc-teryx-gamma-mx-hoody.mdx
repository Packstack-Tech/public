## Overview

The Gamma MX Hoody is Arc'teryx's warmest and most weather-ready softshell, a fleece-backed, stretch-woven hooded jacket that began life as an ice-climbing layer and now covers ski touring, alpine routes and cold-weather hiking. The "MX" stands for mixed weather. It trades breathability and packability for toughness and warmth, which makes it a specialist's piece rather than a three-season thru-hiking staple.

## Performance

**Warmth and wind.** The fleece backer is what separates the MX from the standard Gamma. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers ranked it among the top performers for weather protection and recommended it for colder climates or anyone who wants extra wind blocking. Owners describe a jacket that feels close to windproof and supple enough to move in, with a soft interior that reads more sweatshirt than shell. Switchback Travel called it the warmest in the Gamma family and rated it 4.4 out of 5, though it docked points for weight and limited year-round use.

**Breathability.** This is the trade-off. The same fleece that holds heat also slows moisture escape, and Outdoor Gear Lab found it less breathable than its lighter Gamma siblings during intense effort. A long climb in the sun will overheat the wearer. The jacket suits stop-and-go cold days: belaying, skinning at a measured pace, snowshoeing, or walking out in the shoulder season. One reviewer who owns the MX concluded it works best in a narrow temperature window and that the standard Gamma is the better single purchase.

**Weather resistance.** Fortius 2.0 sheds snow and light rain, and the PFAS-free DWR gives it a respectable first line of defense. It is not a rain jacket. A long-term owner who used it for two years in hiking and winter field use noted that a real downpour will soak through, so a hardshell still belongs in the pack for sustained wet weather.

**Durability.** Durability is the jacket's reputation. The 84/16 nylon-elastane face is built for rock and ice abrasion, and owners across multiple reviews praise the construction and stitching. Resale listings of used examples several years old tend to describe them in excellent condition, which fits the pattern.

**Hood and fit.** The StormHood is designed to cover a climbing helmet. Without a helmet, owners and reviewers consistently describe it as oversized and prone to flapping in wind. Cinched down it is serviceable, but it never looks tidy. The body fit is trim and athletic, which gives good mobility but limits layering. Owners report that a thick midlayer underneath is a squeeze, so the fleece-lined shell is meant to be the insulation, not a layer over it.

**Weight and packability.** At 555 g, the MX weighs more than most insulated midlayers and far more than a typical ultralight wind shirt. It doesn't compress well. Switchback Travel flagged weight as the main drawback, and reviewers consistently note it is better worn than carried. For anyone counting grams, that is the central problem.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Warm fleece backing with strong wind resistance for a softshell
- Abrasion-resistant Fortius 2.0 face that holds up to rock, ice and pack straps
- Excellent mobility from the stretch fabric
- Helmet-compatible StormHood
- PFAS-free DWR
- Clean look that works off the mountain too

**Cons**

- 555 g, heavy for ultralight backpacking and poorly packable
- Limited breathability during hard efforts
- Narrow comfortable temperature range
- Oversized hood is awkward without a helmet
- Trim fit restricts layering
- Premium price for a specialist garment

## Who Should Buy This

Climbers, ski tourers and winter hikers who spend cold days in mixed conditions and want one tough layer that blocks wind, sheds snow and tolerates abuse. It also suits anyone in a cold climate who wears a softshell as a daily outer layer.

Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers should look elsewhere. A wind shirt plus a synthetic or down midlayer covers more conditions at less weight and packs smaller. Buyers who run hot or want a single softshell for year-round use are better served by the standard Gamma, which breathes better.

## Verdict

The Gamma MX Hoody is a well-built, durable and warm cold-weather softshell that does exactly what Arc'teryx designed it to do. It earns its place in a climber's or ski tourer's kit, but at 555 g and with a narrow comfort range, it is a hard sell for weight-conscious long-distance hikers.
