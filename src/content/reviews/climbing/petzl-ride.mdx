## Overview

<cite index="4-1,4-2">
  The RIDE is the lightest ice axe in the Petzl range — at 45 cm long and just
  240g, it's ultra-compact and designed to go easily on your pack.
</cite>
<cite index="10-1">
  While it was designed as a compact and lightweight ski mountaineering axe, the
  RIDE is also a very functional summer mountaineering tool.
</cite>
If your objective involves mostly boot-packing, glacier travel, or consequential
ski descents — and not sustained water ice or technical mixed terrain — the RIDE
is worth a close look.

## Performance

### Pick & Placement

<cite index="1-3,1-4">
  The shape of the RIDE's pick strikes a very nice balance between self-arrest
  performance and steel snow prowess. It's quite tapered for a lighter axe and
  was able to penetrate firm snow and ice surprisingly well relative to its
  weight.
</cite>
That 3mm tip taper is doing real work here —
<cite index="8-28">
  the pick still happily sinks into frozen turf and hard snow, but the angle is
  more suited to less steep terrain than the Petzl Gully.
</cite>

<cite index="11-1">
  Holes in the pick provide the lightest possible weight and the option to
  connect a carabiner
</cite>
— a detail that also makes it compatible with the optional TrigRest hand
support.
<cite index="6-18,6-19">
  The adze is open with metal cut out to save weight, which makes it more
  time-consuming to clear snow, as it tends to pass right through.
</cite>
For step-cutting or digging, the lightweight adze is a real limitation. Consider
it an emergency snow-clearing tool, not a workhorse.

### Shaft & Ergonomics

<cite index="1-6">
  The slight bend in the RIDE's shaft increases leverage on the head while
  self-arresting, as well as when climbing in low and mid-dagger positions.
</cite>
<cite index="1-9,1-10">
  Testers appreciated the curved shaft and found it to be a boon over more
  traditional designs — most notably, it provides better leverage when
  mid-daggering in steeper terrain while also keeping the user's hand warmer and
  drier.
</cite>

<cite index="12-9,12-10,12-11">
  Like most European-made ice axes, the RIDE is most comfortable in piolet-canne
  (pick forward) position. Petzl removed all the teeth on the pick close to the
  adze, allowing for a smooth, comfortable place to hold on to — a small design
  detail that adds up over long carries.
</cite>

### Self-Arrest

<cite index="1-1,1-2">
  The RIDE was above average in its self-arresting ability, even when compared
  to non-ultralight models. Its pick struck a nice balance between providing
  security in steep terrain and executing a smooth self-arrest.
</cite>
<cite index="12-12,12-13">
  It was slightly less comfortable than other models in the self-arrest
  position, though average among ultralight axes — and hardly uncomfortable;
  testers carried it for hours with thin gloves on.
</cite>

One user reported using the RIDE <cite index="15-4">on many winter outings up to 55–60 degree hardpack snow and ice, with consistent performance when it mattered.</cite>

### The Spikeless Design

<cite index="1-21,1-22">
  The spikeless design is one reason the axe achieves such low weight — it
  features a 45-degree cut at the bottom, though Petzl plugged the end to
  minimize snow filling up the shaft.
</cite>
In practice,
<cite index="4-8">
  the beveled spike makes it easier to carry on your pack without damaging your
  gear
</cite>
— a real convenience when the axe spends most of its time strapped outside your
bag. The trade-off is less-than-ideal plunge performance on very firm snow;
you're not getting the solid stick of a traditional steel spike.

### Weight vs. Capability

<cite index="12-22,12-23,12-24">
  Among ultralight models, if you could only have one weight-conscious axe, the
  RIDE is a standout — nearly the lightest in its class and offering the highest
  across-the-board performance of any sub-12-ounce model tested. It features the
  best combination of steep snow climbing performance, self-arrest ability, and
  weight of any model in that category.
</cite>
<cite index="12-25">
  Testers could even pound the occasional snow picket with it and would consider
  using it for basic glacier climbs — something they wouldn't consider with the
  majority of other ultralight axes.
</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- <cite index="4-1">The lightest ice axe in the Petzl range at 240g</cite>
- All-steel head delivers real pick durability and penetration on firm snow
- <cite index="1-6">
    Curved shaft increases leverage during self-arrest and mid-dagger climbing
  </cite>
- <cite index="8-20">
    Beveled spike allows stowing inside a pack, reducing injury risk and
    avoiding pack damage
  </cite>
- <cite index="6-33">
    Being small and light makes it an easy decision to take on
    marginal-requirement days
  </cite>
- CE and UIAA certified; 3-year warranty; manufactured in France

**Cons**

- <cite index="1-24">
    Only available in one length — 45cm — the shortest of the ultralight models
  </cite>
  ; taller users will feel the compromise
- <cite index="2-3">
    Durability suffers slightly as a consequence of its lightness, notably at
    the spike, which is aluminum rather than steel
  </cite>
- <cite index="8-2,8-3">
    The TrigRest is not included — and without it, the axe feels noticeably less
    secure when climbing on steeper terrain
  </cite>
- <cite index="13-33">Not compatible with pick weights</cite>, limiting its
  usefulness on harder ice where swing mass matters
- Undersized adze with cutouts makes it poor for serious step-cutting
- <cite index="9-4">
    The aluminum shaft can dent relatively easily — worth keeping in mind during
    rough handling
  </cite>

## Who Should Buy This

<cite index="12-14,12-15">
  The RIDE is a perfect choice for alpine adventures where every ounce matters
  but the terrain isn't too technical — great for steep booters, technical ski
  descents, approaching alpine rock climbs, or basic snow and glacier climbs.
</cite>
It's the axe you actually bring because it weighs almost nothing, rather than
leaving a heavier tool in the car when conditions are borderline.
<cite index="2-1,2-2">
  It works well for winter mountaineering and summer routes that involve only
  secondary snow and ice — and it's worth considering as part of a quiver,
  paired with a burlier tool for more sustained technical terrain.
</cite>
Step-cutters, technical alpinists, and anyone regularly on water ice or
committed steep mixed routes should look to the Petzl Gully or a full technical
tool instead.

## Verdict

The RIDE is one of the most honest ultralight axes on the market: it knows exactly what it is, and it delivers. At 240g with an all-steel head, it punches above its weight class for self-arrest and firm snow performance — it just won't pretend to be a technical climbing tool. Pick up the optional TrigRest if you think you'll be daggering regularly; it meaningfully improves grip security in steeper terrain. For ski tourers, fast alpine approaches, and anyone who wants genuine avalanche-terrain insurance without the weight tax, this is an 8/10 axe.
