## Overview

The VAR Seal Set is three replacement rubber seals for the VAR 2, the Czech gas stove that has been built in Jablonec nad Nisou since 1994. It weighs 5 g and costs roughly the same as a bread roll. It fits one stove and does nothing else, which is the point: when a seal on a VAR 2 wears out, this is the part you need.

## Performance

A seal set can't be benchmarked like a stove, so the useful questions are narrower: does it fit, is it easy to swap, and does it fix the problem?

**Fit.** The set is made for one stove. VAR lists it as three replaceable seals for the VAR 2, and Czech retailers describe the parts as O-rings measuring 12 x 8 x 2 mm. VAR's own guidance, repeated by retailers, is that the seals suit only VAR appliances. That rules out raiding the hardware-store O-ring drawer on the assumption that "close enough" will do. A seal that is slightly off on a pressurized gas joint is a leak, not a quirk.

**Installation.** No tools are needed beyond a sewing needle. According to the instructions repeated in retailer listings, a worn or damaged seal is pried out with the sharp end of a fairly stout needle. The new ring is then pushed into its seat with the blunt end. It's a kitchen-table job that takes a few minutes, and it's the sort of repair that can also be done at a hut or campsite if the needle is in your repair kit.

**What it fixes.** Seals are the wear item on a canister stove: they are squeezed and heated every time the stove is connected and used. Retailers selling the set point out that a fresh seal can noticeably cut a stove's gas consumption, which is consistent with a worn seal letting some gas slip past. For a stove rated at 2,800 W that weighs 110 g by VAR's own figure, a small leak is a poor trade, since it wastes fuel and adds risk. The VAR 2 also has a patented nozzle position inside the burner, which VAR says makes it close to maintenance-free. That leaves the seals as the main consumable to think about.

**Weight and packing.** At 5 g for all three seals, the set adds nothing worth counting. It fits in a pill bottle, a zip bag, or taped inside the stove's storage pouch next to the needle that installs it.

**Price.** The manufacturer lists the set at 25 CZK, and shops stock it for roughly 19 to 35 CZK. Postage on an online order will cost more than the seals. Buying a set with the stove, or the next time you are in an outdoor shop, makes more sense than ordering one set by itself.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Made by the stove's manufacturer, in the Czech Republic, to the stove's own dimensions
- Three seals per set, which is several repairs' worth on a stove that sees seasonal use
- 5 g total
- Costs a few crowns
- Fitting needs only a needle

**Cons**

- Fits the VAR 2 only; there is no use for it with other stoves
- Hard to find outside Czech and Slovak retailers
- Not worth a dedicated order if shipping is involved
- Won't help if the stove's problem is somewhere other than a worn seal

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who owns a VAR 2 and plans to keep using it, from weekend hikers in the Czech uplands to climbers who value the stove's 110 g weight. Add a set to the repair kit and the cost is essentially nil. Owners of any other stove should skip it, as should anyone who would rather replace a failing stove than repair one. The seals are specific to the VAR 2, and the set has nothing to offer anyone who doesn't have one.

## Verdict

This is the sort of part that decides whether a stove stays in use for decades. It costs next to nothing, weighs next to nothing, and replaces the one component most likely to wear out. Every VAR 2 owner should carry a set.
