## Overview

Sumbu Langit is an Indonesian ultralight outfit that started with hand-built jet stoves and now sells a full line of aluminium pots, bowls and a non-stick fry pan. Its 1100 ml nesting pot is a tall, narrow, uncoated aluminium cylinder sized to swallow a stove, a gas canister and a few small items. The trade-off is the usual one for aluminium: it costs far less than titanium, but it weighs more and scorches more readily.

## Performance

**Weight and packing.** At 136.5 g, the pot is about 4.8 oz. That's heavier than the best titanium pots in its class. Evernew's 0.9 L titanium pot is listed at 4.1 oz, and MSR's 0.85 L Titan kettle at 118 g with its lid. The extra 20 g or so buys a larger 1,100 ml volume and a much lower price. The pot's 12 × 12 cm shape is deliberately proportioned for the standard 230 g canister. The seller says the pot packs a canister, a stove, a folding spoon, the UL gripper and a lighter in one compact cluster. That is the classic solo-hiker kit, and it fits in one pouch.

**Heat and cooking.** Aluminium conducts heat quickly, which suits boiling water for freeze-dried meals, coffee and instant noodles. The 3003 alloy is a common food-grade choice for cookware. This pot is uncoated and not hard-anodized, so it behaves like plain aluminium. Gear testers at Treeline Review note that hard-anodized aluminium spreads heat best for real ingredients such as eggs and vegetables, and that titanium and aluminium pots can both dent, warp and scratch. A bare aluminium pot over a small, hot flame can scorch food, particularly rice, if it isn't stirred.

**Rice, steaming and the accessory ecosystem.** The listing pitches the pot at rice, boiling and steaming. That reflects the Indonesian and Southeast Asian camp-cooking habit of making proper rice on the trail rather than rehydrating a pouch. Sumbu Langit sells a separate multi-cook inner pot, 6 cm tall and 11.5 cm across, designed to sit inside this pot and keep rice from sticking or crusting. It is made for 230 g canister setups. A 600 ml aluminium bowl is sold as a companion piece, and the pot is also sold in a bundle with that bowl. The pot is sold in a brown or black finish.

**Handling.** The kit includes a UL gripper and a second gripper for lifting the hot pot. A cloth wrap and a pouch cover storage and carrying. Without an insulated handle, the gripper is what stands between a hiker's fingers and a boiling litre of water, so it is worth keeping it in the pouch.

**Owner reception.** Ratings on Tokopedia are strong. Sumbu Langit's own store shows a 5.0 average across 207 ratings, with 200 of them five-star and the other seven four-star. A reseller's listing shows 4.9 across 13 ratings. Buyers are mostly Indonesian campers, and the pot also has a following among Hong Kong hikers, where the brand's steaming set is sold. These are retail-platform ratings, not lab tests, so they speak to satisfaction more than to durability over thousands of miles.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 136.5 g for a 1,100 ml pot, with the Lansang included in that figure
- Shaped to nest a 230 g canister, stove, spoon and lighter
- Uncoated food-grade aluminium heats fast and costs about Rp129,000
- Pouch, gripper, UL gripper and cloth included in the box
- Optional rice inner pot and bowl extend it into a proper cook system
- Consistently high ratings on Tokopedia

**Cons**

- Heavier than comparable titanium pots, which weigh roughly 115–120 g
- No coating or anodizing, so food can stick and scorch
- Aluminium dents and scratches more easily than titanium or steel
- Distribution is mainly through Indonesian marketplaces and a few Asian retailers
- No integrated handle or lid is listed; it relies on the supplied gripper

## Who Should Buy This

This pot suits solo hikers and weekend campers who cook real food: rice, noodles, steamed buns, a stew. It also suits anyone who wants a titanium-like packing footprint without paying titanium prices. Southeast Asian and Hong Kong hikers who can buy it locally get the most from it. Gram-counters chasing the lowest possible base weight should buy titanium instead. Anyone who mostly boils water for freeze-dried meals will also do better with a lighter, smaller titanium mug. Couples and groups need more than 1,100 ml.

## Verdict

Sumbu Langit's 1100 ml pot is a practical, inexpensive, well-liked cook pot that packs neatly around a 230 g canister. It gives up about 20 g to the best titanium pots and offers nothing special in heat control, but the price and the rice-cooking accessories make it a sound choice for hikers who cook. A rating of 7.5 reflects strong value and fit, held back by weight and a bare aluminium finish.
