## Overview

The Phantom HMS is DMM's attempt to shrink the classic pear-shaped belay carabiner without losing the pear. At 56 g in screwgate form, it is a true HMS locker, wide enough at the top for a Munter hitch, built from 7075-T6 aluminum in an I-Beam section, and short-gated to leave room for rigging. The trade-off is hand size: owners find it smaller than most full-size HMS lockers, and the short gate is a rigging feature, not a convenience for fat gloves.

## Performance

**Belaying.** The Phantom HMS has a thickened, nearly round top bar, which is the feature that separates it from a plain lightweight D. Climbers on Mountain Project recommend it as a light locker for belaying and rappelling, citing a decent rounded rope-bearing surface. Reviewers on CampSaver go further: one described the wide contact area as smoother than DMM's own Belay Master and called it the smoothest-feeding HMS in the owner's collection, especially in guide mode. DMM says the shape also discourages lockdown on belay plates. The Munter hitch is the stated design brief, and the pear geometry gives the hitch room to flip without jamming against the spine.

**Rigging.** The extra-short gate is the headline feature. It moves the gate hinge down and leaves more clear space at the top of the carabiner, enough, DMM says, for two clove hitches. For anyone building a personal anchor on a multi-pitch route, that is the practical payoff: two clove hitches on one locker without a fight. Retailers and owners repeat the claim, and the 71 x 96 mm envelope is generous for a 56 g carabiner. UK climbers note that the Phantom HMS is smaller than a full-size HMS, so a double-strand clove or three single clove hitches on half ropes is a tighter fit than on bigger lockers.

**Handling.** The keylock nose is snag-free on slings and bolt hangers, and the tapered nose is slim enough to clip into textiles. Owners describe the screwgate as smooth and secure, easy to work one-handed. Buyers on European retail sites praise the handling and quick opening, with a recurring note that it is a bit small next to other HMS carabiners. That is the consistent complaint, and it is fair: a big hand in a glove has less room than on a Petzl William or similar.

**Strength and build.** The 25 kN major-axis rating is standard for the class. Minor-axis strength is 9 kN and open-gate strength is 7 kN. Fit and finish get the usual DMM praise from owners, who call the build confidence-inspiring. Climbers using it on belay duty with tube-style devices, however, say the narrow bearing bar suits thinner ropes better than fat single ropes, and a few UK forum posters prefer a more arced pear shape for device belaying.

**Weight context.** DMM's Rhino, an I-Beam and round-stock hybrid, weighs 73 g, so the Phantom HMS saves about 17 g on a belay locker. Across a rack with three or four lockers, that adds up to a couple of ounces. It is not transformative, but it is real.

**A naming trap.** DMM also sells a plain Phantom Screwgate, an offset-D shape that weighs about 42 g. It is a different carabiner: not a pear, and not suited to Munter hitches. Many online reviews of "the DMM Phantom" cover the D, including some of the most widely cited ones, which note that super-light lockers of that kind are not designed for belaying or rappelling. Check for "HMS" in the product name before buying.

## Choosing a Version

The carabiner itself is identical across versions: same 71 x 96 mm body, same strength ratings. What changes is the gate mechanism.

- **Screwgate (56 g):** the lightest and the simplest, with the widest gate opening at 20 mm. It suits weight-focused users who are happy to check the sleeve by habit.
- **Kwiklock (61 g):** a faster-acting lock, 5 g heavier, with a 19 mm opening. It suits climbers who clip and unclip often and want something quicker than turning a sleeve.
- **Locksafe (62 g):** an auto-locking gate, 6 g heavier than the screwgate, also 19 mm. It suits belayers and guides who want the gate to close on its own, and it is the better choice when a dropped lock is a real concern.

For most ultralight users, the screwgate is the right choice. The auto-locking versions are for people who value convenience over the extra grams.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 56 g in a true pear shape, with a real Munter hitch geometry
- Short gate leaves room for two clove hitches
- Wide, near-round top bar feeds rope smoothly in plate and guide mode
- Keylock nose avoids snagging on slings and bolts
- 25 kN major-axis strength; EN 362 and EN 12275 certified
- Three lock options for different priorities

**Cons**

- Smaller in the hand than most full-size HMS lockers
- Tighter for multiple clove hitches on half ropes than a large HMS
- Narrow bar may feel grabby with thick, stiff ropes
- Priced above many similarly rated HMS lockers; list prices run around $30
- Easy to confuse with the D-shaped Phantom Screwgate

## Who Should Buy This

The Phantom HMS is for climbers who want a single belay-and-anchor locker at the lowest weight that still handles a Munter. Alpine climbers, multi-pitch trad leaders and anyone building a personal anchor system will get the most from it. Those who belay a lot on thick gym ropes, or who want maximum hand room, should look at a larger HMS carabiner instead. Anyone who needs a purely belay-focused locker may prefer a heavier, rounder-section design.

## Verdict

The Phantom HMS gives up some hand room to cut weight, and in return delivers a properly shaped belay locker that also works as a rigging carabiner. It is a strong pick for alpine and multi-pitch racks, but it is not the right choice for climbers who belay full-time on fat ropes. Buy the screwgate for the lowest weight, and check the product name for "HMS."
