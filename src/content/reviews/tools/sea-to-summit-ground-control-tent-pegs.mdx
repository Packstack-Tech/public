## Overview

The Ground Control Tent Pegs are Sea to Summit's replacement stakes for tents, tarps and shelters: eight anodized 7075-T6 alloy pegs with a three-sided profile, a reflective pull cord on each, and a small Ultra-Sil bag to carry them. They are built for people whose stock pegs bend, slip or vanish into the dark. The trade-off is weight. At 14 g apiece, they sit well above the skinniest ultralight pegs.

## Performance

**Holding power.** The three-sided section is the whole point. Owners who have swapped out round-rod factory pegs describe better grip in sandy and loose soil, where the flat faces bite more than a thin cylinder does. One buyer who added a set to a four-person, standing-height tent reported that the pegs did not budge in a windy thunderstorm, even in loose ground. Another described them as staying put in heavy weather while still being easy to place and remove. Customer ratings are consistently high: Bergfreunde's owner reviews average 4.7 from 19 ratings with every reviewer recommending them, and Galaxus shows 4.5 from 34.

**Hard ground.** Three guy points at different heights let the cord sit lower when a peg can't be driven to full depth, which reduces the leverage trying to rip it back out. Sea to Summit builds the design around that trick. A buyer who bought them to replace pegs too soft for rocky ground praised the tri-point shape. Another reported that the profile takes hammering with a plastic mallet without damage.

**The pull loop.** This is the feature owners mention most, and it is easy to see why. Fiddling a thin stake out of packed dirt with cold fingers is nobody's favorite chore, and the cord gives something to grab. It also reflects headlamp light, so a guy line at the edge of a campsite is less likely to catch a shin after dark. The plastic tab is photoluminescent: charged by light beforehand, it glows without being lit. One Australian owner noted the loop makes these far easier to extract than similar pegs he already owned.

**Long-haul durability.** One hiker reported using them for roughly half the PCT, calling them the best stake design he had found and noting they weigh about the same as the competition while holding up. The bag has a practical job too. Owners say it keeps muddy pegs from smearing the inside of a pack, which matters more than it sounds after a wet morning.

**Limits.** Snow is not their game. A European reviewer found them small for snow, though they can work buried horizontally in hard-packed or crusted snow. Anyone expecting a dedicated snow or deep-sand anchor should carry something wider.

**Weight in context.** Eight pegs at 112 g is not a gram-counter's dream. Sea to Summit's own Ground Control Light pegs weigh 7 g each and measure 15 cm, which is half the mass and a shorter, less aggressive stake. Those Light pegs ship with the brand's Alto and Telos tents. The standard Ground Control pegs are the ones to buy when holding power matters more than shaving a few ounces.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Three-sided profile grips in loose, sandy and mixed soils better than thin round pegs
- Multi-height guy points allow a low cord position when the peg won't go in fully
- Reflective cord and luminescent tab make pegs easy to find and pull
- Ultra-Sil bag keeps dirt out of the pack
- Reported to withstand hammering and wind, including on long-distance trips
- Strong owner ratings across retailers

**Cons**

- 14 g each, twice the weight of Sea to Summit's Light pegs
- Eight-peg set is more than a minimalist tarp or single-wall shelter needs
- Too small to anchor reliably in snow
- Cord and tab add a little bulk compared with bare-bones stakes

## Who Should Buy This

These suit backpackers who pitch in mixed conditions, such as a four-season-leaning tent, a tarp in exposed country, or trips where sand, loam and rock can all show up in the same week. They are also a sensible upgrade for anyone whose stock pegs bend on rocky ground or leave the pitch loose in wind. Gram-focused thru-hikers running a two-peg-per-corner minimalist setup should look at lighter pegs, including Sea to Summit's own Light version. Winter campers need dedicated snow anchors.

## Verdict

The Ground Control pegs are a durable, easy-to-extract upgrade for sandy, loose or rocky pitches, and the pull loop alone makes camp breakdown less annoying. They cost a few grams more than the ultralight alternatives, but they hold. For three-season backpackers who value a secure pitch over the last 50 g, they are a good buy.
