## Overview

The Southwest 40 is Hyperlite Mountain Gear's smallest overnight pack, built from Dyneema Woven Composite fabric and aimed at minimalist backpackers who want serious durability without carrying a heavy load. It's the pack you reach for when the trip involves bushwhacking, scrambling, canyoneering, or just a lot of rain — situations where a fragile ultralight nylon pack would get shredded. This isn't a pack for someone hauling a bear canister and a week of food; <cite index="14-12">HMG and reviewers alike position the 40-liter version best for short overnights and experienced minimalist packers</cite>.

## Performance

**Durability and weather resistance.** This is where the Southwest earns its reputation. One reviewer who pushed the pack through blackberry, matagouri, gorse, and barbed wire found <cite index="13-1,13-22,13-23">the ruggedness of the outside pockets impressive, noting the Dyneema fabric just shrugged off run-ins with vegetation and tree branches</cite>. On a section of the Great Divide Trail through 67 miles of steep, brushy, scree-covered terrain, another tester reported the pack's fabric <cite index="11-12,11-13">lived up to its claims — resisting abrasion, retaining its shape and structure, and resisting moisture</cite>. Worth flagging, though: despite the "waterproof" reputation, one long-term owner is careful to point out <cite index="13-25,13-26">water-resistance is one of the pack's biggest selling points, but it is NOT actually waterproof</cite> — taped seams keep the main compartment dry-ish in a downpour, but I'd still pack a liner for real insurance on multi-day wet stretches.

**Fit and comfort.** This is the pleasant surprise for a lot of testers who expect a stripped-down Dyneema sack to feel like carrying a rock in a garbage bag. One review team was <cite index="12-1,12-2">struck by the pack's comfortable design and fit as soon as they put it on</cite>, and ultimately <cite index="12-5">came away more impressed by the pack's comfort and functionality than expected</cite> given its weight and durability. That said, the fit isn't universal — the same reviewers found the shoulder straps ran a bit wide initially, needing a break-in period to soften and snug up, and noted <cite index="12-11">women's-specific packs from companies like Osprey tend to be better options for women with longer torsos</cite>. HMG does offer the Southwest in five torso sizes (XS through Tall), so dialing in fit is possible if you take the time to measure correctly.

**Layout and organization.** The Southwest sticks to the classic ultralight formula: <cite index="10-5">a large main compartment, side water bottle pockets, and a large front pocket</cite>, with a roll-top dry closure rather than a floating lid or zippered brain. It's not the pack for people who like dedicated pockets for everything — there's <cite index="14-1,14-2">excellent build quality and a minimalist yet effective organizational layout, though limited adjustability and sparse padding mean it won't appeal to comfort-first backpackers</cite>. On the trail, that translates to a pack that rewards a disciplined, dialed-in gear list and punishes overpacking — cram it full past 40 lbs and you'll feel the thin foam back panel and lack of load-lifters.

**Capacity and who it's really for.** At 2,400 cu in internal plus 600 cu in of external pocket space, this is explicitly <cite index="10-6">the smallest volume overnight pack in the HMG product line</cite>, and testers agree it fits squarely into <cite index="1-1,1-2">the ultralight-kit-required category — there's no room for anything frivolous, except maybe a bag of chips strapped to the outside</cite>. If you're hauling a lot of camera gear, extra layers, or bulky cold-weather kit, size up to the 55L or 70L Southwest instead.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional abrasion resistance — shrugs off brush, rock scrambles, and off-trail bushwhacking
- Highly water-resistant (though not fully waterproof) thanks to taped seams and DCF construction
- Surprisingly comfortable once broken in, despite minimal padding
- Sturdy, well-thought-out side and front pockets that are accessible while wearing the pack
- Sold in five torso sizes for a more dialed-in fit
- Available in-store at REI for pre-purchase fit checks

**Cons**

- Not truly waterproof — pack liner recommended for sustained rain
- Sparse hipbelt padding and limited adjustability compared to traditional packs
- 40L capacity is tight for anything beyond short trips with frequent resupply
- Premium price tag, consistent with HMG's Dyneema lineup
- Shoulder straps run wide out of the box and need a break-in period
- Not ideal for hikers with longer torsos who need more structured support

## Who Should Buy This

The Southwest 40 is built for experienced ultralight backpackers doing short overnights to multi-day trips with regular resupply — think desert routes, canyon country, or any trip where durability and weather resistance matter more than capacity or plush padding. If your base weight is already dialed in and you're not trying to stuff a bear can or winter kit into 40 liters, this pack rewards that discipline with a genuinely bombproof, comfortable carry.

## Verdict

The Southwest 40 delivers on HMG's reputation: it's tough, weather-resistant, and more comfortable than its minimalist looks suggest, making it a strong pick for minimalist hikers who've already sorted out the rest of their gear list. It's not the most padded or adjustable pack out there, and the price reflects the Dyneema tax, but for off-trail abuse and wet conditions, few ultralight packs in this weight class hold up as well. I'd put it at an 8/10 — excellent at what it's designed for, with trade-offs that are clear rather than hidden.
