## Overview

The CamelBak MultiBev is a double-wall vacuum-insulated stainless steel bottle that splits in half to reveal a fully separate, insulated 16oz travel cup nested inside a 22oz bottle. It's aimed at people who want one vessel that covers cold water, hot coffee, and everything in between — think car camping, commuting, or bikepacking with a coffee habit, rather than a backcountry hiker trying to shave ounces.

## Performance

The headline feature is the split design, and it mostly delivers on the novelty. Twist the bottle apart below the black divider ring and you've got a fully separate 16oz insulated mug with its own silicone lid stashed inside the main Pak Cap. <cite index="12-3,12-4">Visually the bottle looks like a single piece, but gripping below the black divider ring lets you twist the travel cup off.</cite> It's a genuinely useful trick if you're the type who picks up a coffee on the way to the trailhead and doesn't want to leave a paper cup in the car.

Insulation performance is solid for a stainless bottle. <cite index="16-1,16-2">Testers found hot water in the thermos portion stayed a good temperature for drinking coffee the next morning, and CamelBak claims the 22oz bottle keeps liquid hot for around 14 hours and cold for 20</cite>, with the smaller cup holding heat for roughly 3-4 hours. One reviewer specifically noted <cite index="5-6">ice from the 22oz bottle was still frozen the next morning</cite>, which lines up with the vacuum-insulated stainless construction doing its job.

Where it gets more mixed is ease of use and everyday practicality. OutdoorGearLab's testing flagged a real design flaw: <cite index="1-6,1-7">dirt and germs get on the bottom of the bottle, which then goes straight into the bottom of the cup — an aspect of the design that felt overlooked</cite>. That's the tradeoff of nesting a cup where the bottle's exterior normally sits. The same review also found the bottle held onto flavors — <cite index="1-9,1-10">using it for a variety of flavored beverages left it smelling and tasting the same across drinks</cite> without a proper wash, though it cleaned up fine afterward since it's dishwasher safe.

The silicone lid draws mixed reactions. One REI reviewer specifically dinged it for a <cite index="10-11,10-12">lingering silicone taste on the lid, docking a star despite otherwise loving the versatility</cite>. Fit and grip are also a sticking point for smaller hands — a Walmart reviewer noted the bottle <cite index="6-6,6-7">was too fat to hold comfortably with one hand and didn't feel secure holding the 16oz cup one-handed with hot coffee in it</cite> because the exterior isn't especially grippy. That same reviewer also had trouble with the lid popping off the cup when pulling it out of a car cupholder.

Durability seems to be a genuine strength — this isn't a delicate product. <cite index="1-8">Beyond its design quirks, the bottle is a substantial and very durable stainless steel model that passed all durability tests with ease.</cite> The powder-coat finish and silicone base also get consistent praise for resisting scuffs and keeping the bottle from sliding off a table or car seat.

For anyone thinking about this for backpacking specifically, weight is the elephant in the room. OutdoorGearLab's own scoring gave it a **2.0 out of 10 on weight** — by far its worst category — which tells you where this product's priorities lie. At 486.5g, it's roughly the weight of a full 32oz Nalgene plus a backup lighter, cook pot, and titanium spork combined. This is not a bottle you're bringing on a thru-hike; it's a bottle you're bringing on a car camping trip or a bike commute where you also want to justify not buying a $6 latte in a paper cup.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely clever 2-in-1 design — full insulated cup nests inside the bottle
- Strong vacuum insulation performance (multi-hour hot/cold retention, per CamelBak and user testing)
- Very durable stainless build with a scuff-resistant powder-coat finish
- Dishwasher safe and BPA/BPS/BPF-free
- Fits most car cupholders and some bike bottle cages despite the split design

**Cons:**

- Heavy — 486.5g puts it well outside ultralight territory
- Design flaw where bottle-bottom grime can transfer to the cup interior
- Silicone lid can impart a lingering taste for some users
- Bulky diameter makes one-handed use awkward, especially for smaller hands
- Premium price for what is, in pure water-carrying terms, a mediocre ounce-to-weight ratio

## Who Should Buy This

The MultiBev makes the most sense for car campers, commuters, and travelers who want to consolidate a water bottle and a coffee mug into one object and are willing to trade weight for convenience. It's also a reasonable pick for bikepackers who value the built-in mug for camp coffee and don't mind the bulk in a bottle cage. If you're backpacking with a base weight you actually track, this isn't your bottle — a simple 16-24oz plastic or titanium bottle paired with a separate collapsible cup will beat it on weight by a wide margin.

## Verdict

The MultiBev is a smart piece of industrial design that solves a real annoyance — carrying two vessels instead of one — but it does so at a weight and bulk penalty that rules it out for most backcountry use. For car camping, commuting, or travel, it's a solid 6.5 out of 10; for anyone packing by the ounce, it's not on the list.
