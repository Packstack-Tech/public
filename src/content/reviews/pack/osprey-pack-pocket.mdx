## Overview

The Osprey Pack Pocket is a small zippered pouch that clips onto a shoulder strap or hipbelt, giving a pack with no front-of-body storage a place for a phone, snacks, lip balm and a key. It weighs 81 g and holds about a liter. The trade-off is simple: it solves the stop-and-dig problem cheaply, but it is a little large for narrow straps and adds weight that a sewn-in pocket would not.

## Performance

The pitch is access. Anyone who has stopped on a ridge to pull a pack off for a granola bar knows the cost: lost rhythm, a sweaty back, a phone that only comes out when someone else is already at the viewpoint. A strap-mounted pocket puts the day's small, frequently used items within reach of one hand. A UK retailer that carries the pocket points to exactly this use: phone and GPS device on the strap, snacks, medication or sunscreen alongside, so there is no rummaging in the main compartment.

The zippered closure is the main difference from the open stash pockets and shove-it mesh sleeves common on ultralight packs. Contents stay put when the trail turns into a scramble or a stream crossing, and the internal key clip keeps a car key from migrating to the bottom. The 100D x 210D triple ripstop is a sturdier fabric than the featherweight stuff in many ultralight add-ons, and the PFAS-free DWR will shed a shower. It is a water-resistant pouch, not a waterproof one, so a phone in sustained rain still wants a bag around it.

Fit is where owner feedback splits. Buyers praise how securely the pocket sits on Osprey's own pack straps and appreciate that it moves between packs and belts. Two recurring complaints show up in reviews. One owner reports that a smaller-framed user found it too big for both the hipbelt and the shoulder strap. Another says that without the key-clip hook in place, the pocket slides down a shoulder strap and ends up around the back. Osprey's related padded Pack Pocket draws a similar comment from Pack Hacker's testers, who found it bulky on smaller packs with thin shoulder straps; the sizing issue evidently carries over. Slim-strap frameless packs, the kind many thru-hikers carry, are the likeliest to cause trouble.

Capacity is honest to the label. One liter is a phone, a couple of bars, a small tube of sunscreen and a key. Plus-size phones will crowd it, and the 5-inch width limits what else can join. One reviewer wished for a card-sized elastic pocket on the back wall, which would help keep cash, a permit or an ID from sliding around.

At 81 g, the pocket is not a gram-counter's accessory. A pair of sewn-on hipbelt pockets on a pack like a Gossamer Gear or Hyperlite model weigh less in practice because they add almost nothing. But for packs with bare hipbelts or no belt at all, this is the lightest zippered solution from a mainstream brand, and it can be moved between packs, loaned to a partner, or taken off for a town day.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Zippered closure keeps a phone, keys and snacks secure on rough terrain
- Mounts either vertically on a shoulder strap or horizontally on a hipbelt
- Internal key clip
- Tough recycled triple ripstop with PFAS-free DWR
- Transfers between packs; about $20
- 3-year limited warranty

**Cons**

- 81 g is heavy next to a sewn-in pocket or a basic stretch pouch
- Runs large for small frames and slim shoulder straps
- Can slide down a shoulder strap if the key-clip hook is not used
- One liter fills quickly; large phones are a tight fit
- No internal organization

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers whose packs lack hipbelt pockets, or whose existing pockets are too small for a modern phone, will get the most from it. Section hikers, day hikers and travelers on an Osprey pack are the natural audience, since owners report that it seats well on the brand's own straps. Smaller-framed hikers and anyone using a frameless pack with thin straps should try one on the pack first or choose a smaller pouch. Gram-focused thru-hikers with built-in belt pockets have no use for it.

## Verdict

The Pack Pocket does one thing well: it keeps the small, frequently used items within arm's reach and closed behind a zipper. It is a bit big and a bit heavy for the narrowest straps, but at about $20 it is a practical fix for packs with weak front storage. Recommended for Osprey owners and anyone with sturdy straps; skip it if the pack already has good pockets.
