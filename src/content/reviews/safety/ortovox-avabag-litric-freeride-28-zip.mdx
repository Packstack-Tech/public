## Overview

The AVABAG LiTRIC Freeride 28 Zip is not an avalanche airbag pack — it's the storage half of one. This is the Zip-On "bag" component that clips onto Ortovox's LiTRIC BASE unit (the piece that actually holds the airbag, fan, battery, and trigger). If you already own an AVABAG LiTRIC FREERIDE or TOUR BASE and want a dedicated 28L shell tuned for lift-served freeriding and shorter tours, this is that shell. If you're shopping for your first electronic airbag setup, you need the BASE too — this listing alone won't save your life in a slide.

## Performance

Because this is a Zip-On, the "performance" story splits into two parts: how it carries as a bag, and how well the modular zip system actually works day to day.

On the carry side, Ortovox designed the Freeride 28 shell specifically for descending, not multi-day touring, and it shows. <cite index="3-4,3-5">The idea is that just one BASE can be used to attach various sizes of AVABAG LiTRIC FREERIDE and TOUR Zip attachments, each tailored to their intended use, and when combined, the base and the various packs merge to form a single unit.</cite> In practice that means you get freeride-specific organization — <cite index="3-8">ski, snowboard and ice axe fastenings as well as a helmet holder and quick access to the main compartment</cite> — without carrying the weight of a full touring pack when you don't need it.

Real-world owners seem to like the modularity. One long-term user noted <cite index="2-1">the avabag, the zip-on system is convenient, the airbag zipper doesn't rip open anymore on the slopes, and the hip belt with clip is much more convenient than the prior belt using velcro</cite> compared to their older Ortovox airbag pack. That same reviewer flagged two real gripes worth knowing about before you buy: <cite index="2-2,2-3">the backpack seems more designed for skiers than for snowboarders, and they didn't find the straps convenient for bootpacking, so they put the board between the backpack and their back instead</cite>. Snowboarders splitboarding into the backcountry should try this in a shop before committing.

The second gripe is a genuine annoyance for anyone running multiple Zip-Ons off one BASE: <cite index="2-3,2-4">buying a second zip-on for touring doesn't come with a second airbag deflating tool, and the reviewer's shop couldn't provide one, with Ortovox not responding to requests for a spare</cite>. That tool is small and easy to leave attached to whichever bag you swapped off, so if you're planning to swim between a Freeride 28 and a bigger Tour Zip-On depending on the day, budget for buying (or hunting down) a spare deflator, or just be disciplined about moving it every time you swap bags.

On durability, one owner running the Freeride 28 for carrying skis mentioned it's <cite index="3-1">comfortable to carry skis, even long ones (190cm)</cite>, but also flagged a wear point: <cite index="3-2">the fabric between the avalanche and main pockets wore through where the top of the probe and shovel sit</cite>. Worth checking periodically if you're a heavy user, especially since replacing that panel isn't as simple as swapping a strap.

Separately, testers of the full LiTRIC system (BASE + bag combo, tested as the Freeride 28L) came away impressed with the trigger mechanism this bag is built around: <cite index="4-2,4-3,4-4,4-5">initial skepticism about the trigger handle always being out gave way to appreciation for the twist-lock design — twist to arm, twist back to lock — which is far less fiddling than pulling a trigger in and out of a zippered pocket, especially with gloves on, and adds confidence against accidental deployment.</cite> That's a BASE-unit feature, not something unique to the 28 Zip, but it's the reason people buy into this ecosystem in the first place, and it's worth knowing the trigger experience carries over regardless of which Zip-On you're running.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Purpose-built freeride organization: ski/snowboard/ice axe carry, helmet holder, and a dedicated safety compartment for shovel and probe
- At 530 g, adds minimal weight over a standard pack shell — the BASE does the heavy lifting (literally)
- Swaps between BASE units, so one airbag system can flex between a lean 28L resort/sidecountry day and a bigger touring load
- Users report the zip-on interface itself is reliable and doesn't feel like a failure point on the slope

**Cons**
- Not a standalone product — you need an AVABAG LiTRIC BASE (sold separately, and not cheap) for this to function as anything but a regular backpack
- Reported fit quirks for snowboarders, particularly around bootpacking with the ski/board straps
- No spare airbag deflating tool included, and getting a second one from Ortovox has reportedly been difficult
- One long-term owner found a wear point in the fabric separating the avalanche pocket from the main compartment

## Who Should Buy This

This is a smart buy for someone who already owns (or is simultaneously buying) an AVABAG LiTRIC FREERIDE or TOUR BASE and wants a trimmed-down, resort-to-sidecountry-oriented shell instead of hauling a 40L touring bag on shorter days. It's especially compelling if you're building a two-bag quiver — say, this 28L Zip for lift-served freeride days and a larger Tour Zip-On for multi-day missions — off a single airbag BASE. It's a weaker fit for splitboarders who bootpack often, and it's the wrong purchase entirely if you don't already have (or aren't also buying) the LiTRIC BASE unit.

## Verdict

The Freeride 28 Zip does what it's designed to do: give LiTRIC BASE owners a light, freeride-tuned 28L shell with sensible ski/board/ice-axe carry and a dedicated safety pocket, without forcing them into a bigger touring bag. The modular concept works well in practice, but the missing spare deflator tool and skier-leaning fit are real friction points to know about going in. Judged purely as a Zip-On accessory (not as a complete airbag system), I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — solid, but you're buying into an ecosystem, not a complete product.
