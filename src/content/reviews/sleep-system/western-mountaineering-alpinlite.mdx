## Overview

The AlpinLite is Western Mountaineering's roomier take on the UltraLite: a 20°F, 850+ fill goose down mummy sewn by hand in California. It suits backpackers and climbers who want a bag they can roll over in and layer up inside, without giving up much weight. The trade-off is a few ounces and a bit of bulk over the UltraLite, plus a price that sits firmly in premium territory.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Western Mountaineering rates its bags on a nonstandard scale that it places between a typical comfort rating and a lower limit. The ISO numbers REI lists, 25°F comfort and 14°F lower limit, bracket the 20°F claim neatly. Owners tend to describe the rating as conservative. One long-term user in the damp Southeast reported that it went a little below 20°F for an average sleeper. Another GearJunkie tester, a warm sleeper, was warm enough near 20°F and would bring it deep into Colorado's shoulder seasons. Backcountry.com owners report comfortable nights to the low teens with a decent pad, and a few say it runs too warm above about 40°F.

**Loft and baffles.** The numbers on paper are 14 cm of loft. On the ground, GearJunkie measured the bag plumping to about 5.5 inches. The baffles are continuous, so down can be shifted toward the top of the bag on cold nights or pushed aside for warmer ones. That flexibility has a cost. At least one REI reviewer, a cyclist camping along the Pacific coast, woke cold after the down migrated. A quick shake and redistribution before sleep is part of the routine with this design.

**Room.** This is the reason to choose the AlpinLite over its sibling. TrailGroove measured a shoulder girth of 64 inches in the 6'0" size against 60 inches for the UltraLite, which it described as a meaningful gain in space. A 6'2" Trailspace reviewer who normally struggles with shoulder width in mummy bags said he could turn inside it. Outdoor Gear Lab's narrow-shouldered testers found the cut roomy but not cold, with extra space to wear insulating layers on fall nights. Because the bag is cut wide, it gives up some thermal efficiency compared with a snug mummy. In practice, testers said the penalty was small.

**Weight and packability.** At 936 g, the 6'6" is about two pounds of bag for a full-featured mummy. GearJunkie compared it to a 1 lb 6 oz top quilt from Outdoor Vitals and counted 11 ounces extra as a fair price for mummy-bag versatility. It packs to roughly a 9-liter volume in the larger sizes, so it eats more pack space than the UltraLite or a quilt. The 5'6" version is the lightest at 820 g.

**Build and details.** The #5 YKK zipper earns steady praise for sliding without snagging fabric, a known weak spot on lightweight bags. The full down collar and insulated draft tube are generous. The Velcro closure on the draft collar, though, drew a complaint from Outdoor Gear Lab testers as weak. There is no interior pocket. The 12-denier shell is thin, and a few buyers have raised durability worries. One owner who spent 20-plus nights in Alaska reported no problems. Western Mountaineering offers a lifetime warranty, and owners expect these bags to last decades with proper care.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- A wide cut (64–65 inch shoulders in most sizes) lets side sleepers move and leaves room for layers
- 5.5 inches of 850+ goose down loft gives strong warmth for the weight
- Rating reads conservatively; ISO lower limit of 14°F
- Snag-resistant full-length #5 YKK zipper, full draft collar, and insulated draft tube
- Hand-built in the USA with a long track record
- Works as a bag in the cold and as a quilt when opened up in warmer weather

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than the UltraLite, and well over quilt weights
- Expensive
- Down can migrate from the continuous baffles mid-night
- Draft collar Velcro is weak and there is no interior pocket
- Runs warm for midsummer use

## Who Should Buy This

The AlpinLite fits broad-shouldered backpackers, side sleepers, and anyone who dislikes feeling cocooned in a mummy bag. Climbers and shoulder-season hikers who want a margin of warmth and space to layer will also get value from it. Three-season hikers in the Rockies, Cascades, Sierra, and Appalachians in spring and fall get the most from the 20°F rating. Gram-counting thru-hikers who sleep in summer heat, or those who already fit comfortably in the UltraLite, should look at that bag or a quilt instead. Buyers on a tight budget should look elsewhere too.

## Verdict

The AlpinLite is a wide, warm, and meticulously built 20°F bag that spends its extra ounces on comfort rather than frills. It is not the lightest option, nor the cheapest, but owners keep reporting the same thing: it sleeps well and lasts. For anyone who finds narrower mummies confining, it is one of the better 20°F down bags available.
