## Overview

<cite index="18-18,18-19">
  The patented, multi-award-winning Double Rainbow is Tarptent's most popular
  2-person tent. Introduced in 2006, it has had 20 years of refinement and has
  seen use across the world.
</cite>
The design philosophy is straightforward: a single-arch, single-wall shelter
that prioritizes livable volume, fast setup, and genuine storm resistance
without crossing into the kind of ultralight extremism that compromises
durability. It's the tent that PCT and AT thru-hikers keep recommending to each
other at trailheads, and for good reason.

## Performance

### Livability and Space

This is where the Double Rainbow earns its reputation. <cite index="4-16,4-17,4-18">The tent is comfortable for its size, especially for tall, skinny sleepers. Its 88-inch length is more generous than most other sub-three-pound tents. It also has a rectangular, non-tapered footprint</cite> — a detail that matters more than it sounds when two people are trying to sleep side by side. <cite index="4-19">It has a fairly uniform height from door to door, which means two people can more or less sit up at the same time without their heads bumping too much against the ceiling.</cite> Width at 50 inches is snug for two adults with wide pads, but solo hikers will feel like they have a palace.

The dual-door design adds real-world convenience. <cite index="4-22,4-23">It has two side doors and two 7.5 square foot vestibules — comparable to or larger than its close lightweight competitors — large enough for most backpacks and a pair of boots.</cite> <cite index="18-26">A "porch mode" provides a large covered space in front of the doors, perfect for cooking or staying out of the sun and rain.</cite>

### Weather Resistance

The Double Rainbow handles weather seriously. <cite index="4-37,4-38,4-39,4-40">Its sil-poly fly offers very good protection from precipitation. The zippers are fully waterproof, and the fly runs down almost all the way to the ground to limit splashback. Testers found that the materials stay waterproof longer and in heavier rain than almost any other model.</cite> Multiple thru-hikers confirm this: <cite index="6-17,6-18,6-19">one couple reported the tent sheltered them from 80 mph winds, thunderstorms, hail, and snow — surviving five months of daily use on the PCT with barely a scratch.</cite>

The arch-and-strut structure is more capable than it looks. <cite index="8-18">There are additional guy line attachment points on the arch sleeve and perimeter hem, though unless you're looking into the eyes of a tempestuous storm, you're unlikely to need them for day-to-day use.</cite> Trekking poles can also be inserted under the top crossbar for extra structural rigidity in high wind or under snow load.

One non-negotiable gotcha: <cite index="4-41">this tent does not come seam-sealed, meaning you have to do the work before taking it out for the first time if you want it to be fully waterproof.</cite> <cite index="19-28,19-29">You can do it yourself with an ~$8 kit Tarptent sells, or pay them around $35 to do it for you.</cite> Factor that in before the first trip — skipping it is not an option.

### Setup and Teardown

<cite index="3-1,3-2">
  Setup and takedown are fast and easy with just one central pole and no
  separate rainfly to wrestle with. Three minutes is a realistic average if you
  can push the stakes in without a mallet.
</cite>
<cite index="2-13,2-14">
  The freestanding conversion is one of the tent's standout features: by
  attaching a pair of trekking poles to the four staking points, the combination
  of the trekking pole structure and the single tent pole creates a freestanding
  setup, requiring only the doors to be staked out.
</cite>
<cite index="2-15">
  This is particularly useful for those who frequently camp in areas with poor
  staking options or on the wooden platforms common on the Appalachian Trail.
</cite>

Teardown has one quirk worth knowing. <cite index="2-27,2-28,2-29">Taking the tent down can be a bit cumbersome since it's not as easy to pull the single pole out as it is to slide it in. The pole sections separate under tension, so bunching up the fabric and pushing the pole through tends to work best. It's not hard, but it takes some time.</cite>

### Condensation and Ventilation

Single-wall shelters condense moisture — this one included. <cite index="3-26,3-27,3-28">Condensation affects this tent, but no more so than most other single-wall designs. Where you pitch it makes a significant difference; staying away from water, lush greenery, and low-lying depressions keeps condensation to a minimum.</cite> Tarptent has built in multiple ventilation pathways: <cite index="4-28,4-29,4-30">there is a small vent above each vestibule, each fly zipper can be opened from the top and propped open, and the most effective way to maximize ventilation is to open the fly completely.</cite>

<cite index="6-5">
  For cold or damp conditions, Tarptent offers a clip-in liner for around $30
  that adds about 4 oz and acts as an "almost" second wall, helping to regulate
  temperature and keep moisture at bay.
</cite>
<cite index="4-20">
  If you install the optional condensation-resistant liner, it removes a couple
  of inches of functional headroom
</cite>
— a worthwhile trade for shoulder-season camping.

### Materials and Durability

<cite index="14-17,14-18">
  Much of the Double Rainbow's durability comes from its high-quality materials.
  The 20D silpoly fly resists UV degradation better than classic silnylon, while
  the 30D silnylon floor offers exceptional puncture and abrasion resistance.
</cite>
<cite index="14-16">
  The Double Rainbow has long been a favorite on trails such as the Appalachian
  and Pacific Crest, capable of lasting through a full thru-hike with proper
  care.
</cite>

The included stakes are the one weak link in the package. <cite index="1-28,1-29">Tarptent provides Easton Aluminum Nano stakes that look like nails — many reviewers found them insufficiently durable and switched to MSR Groundhog stakes for better holding power.</cite> Upgrade the stakes before your first big trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely roomy for its weight class — 88-inch rectangular floor fits tall hikers up to 6'6"
- Dual side doors and dual vestibules: real convenience for two-person use
- Impressively weatherproof fly and floor, both rated 5000mm+ HH
- Fly-first pitch keeps the interior dry during setup in rain
- Optional freestanding mode with trekking poles is a legitimate feature, not a gimmick
- Porch mode adds covered cooking space
- 20 years of iterative design refinement — the 2026 version adds bigger vents, more tie-outs, and fully magnetic door closures
- Proven multi-month thru-hike durability
- Affordable repair service from Tarptent

**Cons**

- Ships without seam sealing — mandatory homework before first use
- Single-wall condensation is real; the liner fix costs extra weight and money
- Included Easton Nano stakes are fragile — expect to swap them
- Pole extraction at teardown requires a specific technique that takes practice
- At 50 inches wide, two adults with 25-inch pads have very little margin
- Freestanding mode requires trekking poles at least 55 inches long
- <cite index="9-8,9-9">
    One vestibule door faces the opposite direction from the other, meaning you
    can't rotate the tent to orient both openings away from wind simultaneously
  </cite>
  — a quirk that trips up some users

## Who Should Buy This

The Double Rainbow is the right call for thru-hikers and three-season backpackers who prioritize livable volume and proven storm resistance over chasing minimum weight. <cite index="14-23,14-24">Non-trekking-pole users benefit especially — if you hike without poles and adventure with another person, this is one of the best options available.</cite> It's equally solid as a solo shelter for someone who wants the extra room, and it punches well above its price point compared to double-wall options at twice the cost. Hikers over 6 feet tall who've been frustrated by cramped ultralight tents will find it particularly liberating.

## Verdict

The Tarptent Double Rainbow has earned its flagship status the honest way — through two decades of field use on some of the world's longest trails. It's not the lightest 2-person shelter available, and you'll need to seal the seams yourself before it's truly ready, but those are minor levies against a tent that excels at the things that matter most: interior space, weather protection, and long-term durability. At its price point, it remains one of the strongest value propositions in the cottage gear market.

**Rating: 8.5 / 10**
