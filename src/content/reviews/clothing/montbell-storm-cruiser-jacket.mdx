## Overview

The Storm Cruiser is Montbell's flagship hardshell: a 3-layer GORE-TEX C-Knit jacket that adds pit zips, big hand pockets and a three-way adjustable hood to a package that weighs 284 g (10 oz). It's built for backpackers and mountaineers who want real storm protection without the stiff, crinkly feel of a traditional hardshell. The trade-off is weight: it is a full-featured shell, not a 6-ounce emergency layer.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The Storm Cruiser's main job is keeping water out, and reviewers say it does. Halfway Anywhere's tester wore it as his only rain gear through a week of near-constant rain on Japan's Alps traverse and scored its waterproofing a perfect 5 out of 5. A TGO Challenge walker who used it in Scottish downpours reached the same conclusion, noting the 20-denier face is lighter than most 3-layer GORE-TEX fabrics and, in his view, well ahead of Paclite-style constructions. That reviewer cites a hydrostatic head of 50,000+ mm for the fabric.

**Construction.** Montbell cuts the body using its K-Mono Cut pattern, which uses fewer pieces of fabric and therefore fewer seams. Fewer seams mean fewer places to leak, and they also trim weight and packed volume. The hem is bonded rather than stitched so it doesn't hold water. Testers consistently single out the build quality, and the zippers in particular: Halfway Anywhere reported no leaks or snags.

**Feel and breathability.** The C-Knit backer is the other selling point. Backpacker's testers noted that it makes the jacket softer and more pliable than most hardshells, with none of the plasticky cling that makes some shells miserable against bare arms. Breathability reviews are favorable but not glowing. Backpacker's early testers called it good but not excellent, and its 2019 roundup rated it very good. The TGO walker said that after hard uphill efforts the interior got damp but cleared quickly once the pace eased. Pit zips are the real answer to a steep climb in warm rain. Halfway Anywhere rated ventilation just 3 out of 5, mostly because his Japanese-market jacket lacked them.

**Pockets.** This is where the Storm Cruiser pulls ahead of most ultralight shells. The hand pockets are large and start up near the armpit, so they sit above a pack hipbelt and stay reachable under load. Backpacker's testers said each one swallows a fly box. Halfway Anywhere scored the pockets 5 out of 5. There is no chest pocket, which at least keeps one more layer out of the way of moisture vapor.

**Hood.** The weak spot. The three-way adjustment gives a precise fit bare-headed, but the trio of drawcords is fiddly. Halfway Anywhere found it hard to keep cinched over a hat brim and gave the hood 3.5 out of 5.

**Pack size.** Backpacker described it as packing down to about the size of a large burrito, and a long hem covers the seat when bending over or sitting on wet ground.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Reliable waterproofing reported through multi-day, near-constant rain
- Soft, quiet C-Knit interior that feels better against skin than stiff 3-layer shells
- Big hand pockets that clear a hipbelt
- Pit zips on the US version
- Fewer seams thanks to K-Mono Cut; bonded hem; leak-free zippers
- Packs about as small as a burrito

**Cons**

- At 284 g it is roughly 117 g heavier than Montbell's own 5.9 oz Versalite
- Hood is fiddly to cinch over a hat brim
- Only the US version has pit zips; Japanese-market jackets do not
- $289 MSRP, and it is sold mostly through Montbell directly
- No chest pocket

## Who Should Buy This

The Storm Cruiser suits backpackers in wet, cold or changeable climates (the Pacific Northwest, Scotland, the Alps, a Northeast shoulder season) who want one jacket that works for hiking, scrambling and the odd alpine day. It also appeals to anyone who finds ultralight shells clammy and noisy. Gram-counters planning dry-climate thru-hikes, where a rain jacket mostly lives in the bottom of the pack, will be better served by the Versalite or a similar sub-200 g shell. Buyers should also confirm they are getting the US version with pit zips.

## Verdict

The Storm Cruiser gives up a few ounces to a true ultralight shell and returns real storm protection, a comfortable inner fabric and pockets that work with a pack. For hikers who expect to actually use their rain jacket, that is a good trade. A better hood would make it close to flawless.
