## Overview

The NEMO Forte 35 is a synthetic three-season sleeping bag cut in NEMO's "spoon" shape. It gives side sleepers room to bend their knees and shift their hips, which a mummy bag doesn't. It isn't an ultralight bag, and it costs less than a down bag with a similar rating. You pay for that price and comfort in ounces and pack volume.

## Performance

**Comfort and shape.** Comfort is why this bag exists. Backpacker measured the girth at 64 inches at the shoulder, 59 at the hip and 62 at the knee, which is looser at every point than a standard mummy. Backpackers.com's reviewers said the spoon cut gives much more knee room for side sleepers while trapping almost as much heat as a mummy. Forbes called the hood puffy and easy to cinch without feeling restrictive. It also noted an oversized draft collar and a draft tube along the zipper. Testers also describe the shell and liner as soft and not slick or plasticky, as many synthetic bags are.

**Warmth.** The 35°F rating is a threshold for cold sleepers to treat with caution. Backpacker's testers were comfortable at 40°F under the stars near Moab. They advised cold sleepers to add thick socks or down booties in the thirties, since the roomy footbox has a lot of air to heat. A SectionHiker reviewer who sleeps cold was consistently chilly from the low 30s into the low 40s, even with stacked pads, long underwear and a down jacket. Backpackers.com judged the rating fairly accurate for typical use. Treeline Review's tester found the bag right for summer and early fall in the Cascades and the Badlands. That tester sometimes added a liner as temperatures dropped. The practical range for most sleepers is roughly 40°F and up, with the 31°F lower limit reachable only with a warm pad and extra layers.

**Weight and packability.** The 960 g manufacturer weight is for the regular length. SectionHiker's scale read 984 g for the bag plus 82 g for the compression sack. That is fair for a synthetic bag at this rating and well short of what a 35°F down quilt or bag weighs. Reviewers find the stuffed size small for a synthetic. One Great Outdoors tester said she was surprised how small it compressed. Treeline's reviewer, though, measured 6.2 L compressed for a long Forte 35 and said ultralight backpackers may want something more compact. For a sub-10-pound base weight, the Forte is a luxury.

**Wet-weather and durability.** Synthetic fill keeps working when damp, which is the main practical reason to choose this over down in wet climates. A Great Outdoors tester noted it stays warm even when damp. SectionHiker adds the usual caveat: synthetic insulation loses loft with repeated compression and wears out faster than down. Treeline's tester saw no cold spots after a full season but stored the bag outside its compression sack to prevent them. Do the same.

**Zippers and venting.** The zipper layout is the most contested part of the design. The bag has vents and a full-length side zip, and Forbes praised the no-catch zippers and reinforced draft collar. Others disagree. A Great Outdoors tester found the full-length zip snaggy at times. On REI, owners rated the bag 2.6 out of 5 across nine reviews, and one headline complained that the zipper design ruins an otherwise great bag. Anyone who has fought a zipper in a dark tent should weigh that.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Spoon shape gives real knee and hip room for side sleepers
- Synthetic fill keeps insulating when damp
- Compression sack included, and the bag packs small for a synthetic
- Soft shell and liner, with a well-regarded hood and draft collar
- Costs far less than a comparable down bag

**Cons**
- Heavier and bulkier than down bags rated to 35°F
- Real-world warmth runs cooler than the rating for cold sleepers
- Zipper and vent design draws repeated complaints
- Synthetic insulation loses loft with repeated compression
- Roomy footbox is harder to keep warm near the lower limit

## Who Should Buy This

The Forte 35 suits side and back sleepers who hate the squeeze of a mummy bag and want a bag for summer and shoulder-season trips. It also works for the buyer moving up from car camping to backpacking who wants a bag that suits both. Wet-climate hikers who want insurance against damp will get value from it. Gram-counting thru-hikers, cold sleepers who expect to use the whole 35°F range, and anyone who changes position all night should look at a down quilt or a snugger down bag.

## Verdict

The Forte 35 does what it sets out to do: it gives side sleepers a roomy, comfortable synthetic bag at a modest price and a reasonable 960 g. Treat the rating as optimistic, expect to use it above 40°F without extra layers, and accept that the zipper may annoy. For comfort-first backpackers on a budget it is a solid buy. Ultralight purists should skip it.
