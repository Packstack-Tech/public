## Overview

The TOAKS Titanium Cup is the default answer to "what do I drink coffee out of?" on a lot of long trails: a single-wall, uncoated titanium vessel with folding wire handles and a mesh sack. It comes in two sizes, 375ml and 450ml, and suits solo ultralight backpackers who want a mug that can also boil water for a freeze-dried dinner. The trade-off is the usual one for thin bare titanium: it is light and fast to heat, and it gives that heat back to the air almost as quickly.

## Performance

**Heat transfer.** Thin titanium moves heat quickly, and owners of TOAKS cookware consistently report that water comes to a boil fast, which gets coffee or dinner started sooner. The same property works against anyone who likes to linger. Pour boiling water into the cup and the wall is too hot to hold, so the folding handles do the work. A long-term Trailspace owner summed up the other side of the deal: the cup cools quickly, and a cozy is close to mandatory for slow sippers.

**Real capacity.** The biggest recurring complaint concerns the number on the label. A long-term Trailspace user measured their 450ml cup and found it held closer to 420ml brim-full. The rolled lip makes it hard to carry a cup filled to the rim, so around 375–400ml is what it holds in practice. That is the gap between "enough" and "not quite" for a two-serving freeze-dried meal plus a hot drink. The 375ml cup is subject to the same rim-versus-usable math. Plan on a little less than the stamped figure.

**Durability.** Titanium doesn't rust, and the cup is a simple object with little to fail. But it is thin by design. In long-term testing of a TOAKS 750ml pot, which shares the same construction philosophy, hundreds of meals produced some warping of the base and overall round shape. A daily user of the 450ml cup, drinking 5 to 15 cups of coffee a day, reported that the handles went slightly wonky and no longer lined up perfectly. Other owners say the handles still pivot easily and stay firmly attached after years of use. Expect dings and a dull, heat-tinted finish, not failure.

**Cooking.** This is a boil-and-rehydrate vessel. Thin titanium heats unevenly over a flame, and reviewers of TOAKS cookware agree that real cooking invites scorched food unless the heat is low and the stirring constant. Boiling water for oatmeal, instant potatoes or a bagged meal is what it does well. Simmering a sauce is not.

**Packing.** Both sizes share a 79 mm internal diameter and an 87 mm profile with the handles folded, so they nest identically. The 375ml cup stacks inside TOAKS' 550ml pots and also swallows a 27 oz Klean Kanteen bottle. The 450ml cup fits inside the 750ml pot, where a 110 g fuel canister and a small stove can ride alongside it. For hikers already carrying TOAKS pots, that nesting is a real selling point. A cup, a fuel-canister holder and a packing cell in one are hard to beat at this weight.

## Choosing a Version

The two cups differ in height, not width. Both are 79 mm across inside, but the 375ml stands 75 mm tall and the 450ml stands 90 mm. The 14 g difference in weight buys 75 ml more capacity, the oz/ml graduation marks, and a taller, more pot-like shape.

**375ml (62 g).** This is the cup for drinking, not cooking. It is wide and squat, which cools coffee a little faster, and it has no measuring marks. It suits hikers who rehydrate meals in a bag or the pouch itself and only need a vessel for tea, coffee or cocoa. It's also the better choice if it needs to slot into a 550ml pot.

**450ml (76 g).** This is the one for hikers who want the cup to double as a cook pot. The graduations make measuring water for a meal easy, and the extra height gives headroom for boiling without the lip-level slosh. With real usable volume of roughly 375–400ml, it is the more realistic size for a freeze-dried dinner. It nests in the 750ml pot.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 62 g or 76 g, among the lighter true titanium mugs at these sizes
- Boils water quickly thanks to thin walls
- Graduated oz/ml marks on the 450ml make measuring water easy
- Nests with TOAKS 550ml and 750ml pots for a compact solo kit
- Uncoated titanium: nothing to flake, no taste, no rust
- Mesh sack included

**Cons**

- Usable capacity falls short of the stated number, especially on the 450ml
- Single wall means hot sides and fast-cooling drinks, so a cozy is a good idea
- Easy to scorch food if used for actual cooking
- Handles can loosen or misalign after heavy daily use
- No graduation marks on the 375ml

## Who Should Buy This

Solo ultralight backpackers and thru-hikers who want one cheap, light vessel for coffee and boiling water, especially those already carrying TOAKS pots that it nests inside. Choose the 375ml if the cup is purely for drinking and the 450ml if it has to cook. Hikers who cook two-person meals, or who nurse a hot drink for half an hour in the cold, should look at a larger pot or an insulated double-wall mug instead.

## Verdict

The TOAKS Titanium Cup is light, quick to boil and easy to pack, and it asks little of a pack or a wallet. Its stated capacity runs optimistic and its drinks cool fast, but it is a dependable no-frills piece of kit. The 450ml is the better all-rounder; the 375ml is the one for minimalists. Rating: 7.5/10.
