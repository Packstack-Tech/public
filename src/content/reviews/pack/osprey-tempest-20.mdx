## Overview

The Tempest 20 is Osprey's women-specific take on its Talon daypack: a panel-loading 20-liter pack with a ventilated back panel, a wrap-around hipbelt and an adjustable torso. It is built for day hikes, peak-bagging, biking and commuting, not for gram-counting. At 1,016 g, it is a comfortable, feature-rich pack that carries a lot of structure for a bag this size.

## Performance

**Comfort and fit.** Comfort is where the Tempest earns its reputation. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers counted it among the comfiest packs in their women's daypack comparison, and the Velcro-adjustable torso length is a large part of why. SectionHiker's review points to the shoulder straps, which curve around the chest rather than flattening it, and to a hipbelt that wraps the hips without turning into a bulky belt. A reviewer at road.cc, testing the pack as a commuter, found the harness sat correctly without any help from the sternum strap. REI buyers describe the same effect: weight moves off the shoulders and onto the hips, and the lower back stays comfortable even on long days.

**Ventilation and structure.** The AirScape back panel and injection-molded framesheet hold the load close to the body and keep it from sagging. Owners say the pack sits high and stable on technical ground. Osprey rates the pack for up to 20 lb, which covers food, water, a shell and an insulated layer for a full day. It is not meant for much more than that, and Outdoor Gear Lab's advice for anyone hauling heavy gear is to step up to a larger pack with more support.

**Organization.** This is a pack with a lot of straps, pockets and attachment points. The front stretch-mesh pocket has a clip to keep its contents in, a LidLock helmet attachment makes it a natural for commuting by bike, and a blinkie light loop and padded laptop sleeve are included. Reviewers found the features easy to use, and the build quality scored well. The outer ripstop nylon sheds light rain, but there is no built-in rain cover, so a wet forecast calls for a pack liner.

**Where it falls short.** Capacity and pocket sizing draw the most complaints. Several REI reviewers say the top pocket and side pockets are small. The hydration reservoir is the most common frustration: one of the most helpful critical reviews on REI describes a major struggle to get a reservoir into the sleeve, and another reviewer noted that the magnetic hose clip on the chest strap is gone from the current version. Taller women and those with larger frames report a snug fit. The 20-liter volume also gets cramped when winter layers are added.

**Weight.** At 1,016 g, the Tempest is not an ultralight pack. The framesheet, padded hipbelt and abundant hardware are what make it comfortable, and they also account for the weight. Thru-hikers building a sub-5-pound base weight would pick a frameless pack at less than half this weight for resupply-day or town use.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes differ in torso fit and volume. The XS/S holds 18 L and is cut for shorter, narrower frames. The M/L holds the full 20 L and measures 20.08 x 10.63 x 9.06 in. Both have the same adjustable harness, so the torso adjustment can fine-tune the fit within each size. Shorter-torsoed hikers should start with the XS/S, which also suits kids and teens who want an adult-quality pack. Most other users should buy the M/L, which has the extra 2 liters of space that this pack's critics want more of.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Women-specific harness and hipbelt that owners and testers rate as very comfortable
- Torso-adjustable harness extends the range of fit within each size
- AirScape back panel and framesheet keep loads stable and ventilated
- Well-organized, with a helmet attachment, laptop sleeve and stretch front pocket
- Durable construction, backed by Osprey's lifetime guarantee

**Cons**

- At 1,016 g, it is heavy for the ultralight crowd
- Hydration sleeve is tight, and the hose-clip magnet has been dropped
- Small pockets and limited capacity, especially in the XS/S
- No built-in rain cover
- Snug on taller or larger-framed users

## Who Should Buy This

The Tempest 20 suits women who day-hike, scramble, bike or commute and want one pack to do all of it with real support. It fits best when the load is a day's worth of food, water and layers, and when carrying comfort on the hip matters more than ounces. It's a poor choice for ultralight hikers who want to minimize weight, for anyone who needs a lot of room for winter layers, and for hikers who depend on a hydration reservoir and have little patience for a fiddly sleeve.

## Verdict

The Tempest 20 is a well-built, comfortable and thoughtfully organized daypack, held back by its weight and a few small design annoyances. For women who put fit and carrying comfort first, it is an easy recommendation. Ultralight hikers should look to a lighter frameless pack.
