## Overview

The Waratah is the quilt that launched Australian brand Neve Gear (pronounced "Neeve"), and it remains the company's do-it-all three- and four-season option. It pairs 850FP RDS-certified duck down with box baffles, a draft collar and a zippered footbox that lets it switch between sleeping bag and bed blanket. The trade-off is weight: at 800 g it is built for warmth and versatility rather than for shaving the last few ounces.

## Performance

**Warmth and down distribution.** Neve Gear overfills some chambers and underfills others on purpose. Reviewers note extra down in the foot area, overstuffed torso baffles and a down-filled collar, all of it aimed at putting insulation where a sleeper loses heat. SectionHiker's testers describe the baffle layout as vertical over the torso and horizontal over the footbox, a design meant to limit down shifting. Owners using the -8°C model in Canberra's cold and across Atlantic Canada in spring report it kept them comfortable, and the colder rating is the one most reviewers chose.

**The pad attachment system.** This is the quilt's signature feature, and the reason it appears in review roundups. Most quilts put their pad straps on the outer edges, where they can pull the sides up and let air in. The Waratah puts the buckles on interior baffles set in from the edges. The result is that the outer flaps lie flat and block drafts, and the quilt can still be cinched tight at the top. SectionHiker flagged the arrangement as the thing that sets the Waratah apart from other quilts. The cost is that the two outer baffles carry slightly less down, so Neve Gear concentrates fill over the torso and footbox. One reviewer admitted the system was confusing at first, but another praised how easy the setup was. A motorcycle-touring reviewer found it simpler than the strap arrangements on competing quilts.

**Footbox and versatility.** The footbox has a 50 cm zipper and drawcord. Left open, the quilt works as a blanket for restless sleepers or warm nights. Cinched shut, it behaves like a sleeping bag on cold ones. A guiding company that logged more than 250 nights on the Waratah Pro, the 950FP sibling, in the Australian Alps singled out the cinchable footbox as a temperature-regulation tool. The draft collar is designed to seal at the neck and give a tucked-in feel. Side, stomach and back sleepers all get room, according to the manufacturer.

**Packability and shell.** Neve Gear lists 4 L compressed, and reviewers say it packs down well in both a hiking pack and a motorcycle saddlebag. The 10D nylon taffeta has a DWR finish to shed light moisture, and owners describe it as soft against the skin. Neve Gear's redesigned pattern removes dead space and uses a differential cut, with the outer shell cut larger than the inner, so the loft isn't compressed. The current Waratah uses a 10D, 24 gsm fabric, which is sturdier than the 7D shell on the Pro and on the company's lighter summer quilt.

**Value.** Neve Gear sells direct, and reviewers repeatedly call the pricing affordable for a down quilt with this feature list. SectionHiker described it as quite affordably priced, and one adventure outfit called its Pro sibling the best-value high-performance quilt on the market.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Interior-baffle pad straps keep the quilt edges flat and block side drafts
- Box baffles with area-specific fill put the down over the torso and feet
- Zippered, cinchable footbox covers everything from summer blanket to cold-night enclosure
- RDS-certified down, with a 950FP goose upgrade
- Packs to about 4 L
- Priced below many cottage-brand competitors

**Cons**

- At 800 g, it is heavier than the 7D-shell Waratah Pro and the brand's own sub-500 g summer quilt
- The pad system is unconventional and takes a night or two to understand
- Outer baffles carry less fill, so the quilt's edges are the thinnest part
- Sold direct from Australia, so availability and delivery times depend on the stock cycle

## Who Should Buy This

The Waratah suits the three-season and shoulder-season backpacker who wants one quilt for a wide range of conditions. It also suits side sleepers who get drafts with conventionally strapped quilts, and anyone who values a footbox that can open up on warm nights. Buyers with the budget who want the lowest possible weight, or who spend most nights in summer conditions, should look at the Waratah Pro or the UL Summer Quilt. Those chasing true winter-only warmth should look at a dedicated cold-weather bag.

## Verdict

The Waratah is a well-thought-out quilt whose draft-blocking pad attachment and zippered footbox solve problems that cheaper quilts ignore, and it does so at a fair price. It is not the lightest quilt in its class, but its warmth, comfort and adjustability make it an easy recommendation for the all-around backpacker.
