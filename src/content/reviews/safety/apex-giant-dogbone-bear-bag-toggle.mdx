## Overview

The Dogbone is a 3-inch aluminum rod, shaped like its namesake, that stands in for the pencil-thick stick hikers traditionally scavenge for the PCT bear hang. It is built for thru-hikers and section hikers who hang food and want to skip the clove hitch. The trade-off is that a $6.88 piece of metal does exactly one job, and it only matters if hanging is part of your food-storage plan.

## Performance

The PCT method is a single-line hang. A carabiner clips to the bag, the line goes over a branch, and the bag is hoisted until it reaches the limb. A toggle then goes on the line, and the bag is lowered until the toggle catches against the carabiner. Because the system has no ground tie-off, a bear has no anchor line to slash. The usual target is a bag roughly 10 to 12 feet off the ground and about 4 feet out from the trunk.

The traditional toggle is a found stick tied on with a clove hitch, often with one hand while the other holds the line taut. Apex Giant's pitch is that the Dogbone removes the knot. The rod stores on the carabiner when not in use, so it is never lost in a pack pocket or left behind at the last campsite. Because it is a machined, pre-shaped piece, it also lets go of the line cleanly in the morning. A twig tends to bind and sometimes snaps.

Owners focus on convenience and weight. Buyers on the maker's own store describe it as light and well designed. One said he had planned to fabricate his own, then decided the time and effort weren't worth it. Others call it a simple way to finish a hang at the end of a long day without having to remember a knot. Ratings are uniformly high: the Hilltop Packs store, Apex Giant's sister company, shows 44 reviews with no negatives. Garage Grown Gear's listing averages 4.17 of 5 across six reviews, and the Etsy listing shows 5.0 across six. These are retailer reviews rather than long-term field tests, so they say more about satisfaction and build quality than about extended trail abuse. Still, a solid aluminum rod has few ways to fail.

At 5.4 g, the Dogbone adds almost nothing. That is about the weight of a few paperclips. It is heavier than the 2.5 g small version, which Apex Giant recommends for micro carabiners. The large is the right call for everything else, because the toggle has to be long enough to jam against the gate-side opening rather than slip through. A free stick weighs nothing and costs nothing, but it has to be found, sized and often trimmed, usually at dusk.

Two limits are worth stating plainly. First, cord and carabiner are not included, so the Dogbone is a component, not a kit. Second, a hang is not a canister. In regions where land managers require hard-sided canisters, or where bears are known to defeat hangs, no toggle makes the method acceptable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 5.4 g, light enough to leave clipped to the carabiner permanently
- Removes the clove hitch from the end-of-day routine
- Solid machined aluminum with a lifetime warranty
- Made in the USA, at a price under $7
- Consistently positive owner feedback

**Cons**

- Single-purpose, with no use outside the PCT hang method
- Cord and carabiner are sold separately
- Heavier than the small version, and heavier than a free stick, which costs nothing
- Useless where canisters are required

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who already use the PCT method, or who want to start, and who are tired of fumbling with sticks in headlamp light. It suits hikers carrying a standard-sized carabiner, which is the case for most. It is a poor purchase for anyone hiking in canister-required areas, for hikers who prefer a bear bag-free system (a canister or an Ursack), and for hikers who enjoy the found-stick approach and carry the knot in their head.

## Verdict

The Dogbone does one small task well, at a weight and price that make it an easy add to any hanging kit. Buy it if you hang food on trails where hanging is legal and effective. Skip it if you carry a canister.
