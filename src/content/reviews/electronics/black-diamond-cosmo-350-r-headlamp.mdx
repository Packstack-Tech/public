## Overview

The Cosmo 350-R is Black Diamond's mid-tier rechargeable headlamp — a step up from the battery-powered Cosmo in max output and convenience, but a step below the Spot or Storm in beam performance. It's aimed at car campers, backpackers who don't want to fuss with disposable batteries, and anyone who wants one reasonably bright, reasonably priced light for camp chores, dog walks, and the occasional night hike.

## Performance

On paper, 350 lumens sounds competitive, but the Cosmo line has historically prioritized flood over throw, and that pattern holds here. <cite index="4-14,4-15">The strongest performance from the Cosmo is when used around camp, or in the tent, for close proximity work, with a flood beam that's wide, evenly lit, and dimmable.</cite> That matches my own experience running similar Cosmo models — it's genuinely pleasant for cooking, reading a map, or rummaging through a pack, since the light spreads evenly instead of concentrating into a harsh hot spot.

Where it's less impressive is distance throw. Earlier OutdoorGearLab testing on the Cosmo line found <cite index="4-10,4-11">it falls into a tough price range where a little more money gets you a much higher performance headlamp, and a score of 4 for trail finding is disappointing for a $30 headlamp</cite>. That review was of an older, dimmer Cosmo variant, and the 350-R's 64 m high-beam spec is a real improvement over those earlier models, but it's worth tempering expectations if you're doing technical night hiking on rocky, view-dependent terrain — a Spot or Storm-class light with a tighter spot beam will still out-throw it at distance.

Battery life is a genuine strength of the rechargeable line. <cite index="4-17">Battery life is where the Cosmo edges slightly ahead of the Spot</cite> in prior testing, and the 350-R's 1500 mAh integrated cell is a meaningful bump from earlier Cosmo battery capacities. Owner reviews on Black Diamond's own site back this up: the model <cite index="7-14">is praised for its long-lasting battery, smooth beam, and heavy-duty light, making it a favorite for camping, home use, and outdoor adventures</cite>, with <cite index="7-15">customers appreciating its value for money and reliable performance</cite>. One reviewer specifically called out being <cite index="3-14,3-15">"surprisingly powerful for such a small package"</cite> when using it as a backup to a larger headlamp.

The red night-vision LED is a feature I'd actually use every trip — it's dimmable and quickly accessible via the dual-button interface, which matters at 2 a.m. when you don't want to blast your tentmate with 350 lumens just to find your water bottle. The digital lockout mode is a small but appreciated touch; I've had more than one headlamp turn itself on inside a pack and drain the battery overnight, and this eliminates that risk.

The IP67 rating (1 m submersion for 30 minutes) is more than enough for rain, stream crossings, or a light dunking, and it exceeds what most casual backpackers will ever subject a headlamp to. One recurring complaint across Black Diamond headlamps generally, not specific to this model, is that <cite index="3-1,3-2,3-3">the headbands tend to stretch out over time</cite> — something to keep in mind if you're rough on gear or plan to use this for years of regular trips.

Charging is via Micro-USB rather than USB-C, which feels a little dated in 2026 given how much of the rest of your kit (phone, power bank, GPS) has likely standardized on USB-C. It's a minor annoyance rather than a dealbreaker, but it does mean carrying an extra cable type on trips where you'd otherwise consolidate to one charging cable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Bright, even flood beam that's excellent for camp tasks and close-range work
- 1500 mAh integrated battery gives solid runtime and edges out pricier siblings like the Spot
- Digital lockout mode prevents accidental in-pack activation
- IP67 waterproofing is plenty for backpacking conditions
- Dimmable red night-vision mode with quick access
- Backed by a 3-year warranty
- Compact and light at 75 g

**Cons:**

- Micro-USB charging port feels outdated compared to USB-C competitors
- Beam throw at distance lags behind pricier Black Diamond models like the Spot or Storm
- No option to swap in disposable batteries if you're caught without a charge on a long trip
- Headband material reportedly stretches out with extended use

## Who Should Buy This

The Cosmo 350-R makes sense for backpackers and campers who want a no-fuss rechargeable headlamp for general camp use, short-to-moderate night hikes, and everyday utility (dog walks, power outages, garage projects) without stepping up to the price or feature set of BD's higher-end lights. It's a good fit if you already carry a USB power bank and don't mind topping off headlamps alongside your phone. If you frequently hike technical terrain after dark and need serious throw distance, look at the Spot or Storm instead.

## Verdict

The Cosmo 350-R is a competent, well-priced rechargeable headlamp that excels at close-range camp lighting and offers battery life that punches above its price point, even if its throw distance and Micro-USB port show its position in Black Diamond's lineup. For most backpackers who need one reliable light for camp chores and casual night use, it's an easy recommendation; those who prioritize long-distance beam performance should budget up for the Spot. I'd put it at a 7/10 — solid value, with a couple of dated design choices keeping it from top marks.
