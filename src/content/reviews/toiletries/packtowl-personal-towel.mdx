## Overview

The Personal is PackTowl's best-selling camp and travel towel: a looped microfiber that feels closer to a bath towel than to a chamois, built from 50% recycled fabric and treated with Polygiene odor control. It suits backpackers who want something they can actually dry off with after a swim or a cold-water rinse. The trade-off is weight. It is a heavier, softer towel than PackTowl's own thinner UltraLite line, and ultralight purists will notice.

## Performance

**Absorbency and wringing.** The looped, terry-style knit is the Personal's defining trait. A hands-on tester who measured the Body size recorded absorbency of about 3.3 times the towel's own weight, essentially level with a cotton towel in the same test. The difference showed up after wringing. The microfiber held about 37% of its absorbed water, against 46% for cotton. That gap matters on trail. A towel that gives up its water is a towel that is usable twice in one day.

**Drying.** PackTowl claims the fabric dries 70% faster than cotton, and reviewers who have used it find that believable, with the usual caveat that climate decides the outcome. The same Body-size test logged a dry time of 24 minutes, one of the faster results in that tester's roundup. Packhacker, which scored the Personal 7.6 out of 10, made a similar point: it will dry faster in the Arizona desert than in Bali's rainy season. Clipped to the outside of a pack, it generally gets there.

**Odor.** This is the Personal's strongest suit. The Polygiene treatment earned a top odor score in the hands-on test, and owners report that the towel doesn't turn sour even when stuffed away damp. For a multi-day hike without a laundromat, that matters more than any spec on the tag.

**Feel and sand.** The towel is soft enough to wipe off glasses after a rainstorm, and the hand-size version is reported to dry a person from head to foot after a full soaking. The weave also sheds grit well. Beach-goers note that sand doesn't cling, and the hands-on tester gave it high marks for shedding grass and debris. The one recurring quirk is dye: color can bleed over the first two or three washes, so wash it away from light-colored gear.

**Pouch and hardware.** The zippered pouch is half mesh, so a damp towel can breathe. It is an awkward size, though. Packhacker found the easiest way to fit the towel inside was to fold it in thirds, then in half. The carabiner clip feels cheap, but it only has to carry a towel. The snap hanging loop on the towel itself is the more useful attachment point at camp.

**Versus the lighter options.** PackTowl's UltraLite line has been listed at about 40 g for a hand-size towel and about 150 g for a beach-size one, roughly half the Personal's weight. Those towels are thinner and feel it. The Personal gives up grams for comfort and a more cotton-like hand.

## Choosing a Version

Only the size changes. Material, odor treatment and hardware are the same across the range. The Personal also comes in Face (10 x 14 in, about 20 g) and Body (25 x 54 in, about 180 g) sizes, which fall between the two covered here.

- **Hand (80 g, 42 x 92 cm):** The right size for most backpackers. Owners say it covers a full post-swim dry-off, and at under 3 oz it is easy to justify in a thru-hiker's kit. It is long enough to drape over the shoulders but too narrow to wrap around the waist. Anyone who wants to change out of wet shorts discreetly should step up a size.
- **Beach (275 g, 91 x 150 cm):** A full-size towel that is at its best on road trips, at a campground, or on a shared trip where it serves as a picnic blanket or a groundsheet for sandy feet. At nearly 10 oz, it is heavy by backpacking standards. Packhacker noted that a large-size Personal stays compact, but a trail user who spends 275 g on a towel is probably carrying the wrong one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft, looped fabric that feels like a regular towel
- Polygiene control keeps it fresh between washes
- Wrings out better than cotton and dries quickly
- Sheds sand and dirt
- Built-in hanging loop and pouch; machine washable
- 50% recycled fabric; limited lifetime warranty

**Cons**

- Heavier than PackTowl's UltraLite line
- Absorbency per gram is no better than cotton
- Dye can bleed during the first few washes
- Pouch is an awkward fit, and the carabiner is flimsy
- Beach size is too heavy for most backpacking

## Who Should Buy This

The Hand size suits section hikers, thru-hikers and weekend backpackers who bathe in lakes and creeks, and who want a towel that feels decent against the skin and won't smell by day four. The Beach size suits car campers, travelers and paddlers who care about comfort over grams. Gram counters who only need to mop a face and hands, or who plan to wipe down with a bandana, should look to a thinner towel such as the UltraLite or a face-size option.

## Verdict

The Personal Hand is a sensible 80 g luxury: soft, odor-resistant and reliably quick to dry, which is what a camp towel should be. The Beach size is a fine travel and campground towel, but it doesn't belong in a trail pack. Buy the Hand for trail use, and look at the UltraLite line if weight is the only concern.
