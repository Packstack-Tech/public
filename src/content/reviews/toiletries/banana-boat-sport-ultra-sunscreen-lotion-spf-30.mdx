## Overview

Banana Boat Sport Ultra SPF 30 is a drugstore chemical-filter sunscreen lotion built for people who sweat for a living, or at least for a weekend. It carries a broad-spectrum SPF 30 rating, 80 minutes of sweat and water resistance, and a formula free of oxybenzone and octinoxate. The headline trade-off is logistical rather than chemical: the 8 fl oz bottle weighs 237 g, which is a lot of mass to carry for a product most hikers will use a fraction of.

## Performance

**Protection.** SPF 30 filters roughly 97 percent of burning UVB rays, which is adequate for most three-season hiking in moderate UV. Above treeline, on snow, or at altitude in summer, many dermatologists recommend stepping up to SPF 50 or higher, and Banana Boat sells Sport Ultra in higher SPFs for that purpose. The 80-minute water resistance rating is the standard maximum for the category: after a river crossing or a long sweaty climb, the clock effectively resets, and reapplication is on the hiker.

**Feel on skin.** This is where the Sport line earns its reputation. The current formula rubs in clear on all skin tones, an improvement over older versions that left a faint white cast. It absorbs quickly, reads as light and non-greasy, and is marketed as not running into the eyes. Retail reviewers give the 8 oz bottle strong marks: 4.6 stars across 243 ratings at Walmart and 4.3 stars across 176 at CVS. A smaller sample at Walgreens is less enthusiastic, at 3.2 stars across nine reviews. Users of the Sport face version describe it as light and not greasy, though some note it stings if it gets in the eyes, which is true of nearly every chemical sunscreen.

**Skin extras.** The lotion includes moisturizing ingredients, including aloe, vitamin E and ceramides, with a claim of 12-hour hydration. On multi-day trips where wind and sun strip skin, that is a minor bonus rather than a reason to buy.

**Environmental profile.** Because it omits oxybenzone and octinoxate, the formula is marketed as reef friendly, a meaningful point for anyone hiking coastal routes or swimming in marine environments where those ingredients are restricted. It is still a chemical-filter product, so hikers who want a mineral formula will need to look elsewhere.

**Packability.** The real issue for ultralight users is the container. At 237 g, the 8 oz bottle outweighs many people's entire first-aid kit. Banana Boat also sells the same formula in a 1 fl oz mini tube, which ultralight retailers have listed at around 1.27 oz (roughly 36 g) with packaging, and in 0.4 fl oz packets for day hikes. A retailer that stocks the mini tube pitches it as a weekend supply, or a week in some conditions. The practical approach for most hikers is to buy the big bottle for home use and the car, and decant a trip's worth into a small container.

**Price.** This is cheap sunscreen. Multi-packs of mini tubes run around $2 per tube, and the full-size bottle is widely available at big-box and drugstore chains. For a thru-hiker restocking in trail towns, that availability matters as much as the formula does.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**
- Rubs in clear and feels light and non-greasy
- 80 minutes of sweat and water resistance
- Free of oxybenzone and octinoxate
- Available almost everywhere, including trail-town grocery and drugstores
- Inexpensive, with the same formula sold in 1 oz tubes and packets

**Cons**
- The 237 g bottle is heavy and bulky for backpacking
- SPF 30 is thin cover for high-altitude, snow, or desert exposure
- Chemical filters, not a mineral option
- Requires reapplication at least every 80 minutes when sweating or wet
- Can sting if it runs into the eyes

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who want a comfortable, no-fuss sunscreen for sweaty three-season trips, and who are willing to decant into a small container or buy the mini tube, will be well served. It suits trail-town resupply, day hiking, and car camping, where weight is irrelevant and a pump of lotion is the main concern. Hikers headed above treeline for days, crossing snowfields, or walking in desert sun should choose a higher SPF. Anyone who prefers mineral filters should skip it, and so should anyone planning to carry the full bottle in the pack.

## Verdict

Banana Boat Sport Ultra SPF 30 is a pleasant, affordable sunscreen with a trail-friendly feel and a cleaner ingredient list than much of the drugstore aisle. The 237 g bottle, though, is better left at home or decanted into a small container. Buy it for the formula, and leave the full bottle behind.
