## Overview

The Helinox Ground Chair is a low-slung, four-legged camp chair built around a square DAC aluminum frame with oversized resin hubs at the corners. It is aimed at backpackers, paddlers, and beach-goers who want a real backrest in places where spindly chair feet would punch into the dirt. The trade-off is plain: at roughly 1 lb 7 oz, it weighs about as much as a tent, and it sits only a few inches off the ground.

## Performance

**Stability on soft ground.** This is the reason the chair exists. Most ultralight chairs stand on four narrow feet or a pair of skids, and they find the bottom of a sandy beach or a soggy meadow quickly. The Ground Chair spreads its load across a square footprint and large corner hubs. SectionHiker's review singles out that frame and hub design as what keeps it from sinking into soft or damp ground, a common failing among similar chairs. Helinox's own pitch is the same: low profile, wide base, balance in sand.

**Comfort.** Reviewers tend to be surprised by it. The seat is a supportive polyester sling with breathable mesh side panels, and the flexible frame gives slightly under load. The 16-inch backrest supports the lower and mid back but stops well short of the shoulders. Because the seat sits so close to the ground, the posture is a reclined, knees-up lounge rather than an upright dining-chair sit. One Trailspace reviewer, who carried it on England's Cumbria Way, credited it with saving him from backache at camp. A 6'4", 215-lb owner on a retailer page described it as solid and comfortable.

**Weight and packed size.** TrailGroove's scale put the chair at 22.5 oz: about 14 oz for the pole system, 7.7 oz for the seat, and 0.75 oz for the stuff sack. That is close to Helinox's number, and TrailGroove cautioned that some retailers list lower weights. The packed cylinder is about the size of a large water bottle, comparable in footprint to a folding foam sit pad, though a pound or so heavier, as SectionHiker notes. Trailspace's reviewer thought the weight reasonable for trips of four or five nights. Hikers counting every gram will see it differently, since the same reviewer noted that it also works as a replacement for sitting on a pack.

**Setup.** The poles and feet form a single shock-corded piece. Four ends point upward and slot into color-coded, reinforced pockets on the seat. Setup takes about a minute. The one catch, flagged by Trailspace: if the fabric isn't seated properly on the frame, the chair can collapse, so it pays to check all four corners before sitting down.

**Durability.** After a year of use, the Trailspace reviewer reported no fatigue in the frame or hubs and no wear on the seat fabric. The sewn pockets where the frame meets the sling are the obvious stress points, but they carry extra reinforcement and held up. Even so, he advised gentle treatment, such as lowering yourself into it instead of dropping in.

**Low height.** The low seat has one upside: you can stay seated while cooking on a stove at ground level. It also makes standing up harder, especially for taller people and bad knees. Trailspace also noted limited stability on uneven ground, a reminder that a wide base helps with softness but not with slope.

**Swapping seats.** TrailGroove points out that the lighter Chair Zero seat fits the Ground Chair's pole system. That could save a few ounces, at the cost of the Ground Chair's mesh paneling and its specific seat geometry. It also means the frame is not a dead end if the fabric wears out.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Square frame and large hubs resist sinking in sand, mud, and grass
- Packs to 12" x 4.5" x 4.5", about the size of a water bottle
- 265 lb capacity, with owners over 6 ft reporting a solid feel
- Breathable mesh side panels for hot weather
- Quick, tool-free setup with a shock-corded frame
- DAC poles and a five-year warranty

**Cons**

- About 1 lb 7 oz, which is heavy for an ultralight base weight
- Seat only a few inches off the ground; hard to get out of
- Short backrest gives no shoulder or head support
- Can collapse if the seat isn't fully engaged on the frame
- Priced around $140 at launch-era listings, high for a seat

## Who Should Buy This

The Ground Chair suits backpackers on short or moderate trips who camp on sand, soft forest floors, or damp ground, and who value a real backrest at the end of the day. Kayakers, canoeists, bike tourists, and beach campers will get the most from it, since weight matters less than packability there. Sub-10-lb base weight hikers and long-distance thru-hikers should skip it. Taller hikers and anyone with knee or hip trouble should pick a higher chair like the Chair Zero, which sits upright and weighs less.

## Verdict

The Ground Chair does one thing better than its lighter siblings: it stays on top of soft ground while giving real back support. That is worth 23 ounces on a weekend trip to a sandy shoreline. For a long-distance hike, the weight and low seat are hard to justify.
