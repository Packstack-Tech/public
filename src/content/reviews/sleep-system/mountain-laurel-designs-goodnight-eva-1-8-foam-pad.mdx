## Overview

The GoodNight EVA is a sheet of 3.1 mm Evazote closed-cell foam, cut in sizes from a 20" x 40" torso piece to a longer standard cut. It is made in the USA by Mountain Laurel Designs and priced from $28 to $59, depending on size. It is a utility layer more than a mattress: it protects an inflatable pad, adds a little warmth, and keeps the pad from skating around the tent floor. The trade-off is that at 1/8" thick it offers almost no cushioning on its own.

## Performance

**As an under-pad.** This is where the GoodNight earns its place. A thin foam sheet beneath an ultralight inflatable shields the pad's fabric from sticks, grit and the occasional thorn, and it adds a small bump in insulation. MLD rates the foam at R 0.4 and describes that figure as conservative. Body weight and the rest of the sleep system change the real-world number, so treat it as a shoulder-season nudge rather than a way to turn a summer pad into a winter one. Backpacking Light forum members who run thin EVA or similar 1/8" foam under an inflatable describe the same benefits: puncture protection, a bit of extra warmth, less sliding, and a fallback if the air pad fails. One user has run a comparable 1/8" pad this way since 2016 with no regrets.

**Grip.** The foam has a tacky surface. Under a slick nylon pad or a silnylon bivy, it keeps things in place on sloped ground or a slippery floor. One Reddit user who liked the pad noted that it clung to clothing at first, a quirk that faded as the foam wore in. A forum member who compared it with Gossamer Gear's Thinlight found the MLD sheet a little less smooth in finish.

**As a standalone pad.** Opinions split sharply. A Reddit user found it far more comfortable than two other closed-cell pads they had tried, crediting the thin, lower-density foam. A self-described closed-cell believer tried one night and gave up. Another owner bought the largest size and folded it four times into a thick torso pad, which was the only way they could use it alone. At 3 mm, the pad is a ground sheet with ambitions. Side sleepers on hard dirt will feel every root and rock.

**In a hammock.** One verified owner used it in a double-layer hammock in 40-degree-range temperatures, on top of a loosely adjusted underquilt. The pad eliminated the cold spots the underquilt left behind. The same owner noted that the thin foam is a bit floppy and takes some fiddling to position.

**Durability and versatility.** MLD uses Evazote rather than the cheaper Plastazote, which it says is tougher and more resilient. Backpacking Light participants make the same distinction: Plastazote is lighter and warmer per ounce but compresses quickly and falls apart at thin gauges. The 1/8" Evazote is not rugged enough for rough use as a sit pad, according to one owner, but at this weight, wear is a cheap problem to have. MLD also suggests using it as a ground cloth, a gear-sorting surface, or folded as a virtual frame in a frameless pack. The 20 x 80" size rolls to a diameter of about 3.5 inches.

## Choosing a Version

Only the cut changes: the foam, thickness and R-value are identical across versions.

- **20" x 40" (47 g):** A torso-length piece. It suits hikers who pair it with a short inflatable, put it under the hips and shoulders only, or want a pack frame sheet and sit pad for the lowest possible weight.
- **Standard (94 g):** The larger cut, for hikers who want full coverage under a regular-length inflatable. It is the better pick if the pad may need to serve as a backup mattress, since a torso piece leaves legs on the dirt.

Cutting down a bigger sheet is easy with scissors, so buying the larger size and trimming it is the lower-regret choice for anyone unsure.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 47–94 g for puncture protection, a grip layer and a backup pad in one
- Evazote foam is tougher than cheaper alternatives, according to MLD and owners
- Tacky surface keeps inflatables and bivies from sliding
- Available in a range of sizes, and routinely in stock
- Made in the USA, with a perfect rating from verified owners on MLD's site

**Cons**

- At R 0.4, the insulation boost is modest
- Too thin to be a comfortable standalone pad for most sleepers
- Floppy and awkward to position, with a tendency to cling to clothing at first
- Foam pads cannot be returned

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who sleeps on an ultralight inflatable and wants cheap insurance: thru-hikers who have lost nights to a punctured pad, hammock campers with underquilt cold spots, and bivy users tired of sliding downhill. It also suits people building a frameless pack setup who want a fold-flat back panel. Hikers hoping to leave the inflatable at home should look elsewhere, toward a thicker closed-cell pad like MLD's own 1/4" option or a standard accordion pad. Anyone who needs real cushioning for side sleeping will want air.

## Verdict

The GoodNight EVA 1/8" is a good accessory and a questionable mattress. For under $60 and 47–94 g, it adds durability, grip and a little warmth to an inflatable setup. Buy it for that job, and keep expectations low if the plan is to sleep on it alone.
