## Overview

The Merino Fresh is Decathlon's lightweight short-sleeve trekking tee, a 70 percent merino, 30 percent acrylic shirt built for people who want wool's odor control without paying wool's prices. It weighs 148 g in a men's large, which puts it among the lighter merino tees on the market. The trade-off is the blend: the acrylic helps with drying and cost, but this is not a pure-wool shirt, and it won't feel like one.

## Performance

**Weight and fabric.** At 155 g/m², the Merino Fresh sits in the lightweight tier of merino tees, the weight class thru-hikers typically wear as a single hiking shirt through summer. The 148 g measured in a size L is nearly identical to Decathlon's own MT500 short-sleeve (listed at 150 g in L with 160 g/m² fabric), and it is far lighter than the 222 g MT500 long-sleeve at 190 g/m². Next to that heavier shirt, this one is meant for warm and mild conditions, not for doubling as a cold-weather base layer.

**Odor control.** This is the reason to buy merino, and Decathlon positions the Fresh around it: the wool in the blend limits perspiration odor. Forclaz's merino shirts are tested to the ISO 17299-3 standard for odor, and owners of the MT500 line regularly describe wearing the shirt several days running without it turning ripe. With 30 percent acrylic in the mix, expect somewhat less of that effect than a 100 percent wool shirt delivers. On a week-long section, it will still outlast a polyester tee by a comfortable margin.

**Drying and moisture.** Wool absorbs moisture without feeling clammy, and the acrylic fraction helps the fabric shed it faster than a pure-merino tee of similar weight. For hikers who sweat through a shirt on a climb and need it dry by the time they hit camp, that is a practical advantage. It is not a mesh synthetic, so it will hold some dampness on a humid day.

**Comfort and fit.** Decathlon's merino is generally described by reviewers as comfortable and non-itchy, though a side-by-side with premium brands such as Icebreaker finds it less soft and with a looser weave. The Fresh follows the same pattern: good enough against the skin for all-day wear, without the silky hand of a $90 shirt. The cut is relaxed rather than snug, which suits a hiking tee worn under a pack's shoulder straps and hip belt, and it allows airflow in warm weather. Hikers who prefer a close, next-to-skin base layer may find it roomy.

**Durability.** Blending in synthetic fiber is the standard way to make merino last longer, and Forclaz uses polyamide-wrapped yarn across much of its wool range for abrasion resistance. The Fresh uses acrylic instead, so the durability gains come from the blend itself. Pack-strap and hip-belt rub zones are the usual failure points for any lightweight merino tee, and a 155 g/m² fabric will wear faster there than a heavier one. Machine-washing on a gentle cycle and air drying will help.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 148 g in a large; light for a merino tee
- Wool content limits odor over multi-day wear
- Acrylic blend speeds drying compared with pure merino
- Relaxed cut works well under pack straps and in heat
- Decathlon pricing undercuts most name-brand merino

**Cons**

- Only 70 percent merino; odor resistance and softness trail 100 percent wool shirts
- Less refined feel than premium merino
- Light fabric weight means limited warmth and less abrasion tolerance
- Relaxed fit is less suited to use as a snug base layer
- Acrylic is not a fiber many ultralight hikers choose for next-to-skin wear in the first place

## Who Should Buy This

Budget-minded hikers who want a lightweight merino tee for summer and shoulder-season trekking will get what they need here: low weight, decent odor control, and a price that makes a second shirt easy to justify. It suits travelers and section hikers who rotate one or two shirts and want them to stay presentable. Thru-hikers chasing the best possible wool-on-skin experience, or anyone who wants a fitted base layer, should look at a 100 percent merino or the MT500 short-sleeve, which carries a higher wool content at nearly the same weight.

## Verdict

The Merino Fresh is a light, inexpensive hiking tee that delivers most of the benefit of merino at a fraction of the cost, with the compromises you would expect from a 70/30 blend. It earns a place in the pack of hikers who value weight and price over wool purity.
