## Overview

The MH500 kitchen set is Decathlon's answer to the question of what to do with your hands once the water boils: a folding spatula, a folding ladle, a roll-up cutting board, a dish towel and a zip-up case, all sold together for roughly 10 to 13 euros depending on the market. It is built for people who actually cook on trail, not people who pour boiling water into a bag. The trade-off is plain: at 225 g, it is a lot of plastic for a kit that a dedicated ultralighter would replace with a spoon.

## Performance

**Packability.** The whole idea is the fold. The spatula and ladle collapse, the board rolls, and everything disappears into a cylinder about 9.5 cm across and 20 cm long. That is roughly the footprint of a large water bottle, which makes it an easy fit in a side pocket or alongside a pot in a bikepacking bag. The case doubles as a clean place to stow wet utensils, and since it is polyester like the towel, it dries quickly.

**Heat and cookware compatibility.** The spatula and ladle are molded from polyamide 6.6, a nylon that Decathlon rates to 150°C. That covers stirring a pot of pasta or flipping eggs in a pan, and the material will not scratch non-stick coatings, which matters for anyone carrying a coated frypan. It does not cover resting a spatula on a hot pot wall or a stove burner. Nylon softens, and a folding hinge is the first place that will show it.

**The utensils themselves.** Decathlon describes the spatula as wide, aimed at breakfast eggs. The ladle holds 50 ml, which is a serving-spoon volume rather than a soup-kitchen one. Filling a standard 450 ml mug takes about nine scoops, so the ladle is better for portioning a stew or stirring than for serving four people quickly. For one or two hikers it is adequate.

**The cutting board.** At 0.3 cm thick, the TPU board is flexible enough to roll and soft enough that Decathlon says it will not blunt knife edges. It is also the heaviest item in the kit by a wide margin: at 108 g, it accounts for nearly half the total weight. Whether that is worth carrying depends on how much you slice. Salami and cheese on a picnic, yes. A dehydrated-meal diet, no.

**The towel.** At 35 x 45 cm and 29 g, the towel is a pot-wiping and hand-drying cloth, not a packable camp towel for a group. It is polyester, so it absorbs well and dries fast, which is the main thing a trail towel has to do.

**Value and owner feedback.** Decathlon's own storefronts show a 4.5 out of 5 average across 133 customer reviews. At under 13 euros for five items, the price is hard to argue with, and the utensils are cheap enough to lose without grief.

**The weight problem.** Context is useful here. Decathlon's own stainless-steel spatula-and-fork barbecue kit weighs 160 g for two pieces. The MH500 set undercuts that per item, but it still adds up to 225 g of kit that does nothing a long-handled titanium spoon cannot do for boil-in-bag meals. For a base-weight-conscious thru-hiker, this is a luxury item.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Folding spatula and ladle plus a roll-up board in a single compact cylinder
- Safe on non-stick coatings, with a 150°C heat rating on the utensils
- Dishwasher-safe utensils and board; towel and case are machine washable at 40°C
- BPA-free and food-contact compliant
- Costs roughly 10–13 euros, with a 4.5/5 average across 133 customer reviews on Decathlon's storefront

**Cons**

- 225 g is heavy for solo lightweight backpacking
- The cutting board alone is 108 g
- The 50 ml ladle is slow for serving anything liquid
- Nylon hinges and bowls are not for resting on hot cookware

## Who Should Buy This

This is a kit for two kinds of people: hikers who cook real meals on multi-day trips and want something better than a spoon, and campers, bikepackers or families who care more about price and tidiness than grams. It also suits anyone building a first camp kitchen on a budget. Solo ultralighters, freezer-bag cooks and anyone counting every ounce should look elsewhere. A single long spoon covers their cooking.

## Verdict

The MH500 set does exactly what it claims: compact, non-stick safe, easy to clean and cheap. It is a good camp kitchen accessory and a poor ultralight one. Buy it for car camping, bikepacking or two-person trips with proper cooking, and leave it at home if your base weight is the goal.
