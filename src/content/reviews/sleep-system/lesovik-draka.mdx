## Overview

The DRAKA is Lesovik's flagship hammock — a complete sleep system built around a 330 × 150 cm ripstop nylon body, a fully removable mosquito net, and a knotless suspension setup, all bundled together rather than sold piecemeal. It's aimed at hammock campers who want a single, no-assembly-required package that works across three seasons, from bikepackers doing multi-week tours to backpackers who've already made the switch from ground sleeping and don't want to hunt down straps, a bug net, and a hammock body from three different brands.

## Performance

The headline number here is the 330 cm body length. <cite index="5-13,5-14">Lesovik extended the sleeping area to 330 cm, which they say is over 22% more than typical hammocks, and even people as tall as 210 cm should feel comfortable in it.</cite> That extra length matters more than it sounds — it's what lets you get a flatter, more diagonal lay without your feet or head crowding the end of the hammock, which is the main comfort lever in asymmetric hammock camping. The body is also symmetrical, so <cite index="5-15">you can lie in it in any direction, on either side</cite>, which is a small but genuinely useful detail if you like to switch up your lay throughout the night.

Fabric-wise, Lesovik uses <cite index="5-16">ripstop military grade nylon that resists damage from careless use, can withstand several hundred kilograms of weight, and is fully breathable</cite>. One long-term reviewer who put the DRAKA through a full year of use, including winter trips, noted <cite index="1-1">the material is pleasant to the touch and only lacks the velvety softness of Warbonnet's more expensive Dream-Tex fabric</cite> — a fair comparison point if you've used a US-made hammock before and are wondering how the DRAKA's fabric feels against your skin.

The mosquito net is the feature Lesovik leans on hardest, and it's a genuinely clever piece of engineering. <cite index="4-8,4-9">It's the element that distinguishes the DRAKA from competitors most — a custom zipper system developed specifically for the company.</cite> It doesn't rely on a separate hoop or ridgeline tensioners to stay off your face: <cite index="4-13,4-14">the net needs no extra anchor lines or separate hanging system — its cut and construction alone hold it in position.</cite> And because it's removable rather than sewn in, you're not stuck carrying bug protection on a cold shoulder-season trip when there's nothing biting: <cite index="4-12">you can remove the net in about half a minute and stash it in the included accessory bag.</cite> The mesh itself is dense enough to matter in bug season — <cite index="3-6">it's constructed with a tight weave and irregular pattern for maximum coverage, with over 700 holes per cm²</cite>.

The SNEKA suspension (branded SMUK in some of Lesovik's European listings) is a fully knotless setup: <cite index="3-9">two tree hugger webbing straps that avoid damaging trees, two ultralight carabiners, two Dyneema whoopie slings, and a ridgeline</cite>. This is the same basic architecture you'll find on higher-end American hammocks (think Warbonnet or Dutchware setups), and in practice it means fast pitching — no knots to fuss with in the dark or in the rain, and the whoopie slings let you dial in your hang angle in seconds. The included organizer (5 pockets) and elastic side guy-lines are nice touches for keeping a headlamp, phone, and water bottle within reach without them sliding to the bottom of the hammock overnight.

One year-round user reported taking the DRAKA on trips through <cite index="1-8">not just summer but winter as well</cite>, which speaks to the fabric's durability, though it's worth noting a bare hammock like this still needs an underquilt or pad for cold-weather insulation — Lesovik doesn't include one, and this is a hammock/net/suspension system rather than a full sleep system in the sleeping-bag-and-pad sense.

Where the DRAKA is a little thin is independent, English-language long-term testing. Most of what's out there comes from European retailers and a handful of bikepacking blogs rather than the usual suspects (r/hammockcamping, Outdoor Gear Lab, BPL) weighing in with side-by-side comparisons, so if you're used to cross-referencing five teardown reviews before buying, you'll have to do more legwork here than you would with a Warbonnet or ENO.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 330 cm body length accommodates users up to 205 cm with room for a proper diagonal lay
- Fully removable mosquito net with a dedicated zipper design — no extra poles or lines needed
- Knotless SNEKA suspension (tree straps, carabiners, Dyneema whoopie slings) sets up fast
- Complete system out of the box: hammock, net, suspension, organizer, and two stuff sacks
- Military-grade ripstop nylon rated for several hundred kilograms — durability shouldn't be a concern
- Made in Poland with direct manufacturer oversight of production

**Cons**

- No included underquilt or pad — you'll need to source insulation separately for cold weather
- Fabric feel is reportedly a step below premium options like Warbonnet's Dream-Tex
- Sparse independent, English-language long-term reviews compared to established US hammock brands
- At 725.5 g it's competitive but not class-leading against the lightest single-wall hammock bodies on the market

## Who Should Buy This

The DRAKA makes the most sense for hammock campers who want to buy one box and be done — bikepackers and backpackers doing multi-day trips through bug season who don't want to piece together a hammock, net, and suspension from three different vendors. It's also a solid pick if you're tall (up to 205 cm) and have struggled to get a flat lay in shorter hammocks. If you're chasing the absolute lightest possible hammock body and are willing to run a separate bug net only when needed, there are lighter cottage-industry options out there, but you'll be trading some of the DRAKA's plug-and-play convenience to get there.

## Verdict

The DRAKA delivers on its promise as a complete, well-thought-out hammock system — the extended 330 cm body and toolless-removable bug net are the standout features, backed by a knotless suspension that's genuinely fast to pitch. It's not the last word in ounce-counting, and you'll want to budget for an underquilt separately, but as an all-in-one package from a manufacturer that clearly sweats the details, it earns a solid 8/10.
