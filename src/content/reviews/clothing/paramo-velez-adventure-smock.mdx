## Overview

The Velez Adventure Smock is Páramo's pullover rain shell for high-output days in cold, wet weather. It swaps the usual waterproof-breathable membrane for Nikwax Analogy, a directional fabric system that pushes moisture away from the skin rather than trapping it inside. The trade-off is plain: at 720 g it carries nearly twice the weight of a typical ultralight hardshell, and it makes sense only for hikers who will actually wear it all day.

## Performance

**Wet-weather protection.** Analogy works as a two-layer system: a pump liner that moves liquid water away from the body, and an outer shell that sheds rain and blocks wind. Owners describe a jacket that handles most British hill weather without complaint. Páramo's own long-term reviewer, who has worn one for four years, says it will eventually let some moisture in during prolonged, extreme rain. He adds that the dampness fades quickly once the rain eases, because the system keeps pushing moisture out. Wicking layers underneath help it do that.

**Breathability and sweat management.** This is where the Velez earns its price. Climb GB's reviewers found the fabric softer and more comfortable than the membrane shells they were used to. They also found that it moves sweat away from the body well. Users of Páramo's lighter sibling report the same character: a little warmer than Gore-Tex, but much less clammy. For uphill grinds, cycling or scrambling in drizzle, that is a real advantage over a hardshell that turns into a sauna at the first steep pitch.

**Ventilation.** The twin front zips are the signature feature. Chris Townsend, writing in The Great Outdoors, notes that although the smock goes on over the head, its two long two-way zips open airflow across the lower chest and midriff better than most full-length jackets manage. Zipped arm vents and wide cuffs that push up the forearm add more options. The front zips also let a pack's hipbelt pass through, so venting does not have to stop when the pack goes on. Townsend adds that the zips can be cracked open to warm cold hands.

**Hood and pockets.** The wired peak keeps rain off the face without cutting into peripheral vision, and the hood fits snugly and accommodates a helmet. The large chest pouch holds a map, snacks or a phone, though the internal zipped pocket across the front sits under a hipbelt and is hard to reach with a loaded pack.

**Fit.** Reviews of the Velez line often mention a short, slightly boxy cut. A tester of the lighter version found the hem too short at the front of the waist, and a Backpacking Light forum poster compared the cut to a cycling jacket. That is fine for cyclists, but backpackers who like generous coverage over a hipbelt should try one on first.

**Weight and packability.** This is the weak point. Páramo itself calls the Velez one of its lighter jackets that is still heavy next to membrane shells, and says it is not for ultralighters. Its own long-term reviewer leaves it at home on warm, fair-weather multi-day trips. A smock that stays on the body from trailhead to camp is a different proposition from one that lives in a pack lid for the rare squall.

**Care.** The fabric is treated with water-based Nikwax chemistry, and Páramo says it makes the jacket without intentionally added PFAS. Owners accept some maintenance in return, and long-term users call the garments hardwearing and repairable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Directional fabric moves sweat well, so it feels less clammy than most membrane shells
- Twin two-way front zips and arm vents give unusually flexible ventilation
- Wired, helmet-compatible hood with good visibility
- Soft, quiet fabric that is comfortable against the skin
- Made without intentionally added PFAS, and described by owners as durable and repairable

**Cons**

- 720 g is heavy for a rain shell
- Warm, so it suits cold and cool conditions better than summer
- Short front hem on a boxy cut
- Pullover design makes layering changes slower than a full-zip jacket
- Needs periodic care to keep the treatment working
- £290 RRP

## Who Should Buy This

The Velez suits hikers, climbers and cyclists who work hard in cold, persistently wet climates. Think shoulder-season Scotland, the Lake District or Pacific Northwest winters, where a shell stays on for hours and sweat management matters more than grams. It also suits anyone who has grown tired of the damp-from-inside feeling of a membrane jacket. Ultralight backpackers counting every ounce, and anyone hiking in warm or arid conditions, should look at a conventional shell weighing a third as much.

## Verdict

The Velez Adventure Smock is a well-designed, comfortable and exceptionally well-ventilated rain layer. It rewards people who wear it constantly in cold, wet weather. It is the wrong choice for anyone who wants a featherweight emergency shell, and the weight keeps it out of most ultralight kits.
