## Overview

The Kryos is the warmest bag in Mountain Equipment's Extreme Alpine range, built for winter mountaineering, glacier camps and ski expeditions where every gram counts and condensation is a given. It pairs 743 g of 800+ fill goose down with a 10-denier DRILITE LOFT shell that sheds frost and tent drip. The trade-off is straightforward: at 1,150 g and a roughly £500–£660 street price, it is far more bag than most three-season thru-hikers need.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Mountain Equipment rates the Kryos at -9°C comfort and -16°C limit, with a looser "Good Night's Sleep" figure of -20°C that is the brand's own metric rather than a standardized one. The standardized numbers are the ones to plan around. The bag's warmth comes from construction as much as fill: trapezoid baffles run horizontally through the body, and an elastic EXL system on both the top and bottom of the inner pulls the baffles toward the sleeper to cut dead air space and drafts. Ground-level side seams with a V-baffle let the top sheet wrap over the body rather than leaving a gap at the hip. One owner who came to it from an earlier Mountain Equipment expedition bag called it very warm for its weight and noted how small it packed for a sub-zero bag.

**Weight and packability.** About 65 percent of the bag's weight is down, which is why 1,150 g reaches a -16°C limit. Packed, it measures 34 x 25 x 24 cm, a dense cylinder that fits comfortably in an alpine pack. Within the winter-bag class that is a strong ratio. Against a typical 3-season quilt or a 0°C bag, it is a lot of weight to carry for cold that never arrives.

**Moisture handling.** This is where the Kryos separates itself from most down bags. The DRILITE LOFT shell is breathable and water-resistant, and the fabric continues into the hood, collar and footbox, the three places that collect breath moisture and tent condensation. Retailers list a 1,500 mm water column for the outer. UK winter campers in the Cairngorms report the bag handles tent condensation well, and one tester writing from New Zealand's backcountry praised the result given that the down itself is not hydrophobic. The down is untreated, so a multi-week trip in persistent damp still calls for care. The shell buys time, not immunity.

**Fit and comfort.** The Alpine Fit 2.0 cut adds a little length and room over earlier versions. Owners describe the stretch from the EXL baffles as snug without feeling confining, and side sleeping is workable. The offset, six-baffle footbox leaves toes uncrushed. A 75 cm shoulder girth is trim by expedition standards, which is part of the weight story but will feel close to larger sleepers wearing a down jacket inside.

**Details.** The zip is set low so the neck collar stays put, and a cross-baffle above the zipper stops down from sagging away from the chest. A magnetic Lode Lock closure at the top of the zip snugs the collar around the neck. The elastic-drawcord hood is anatomically shaped from seven baffles. One reviewer's only complaint was the lack of an internal pocket for a phone, radio or GPS communicator, a real nuisance in a bag where batteries need to stay warm.

## Choosing a Version

The Kryos comes in Regular and Long, and the difference is length rather than a different tier of warmth. The Regular fits sleepers up to 185 cm. The Long is a 205 cm bag with a 200 cm maximum user height, built for taller climbers who would otherwise have their feet pressing into the footbox and compressing the down there, which costs warmth exactly where it is needed. Sleepers between about 180 and 185 cm who want room for boots or a water bottle in the footbox can reasonably step up to the Long, at the cost of a little extra weight. Anyone over 185 cm should buy the Long. Zip side is also a choice; the left-zip is the version listed most widely.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low weight for the temperature rating: 743 g of 800+ fill down in a 1,150 g bag
- DRILITE LOFT shell extends into the hood, collar and footbox, where moisture collects
- Trapezoid baffles with EXL elastic pull the bag close and limit cold spots
- Draft collar, Lode Lock closure and zip baffle keep heat in
- Down Codex certified; PFAS-free DWR
- Waterproof roll-top stuff sack included

**Cons**

- Overbuilt and heavy for three-season thru-hiking
- Expensive, with retail prices around £500–£680
- Down is not hydrophobic, so prolonged wet conditions still demand care
- Trim 75 cm shoulder girth
- No internal pocket for electronics

## Who Should Buy This

Winter mountaineers, ski tourers, and anyone camping on snow or glaciers at -10°C and below will get what they pay for here, particularly in humid maritime ranges like the Scottish Highlands, where tent condensation soaks bags. Cold-sleeping thru-hikers headed for the high Sierra in shoulder season, or on a winter Arctic traverse, could justify it. Hikers who mostly see nights above freezing should look at a quilt or a 0°C to -5°C bag that saves several hundred grams and a few hundred dollars.

## Verdict

The Kryos is a purpose-built cold-weather bag that pairs high-quality down with a shell that deals with moisture, and it delivers an honest -16°C limit at 1,150 g. It is not a thru-hiker's bag, but for alpine and expedition use it earns its price. Buy it for real winter; skip it for everything else.
