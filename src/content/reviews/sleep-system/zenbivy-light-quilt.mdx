## Overview

The Light Quilt is the workhorse of Zenbivy's lineup: a 25°F, 800+ fill duck-down quilt built to clip into the company's Light Sheet, which wraps the sleeping pad and adds a hood. It suits side sleepers and restless sleepers who want a bag-like, draft-free night without a zipper. The cost is weight. It is not the lightest 25°F quilt, and it works best with a sheet that adds more.

## Performance

**Comfort.** The newest Light Quilt swaps the older shell-fabric liner for a 25D pongee that Zenbivy describes as feeling like a real liner rather than a shell material. Reviewers who have used earlier versions of the system single out the fabric softness and the freedom of movement as the main reasons to buy. Section Hiker's tester called the Light Bed bundle remarkably comfortable and the best system he had seen for "rotisserie" sleepers who flip through the night.

**The clip system.** Attachment loops sit roughly 10 inches in from each side edge, so the quilt overlaps the sheet's side flaps instead of simply draping over the pad. Testers say this stops the cold air that gets in when a strap-on quilt rides up as you roll. A draft collar at the neck cinches from the side, which avoids the cord-in-the-face problem on many quilts. One Section Hiker tester clipped one side closed and left the other open. That is the advantage of clips over a zipper: venting takes seconds.

**Fast Footbox.** A single clip lets the footbox stay narrow for warmth or open up for room. Section Hiker's tester mostly ran it in mummy mode with the footbox hooked to the bottom of the pad to keep it from sliding off while leaving the legs free to move.

**Warmth.** The 25°F rating is a limit rating, with a 35°F comfort figure. One Pacific Northwest reviewer found the 25°F quilt best for overnight lows down to about 45°F. Combined with the insulated sheet, the system has been used by Section Hiker's tester in snow at 9,600 feet and on nights in the low 20s. Shoppers should treat 35°F as the realistic lower bound for the quilt alone and use the sheet for anything colder.

**Weight and packing.** At 321 g of fill in the regular, the quilt is competitive but not exceptional. Cottage-brand quilts at similar ratings generally come in lighter. The sheet adds further weight, and Section Hiker's verdict was that the bundle is low enough for backpacking but not ultralight. The quilt arrives in a mesh storage sack to keep the down lofted at home. Stuffed into a user's own sack or the bottom of a pack, it compresses easily.

## Choosing a Version

Both listed weights carry the same 25°F / -4°C limit rating, so the choice comes down to weight and size. The 567 g listing is 57 g lighter than the 624 g regular. In the regular, the quilt is 54 inches wide. That is enough for back and side sleepers on a standard 20-inch pad.

The large (737 g, 58 inches wide) is for hikers around six feet tall or those who want extra overlap when clipped to a wider 25- or 30-inch sheet. The XL (851 g, 64 inches wide) is for tall or broad sleepers. It adds 227 g over the regular, which is a high price in grams. Hikers who need more than a 25°F rating can look to Zenbivy's 10°F fill, which carries 579 g of down in the regular alone.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft pongee liner that feels closer to bedding than a shell fabric
- Clip system blocks drafts for side and restless sleepers
- Fast Footbox adjusts between narrow warmth and open space
- Thermally mapped baffles put down where it matters
- Vents quickly by unclipping, and works as a plain quilt in summer
- PFAS-free treatments on down and shell

**Cons**

- Heavier than the lightest 25°F quilts, especially with the sheet
- Needs the Light Sheet ($139 extra) to deliver its full benefit
- 15D shell and 25D liner are comfortable but not the lightest fabrics available
- Clip setup takes a few nights to get right
- At $309, the price does not include a sheet or pad

## Who Should Buy This

Side sleepers, stomach sleepers and anyone who has thrashed out of a mummy bag will get the most from the Light Quilt. It is for three-season hikers who value sleep quality over the last 100 grams and who want a bag-like feel without a bag's constriction. Gram-counting thru-hikers who sleep still on their backs and use a strap-on quilt without trouble can find a lighter option elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Light Quilt is a comfort-first quilt with sound materials and a draft-blocking system that works. It is best bought as part of the Zenbivy sheet system, not on its own. Restless sleepers should buy it. Hikers chasing the lowest base weight should not.
