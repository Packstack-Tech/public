## Overview

The Kimberfeel Milos is a no-frills EVA sandal from the French Decathlon-adjacent outfit Kimberfeel. It has textile straps, hook-and-loop closures and a soft foam sole. It lists at €20 direct from the maker, which puts it closer to the price of a pair of pool slides than a pair of Chacos. That makes it a credible camp shoe for hikers who want something to change into at the end of the day. It isn't built to be a primary trail sandal for hard miles.

## Performance

**Comfort.** The Milos is built around a single idea: a soft, cushioned EVA footbed under a minimal strap system. Kimberfeel pitches the foam as soft enough for cushioning and support over a day of strolling, and buyers back that up. Customers on Trekkinn describe the sandals as comfortable for the money, and one calls them the best option at the price. For camp use, which means a few hundred meters to the water source, a trip to the privy and hours of standing around a stove, soft foam and an open design are what the job asks for.

**Fit and adjustment.** The ankle and toe straps are both hook-and-loop, so the fit can be adjusted quickly with cold hands or with feet that have swollen after a long day. That matters more than it sounds. Slip-on foam camp shoes tend to flop around when the foot swells, and these don't. Sizing is the recurring complaint. Multiple buyers report the Milos runs small, and a reviewer who wears a 44 and bought a 44 said a 45 would have fit better. Hikers between sizes, or anyone planning to wear them over socks on cold evenings, should size up.

**Weight.** The manufacturer lists 230 g at EU 37. Larger sizes will weigh more, and the Milos will not be the lightest item in an ultralight kit. Foam flip-flops and slides give up the straps and cut weight further. What the Milos offers in return is a closed fit that stays on the foot, which makes it more useful for walking than a slide.

**Durability and traction.** This is the weak point. The outsole is the same soft EVA as the midsole, with no rubber layer, and foam wears quickly on abrasive ground. An early buyer at Alpinstore rated the sandals 4 out of 5 and said they don't look very strong, adding that time would tell. Amazon UK shoppers gave the men's version 3.4 out of 5 across seven ratings. Retail copy from French outdoor shops steers the Milos toward easy terrain, light summer walks and travel in warm, dry places, and that is a fair description. On wet rock, scree or muddy descents, an EVA sole offers less grip than a lugged rubber outsole, so the Milos is a camp and town shoe, not a river-crossing shoe.

**Value.** At €20, replacement is not a financial event. A thru-hiker who wears them only in camp should get a season out of them without much trouble. Someone who walks in them daily will wear through the sole sooner.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Very low price, around €20 from the maker
- Soft EVA footbed is comfortable for standing and short walks
- Hook-and-loop straps at ankle and toe adjust easily as feet swell
- Textile straps dry quickly and don't hold heat the way a closed shoe does
- Wide size range, EU 35–46

**Cons**

- Reported to run small; sizing up is the standard advice
- EVA-only outsole with no rubber, so traction and wear lag behind trail sandals
- At 230 g in EU 37, heavier than slides or flip-flops
- Buyers question long-term durability
- Not suited to wet, technical or abrasive terrain

## Who Should Buy This

The Milos suits budget-minded section hikers and thru-hikers who want an adjustable camp shoe that stays on their feet. It also works for travelers who want a cheap sandal for hostels, beaches and town walking. Hikers who plan to hike in their sandals, ford rivers in them or put serious mileage on them should look at a rubber-soled trail sandal from Teva, Chaco or Luna. Gram-counters should consider a foam slide instead.

## Verdict

The Milos is a cheap, comfortable and adjustable camp sandal that does the one job it was designed for. It isn't a trail sandal, and its foam sole and size-small fit keep it from rating higher. Buy it for camp and town, size up, and don't expect it to survive as a daily hiker.
