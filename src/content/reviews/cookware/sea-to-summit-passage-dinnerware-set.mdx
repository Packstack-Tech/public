## Overview

The Passage Dinnerware Set is Sea to Summit's six-piece kit for one: a 720 ml bowl, a 355 ml cup with a folding silicone sipping lid, and a knife, fork, spoon and spork that clamp together. It's built for car campers, section hikers and anyone who wants real tableware on the trail without going the collapsible-silicone route. The trade-off is weight. At 168 g (5.9 oz), it's more dinner service than most ultralight kitchens need.

## Performance

**Construction and durability.** The bowl and cup are molded from glass-reinforced polypropylene. It's stiffer and tougher than the plain plastic in typical camp dishes, and it's the material Sea to Summit credits for the set being lighter and stronger than conventional camp plates, bowls and mugs. Owners' feedback follows that line. Buyers describe the pieces as built to last and say they hold up to being tossed in a bag. One reviewer who carries the set to work reports no cracking or breakage. Another, who bought several sets for a family, praised the sturdy, unbreakable cup for kids.

**Heat handling.** Both the bowl and cup have fluted Cool Grip fins molded into the sidewalls. The ribbing cuts heat transfer, so a bowl of fresh-rehydrated dinner or a full mug of coffee can be held bare-handed instead of balanced on a knee. It's a small feature, but on a cold morning it's the one that gets used most.

**The bowl.** Steep, near-vertical sidewalls keep a loaded bowl from sloshing over, and the curve where wall meets base is shaped to match Sea to Summit's spoons and sporks, so the last of a freezer-bag meal can be scraped out cleanly. At 720 ml it fits one hungry hiker's dinner, or a cereal breakfast, without crowding. The set's one frequent complaint is that the bowl has no lid, which would help for carrying leftovers or using it as a food container.

**The cup.** The 355 ml cup comes with a silicone sipping lid that folds down and stores inside it, so the lid doesn't take up extra room. That makes it workable for coffee or tea in a windy camp. It's a modest capacity, closer to a coffee mug than a big backpacking mug.

**The cutlery.** The four utensils snap together for transport, which means nothing rattles around or goes missing in the bottom of a food bag. Reverse one utensil and click the handles to a second, and the pair becomes a long-handled spoon or spork. That's a useful trick for reaching to the bottom of a deep freezer bag or pot. Owners say the utensils are better than they look, though the full four-piece cutlery set is overkill for most backpackers, who'll use the spork and nothing else.

**Cleaning.** Polypropylene is easy to wipe out, and it's microwave- and dishwasher-safe, which is a plus for a set that doubles as home or office lunch gear. Several owners say it's their everyday work set as well as their camp kit.

**Weight and pack size.** This is where the set loses ground with the ultralight crowd. A bowl, cup and a full cutlery suite at 168 g outweighs the single-spork, single-container kits that many thru-hikers carry. The pieces nest, but it's still a rigid, bulky bundle, not a collapsible one.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tough glass-reinforced polypropylene that owners report holds up to rough use
- Cool Grip fins make hot food and drinks comfortable to hold
- Steep-walled, spoon-matched bowl scrapes clean and resists spills
- Cup lid folds inside the cup, so it adds no bulk
- Cutlery clips together and can link into a long spoon or spork
- Microwave- and dishwasher-safe, with a lifetime guarantee
- Often discounted to around $18–20

**Cons**

- 168 g is heavy for ultralight and thru-hiking kits
- Rigid pieces don't collapse, so the set takes up fixed space
- No bowl lid
- Four utensils is more cutlery than most solo hikers will use
- 355 ml cup is small for big drinkers

## Who Should Buy This

This set suits car campers, weekend backpackers, families outfitting kids and solo travelers who want a cheap, durable, grippable meal setup that also works at home and the office. It's an easy gift for someone building a first camp kitchen. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. A lone spork and a lightweight container or the pot itself does the same job for far less weight, and users who want a bowl that collapses or seals should look at collapsible or lidded options instead.

## Verdict

The Passage set is a well-designed, durable meal kit at a modest price, and the Cool Grip walls and clip-together cutlery are thoughtful touches. It's a strong buy for general camping and casual backpacking, and a hard sell for anyone whose base weight is measured in grams.
