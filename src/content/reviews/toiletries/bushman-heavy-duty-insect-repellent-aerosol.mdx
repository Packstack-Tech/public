## Overview

Bushman Heavy Duty is an Australian-made aerosol repellent built around a 40% DEET concentration, positioned for hikers, tradespeople, and outdoor workers who need repellent that holds up through sweat and rain rather than just a backyard barbecue. It's less known outside Australia, but within the bushwalking community it's something of a cult classic — the repellent people reach for when they're heading into leech country or sandfly-infested coastal scrub, not just a mozzie-at-the-campsite situation. This one's for anyone who wants a single can that handles the whole spectrum of Australian biting pests rather than swapping between products.

## Performance

The headline number is 40% DEET with a claimed <cite index="4-7">10 hours protection with professional strength 40% DEET, water, sweat, and rub resistance</cite>. Independent testing from CHOICE Australia is a bit more conservative — <cite index="3-2,3-3">their claimed repellency figure lands at 8 hours</cite> rather than the 10 the manufacturer advertises. That's still a long window compared to most 10-15% DEET pump sprays you'll find at a pharmacy, and it tracks with how DEET actually works: the concentration mostly dictates duration, not how well it repels at any given moment.

Where this product distinguishes itself from a generic DEET spray is breadth. It's marketed and used against more than mosquitoes — <cite index="1-1">specifically formulated to provide long-lasting, heavy-duty protection against mosquitoes, sandflies, ticks, leeches, and marchflies</cite>. That leech claim isn't just marketing copy. One hiker's account from the K'gari (Fraser Island) Great Walk described using the aerosol on their shoes when leeches started climbing aboard in the rainforest sections, noting that <cite index="11-10">given I had the spray version it was very easy to spray over my shoes – any leeches that found their way onto my shoes dropped off and those leeches nearby waiting for their turn retreated</cite>. That's a genuinely useful trick if you're hiking anywhere leeches are a problem — spraying boots and gaiters rather than just skin.

On raw mosquito/march fly effectiveness, older bushwalking forum feedback on Bushman's stronger 80% DEET variant is glowing — one user reported <cite index="10-2,10-3">telling you with confidence that it works very well, 100% no bites — and the March flies and Mozzies were everywhere</cite> during a Tasmanian trip. The 40% version won't hold quite as long as the 80%, but the active mechanism and formulation are the same, so I'd expect similarly strong knockdown, just over a shorter window before reapplication.

The water/sweat resistance claim also holds up in practice — the same hiker noted the spray was <cite index="11-11">easy to reapply when the rain diluted the spray</cite>, which is really just an admission that any topical repellent needs reapplication in wet conditions, aerosol or not. Don't expect it to survive a swim or a full day of monsoon rain untouched.

Scent is the one place opinions diverge. Bushman's own marketing insists the formula has <cite index="7-9,7-10">a pleasant, neutral scent, with the otherwise strong smell of the active completely masked throughout the range</cite>. Real-world noses disagree. One reviewer was blunt about it: <cite index="11-1">it smells like very cheap and bad perfume to me</cite>. That's a meaningful data point if you're sensitive to fragrance or hiking with people who are — this isn't an odorless product, whatever the marketing says.

Worth flagging: DEET at this concentration is not sunscreen, and it won't protect exposed skin from UV. One reviewer using the 80% version noted <cite index="10-4">even with liberal usage I still got burnt on the arms and hands, so don't rely on it as your sun block</cite>. Layer sunscreen separately and apply DEET afterward if you're using both.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 40% DEET gives real staying power — hours longer than typical drugstore 10–15% sprays before you need to reapply
- Multi-pest coverage: works against sandflies, ticks, marchflies, and — notably — leeches, not just mosquitoes
- Water, sweat, and rub resistant, so it survives actual trail conditions better than a basic spray
- Aerosol format sprays evenly onto boots, socks, and gaiters, useful for leech-prone terrain where you're not just protecting skin
- Widely trusted within the Australian bushwalking community, with a long track record

**Cons**

- Scent is polarizing — several users find it noticeably perfumey rather than neutral, despite the marketing claims
- Independent testing (CHOICE) puts real-world protection closer to 8 hours than the advertised 10
- 40% DEET can degrade some plastics, synthetics, and printed fabrics — spot-test before spraying on pack straps or rain gear
- Aerosol cans add weight and bulk versus a small DEET lotion or wipes, and you can't fly with a full-size can in carry-on
- Not widely available outside Australia/NZ, so overseas hikers may need to order in advance or hunt for a supplier

## Who Should Buy This

This is the repellent to reach for if you're hiking anywhere in Australia's wetter, buggier regions — rainforest sections, coastal tracks with sandflies, or leech territory like Tasmania's wet forests or the Queensland coast. It's overkill for a dry desert trip with the occasional mosquito, where a lower-DEET lotion would do fine and save weight. If you've been burned (bitten, rather) by weaker repellents failing after an hour or two, the 40% concentration and multi-pest claims make this worth the extra grams in your toiletries bag.

## Verdict

Bushman Heavy Duty delivers on its core promise — long-lasting, broad-spectrum protection that goes beyond mosquitoes to ticks, sandflies, and leeches, backed by real hiker accounts rather than just marketing copy. The scent isn't as neutral as advertised and independent testing trims the claimed duration a bit, but for anyone tackling Australia's buggier trails, this earns a solid 7.5/10 and a spot in the pack.
