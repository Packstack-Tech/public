## Overview

The Jetboil Pot Support is a small stainless steel attachment that clips onto the burner of select Jetboil stoves, letting you use a regular pot, pan, or the Jetboil Summit Skillet instead of being locked into the proprietary FluxRing cup. It's aimed at Jetboil owners who want to cook actual food — frying eggs, simmering a big pot of rice, boiling water for a group — rather than just rehydrating a pouch of freeze-dried noodles in the stock cup.

## Performance

This is about as simple a piece of gear as you'll find, but it solves a real problem. The stock Jetboil FluxRing cup is great at boiling water fast, but it's a poor vessel for actual cooking — you can't really fry or simmer a real meal in it without scorching something. Clip this support onto the burner and you've got a proper cooktop that'll hold a skillet or a wider pot.

The mounting mechanism is the one place where reviewers consistently get tripped up. Several owners <cite index="2-7,2-8">admitted being flummoxed by the fold-out tine design when they first got it, and needed extra pictures or a description from Jetboil before it clicked</cite>. Once you know the trick, though, it's a five-second job: <cite index="2-10">the support has to be rotated about an inch clockwise so the tines line up with the indent circles on the stove</cite>. One eBay reviewer mentioned that some buyers get frustrated and resort to bending the tines with pliers before figuring out the correct twist-lock motion, which tracks with what I've seen elsewhere — it's a "watch a 30-second video once and you'll never struggle again" kind of quirk, but it's a legitimate first-use annoyance.

Once it's on, stability is solid. A Trailspace reviewer running it under the MiniMo said the combination let them cook <cite index="1-17">"a variety of foods, from frying a pound of bacon to boiling a large pot of rice."</cite> That matches the general consensus: the support doesn't wobble, and the pan sits close enough to the burner that you're not losing a ton of efficiency compared to the stock cup. Paddling.com's reviewer specifically noted the design keeps things tight, observing that <cite index="2-17">the support doesn't significantly increase the contact diameter of the stove</cite>, which is part of why it stays stable with wider cookware.

Compatibility is the biggest thing to check before buying, and it's more fragmented than you'd expect for a $13 accessory. The current-generation part (sometimes listed as "Pot Support 2.0") only works with <cite index="7-4,7-5">MicroMo, MiniMo, SUMO, Flash (pre-2025 model), and Zip (pre-2025 model) — it is explicitly not compatible with 2025+ Flash 1.0L, Flash 1.8L, Zip 0.8L, TrailCook, Stash, MightyMo, Genesis, HalfGen, or the Luna Satellite Burner</cite>. If you bought a Flash or Zip in the last year or so, don't assume the old part number fits — Jetboil redesigned the mount for the 2025 systems specifically so it <cite index="4-7">stows neatly under the burner assembly for seamless packing</cite>, and the new and old versions aren't interchangeable.

Packability is a non-issue. At 1.2 oz and with tines that fold flat, it tucks inside the Jetboil cup alongside the burner with zero added bulk in your pack — several buyers specifically call out that <cite index="3-15">it fits perfectly into the Jetboil canister along with the stove</cite>. I haven't found any durability complaints in stainless steel construction; the tines are thick enough that they're not going to bend under normal use unless you're the person fighting the mounting mechanism with pliers.

One limitation worth flagging: this is a support, not a heat diffuser. You're still running a small, hot, centrally-focused Jetboil burner under a wide pan, so expect hot spots and the need to shuffle food around, especially with anything wider than 8-9 inches. It converts the system from "boil water" to "cook food," but it doesn't turn a Jetboil into a simmer-happy backpacking stove like an MSR WindBurner Duo or a canister stove with a separate wide burner head.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely expands what a Jetboil can do — frying, simmering, and group cooking become viable
- Only 1.2 oz and folds flat to store inside the Jetboil cup
- Stainless steel construction feels durable; no reports of tines bending or corroding under normal use
- Cheap — roughly $13-15, so it's a low-risk add-on if you already own a compatible stove
- Comes bundled with the canister stabilizer tripod on some packages, so you may get a useful spare

**Cons**

- Confusing first-time attachment — the twist-lock motion isn't intuitive and isn't well explained on the packaging
- Compatibility list is a moving target; Jetboil redesigned the mount for 2025+ Flash and Zip models, so older Pot Supports won't fit newer stoves
- Not compatible with several current Jetboil models (Genesis, HalfGen, Stash, MightyMo, TrailCook)
- Doesn't solve the underlying hot-spot issue of cooking with a small, centrally-focused burner under a wide pan

## Who Should Buy This

This is for existing Jetboil owners — specifically MicroMo, MiniMo, SUMO, or pre-2025 Flash/Zip owners — who want to use their stove for more than boiling water. If you're base camping, car camping, or on a trip where you want to fry bacon or cook for more than one person off a Jetboil, it's a cheap and genuinely useful upgrade. If you own a 2025+ Flash or Zip, a Stash, or a MightyMo, skip it — it either won't fit or Jetboil doesn't make a compatible version for your stove at all, so double check your model before ordering.

## Verdict

For around $13-15 and 1.2 oz, the Jetboil Pot Support does exactly one job — letting you use normal cookware on a Jetboil burner — and does it well once you figure out the slightly fiddly twist-lock attachment. It's not going to turn your Jetboil into a full camp kitchen stove, but as a low-cost way to unlock real cooking from a system built for boiling water, it earns its keep. I'd call it a 7.5/10: essential if your stove is on the compatibility list, irrelevant if it isn't.
