## Overview

The Nalgene Wide Mouth is the hard-sided one-liter bottle that has ridden in pack side pockets, ammo-can kitchens and dorm-room backpacks for decades. The Sustain edition is made in the USA from BPA/BPS-free Tritan Renew, a resin derived from 50% waste plastic through ISCC-certified mass balance. It's a tough, leakproof bottle for camp, day hikes and cold-weather trips, and the same shape is also sold in a lighter HDPE build. The trade-off is weight: the Tritan versions run about 6.25 oz, several times what a disposable Smartwater bottle weighs.

## Performance

**Durability and sealing.** This is why the bottle exists. Long-term owners describe daily use stretching back decades, including trips through the freezer and repeated drops onto rock and pavement without cracks. The continuous semi-buttress threads on the attached-loop cap seal tightly, and leaks are rare enough that owners trust the bottle in a laptop bag. The graduated markings on the side double as a measuring cup for cook water.

**Wide-mouth usability.** The 63 mm opening takes ice cubes, powdered drink mix and a scooped refill from a shallow stream without fuss. It also admits a SteriPen lamp easily, which is why UV-treatment users have long favored it. Hand cleaning is simple, and the bottle survives the dishwasher. Printed graphics on some bottles can peel over time as the bottle sweats.

**Hot water.** Unlike a Smartwater bottle or a soft flask, the Tritan Nalgene accepts boiling water. On a cold night in the shoulder seasons, that turns a water carrier into a hot-water bottle for the foot of a sleeping bag. It also works as a vessel for cold-soaking or rehydrating food. Insulation is the flip side: cold drinks warm quickly in summer sun, and hot drinks cool just as fast. Nalgene sells insulating sleeves for those who want to slow the process.

**Filtration.** The wide opening suits UV treatment and many drop-in or cap-style devices. Squeeze filters are the sticking point. A rigid Nalgene can't be squeezed to push water through a Sawyer Squeeze, and the filter's 28 mm threads don't match the 63 mm mouth without an adapter. Backpackers who build their system around a squeeze filter and a Smartwater bottle will find the Nalgene awkward.

**Weight and fit.** At 177–178 g, the Tritan bottle weighs roughly four to five times as much as a 1 L Smartwater bottle (about 1.3 oz). Nalgene's HDPE Ultralite version, rated at 3.75 oz (106 g), cuts about 70 g from that. Two of the HDPE bottles still weigh roughly 4.7 oz more than two Smartwater bottles, according to a Trailspace reviewer, so the weight penalty shrinks without disappearing. The 89 mm diameter fits most side pockets on roomy packs but can crowd a streamlined frameless pack or a shoulder-strap pocket. It's too fat for most car cup holders.

## Choosing a Version

Four listings cover two different builds of the same 32 oz wide-mouth shape. All share the 63 mm cap and the 89 mm diameter.

**Tritan 32 fl oz (177.2 g) and Sustain (178 g).** These are effectively the same bottle. The weight difference is under a gram. Both are the clear, heavy-duty Tritan build, and Nalgene's Sustain line is the one that carries the recycled-content message. Buyers who care about that should pick the Sustain-branded bottle. Everyone else should buy whichever is in stock at the lower price. Neither suits a gram counter, but both are the right choice for hot water, hard use and a see-through body.

**32 oz HDPE (125 g) and 32 fl. oz. (110 g).** These are the lightweight builds. The 110 g listing lines up with Nalgene's own 3.75 oz (106 g) Ultralite HDPE spec. HDPE gives up the clear body for a translucent one, and the printed markings on the Ultralite stop at 30 oz, though the bottle holds 32 oz when filled to the shoulder. Backpackers say the HDPE bottle keeps the Nalgene shape and cap while saving about 70 g. Choose this build for three-season backpacking, and choose the Tritan or Sustain for car camping, winter trips and anything involving boiling water.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Survives drops, freezing and years of daily abuse
- Leakproof semi-buttress threaded cap with attached loop
- 63 mm mouth suits ice, drink mixes, scooping and SteriPen use
- Tritan versions handle boiling water and double as a sleeping-bag warmer
- Made in the USA, with up to 50% waste-derived content in the Sustain version
- Dishwasher safe, with measurement markings on the side
- HDPE versions drop to 110–125 g

**Cons**

- Tritan and Sustain versions weigh about 177–178 g, several times a Smartwater bottle
- Rigid body doesn't work with squeeze filters like the Sawyer Squeeze without an adapter
- No insulation
- 89 mm diameter is bulky for slim pockets and car cup holders
- Printed graphics can peel with heavy use

## Who Should Buy This

Car campers, day hikers, climbers, winter backpackers and anyone who wants one durable bottle that will outlast the pack it rides in are the natural buyers. SteriPen users and cold-weather campers who want a hot-water bottle get the most from the Tritan or Sustain bottle. Backpackers who like the Nalgene format but watch weight should take an HDPE version. Thru-hikers running a Sawyer Squeeze, or anyone whose base weight is already trimmed to the gram, should look at a Smartwater bottle or a soft flask instead.

## Verdict

The Nalgene Wide Mouth does what it has done for decades: it carries a liter of water and shrugs off abuse that would finish most bottles. The Sustain's recycled-content Tritan Renew changes the bottle's footprint, not its performance. It's an easy recommendation for camp and everyday use, and a poor one for three-season thru-hiking, where the weight and the squeeze-filter mismatch count against it.
