## Overview

The Quechua MH500 is Decathlon's mainstream men's hiking pant: a slim, stretchy nylon trouser with four zip pockets, preformed knees and a belt that comes in the box. It is aimed at three-season walkers who want technical mobility without technical pricing. The trade-off is a fitted cut and a fabric that owners say runs warm once summer arrives.

## Performance

**Movement.** Freedom of movement is the MH500's calling card, and the reason owners return to it. The 15% elastane content stretches in two directions. Preformed knees and a crotch gusset add to that. One reviewer who wore a blue pair as the main trousers on a 35-day, roughly 800 km Camino de Santiago walk in 2024 reported no tearing, no stretching out and no loss of shape. Long strides, high steps and scrambling don't bind. The pants are also quiet on the move, which is not a given with synthetic trousers.

**Fit.** The cut is slim by design, closer to a skinny taper than a traditional straight-leg trekking pant. Reviewers with strong thighs recommend sizing up. Some owners find the waist snug while the leg is roomier than expected. The siliconized waistband grips a base layer or shirt hem so it stays tucked, and the supplied belt and adjustable leg bottoms handle fine-tuning. Those ankle cinches are useful for keeping fabric off wet grass or out of a chainring.

**Breathability and drying.** Decathlon's own perspiration test gives the fabric 3/5, and the company applies that same rating to its whole hiking trouser range. That is middling. The material dries quickly after sweat or a drizzle, but it is a fully woven fabric with no vents. Owners on Decathlon's site describe it as comfortable and breathable in general use. A recurring complaint is that it gets a bit hot in summer, and one buyer judged the pants better for forest walks than for long hikes. Hikers heading into serious heat will want something lighter or a pair of shorts.

**Weather.** These pants are not windproof and have no water-repellent claim. Decathlon positions them for spring through autumn. Owners describe cold or windy days as base-layer territory: the pants work under thermal underwear and an outer shell, but alone they are not a cold-weather answer.

**Storage and details.** Four zip pockets keep a phone, keys and a card wallet from falling out while scrambling. The thigh pocket is large enough to be practical. There is no drop-seat, no zip-off option on this model, and no stretch-woven reinforcement at the cuff, so abrasion on rock and brush is the wear point to watch.

**Weight.** The 194 g catalog figure puts the MH500 well below most mid-range trekking pants. Decathlon's retail listing for the men's trouser puts a size L at about 300 g, which is still light for a four-pocket pant with a belt, though not in ultralight-shell territory. Either way, the weight is a side benefit, not the reason to buy. The reason is the fabric's give.

**Value.** At £29.99 in the UK, and around AU$60 on sale in Australia, the MH500 costs a fraction of comparable pants from the big outdoor names. Decathlon's 2-year warranty and strong aggregate ratings (4.7–4.8 across thousands of reviews on its regional sites) support the low price. Tellingly, the Quechua name keeps coming up in value roundups.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Bi-stretch fabric with preformed knees and a gusset moves freely
- Four zip pockets, including a thigh pocket
- Belt included, plus adjustable leg bottoms
- Quick-drying synthetic that stays quiet on the trail
- Very low price for the feature set
- One long-distance owner reported no tearing or bagging over roughly 800 km

**Cons**

- Slim cut; sizing up is common advice for larger thighs
- 3/5 wicking rating and no vents, so owners report it runs hot in summer
- No wind or water resistance
- No zip-off or convertible option
- Care label calls for cool washing and no tumble drying

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season hikers, section hikers and travelers who want a mobile, durable pant on a budget will find it hard to beat for the money. It suits spring and fall trips, cool-to-mild summer days and long days on the trail with a pack. Hikers who run hot, thru-hikers working through desert summers, and anyone who likes a loose, baggy fit should look to shorts or a lighter, roomier woven pant. So should cold-weather hikers who want a pant that stands on its own.

## Verdict

The Quechua MH500 is a fitted, comfortable, well-priced hiking pant that does what it promises in mild weather. The slim cut and modest breathability keep it from being an all-conditions choice, but at this price it is a sensible buy for most three-season walking.
