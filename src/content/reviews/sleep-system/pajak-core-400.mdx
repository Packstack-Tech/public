## Overview

The Core 400 is the workhorse of Polish down specialist Pajak's range: a 700-fill duck down mummy bag for spring-to-autumn trekking, rated to 0°C comfort. It weighs 800 g in the Regular length and costs roughly €290–325 at European retailers. The trade-off is plain: it isn't the lightest bag at its temperature rating, but it undercuts most of the competition on price and gives back some of that weight as room to move.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The Core 400's headline numbers are 0°C comfort, -6°C limit and a -24°C extreme figure that belongs on the spec sheet and nowhere near a night out. Owners put the real range closer to the limit than the comfort number. One long-distance hiker in the Alps reported sleeping comfortably at around -5°C, and suggested the bag could go lower. That fits the construction: the Z-shaped baffles angle the walls between down chambers rather than stacking them in a conventional H pattern, which Pajak says improves insulation. A 3D draft collar and a flap behind the zipper address the usual leaks at the neck and along the zip line.

**Weight and packability.** At 800 g with 422 g of fill, the Core 400 is competitive for a bag rated to 0°C, but it isn't in the cottage-industry weight class. The 700 fill power is a step below the 800–900 fill that dominates premium ultralight bags, and that costs a few hundred grams of weight or a little packed volume. In its compression sack the Regular packs to about 5 L, around 29 x 18 cm. The sack offers two compression levels, and one owner noted that cinching it fully makes for a tight squeeze.

**Breathability and range.** Owners mention the bag doesn't turn clammy when temperatures rise. The same Alps hiker used it at +10°C without trouble, and the full-length zipper lets it open out into a blanket on warm nights. The two-way zip also means a foot vent is easy to create when the hut is stuffier than expected.

**Fit and comfort.** This is a roomy mummy by modern ultralight standards. Reviewers on retail sites return to the same words: roomy, easily compressed, adjustable hood. Side sleepers and restless sleepers benefit. Thru-hikers chasing the narrowest, lightest cut will see that room as extra fabric and extra air to heat. The shell is soft, though one owner said it has a slightly plastic hand, which takes getting used to.

**Moisture and durability.** The hood and footbox carry a water-repellent DWR treatment, which is useful where condensation from a tent wall or a damp breath collects. The down is treated as standard, not hydrophobic, so it still needs a dry bag in sustained rain. Owners describe the bag as durable and well finished, and the down is traceable via a QR code on the product. It's made in Europe.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 800 g for a 0°C comfort rating, with 422 g of down
- Real-world use reported at around -5°C, consistent with the -6°C limit
- Breathable across a wide range, from near-freezing to about +10°C
- Roomy cut, full-length 2-way zipper, 3D draft collar and anti-cold flap
- RDS-certified Polish down with QR traceability
- Priced well below most European and North American down bags of similar rating

**Cons**

- 700 fill power means more weight and bulk than 800–900 fill bags
- Roomy cut is less thermally efficient than a snug ultralight mummy
- Full compression in the stuff sack is tight
- Shell has a slightly synthetic feel
- Standard (non-hydrophobic) down needs careful moisture management

## Who Should Buy This

The Core 400 suits three-season trekkers, alpine hut-to-hut hikers and weekend backpackers who want a warm bag at a fair price and don't need to shave the last 150 g. It also suits anyone who sleeps badly in a tight mummy. Thru-hikers on a strict ultralight base-weight budget, or anyone planning regular nights well below -5°C, should look at a higher-fill-power bag or a warmer model. It comes in Short, Regular and Long lengths, with the Regular covering users up to 180 cm.

## Verdict

The Core 400 delivers honest 0°C-to-slightly-below warmth, a comfortable cut and sensible details at a price that makes premium down bags look inflated. It gives up some weight and packed size to 800-fill competitors, and that rules it out for gram-counters. For most three-season hikers, it's the better value.
