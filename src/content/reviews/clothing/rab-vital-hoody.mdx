## Overview

The Rab Vital Hoody is a 130 g hooded windshell cut from Atmos, Rab's tightly woven 20D nylon. It is aimed at spring and summer hikers who want a layer that disappears into a pack until a ridgeline demands it. It does that for $100, and it comes with more features than most windshells at this weight. The trade-off is pocket placement, which fights with a hipbelt.

## Performance

**Wind protection.** The Atmos fabric uses very fine yarns in a dense weave, and testers consistently rate it near the top of its class for blocking wind. Outdoor Gear Lab's men's test scored the Vital Hoody 7.7 for wind resistance, just behind the Patagonia Houdini's 7.8. The women's version scored 9.0 in the same category. CleverHiker's testers took it into a windy day in Alaska's Chugach Mountains. They found the elastic hood and wrist cuffs kept the jacket snug and secure.

**Breathability.** A windshell that traps heat is a bad trade, and the Vital mostly avoids that. Outdoor Gear Lab scored it 8.2 for breathability in the men's test, high enough for climbing and trail running. CleverHiker was less effusive: the Vital is not the most breathable jacket it tested. It does carry a feature that helps. A small chest snap lets the jacket be fully unzipped while staying in place, so it doesn't flap open. CleverHiker's testers used it on trail runs when they started to overheat. Owners make the same point: the clip stops the jacket billowing when the zip is open.

**Weather resistance.** The fluorocarbon-free DWR handles passing showers and mist. It is not a rain jacket. Outdoor Gear Lab scored water resistance at 7.5 for the men's jacket and 8.5 for the women's, a respectable result for a windshell. One Backcountry reviewer describes the ideal use: the weather is raw but dry, and a hard shell would be sweaty overkill.

**The hood.** This is where the Vital separates itself from cheaper windshirts. The hood has an elasticated rim, with a stiffened peak set back above it and a rear tab to adjust volume. Chris Townsend, reviewing for The Great Outdoors, called the hood good and noted the absence of front drawcords didn't bother him. SectionHiker found the rear adjustment lets the hood fit over a hat without limiting visibility. Outdoor Gear Lab's women's testers singled out the structured brim and close fit as features they would not want to do without.

**Pockets.** This is the recurring complaint. Reviewers like having zippered hand pockets, which most windshells skip, and CleverHiker's testers used them for gloves and a phone. But Townsend and CleverHiker both note they sit where a pack's hipbelt runs, which makes them hard to reach while carrying a loaded pack. Backpackers wearing a belt-mounted pocket system won't care. Those who don't will find the pockets are mostly useful in camp.

**Packability and feel.** The jacket stuffs into its own pocket to roughly the size of an apple, according to one owner. Townsend noted the fabric doesn't have the slick, shiny feel that nylon often has, which matters when it's worn against bare arms.

**Durability.** Outdoor Gear Lab calls the Atmos fabric extremely durable for its weight, and the Vital has been a long-standing favorite among its testers. The women's review credits successive upgrades with improving the jacket's handling of active use.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 130 g, and it packs to about the size of an apple
- Dense Atmos weave blocks wind effectively
- Stiffened hood peak and rear adjustment work well over a hat
- Chest snap keeps the open jacket from flapping
- Zippered hand pockets, which most windshirts lack
- $100 list price undercuts most rivals; Outdoor Gear Lab gave it a Best Buy award

**Cons**

- Pockets sit under a pack hipbelt
- Not the most breathable windshell tested
- DWR handles showers, not sustained rain
- Regular fit leaves little room for bulky layers underneath

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want a single windshell for ridgelines, cold mornings and camp. The hood suits hikers who skip a separate hat or buff. It also fits trail runners and day hikers who value a feature-rich layer at a modest price. Hikers who want to reach pockets under a hipbelt, or who need real rain protection from the same layer, should look at a dedicated minimalist windshirt or a proper rain jacket.

## Verdict

The Vital Hoody delivers wind protection that matches pricier jackets, with a better hood than most and a chest snap that addresses a common windshell annoyance. It weighs 130 g and costs $100. For most three-season hikers, that is a strong value, and the pocket placement is the only real quibble.
