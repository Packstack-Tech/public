## Overview

The Tiny Helio 2025 is FLEXTAIL's latest ultralight headlamp, built around a Luminus SST-20 LED, a removable 700mAh USB-C battery, and a claimed 600-lumen max output packed into a 62.4g package. It's aimed at thru-hikers and fastpackers who want Nitecore NU25-tier brightness without the weight penalty of a traditional headlamp, and who like the idea of swapping in a spare battery instead of babysitting a single sealed cell.

## Performance

**Brightness and beam quality.** The headline 600-lumen figure only shows up when you run the spot and flood LEDs together in mixed mode — <cite index="15-17">the lights must be mixed together in a mixed light mode to reach the 600 lumens</cite>. Run just the spotlight and you're closer to 400 lumens, which one reviewer found was actually the more usable setting for trail use since <cite index="15-4,15-5">it reduced the lumens from a claimed 600 to 400, and it seemed to reach the same distance ahead but didn't cast light everywhere</cite>. Multiple testers noted the max mode is honestly overkill for most nights out — full brightness <cite index="15-3">was a bit too bright in the highest mode</cite>, and one reviewer now just <cite index="16-2">uses the mid setting instead of high since it provides plenty of light without draining the battery as quickly</cite>. That tracks with how most ultralight headlamps get used in practice: you're rarely running max lumens except for scanning campsites or navigating gnarly terrain after dark.

At the low end, the 1-lumen mode is where this light earns its "100 hour" runtime claim, and reviewers confirm it's genuinely useful — <cite index="15-9">the 5 lumens was plenty, and it was great to know that I had 50h of runtime</cite> in the older flood-light spec, with the 2025 version pushing the ultra-low mode down to 1 lumen for even longer legs. For reading in a tent or shuffling around camp without blowing out your night vision, this low mode is arguably the more valuable spec than the peak brightness number.

**Build and comfort.** At 62.4g it's genuinely light on the head — one reviewer called it <cite index="13-4">impressively light</cite> even after their own scale showed it running slightly heavier than the claimed spec. The headband setup uses a bungee-style cord with a foam pad, and testers found it comfortable for extended wear: <cite index="16-8">the bungee cord headband is easy to adjust while wearing, and the soft foam panel on the back adds comfort when wearing it for long periods</cite>. The lamp housing also tilts on a six-position base, giving you <cite index="16-9">the lamp also tilts easily so you can point the light exactly where you need it</cite>.

**Controls.** The two-button interface is simple by design — <cite index="16-6,16-7">a double-click powers it on, a single-click switches between modes, and a long-press enters special modes</cite>. Most reviewers found this intuitive after a few uses, though it's not universal: one buyer's biggest complaint was that <cite index="10-9">the selectors below which are confusing had to order this twice because I ordered the wrong item the first time</cite> — more a gripe about product-page SKU confusion than the headlamp itself, but worth flagging if you're ordering. Another common critique across reviews of the standard 600Z is that <cite index="4-2">the default startup mode should be the lowest brightness setting, not the highest</cite> — getting blasted with 600 lumens to the face at 2am when you just want a dim light to find your water bottle is an annoyance FLEXTAIL hasn't fully addressed.

**Removable battery.** This is the Tiny Helio's calling card. The 700mAh cell pulls out and charges independently via USB-C, and it's <cite index="2-10">interchangeable with the Zero Pump</cite>, so if you're already carrying a FLEXTAIL pump you can share batteries and shave a few grams. Practically, this means you can carry a $13 spare battery instead of a power bank for extending trips, and swap a dead cell without waiting around for a full USB-C charge cycle.

**Water resistance.** The IP67 rating held up in casual use — reviewers didn't report failures from rain or splashes, and FLEXTAIL's own claim is that it's <cite index="2-15">dust-tight and protected against temporary submersion in water up to 1 meter (3.3 feet) for up to 30 minutes</cite>. That's plenty for typical backpacking exposure, though I wouldn't treat it as swim-proof.

**Red light mode.** One recurring complaint on the previous-gen 600Z: the red light isn't dim enough for its intended purpose. As one reviewer put it, <cite index="4-5">the red light mode is far too bright — defeating its main purpose, which is to preserve night vision and avoid eye strain at night</cite>. If FLEXTAIL hasn't tuned this down for the 2025 refresh, it's worth testing before you count on it for stealthy camp moves or preserving your night vision on a star-gazing trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely light at 62.4g, competitive with or lighter than comparable Nitecore/Black Diamond ultralight models
- Removable, swappable battery is a real advantage over sealed-battery competitors
- 1-lumen mode with up to 100-hour runtime is legitimately useful for multi-day trips
- USB-C charging, and the battery can charge independently of the headlamp body
- IP67 rating held up in real-world rain and splash exposure
- Comfortable bungee headband with foam padding for long wear

**Cons**

- Full 600-lumen output only available in mixed mode, which several reviewers found too bright/inefficient for most trail use
- Default startup brightness is the highest setting, not the lowest — annoying for quick nighttime tasks
- Red light mode reportedly too bright for genuine night-vision preservation
- Beam distance (328 ft) is respectable but not class-leading for night hiking or trail running at pace
- Confusing product variant/SKU selection has tripped up some buyers ordering direct from FLEXTAIL's site

## Who Should Buy This

The Tiny Helio 2025 makes the most sense for thru-hikers and fastpackers who prioritize gram-counting but still want real brightness on tap and don't want to be stuck with a sealed battery if it degrades mid-trip. If you mostly need a headlamp for camp chores, reading, and the occasional night hike, the low-lumen modes and long runtime will serve you well. If you're a trail runner logging serious mileage in full dark and need a consistently strong, evenly-distributed beam for pace, you may want to look at dedicated running headlamps with better beam throw and a more sophisticated dimming curve.

## Verdict

The Tiny Helio 2025 delivers on the core promise: a sub-2.5oz headlamp with legitimate brightness, a swappable battery, and enough runtime flexibility to cover most backpacking scenarios. It's not without rough edges — the overly bright default startup and red mode are minor but persistent annoyances — but for the weight and the removable-battery convenience, it's a solid pick. I'd rate it a 7.5/10: excellent for the ounce-counters, with a couple of firmware/tuning fixes away from being genuinely excellent.
