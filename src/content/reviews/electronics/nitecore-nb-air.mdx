## Overview

The Nitecore NB Air is a 5,000 mAh USB-C power bank in a carbon fiber frame, built for hikers who want one phone charge and nothing more. It weighs 89 g and is thinner than most phones. The trade-off is the usual one for this weight class: one port, no pass-through charging, and a single top-off rather than a multi-day reserve.

## Performance

**Capacity.** The 19.4 Wh figure on the box is the cell's nominal rating. Real-world delivery is lower, which is true of every power bank. The rated energy at 5 V is 3,300 mAh, or roughly 16.5 Wh. CleverHiker's testing found about 16 Wh available, a few short of the advertised number but in line with what the category delivers. That testing also put the NB Air above average for watt-hours per gram. In practice, that is enough to charge most modern smartphones about once, or a little more if the phone isn't in use while it charges. Heavy users and anyone running GPS apps all day will find 5,000 mAh thin.

**Weight and carry.** At 3.14 oz, the NB Air is lighter than a lot of the hardware it recharges. It slides into a hip-belt pocket, a running vest or a pants pocket without being noticed. The rounded corners keep it from snagging on pack fabric. CleverHiker's reviewers called it a standout small-capacity bank for quick overnights and said it practically disappears in a pack.

**Charging speed.** The single USB-C port handles both input and output at up to 18 W, with PD and QC 3.0 support. That is fast enough for phones, headlamps, earbuds and watches. CleverHiker timed a full recharge at 1 hour 38 minutes, which suits a camp-meal or town-stop top-up. An automatic low-current mode keeps sensitive devices such as true-wireless earbuds and fitness watches from tripping the bank's auto shut-off.

**Weather and durability.** IPX7 means the bank survives immersion in 1 m of water for up to 30 minutes, a higher rating than the other banks Pack Hacker has tested. On a wet trail or sweaty trail run, that means no worries about rain, condensation or a dropped bank in a creek. The carbon fiber panels and composite frame are meant to protect the cell and resist flexing in a pack.

**Limitations.** Testers flagged three things. First, there is no pass-through charging, so the bank cannot top up a phone while it is itself being recharged from a wall. Second, there is just the one port. Third, CleverHiker saw a significant drop in efficiency in cold temperatures. Winter and high-altitude hikers should keep it in a pocket or sleeping bag at night. The cable isn't included, so ultralighters will want to budget a few extra grams for a short USB-C cord.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 89 g, thin enough to disappear in a pocket
- IPX7 rating, higher than most competing banks
- 18 W PD/QC in and out from the one port
- Fast recharge, about 1 hr 38 min in testing
- Low-current mode works with earbuds and watches
- Rounded carbon fiber body resists abrasion and impact
- UN38.3 certified for air travel

**Cons**

- One phone charge per cycle, with little margin for heavy users
- No pass-through charging
- Single port means one device at a time
- Measured capacity runs below the 19.4 Wh nominal rating, and it loses more in the cold
- No cable in the box
- About $40 is a premium for 5,000 mAh

## Who Should Buy This

The NB Air suits weekend backpackers, section hikers, trail runners and fastpackers who charge a phone once, run a watch and maybe a headlamp, and want the lightest bank that will do it. It also suits anyone who wants a pocketable backup for travel or commuting. Thru-hikers on long stretches between resupplies, photographers and anyone running a phone, a camera and a GPS watch together should look at a 10,000 mAh bank, such as Nitecore's own NB10000 line, or carry a second battery. People who need to charge two devices at once need a bank with a second port.

## Verdict

The NB Air does one job: a single reliable phone charge at 89 g, in a waterproof body that shrugs off trail abuse. Its limits are real but predictable, and ounce-counters who plan around them get one of the better small banks available. Buy it for overnights and short trips. Skip it for anything that needs more than one charge.
