## Overview

The Urberg Pump Bag is a roll-top polyester sack that does two jobs: it stores gear, and it inflates a sleeping pad without anyone's breath going into it. It suits backpackers who want a cheap, simple fix for mouth-inflation moisture and already own a pad with a 19 mm valve. The trade-off is weight and weather protection. At 134 g it is a hefty accessory for the ultralight crowd, and Urberg lists it as not waterproof.

## Performance

**Inflation.** The method is the standard pump-sack routine. Hold the open end wide to scoop in air, roll the top closed to trap it, then squeeze the bag against the pad's valve. Urberg says the bag fits any sleeping pad with a 19 mm valve, which covers its own pads and many others. The retailer's pitch is that it makes inflation "easy and quick". A bag this size holds a large volume of air per scoop, so a full-length pad typically takes only a few fills.

**Moisture and mold.** This is the reason to own one. Urberg's guidance is blunt: breath carries moisture into the pad and raises the risk of mold, and a pump or pump bag avoids that. For a pad that goes on every trip, that argument is the main case for the product. It also saves a winded hiker from a dizzy few minutes at altitude or after a long day.

**Dual use.** Urberg also sells the bag as a packing sack, and the roll-top opening makes it workable as a stuff sack for clothing or a sleeping bag. The limits are in the spec sheet. It has no compression, a single compartment and no waterproofing. Rain on the trail means a separate dry bag or pack liner for anything that must stay dry, so the "dry bag" use some retailers suggest is optimistic. In practice it is a stuff sack that happens to inflate pads.

**Weight.** At 134 g the bag is the main sticking point for gram-counters. Most thru-hikers weigh a pump sack against the pad it serves and the stuff sack it replaces. Because this one can replace a pad stuff sack or a clothing sack, it can justify its weight on a trip where it does both jobs. As a dedicated pump carried alongside other sacks, it is dead weight.

**Owner ratings.** Shoppers who bought it through Outnorth rate it 4.6 out of 5 across 54 reviews. Norwegian price-comparison listings are more mixed, but those ratings rest on a handful of votes.

**Durability.** 190T polyester is a modest fabric. It is fine for scooping air and stuffing soft gear. It is not built to survive being dragged through brush or stuffed with sharp gear, and it has no coating to resist snags or damp.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Keeps exhaled moisture out of the pad, which protects it from mold
- Fits any 19 mm valve, not just Urberg pads
- Roll-top works as a stuff sack for clothing or a sleeping bag
- Inexpensive, at roughly €24–30
- Strong shopper ratings (4.6/5 from 54 reviews at Outnorth)

**Cons**

- 134 g is heavy for a pump sack
- Not waterproof, so it can't replace a dry bag
- No compression and only one compartment
- Only works with 19 mm valves
- 190T polyester is a basic, unreinforced fabric

## Who Should Buy This

This bag is for weekend and three-season backpackers who own a 19 mm-valve pad, want to stop blowing into it, and like a single item that doubles as a stuff sack. It is especially sensible for owners of Urberg's own pads, which the brand points to as the intended match. Gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere. A purpose-built pump sack or a roll-top dry bag with pump function would do the job with less bulk and with real weather protection. Anyone with a pad that uses a different valve size should check compatibility before buying.

## Verdict

The Urberg Pump Bag solves a real problem, mouth-inflation moisture, at a low price and with a sensible dual-use design. It is a good fit for casual and three-season trips, and a poor one for ultralight setups where every gram and every wet-weather contingency counts. Buy it if you own a 19 mm pad and want a simple upgrade, and skip it if you need a waterproof or featherweight sack.
