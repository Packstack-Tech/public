## Overview

Wool & Prince's Boxer Briefs are a 4.5-inch-inseam merino underwear built around one idea: the leg shouldn't creep up when you walk. They suit hikers and travelers who want to rewear a single pair for days without the smell, and who are willing to pay $48 for the privilege. The trade-off is price and a few durability complaints from long-term testers.

## Performance

**Leg stability.** The design's signature feature is its rotated fabric grain. In most knit underwear the fabric's greatest stretch runs around the leg, so squatting or stepping up pulls the hem upward. Wool & Prince turned the grain 90 degrees so the stretch runs along the leg instead, which lets the leg tube lengthen without dragging the hem north. The design came out of a collaboration with travel blogger Eytan of Snarky Nomad, who had spent years rejecting underwear with loose pouches, wandering legs and badly placed seams. Testers at Pack Hacker reported that the Boxer Briefs 2.0 did not ride up at all, even after a full day of wear. For anyone who walks 20 miles in a day, that is the main reason to buy these.

**Odor and wear time.** Merino does the work here. Pack Hacker's testers wore a pair for seven days in a row without washing; by day seven the pair needed laundering, but with a night of airing out between wears, multiple days in a row were no problem. Loophole Travel's reviewer, who tested the earlier version, described going days without odor, and noted that the underwear dries overnight after a sink wash. That makes a two- or three-pair rotation realistic for a long trip.

**Comfort and fit.** The 200 gsm knit is light, breathable and cool to the touch, and the spandex gives enough stretch to move with the hips. Owners describe the fabric as soft with very little of the itch common in wool, and the waistband gets particular praise. One buyer did find more material in the crotch than expected. The pouch is built for support rather than a minimalist cut. Wool & Prince will send a free pair in a different size if the first doesn't fit, which takes some of the risk out of ordering online.

**Durability.** This is where opinions split. Loophole Travel's writer reported that an original pair was still in rotation after more than two years of global travel, with almost no wear, and said the nylon-and-spandex update was tougher still. Pack Hacker's three-pair test over several months, scored 7.7/10, was less rosy: a hole developed in one pair, the tag started washing out, and testers saw more pilling in the crotch than they'd like. Merino blends are never indestructible, and underwear takes constant friction from a pack's hip belt and sweaty shorts. Line drying, as Wool & Prince recommends, helps.

**Weight and packability.** At 85 g, a pair weighs about as much as a small handful of trail mix. The thin knit packs down to nearly nothing, and a two-pair system adds roughly 170 g to a base weight.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rotated fabric grain keeps the legs in place, with no ride-up reported by testers
- Odor resistance holds up over multiple days of wear
- 85 g and quick to dry
- Soft against the skin, with little wool itch
- Supportive pouch and minimal seams
- Free size exchange; buyers rate the line 4.57 out of 5 across roughly 280 reviews on the brand's site

**Cons**

- $44–48 a pair is several times the price of synthetic hiking underwear
- Reports of holes and crotch pilling from at least one multi-pair test
- Line-dry care takes more effort than synthetics on a resupply day
- The 4.5-inch inseam may feel short to hikers prone to thigh chafe, who will want the 6.5-inch version

## Who Should Buy This

Section hikers and thru-hikers who rewear one or two pairs and want underwear that doesn't smell by day four. Frequent travelers will like it even more. Anyone who has spent a mile tugging at a hem will notice the leg design. Hikers who run through underwear quickly, who are hard on gear, or who see trail underwear as disposable should look at cheaper nylon-spandex options, or at the longer-inseam version if chafe is a worry.

## Verdict

These are among the most thoughtfully designed merino boxer briefs available, and the leg stability and odor control are real. The price and a few durability complaints keep them from a higher score, but for hikers who value comfort over cost, they're a sound buy.
