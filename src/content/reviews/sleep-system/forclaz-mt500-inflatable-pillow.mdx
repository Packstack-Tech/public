## Overview

The Forclaz MT500 is Decathlon's entry-level inflatable camp pillow — a two-piece design with a separate TPU/polyester air bladder and a removable Tencel (Lyocell) cover. At 135g and roughly $15-25 depending on region, it's aimed at backpackers, thru-hikers, and travelers who are tired of stuffing a puffy jacket into a stuff sack and calling it a pillow, but who don't want to spend $40-60 on a Sea to Summit or Nemo equivalent.

## Performance

**Comfort.** This is where the MT500 surprises people. The cover has a small padded foam zone — <cite index="6-8,6-9">a soft Tencel fabric cover lined with foam, paired with an inflatable inner chamber with adjustable air pressure</cite> — which softens the "balloon under your head" feeling that plagues cheaper inflatable pillows. One long-term user who wasn't expecting much reported the pillow was <cite index="9-6">way more comfortable than what I was expecting considering it's inflatable</cite>, and found the trick was <cite index="9-7,9-8">not blowing it up the whole way, but leaving a bit of softness, which made it work well for moving around to different positions in the night</cite>. That matches my own experience with similar half-fill inflatable pillows — max pressure is almost always the wrong call for side sleepers.

**Inflation.** It's genuinely quick to set up. Reviewers note the whole process takes <cite index="8-10,8-11">removing the red valve cap and breathing into it, with 2-3 breaths enough for a comfortable pressure</cite>. One reviewer initially over-inflated it before dialing back, eventually settling on <cite index="8-17,8-18">1.5 to 2 breaths as the perfect amount</cite> after learning that pumping it up as hard as possible was way too firm. The valve has a non-return function, though be warned: <cite index="8-13,8-14">air will rush back at you between breaths</cite>, which catches first-time users off guard.

**Durability & care.** The two-piece cover/bladder design is a real strength — you can wash the Tencel cover without risking the air chamber, and swap the bladder alone if it ever fails. A four-year owner called it <cite index="4-1,4-2">a pillow that, thanks to its durability and comfort, provided a reliable and pleasant sleeping experience over four-plus years</cite>. Decathlon's own review base backs this up — the pillow sits at 4.5-4.7 stars across thousands of reviews, with one user noting it was <cite index="1-1,1-2">an amazing difference in sleep quality, pretty light and compact, easy to clean, and surprisingly tough</cite>.

**Quirks.** A few recurring complaints show up across reviews. First, repacking it into its stuff sack is fiddly — several users mentioned it's <cite index="1-2">tricky to get back into its pouch</cite> and simply stopped bothering. Second, because it's a single air chamber rather than baffled, some testers noted that <cite index="1-8">when your head moves on the pillow, air gets pushed to one side and it deforms, making it less stable than a traditional pillow</cite>. Third, the smooth fabric can be slippery on certain sleeping pad surfaces — one reviewer found it <cite index="8-1,8-2">slippy on their Forclaz MT500 Air trekking mattress, causing it to fall off the mat and wake them during the night</cite>, eventually solving it by tucking the pillow inside their sleeping bag hood. Fourth, the rectangular shape isn't a perfect match for hooded mummy bags — <cite index="4-3">the rectangular shape may not be perfect in hooded sleeping bags</cite>, though this is a minor gripe rather than a dealbreaker.

Compared to the pricier competition, this isn't going to out-plush a Nemo Fillo or Sea to Summit Aeros. But given the sub-$25 price and sub-5-ounce weight, expectations should be calibrated accordingly — and by most accounts, it clears that bar comfortably.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely comfortable for an inflatable pillow, thanks to the foam-padded cover
- Removable, machine-washable Tencel cover — a nice hygiene upgrade over sewn-in covers
- Quick 2-3 breath inflation with adjustable firmness
- Very light (135g) and packs down to 0.6L
- Excellent value — a fraction of the cost of premium competitors
- Reports of multi-year durability from long-term owners

**Cons:**

- Fiddly to repack into its included stuff sack
- Single air chamber can feel unstable/shifty compared to baffled designs
- Slippery cover can slide off some sleeping pad fabrics
- Rectangular shape doesn't tuck neatly into hooded mummy bags
- Not as plush as pricier baffled/foam-hybrid pillows

## Who Should Buy This

This is the right pillow for budget-conscious backpackers, thru-hikers counting ounces, and travelers who want a step up from a stuffed jacket without spending premium-pillow money. If you're a restless side sleeper who needs a pillow that stays put and holds its shape all night, you may want to look at a baffled or contoured design instead — or plan to tuck this one inside your sleeping bag hood as a workaround.

## Verdict

The Forclaz MT500 delivers outsized comfort and durability for its weight and price, with the washable two-piece design being a genuinely thoughtful touch rarely seen at this cost. It's not the most stable pillow on the market and packing it away takes a bit of practice, but for the money, it's hard to beat. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — a smart budget pick that doesn't feel like a compromise.
