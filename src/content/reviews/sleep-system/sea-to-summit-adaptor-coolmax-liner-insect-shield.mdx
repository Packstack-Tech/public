## Overview

The Adaptor COOLMAX Liner is Sea to Summit's warm-weather sleep liner, built from a stretchy polyester knit and treated with Insect Shield permethrin for built-in bug protection. It's aimed at travelers, thru-hikers, and hostel/hut users who want a cool, breathable layer that doubles as a standalone summer "sleeping bag" or a clean, bug-resistant liner inside a proper bag.

## Performance

**Temperature regulation and comfort.** This is where the Adaptor earns its keep. Multiple owners describe it as the Coolmax material transferring moisture out away from your body and stretching as you dream about the next day's travels. One long-term reviewer who used it on a weeklong hut trek in Norway's Jotunheimen National Park noted that it feels like cotton and wicks moisture effectively, making it versatile in mild or cooler temperatures. On the hot end, a Grand Canyon rafter used it while rafting the Grand Canyon in July and felt that it actively wicked moisture and definitely kept the insect world at bay. If you run warm at night or camp in humid climates, this liner is genuinely more comfortable against skin than most silk or cotton alternatives — it has real stretch and doesn't feel clingy.

**Bug protection.** The Insect Shield treatment is the liner's main differentiator from Sea to Summit's non-treated Adaptor and Reactor lines, and it's effective at withstanding up to 25 washes and not dissipating, unlike permethrin sprays. Field reports are generally positive but not universal — one Amazon reviewer called it "excellent for abroad travel in hot and chilled conditions" but only gave 4 stars because there is a smell associated with the insect repellent, a smell that reportedly airs out within a week or so of hanging it up. A hammock forum poster referenced a secondhand account of someone backpacking through jungles in Cambodia who said he stayed bug free with this and no net, though that's an anecdote worth taking with a grain of salt rather than a controlled test.

**Durability and packed size.** The knit fabric holds up to regular washing without falling apart the way silk liners can — one reviewer who'd previously destroyed a silk liner from heavy use called the Coolmax replacement's durability a clear improvement, noting Sea to Summit's CoolMax fabric Traveler version worked out great, with enough room to stretch out and move throughout the night. It also packs small and washes well in day-to-day use. That said, it's not a featherweight: reviewers comparing it against Sea to Summit's own silk and silk-cotton liners found it the heaviest and bulkiest of the three, at 10 oz. and 3×5 inches in its stuff sack — consistent with the 248 g spec here. If you're chasing the lightest possible base weight, this isn't the liner for that; you're paying a weight premium for the bug protection and stretch comfort.

**Fit.** At 210 cm long, most users find room to spare. One reviewer at 5'8" said the roomy dimensions meant it may be the best choice for bigger people, but is not oversized for me — so taller hikers shouldn't feel cramped, and average-height users won't be swimming in it either.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely cooling and moisture-wicking — good for hot, humid nights or as a standalone summer bag
- Insect Shield permethrin treatment adds real bug protection without spraying gear yourself, rated for up to 25 washes
- Stretchy knit fabric is more comfortable than typical liner materials and machine washes easily
- Generous 210 cm length works well for taller users
- Doubles as a clean layer inside hostel beds or rental sleeping bags

**Cons**

- Heaviest and bulkiest option in Sea to Summit's own liner lineup — not an ultralight pick
- Some users report a noticeable chemical smell from the Insect Shield treatment that takes time to air out
- Insect Shield protection isn't permanent — degrades after roughly 25 washes or six months of exposure
- Not available for sale in Canada due to the permethrin treatment
- Premium price relative to basic cotton or silk-blend liners

## Who Should Buy This

This liner makes the most sense for travelers and hikers heading somewhere hot, humid, and buggy — think Southeast Asia, the Gulf Coast, or summer thru-hikes in mosquito country — where you want a washable, cooling layer that also cuts down on bites without a separate permethrin treatment step. It's also a solid choice for hostel-heavy trips like the Camino de Santiago, where a clean sleep layer matters as much as bug protection. If your priority is shaving grams for an alpine ultralight kit, look at Sea to Summit's lighter silk or silk-cotton liners instead.

## Verdict

The Adaptor COOLMAX Liner delivers on its core promise: a cool, stretchy, washable sleep layer with genuinely useful built-in bug protection. It's not the lightest or cheapest liner on the market, and the initial insect-repellent smell is a minor but real complaint, but for hot-climate and bug-heavy trips it's a dependable piece of kit. I'd put it at a 7.5/10 — excellent for its intended use case, less compelling if ultralight weight is your only metric.
