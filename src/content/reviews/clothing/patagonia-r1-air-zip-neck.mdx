## Overview

The R1 Air Zip-Neck is Patagonia's lightest take on its zigzag-grid fleece. It is a quarter-zip pullover built for people who generate a lot of heat in cold air: ski tourers, winter hikers, and shoulder-season backpackers. The trade-off is built into the fabric. The open weave that dumps sweat so well also lets wind straight through, and it pills faster than a flat fleece would.

## Performance

**Breathability.** The R1 Air's defining feature is its fabric. A zigzag pattern alternates thick, raised polyester with recessed, near-see-through mesh channels, and the yarns are hollow to trap air. SectionHiker describes the raised portions as providing warmth and the recessed portions as providing ventilation, in the same spirit as Polartec PowerGrid fleece but warmer. Treeline Review tested the R1 Air on a 10-mile winter run that began near 30°F and ended around 50°F in sun. Its tester never overheated and found the fleece stayed relatively dry even when the base layer underneath was soaked. Outdoor Gear Lab was more measured. It rated the Full-Zip Hoody's breathability as average for the fleece category and noted its testers got sweaty on steep climbs and fast bike rides. Most of the praise comes from people moving steadily in cold air, not from people sprinting in mild conditions.

**Warmth.** The 5.7 oz fabric is light on paper but insulates like a heavier fleece. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found the hoody version comfortable as a stand-alone layer down to roughly 40–45°F unless they were being active, and described the warmth of the women's version as closer to a midweight fleece. Better Trail found it both warmer and more wind-resistant than the classic R1 grid, after high-output hikes in Norway's Lofoten Islands in the 40s and 50s. The Zip-Neck has no hood, so it gives up some of the warmth that testers credit to the Hoody's snug, balaclava-style hood. A quarter-zip does still let you open the chest quickly when a climb heats up.

**Wind and weather.** The same open structure that breathes also leaks. Reviewers consistently describe the R1 Air as a midlayer, not an outer layer. It is not windproof and it has no meaningful water resistance. Under a shell, though, a tester at The Great Outdoors reported it worked well beneath waterproof, windproof, and insulated jackets in different conditions.

**Comfort and durability.** The fuzzy face is soft against skin, and Outdoor Gear Lab's testers liked the lack of abrasive seams on top of the shoulders and in the armpits. The wear is where owners get less enthusiastic. Switchback Travel and Better Trail both note that the fabric loses softness over time. Outdoor Gear Lab says it pills quickly, and its fuzzy exterior collects debris, which is a real consideration if it will be dragged through brush or stuffed in a pack with gritty gear. The slim fit suits layering under a shell, but it runs trim for anyone who wants a loose, casual cut.

**Weight and packability.** At 289 g (about 10.2 oz) the Zip-Neck is a few ounces lighter than the Full-Zip Hoody, which Better Trail weighed at 13 oz. That is not ultralight by cuben-and-down standards, but few fleeces deliver this much warmth per gram. It compresses fairly well, though it is bulkier than a synthetic active-insulation piece.

**Category note.** Patagonia positions this as a midlayer. It can serve as a cold-weather base layer over a thin wicking tee or against bare skin for sleeping, but it is a heavier and fuzzier garment than a standard base layer.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Breathes and dries quickly, even under a sweat-soaked base layer
- Warmer than its 5.7 oz fabric weight suggests
- 289 g, lighter than the hooded version of the same fleece
- Soft against skin with no abrasive shoulder or underarm seams
- Recycled polyester, Fair Trade Certified sewing and bluesign approved fabric

**Cons**

- Open weave offers essentially no wind or rain protection
- Pills quickly and loses softness with use
- Fuzzy surface picks up debris
- No hood, so less warmth for the neck and head than the Full-Zip Hoody
- Slim cut won't suit everyone, and $145 is steep for a fleece

## Who Should Buy This

This is for hikers, skiers, and runners who stay moving in air from freezing to the 40s and want one fleece that will not turn into a wet sponge. It also works for thru-hikers who want a camp and sleep layer that doubles as a hiking midlayer. Buyers who stop often in exposed wind should add a shell, and anyone who wants a hood for cold starts should step up to the Full-Zip Hoody. Those who want a soft, casual fleece that looks new after a season of heavy use should look at flatter, denser fleeces.

## Verdict

The R1 Air Zip-Neck is one of the best fleeces for sustained effort in the cold, and it is lighter than the hooded version. It is a midlayer, and it wears like a performance piece, with pilling and no wind resistance. Buy it to keep moving in the cold, and pair it with a shell for anything else.
