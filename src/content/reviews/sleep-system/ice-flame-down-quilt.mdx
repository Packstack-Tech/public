## Overview

Ice Flame is a China-based maker that sells direct, and its down quilt is the sort of gear that tends to turn up in r/Ultralight threads about getting cottage-brand performance without cottage-brand prices. The quilt weighs 615 g and has a zippered footbox and a DWR-treated shell. It is built for three-season hiking. The trade-off is familiar for budget direct-from-factory gear: a lot of warmth for the money, with a thin shell and a few basic touches that need tending.

## Performance

**Warmth.** On paper, 15°C is a summer rating, and by that measure 615 g is not a particularly svelte quilt. Owners report that it runs warmer than that number suggests. One UK reviewer at Adventure Media described the loft as generous and used an Ice Flame quilt down to 2°C. That was on a pad with an R-value of 4.5, with a light puffy jacket on as well. The same reviewer put comfort at around 5°C and the lower limit near 0°C, noting that figures vary by model. Those are layered-system numbers rather than a bare-quilt rating. Shoppers who sleep cold should treat the quilt as a spring-to-autumn piece and carry a down jacket as insurance.

**Construction.** Box baffles keep the fill from shifting and cut down on cold spots. In a year of use, including one Nikwax down wash, that same reviewer reported no down leakage, no zipper failures and stitching that held, with loft returning after washing. The draft collar and neck snap help seal the top edge. The quilt is roomy enough for side sleepers who shift around at night.

**Footbox and ventilation.** The zippered footbox and cinch cord let the quilt work two ways. Closed, it behaves like a sleeping bag with the footbox sealed. Opened, it becomes a flat blanket for warm nights. A hiker who used an Ice Flame split quilt in the Cascades describes leaving the footbox open for ventilation early in the night and zipping it as temperatures fell. That is the standard quilt trick, and here it costs no extra gear.

**Weather resistance.** The DWR shell sheds dew and light condensation. It is not waterproof, and reviewers recommend pairing it with a tarp or bivy when exposed. One owner used it inside a Lanshan 2 and under a Helium Bivy without trouble. Another reported that a quilt in the line dried in under three hours hung in direct sun. That is a single anecdote, but it fits how down in a light shell generally behaves.

**Packability.** At just over 600 g, it compresses to about the size of a Nalgene bottle. A reviewer fitted it into a 10 L dry bag for bikepacking. A stuff sack and a larger storage bag are included.

**Durability and fit-out.** The shell fabric is thin, and owners treat it that way. One reviewer cautions against cowboy camping on gravel. Pad straps are included on some models but are basic, and at least one owner swapped them for elastic loops. With some DIY rigging it can also serve as a hammock top quilt or underquilt.

**Buying experience.** Ice Flame sells direct, and recent buyers on its site, including US customers, report deliveries in a little over two weeks and fast replies from customer service. Those reviews are for other quilts in the range, but they speak to the brand's order handling. Buyers in the US should expect import timing, not a two-day shipment.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Warmth-to-weight that owners say beats the price
- Box baffles limit down migration and cold spots
- Zippered footbox converts between closed and blanket modes
- DWR shell handles dew and light condensation
- Packs to roughly a water bottle, and fits a 10 L dry bag
- Year-long owner reports of intact stitching, zipper and loft after washing

**Cons**

- 615 g is heavy for a quilt with a 15°C rating
- The shell is thin and needs care on rough ground
- Pad straps are basic and some owners replace them
- Specifications vary by model, so check the product page carefully before ordering
- Direct-from-China ordering means longer waits and a harder return process than a US cottage brand

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers who want a first quilt, or a spare for a hammock or bikepacking rig, will find the most here. It suits those who already carry a puffy and a decent pad and will use the quilt as one layer in a system. It also suits shoppers who care more about price than a brand name.

Gram-counters chasing a sub-500 g quilt, cold sleepers who want a real winter rating in a single piece, and anyone who needs fast domestic returns should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Ice Flame Down Quilt delivers well-built, roomy warmth at a price few established brands can touch, as long as it is treated as a layered three-season system. It is not the lightest quilt for its rating, and the thin shell calls for care. For budget-minded hikers who can wait for shipping, it is an easy recommendation.
