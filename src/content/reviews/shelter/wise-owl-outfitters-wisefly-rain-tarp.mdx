## Overview

The WiseFly is a rectangular 11 x 9 ft ripstop nylon tarp sold mainly as a hammock rain fly, bundled with stakes, guy lines and a waterproof carry bag. It is aimed at hammock campers and weekend backpackers who want generous coverage without paying cottage-cottage-cuben money. The trade-off is plain: at 737 g (26 oz) it is a value tarp, not an ultralight one.

## Performance

**Coverage.** The 11 x 9 ft footprint is the WiseFly's main selling point. Reviewers describe it as ample for a single hammock plus gear underneath, with enough spare width to shade a cooking area or a pack. A reinforced seam runs down the center of the tarp and doubles as a ridgeline reference. Hang the two ridge guy lines taut, then stake out the other four corners and edges. One reviewer at SummitSurfers had it up within minutes, and noted that setup is easier with two people but manageable solo with a few extra tension adjustments.

**Weather protection.** The fabric is PU-coated ripstop nylon rated to 3000 mm, with taped seams and extra stitching at the stress points. That is a sensible spec for a tarp, which sheds rain at an angle and doesn't sit under pooling water the way a tent floor does. Owners commonly use it as a hammock fly, as a standalone shelter over a ground sleeper, and as a rain or wind break for glassing and camp chores. The rectangular cut has no doors or beaks, so coverage in wind-driven rain depends on pitching it low and angling it to the weather. It's a flat sheet, and it asks the user to know a few pitches.

**Weight and packability.** This is where the WiseFly shows its price point. Rokslide members called 26 oz heavy for the size, and one hammock camper there said the low price made the weight penalty tolerable for the occasional trip when a larger tarp is wanted over an ultralight one. For context, the Hammock Bliss XL fly lists at 18 oz, and the Onewind 12 x 10 ft silnylon tarp lists at 1.8 lb with 4000 mm silicone/PU treatment. It stuffs down to roughly 9 x 5 in, which is a reasonable bundle but not a small one.

**Build and kit.** Forum posters who handled it describe the construction as well sewn and sturdy rather than delicate. The kit is complete. SummitSurfers notes the aluminum stakes are bright blue with neon pull cords that help in undergrowth and keep campers from tripping on them at night. The six guy lines are 10 ft long, which is generous. Some users may want to trim them or add tensioners, but they are enough to pitch in most terrain without a trip to the hardware store.

**Reliability record.** Aggregated Amazon sentiment is strongly favorable. One review aggregator that analyzed 429 reviews put about 83 percent as positive and about 11 percent as negative. That pattern fits a product that does its job at a modest price and doesn't try to be anything more.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 11 x 9 ft coverage handles a hammock, gear and a bit of living space
- Taped seams and reinforced stitching at stress points
- Complete kit: four aluminum stakes, six 10 ft guy lines, stake bag, waterproof carry bag
- Low price relative to branded hammock flys
- Simple ridge-line setup that takes only minutes

**Cons**

- 737 g (26 oz) is heavy for a tarp of this size; thru-hikers counting grams will find lighter options
- 210 nylon and a 3000 mm PU coating are fine but not premium; PU-coated nylon can sag when wet
- Plain rectangle with no doors or beaks, so wind-driven rain requires good pitching
- 9 x 5 in packed size is bulkier than silnylon or Dyneema alternatives

## Who Should Buy This

The WiseFly suits hammock campers and car-camping or weekend-backpacking users who want a large, dependable rain fly at a low price and don't mind carrying 26 oz. It also works as a loaner tarp or a backup larger shelter for foul-weather trips. Thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers building a sub-10-pound base weight should look at silnylon or Dyneema tarps, which save a substantial amount of weight. Hammock campers who want doors or full enclosure for cold, windy conditions should consider a hex or catenary-cut fly.

## Verdict

The WiseFly Rain Tarp delivers generous coverage, sensible waterproofing and a full accessory kit at a price that is hard to argue with. It is heavy by ultralight standards, and it earns its keep as a budget workhorse rather than a gram-shaving thru-hike shelter. Recommended for casual hammock campers, not for weight-focused long-distance hikers.
