## Overview

The Blue Ice Firecrest is a 28-liter, frameless day pack built for people who climb in summer and ski in winter and would rather own one bag than three. It borrows the chest pockets of a trail-running vest, adds dual ice tool loops and a ski carry system, and keeps the whole thing at 835 g. The trade-off is structure: it is light and quick, but it asks for tidy packing and modest loads.

## Performance

**Carry and comfort.** The shoulder straps show clear running-vest DNA, with a zippered pocket on one side and a stretch pocket on the other for a phone, snacks or a soft flask. WildSnow's reviewer liked the setup for fast-moving days, and a reviewer at Wacky Yar Adventures found that the pack carries weight well for a frameless design. The padded hip belt stows away when it isn't wanted. On summer approaches with a rope and rack, it moves some of the load to the hips. Owners on Bergfreunde mostly rate the carry as comfortable, though two flag poor back ventilation.

**Structure is the sore spot.** With no frame, the Firecrest depends on what is inside it. Backcountry Snob's reviewer called the lack of structure the main complaint, warning that heavier skis and bulkier kit make the pack finicky to balance. The same reviewer found the shoulder straps thin, and noted that they tend to slide off the shoulders while skiing. Wacky Yar Adventures reached a similar conclusion in a friendlier tone: the shovel handle and probe add some rigidity, but they leave the pack lopsided when it's underfilled.

**Ski and tool carry.** The quick ski carry kit is the feature owners praise most. A stainless steel hook allows diagonal carry, and aluminum G-hooks on the side compression straps handle A-frame carry. WildSnow noted that taking skis on and off without removing the pack saves time at transitions. A few buyers on Blue Ice's own site were puzzled by how the hook is meant to work, so expect a short learning curve. The two ice tool holders get steady credit for being simple and reliable. The pack has no external crampon carry, so spikes go inside.

**Access and storage.** The full-length side zip reaches gloves, a spare bottle or anything at the bottom without unpacking. One buyer worried that a zip under load is the part most likely to blow out when the pack is stuffed. The avalanche pouch is a weaker point. It sits against the back panel, which Backcountry Snob and another Blue Ice reviewer say makes the pack floppier, and WildSnow's reviewer found it eats volume and fits even compact metal shovels tightly. It is removable, which helps in summer. Ropes are another awkward fit: one owner found the rope slipped out from under the lid and needed extra straps, while another carried it hanging outside the pack. A separate owner called the pack a go-to for multipitch climbing, and another carried tools and a rope for ice climbing without trouble.

**Durability.** Reports split. A reviewer with two winters of skiing and two summers of climbing called it very durable. One buyer, however, reported wearing a hole through the pack on a first trip and found the fabric and webbing lighter than he wants for ski mountaineering. WildSnow's verdict was that the pack is light but not a featherweight, with weight traded for durability. In practice, that means a 210d nylon pack that suits climbers who respect it, not people who bash through brush.

**Volume.** Twenty-eight liters is tight for mid-winter touring with full avalanche gear and layers. WildSnow's reviewer said not everyone will get away with it, though a Wacky Yar reviewer said the pack felt larger than its rating when compared with a friend's 38-liter bag.

## Choosing a Version

The Firecrest 28 comes in two back lengths, S/M and M/L. Volume and features are the same in both, so the choice is fit: pick by torso length, since a frameless pack with a hip belt and load lifters depends on the belt landing at the right height. Blue Ice also makes a larger 38 L Firecrest, listed at 965 g, for those who find 28 L too tight for winter.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 835 g with a full set of ski and tool carry features, and 748 g with the ski parts removed
- Chest pockets and a stretch side pocket keep snacks, a phone and a flask reachable without stopping
- Full-length side zip reaches the bottom of the pack
- Quick ski carry with both diagonal and A-frame options
- Stashable hip belt and removable avalanche pouch suit year-round use
- PFC-free, Bluesign-approved fabric at a list price of $159.95

**Cons**

- No frame, so heavy or awkward loads pack unevenly
- Avalanche pouch placement hurts balance, and shovels fit tightly
- Thin shoulder straps can slip, according to one reviewer
- Mixed reports on fabric durability
- No external crampon carry
- Rope attachment under the lid is awkward, and back ventilation is poor, according to some owners

## Who Should Buy This

Ski tourers and alpine climbers who go out for single days and keep their kits tight. It suits someone who wants one pack for spring ski traverses, ice routes and summer multipitch, and who values chest pockets and a fast ski carry. Anyone hauling heavy skis, a big rack plus a rope on a regular basis, or full winter layers should look at a bigger or more rigid pack. Hard-use mountaineers who worry about abrasion should also look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Firecrest 28 does a lot at 835 g and delivers a thoughtful, versatile day pack for light-and-fast alpine and ski days. Its softness and fabric weight keep it from being a do-it-all workhorse, so it rewards careful packing. It is a strong buy for minimalist single-day users and a poor one for anyone who regularly overloads a pack.
