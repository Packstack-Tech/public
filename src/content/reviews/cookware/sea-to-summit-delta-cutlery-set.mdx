## Overview

The Delta Cutlery Set is a three-piece plastic knife, fork and spoon kit that clips together on a small carabiner and slips inside Sea to Summit's Delta Bowl. It's priced around $12, and it's built for people who want a full place setting rather than a single spoon. The trade-off is weight: at 43 g it is a comfortable-camping set, not a gram-counter's one.

## Performance

**Size and fit.** At 6 inches, the pieces are shorter than a standard camp spoon, and that's deliberate. They are cut to ride inside a Delta Bowl, and testers at Backpackgeartest.org found the fit there snug but workable. The short length has a cost in deep pouches: reaching the bottom of a freeze-dried bag means getting knuckles close to the food. Owners with large hands have also described the handles as a touch too small.

**The spoon.** The bowl is wide and shallow, and the packaging rates it at about 10 ml, which is enough to scoop oatmeal or macaroni without fuss. It is not a long-handled spoon, so it suits bowls and mugs better than a bag.

**The fork.** The tines are blunt, with a flat face made for scooping and lifting rather than spearing. One tester's first reaction was skepticism about stabbing anything with it. Pasta, rice and beans are fine. A chunk of salami is a negotiation.

**The knife.** This is the surprise of the set. One edge is serrated and the other is a plain spreader, so it handles peanut butter and cream cheese as well as sausage and cheese. One reviewer reported that it cuts better than its price suggests and could get through a steak. Testers note that the serrated section is longer than the manufacturer's photos imply, roughly three-quarters of the blade on one reviewed set, so spreading is the lesser job.

**Feel and durability.** The glass-reinforced polypropylene flexes noticeably in the hand, which reads as flimsy at first. Reviewers and retailers describe it as strong in practice, and the material resists stains and odors, so tomato sauce and curry should not leave a permanent mark. Washing is easy on trail, and the set goes in a home dishwasher as well. REI shoppers rate it 4.3 out of 5 across 53 reviews, and Bergfreunde customers gave it 4.8 out of 5, with 94 percent recommending it.

**The carabiner.** It's a small plastic clip that keeps the three pieces together. One tester found it worth carrying despite the added grams, since the smooth pieces slide around each other otherwise. It also lets the set hang off a pack or a stove stand, out of the dirt.

**Weight in context.** A reviewer's decade-old polycarbonate spoon weighs about half as much as this entire set. Sea to Summit's own Delta Spork with knife is listed at 16 g. For a thru-hiker who eats everything with one spoon, the extra 25 g or more is not buying much.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- A real knife, fork and spoon for about $12
- Serrated/plain knife handles both spreading and cutting
- Nests inside the Delta Bowl, so it adds little pack volume
- Resists stains and odors, and is dishwasher-safe
- Carabiner keeps the set together and doubles as a hang loop

**Cons**

- 43 g is heavy for the ultralight crowd, and a single spoon weighs far less
- Short handles are awkward in deep bags and small for large hands
- Blunt fork tines; the plastic flexes under load
- Most useful with the Delta Bowl, which is sold separately

## Who Should Buy This

Buy it if you already carry a Delta Bowl, if you cook real meals on trail (cheese, sausage, tortillas and spreads), or if you want a single cheap kit that moves between the pack, the car and the office. It also works for kids and for anyone short on pack space.

Skip it if you hike on freeze-dried meals from the bag, or if you count grams. A long-handled spoon, or a spork with a spreader knife, covers that job at a fraction of the weight.

## Verdict

The Delta Cutlery Set is a well-sized, durable and inexpensive kit whose knife outperforms its price. It is a good fit for weekend backpackers and Delta Bowl owners. Thru-hikers and long-handle diners should choose a lighter, longer single utensil.
