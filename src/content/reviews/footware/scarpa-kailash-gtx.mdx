## Overview

The SCARPA Kailash GTX is a mid-to-heavyweight leather backpacking boot that spent years as one of the go-to choices for hikers who wanted real ankle support and all-day durability without stepping up into a full mountaineering boot. It's built in Italy from suede leather with a Gore-Tex Performance Comfort liner and a Vibram outsole — the kind of spec sheet that signals "this boot is meant to carry a loaded pack, not just stroll a local trail." SCARPA has since discontinued the Kailash GTX in favor of the Kailash Trek GTX and Kailash Plus GTX, but a lot of the DNA (and a lot of secondhand pairs still floating around) makes it worth understanding what this boot did well and where it fell short.

## Performance

The Kailash GTX earned its reputation as a load-hauler. <cite index="9-3">Backpacker's testers called it the best combination of protection, traction, and stability in its category, an update to a boot they'd long loved as an Editors' Choice winner back in 2008.</cite> That's not a small claim — this boot had staying power in a market that churns through models every couple of seasons.

Underfoot, the story is stiffness and stability rather than cushioned comfort. <cite index="9-4">The midsole is durable PU augmented by a TPU film that adds rigidity and structure to the boot</cite>, and testers felt it — <cite index="9-6">one described the midsole as firm and torsionally stiff, and combined with the V-shaped Vibram lugs, it made them feel "mountain-goat sure" even carrying 45-pound loads on sketchy terrain.</cite> That's the tradeoff you're signing up for with a boot like this: it's not going to feel nimble on a casual day hike, but load it up with a full pack and it starts to make sense.

Lacing and ankle lockdown get consistent praise across reviews. <cite index="9-1">Testers found lacing them up tight from toe to ankle fast and fluid thanks to pull-through hardware, with grippy webbing lace loops at the ankle keeping feet completely slip-free inside the boot.</cite> <cite index="9-8">One tester praised the ankle support specifically, noting the contoured, ample padding provided the right amount of buttressing without feeling so stiff that it hindered a comfortable stride.</cite>

Waterproofing performance is where the Gore-Tex liner earns its keep. On the women's version (mechanically similar construction), <cite index="8-16,8-17">testers reported that after a spring hike through snow patches and sloppy mud, their feet stayed completely dry thanks to the Gore-Tex Performance Footwear lining.</cite> Long-term Sierra customer reviews back this up in practice — one buyer on their fifth pair over the years noted they'd stuck with the boot specifically because it solved a chronic foot pain issue for them, which says something about fit consistency across purchases.

Durability is where opinions split. Trailspace reviewers are all over the map: one long-term owner said <cite index="10-6,10-7,10-8">they loved the boot, calling it extremely durable with little wear after many miles and very breathable, adding they no longer got blisters even in humid conditions</cite>. But right above that in the same reviews thread, another owner reported the opposite experience — <cite index="10-1,10-2">despite being impressed out of the box with the comfort, support, and performance, they had not found the boot to be durable, unlike other Scarpa boots they'd owned</cite>. That's a real split, and it's worth taking seriously — durability on this model seems to vary more than you'd expect from a brand known for rugged construction.

Fit is another recurring theme. <cite index="10-17">One reviewer flagged it as a relatively narrow boot, noting not everyone has broad feet</cite> — worth knowing if you're used to wider lasts. Sizing seems mostly true-to-size according to buyer feedback, though as always with leather boots, expect a break-in period and consider sizing carefully if you're between widths.

One nitpick that shows up more than once: the stock footbed doesn't quite match the rest of the build quality. <cite index="10-15">One reviewer specifically called out that the footbed inserts do not match the quality of the rest of the boot</cite> — an easy fix with an aftermarket insole, but worth budgeting for if comfort out of the box matters to you.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent ankle support and lockdown, even under heavy pack loads
- Genuinely waterproof — multiple testers report dry feet through snow, mud, and rain
- Vibram outsole with aggressive lugs delivers confident traction on rough, technical terrain
- Torsionally stiff midsole rewards hikers carrying real backpacking weight
- Long track record — this wasn't a one-off design, it earned repeat buyers over years

**Cons**

- Durability reports are inconsistent — some owners get years of hard use, others see premature wear
- Runs narrow; not ideal for wide or flat feet without sizing up
- Stiffness that helps under a loaded pack feels overkill and clunky on casual day hikes
- Stock footbed is a weak point relative to the rest of the boot's construction
- Discontinued — you're now shopping secondhand or clearing out old stock, with the Kailash Trek GTX and Kailash Plus GTX as the current equivalents

## Who Should Buy This

This boot made the most sense for backpackers carrying real weight — think 30+ lb packs over multi-day trips on rough or off-trail terrain — rather than for day hikers or thru-hikers prioritizing minimal weight. If you have narrow-to-average width feet and want a leather boot that trades a bit of everyday nimbleness for load-bearing stability and genuine waterproofing, the Kailash GTX (or its Trek/Plus successors) fits the bill. It's not the right call for anyone chasing an ultralight kit or anyone with wide feet who hasn't tried the fit first.

## Verdict

The Kailash GTX built its reputation fairly: strong ankle support, reliable waterproofing, and a Vibram sole that handles rough terrain with authority. The durability inconsistency in user reports and the narrow fit keep it from being a universal recommendation, and since it's discontinued, most buyers today will end up looking at the Kailash Trek GTX or Kailash Plus GTX anyway. I'd rate the original Kailash GTX a 7.5/10 — a genuinely capable backpacking boot with a couple of rough edges that its successors have presumably tried to sand down.
