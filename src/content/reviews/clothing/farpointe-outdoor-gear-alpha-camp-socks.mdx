## Overview

The Alpha Camp Socks are a single-purpose item: a pair of fleece socks, made in the USA from Polartec Alpha Direct, that weigh roughly what a pair of AAA batteries does. They're built for the hour between peeling off damp trail socks and falling asleep, and for the cold-foot hours after. The trade-off is built into the design: a porous, gauzy fabric that breathes and weighs next to nothing is not a fabric that tolerates abrasion, so these never leave camp.

## Performance

Polartec Alpha is the fabric that took over ultralight hoodies: an open, gauze-like polyester knit that traps warmth without trapping sweat. FarPointe, a Newport, Oregon, maker that has been sewing since 2021, applies the same logic to the feet. SectionHiker's review describes the result as a surprising mix of warmth and airiness, and notes that the socks feel cozy rather than stuffy when pulled on over feet fresh out of damp hiking socks. That is the whole pitch, and it holds up.

Alpha Direct's structure matters here. Because the fabric breathes and dries quickly, a sleeper who runs warm at the feet isn't left with a clammy sock by 2 a.m., a common complaint with thicker fleece or wool. Against the skin the fabric is soft and non-itchy, according to SectionHiker, which also points to a use beyond the sleeping bag: wearing them in camp shoes or down booties.

Durability is where the limits show. FarPointe is explicit that the socks aren't meant for walking in, and SectionHiker's guidance is to avoid rough ground without shoes and not to expect them to survive constant abrasion inside footwear. A thru-hiker who treats them as a sleep-system layer should get long service. One who wears them to the privy barefoot-in-socks at 3 a.m. over gravel will shorten that.

At 21–25 g for a 90gsm pair (Large runs about 32 g), the weight is a rounding error in a base weight. The packed volume is small enough to stash in a quilt stuff sack or a pocket. Care is simple: cold wash, air dry, ideally in a mesh bag.

One practical note: FarPointe ships to retailers in quarterly batches, and Garage Grown Gear says color runs are limited and may change between restocks. Specific sizes and colors sell out and come back.

## Choosing a Version

Two choices matter: size and fabric.

**Size.** Small fits up to Men's US 10; Large fits up to Men's US 13. Those on the border should size up, since a snug fleece sock that squeezes the toes works against the circulation it's meant to help. Large 90gsm pairs run about 32 g.

**Fabric.** The 60gsm version is the lightest at about 16 g and suits three-season trips or warm sleepers. The 90gsm, at roughly 21–25 g, is the standard option and the right default for cold shoulder-season nights. The Duo 60gsm, a 2-layer build at about 29 g, puts extra loft between the layers for more warmth per gram, and suits cold sleepers heading into freezing temperatures. Its small premium in weight over the 90gsm is the price of that extra insulation.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Roughly an ounce for noticeable warmth in a sleeping bag or quilt
- Breathable, quick-drying Alpha Direct fabric avoids the damp-foot problem
- Tall cuff tucks under thermal bottoms to close the gap at the ankle
- Three fabric weights allow tuning to conditions
- Soft against the skin; packs tiny
- Made in the USA

**Cons**

- $35 is a lot for a pair of socks that only work at camp
- Not durable enough for walking, rough ground, or footwear
- Colors and sizes sell out between quarterly batches
- Single-use item that does nothing on the trail

## Who Should Buy This

Cold-footed sleepers who already count grams will get the most from these: thru-hikers and section hikers with a quilt or light bag who want a dedicated dry pair for sleeping, and shoulder-season campers who find regular wool socks too bulky or too sweaty in the bag. Anyone who wants one sock for hiking and sleeping should look elsewhere, as should hikers on warm-weather trips where a spare pair of thin merino does the job. For those who want something durable and don't mind the extra weight, wool is the better pick.

## Verdict

The Alpha Camp Socks do one job well: they turn Polartec Alpha's warmth-to-weight advantage into a sleeping-bag luxury that costs about an ounce. The price and the keep-them-off-the-trail rule keep them from being a universal pick, but for ultralight hikers who sleep cold, they earn their place in the stuff sack.
