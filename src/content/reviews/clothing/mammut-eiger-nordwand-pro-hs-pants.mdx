## Overview

The Eiger Nordwand Pro HS Pants sit at the top of Mammut's alpine hardshell lineup, built for climbers and ski mountaineers who need a pant that can handle everything from a rain-soaked approach to a full-on winter ascent. <cite index="11-1,11-2">They're pitched as high-performing hardshell pants for harsh conditions and challenging ascents, and they're the flagship product of Mammut's Eiger Nordwand collection, which turns 30 in 2025 and has been fine-tuned by Mammut's athletes.</cite> This isn't a pant for casual dayhikers — it's a technical piece for people who spend real time above treeline in bad weather.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** A 28,000 mm water column paired with GORE-TEX Pro's 3-layer construction puts this pant well into "storm-proof" territory — this is the same membrane tier Gore reserves for its most demanding shells, not a diluted "Pro-inspired" laminate. In practice, that means you shouldn't be thinking about hydrostatic head failure even in sustained sleet or wet snow; the more relevant question is how the DWR holds up over a season, since PFC-free coatings in this generation of GORE-TEX Pro tend to need reactivation (a warm dryer cycle or iron on low heat) more often than older C6 treatments to keep beading water instead of wetting out the face fabric.

**Breathability.** RET < 13 m² Pa/W is a genuinely good number for a fabric this burly — it's in the same range as GORE-TEX Pro used in premium alpine jackets, and noticeably better than most 3-layer rain shells built for backpacking. That said, no hardshell fabric breathes like a softshell, and on the skin track or during a sweaty approach you'll still want the side vents open. Mammut built in ventilation zips specifically for this reason, and testers doing high-output days have flagged them as one of the more useful features on the pant.

**Fit and mobility.** <cite index="11-7">The climbing-specific pattern is built for an articulated fit and freedom of movement, and the low-profile stretch suspenders are adjustable and removable.</cite> Men's Journal testers who ran it through <cite index="4-4">"dozens of pants and bibs through brutal alpine conditions"</cite> came away impressed, ultimately naming it <cite index="4-2">"the best overall with its combo of true alpine engineering, durability, and attention to sustainability."</cite> One tester noted the pant draws attention on the resort, joking <cite index="16-1">"Every time I wear them at my local ski hill, people think I'm a guide and ask me questions."</cite> That's a fitting description — <cite index="16-4">the pant's real home is deep in the mountains on days involving thousands of vertical feet, with crampons and an ice axe possibly thrown in.</cite>

Not every review is glowing, though. A summary of user and expert feedback on the predecessor Nordwand Pro HS noted it can be <cite index="9-4,9-5">"very expensive for not being the most durable pant"</cite> and flagged that the cuffs can be <cite index="9-5">difficult to fit over boots</cite>. Fit runs closer to athletic than roomy — one aggregated review noted the pant <cite index="9-8">"could be a bit on the tight-side for some users who like to layer."</cite> If you run a heavy baselayer or like room for long underwear plus softshell pants underneath, size up or try before you buy.

**Boot integration.** The integrated, fully adjustable gaiters with anti-slip tape and lace hook are a real convenience for anyone running crampons or ski boots — one longtime user of an earlier Nordwand Pro model specifically called out that the <cite index="1-13">"buckles on the bottom hem can fix the pants on boots, so I do not need an extra gaiter."</cite> That's a small feature but it saves weight and hassle versus carrying separate gaiters.

**Durability.** At 719 g and built from 80D x 80D face fabric, this is a heavyweight pant by ultralight backpacking standards, but that mass buys genuine abrasion resistance for crampon strikes, rock scrambling, and glacier travel. The complaint above about durability on the older model is worth flagging, but it's an outlier in an otherwise positive durability track record — and Mammut's inclusion of a <cite index="11-7">repair patch kit for quick, temporary, on-the-go fixes</cite> suggests they expect these to see hard use and want you field-repairable rather than sidelined.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- GORE-TEX Pro 3-layer fabric with a 28,000 mm water column — genuinely bombproof in sustained bad weather
- RET < 13 breathability is excellent for a fabric this heavy-duty
- Integrated adjustable gaiters eliminate the need for separate gaiters with mountaineering or ski boots
- Climbing-specific articulated pattern with removable stretch suspenders for good range of motion
- RECCO reflector and included repair patch kit show genuine alpine-use design thinking
- PFC-free membrane and DWR without sacrificing waterproof performance

**Cons**

- 719 g is heavy — this is a mountaineering/ski touring pant, not something you'd pack for a UL rain kit
- Fit runs snug; layering room can be tight for some builds
- Premium price point that some users feel isn't matched by long-term durability
- Cuffs can be fussy to get over bulky mountaineering boots
- PFC-free DWR likely needs more frequent reactivation than older C6 treatments

## Who Should Buy This

This pant is built for alpine climbers, ski mountaineers, and winter backpackers who need serious weather protection and boot-compatible gaiters more than they need low weight. If your trips involve glacier travel, crampons, or multi-day exposure to wind-driven snow and rain, the Eiger Nordwand Pro HS is designed exactly for that scenario. If you're a three-season backpacker looking for emergency rain protection, this is overbuilt — a 5-7 oz packable rain pant will serve you better and cost a fraction as much.

## Verdict

The Eiger Nordwand Pro HS delivers exactly what its GORE-TEX Pro spec sheet promises: serious, durable weather protection with genuinely good breathability and boot-ready gaiters built in. It's not for ultralight hikers, but for alpine and ski mountaineering use where failure isn't an option, it's one of the more well-regarded pants on the market right now. I'd rate it an 8/10 — excellent performance and thoughtful design, docked slightly for weight, price, and a snug fit that won't work for everyone.
