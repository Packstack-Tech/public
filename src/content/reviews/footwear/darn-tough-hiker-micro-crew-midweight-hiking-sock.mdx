## Overview

The Darn Tough Hiker Micro Crew Midweight, style 1466, is the sock that shows up on more Appalachian Trail and Pacific Crest Trail feet than any other. It's a 73 g merino-nylon blend with medium cushioning, knit in Vermont and sold with an unconditional lifetime guarantee. The trade-off is price: it costs several times what a bargain-bin synthetic does, and it asks you to believe the guarantee will make up the difference.

## Performance

**Durability.** This is the sock's reputation, and the reports back it up. Darn Tough describes a fine-gauge knit that resists snags and pulls, and a reinforced footbed and Achilles cushion. Owners put the wear into plainer terms: one reviewer said he lent three pairs to a friend after four years of use without a single hole, and another described a pair bought several years earlier as still like new. A REI buyer said the socks replaced a pair of Smartwool that had a hole inside 100 miles.

**Fit and blisters.** The fit is what separates the 1466 from ordinary wool socks. The Treeline Review team put the sock through thru-hikes of the AT and PCT with dozens of hikers, in temperatures from the teens to above 100°F. They found blisters rare to nonexistent and said the sock keeps its shape through the day. Outdoors Magic's long-term tester made a similar claim, saying he can't recall a blister in these socks. Darn Tough's own review summaries single out fit as the top-mentioned quality, with an overall rating of about 4.9 out of 5 across more than 4,400 reviews on its site.

**Cushion and warmth.** The medium cushion uses terry loops under the foot, with protection that extends over the ankle and Achilles. Treeline calls it a "Goldilocks" level: too thin and the sock wears through in a week, too thick and the shoe needs a half-size up. That matches what trail runners and boot wearers report. It cushions without taking up toe-box volume. A midweight sock is also an all-season one, warm enough for shoulder-season mornings in Minnesota and breathable enough for summer, though people who run hot in desert heat may prefer Darn Tough's Coolmax version of the same sock.

**Moisture and odor.** Merino moves moisture off the skin, and the sock dries quickly for a cushioned wool. Owners consistently point to the odor resistance. A first-time wool buyer said it works as advertised, and thru-hikers who rotate two pairs for weeks tend to agree.

**Quirks.** The 8.5 in. micro-crew height clears a low shoe by about 6 in. and ends just above a standard hiking boot. Some buyers find that too short, and one complained of the cuff barely clearing the ankle bone. A different owner reported extra fabric bunching where the foot meets the shin, right under the tongue. Sizing reports are mixed. Some customers say to size down, while at least one owner moved up from a large to an XL compared with Smartwool. Anyone between sizes should try a pair before buying more.

**Guarantee.** The lifetime promise is simple. If a pair fails, send it back for a new one. Treeline's testers report that the process is easy and works, and they've done it themselves.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Thru-hike durability, with reports of multi-year wear and no holes
- Fit that stays put, with very few blister reports
- Medium cushion that doesn't force a bigger shoe
- Merino blend resists odor over multi-day wear
- True Seamless toe, with no seam rubbing
- Unconditional lifetime guarantee, made in Vermont

**Cons**

- High price per pair
- Micro-crew height is short for some, especially with tall boots or gaiters
- Sizing runs inconsistent according to owner reports
- Medium cushion can feel warm in extreme heat
- A few owners report fabric bunching under the tongue

## Who Should Buy This

Anyone who hikes more than a few weekends a year. That means section hikers, thru-hikers and backpackers in low-cut shoes or trail runners, who want one sock for three seasons and are willing to pay up front for a pair they won't replace. Long-trail hikers get the most from the guarantee, since a worn heel on trail becomes a free pair at home. Those who want the lightest possible sock, or something for deep summer heat, should look at a thinner no-cushion sock or the Coolmax version. People who wear tall boots and want the cuff up high should choose a taller height.

## Verdict

The 1466 earns its place by doing the basics extremely well: it fits closely, resists wear, and doesn't stink after a week. It costs more than most socks, but years of owner reports and thru-hiker testing suggest the price is spread over a long life. For most backpackers, it's the sock to buy first.
