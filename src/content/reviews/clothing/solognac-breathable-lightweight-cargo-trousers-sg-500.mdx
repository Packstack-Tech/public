## Overview

The Solognac SG-500 is Decathlon's warm-weather field trouser: a thin, stretchy polyamide cargo pant designed for stalking, fishing and wildlife watching, and one that has quietly built a following among walkers for the same reasons. It weighs 355 g in size L, carries five pockets, and sells for roughly ₹1,999 in India and £29.99 in the UK. The trade-off is straightforward. It is cheap, tough and quick-drying, but it is a hunting pant first, with a cut and a rustle to match, and not a purpose-built ultralight hiking trouser.

## Performance

**Fabric and breathability.** At 157 g/m², the polyamide weave is thin enough to stay comfortable when the temperature climbs, and owners describe it as breathable for a synthetic. A UK reviewer who wore a pair for about two years reported using them for everything from dog walks to a Snowdon ascent, and rated them more comfortable than other trousers he had tried. The 6% elastane gives enough give for scrambling, high steps and crouching, and the preformed knees help on climbs. Polyamide dries quickly, so a stream crossing or a shower is a short-term nuisance rather than an all-day one.

**Durability.** Solognac says the main fabric survived 60,000 rubs without breaking, and the construction backs that up on paper: double-stitched assembly seams plus bar tacks at the stress points that usually fail first, namely pocket corners, belt loops and the bottom of the fly. Hunters on UK stalking forums describe Solognac's trousers as comfy and tough, and several note the price is a fraction of rival field brands. There is no reinforced panel at the knees or seat, so this is a thorn-and-bark pant rather than a bramble-armor one.

**Pockets.** The layout is practical: two hand pockets, two thigh cargo pockets and a zipped pocket on the right leg for a phone or keys. The thigh pockets are lined with bright orange open-weave mesh, an odd choice but a functional one, since it lets the pockets drain and vent.

**Fit and sizing.** The semi-elastic waist adapts across body shapes, and the belt loops take a 50 mm belt. The size run is wide, from S to 3XL. The leg is long, though, and buyers around 5'9" frequently mention hemming them. Some UK owners also report that sizing can be inconsistent between pairs, so it pays to try them on before a trip.

**Noise and weather.** The thin synthetic can swish. One stalker returned a pair of Solognac 500s as too noisy, which matters for hunting and wildlife watching and not much for walking. The trousers are neither windproof nor waterproof, and owners agree they are for warm, dry conditions. Cold starts call for a thermal layer underneath, and rain calls for an overtrouser.

**Value.** Decathlon's India listing shows 4.8 stars from roughly 3,500 reviews, with a large majority saying the fit is as expected. Few pants at this price match that consistency.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 355 g with a thin, breathable 157 g/m² fabric that suits hot days
- Stretch and preformed knees move well on scrambles and steps
- Double stitching and bar tacks at the typical failure points
- Five pockets, including a zipped one for small items
- Very low price with a 2-year warranty
- Wide size range, S to 3XL

**Cons**

- Fabric can rustle, a problem for stalking and wildlife watching
- Long legs and occasional size variation between pairs
- No wind or water resistance
- No reinforcement panels at knees or seat
- Heavier and bulkier than dedicated ultralight hiking pants, with a cargo cut that carries more fabric than a minimalist hiker needs

## Who Should Buy This

Warm-weather walkers, anglers, birders and day hikers who want a tough, quick-drying pant with real pockets and don't want to spend much. It also suits budget-minded section hikers heading into dry, hot terrain, where a cheap pair that can be hemmed and abused is more useful than a premium one. Gram-counting thru-hikers, stealth-focused hunters who need near-silent fabric, and anyone expecting wind or rain protection should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The SG-500 does a narrow job well: it keeps legs cool, dries quickly and shrugs off scrub, for the price of a lunch for two. Buyers should check the length, expect some fabric noise, and treat it as a warm-weather pant only.
