## Overview

The NEMO Forte 35 is a synthetic three-season sleeping bag cut in NEMO's Spoon shape, with extra room at the elbows and knees for people who sleep on their sides. It costs far less than a down bag with the same rating, and it keeps insulating when damp. The trade-off is weight, bulk, and a 35°F rating that cold sleepers should treat as optimistic.

## Performance

**Comfort and shape.** Comfort is the reason this bag exists. NEMO's published girth runs 64 inches at the shoulder, 59 at the hip and 62 at the knee, which is looser at every point than a standard mummy. Backpackers.com's reviewers said the spoon cut gives side sleepers much more knee room while holding almost as much heat as a mummy. Forbes called the hood puffy and easy to cinch without feeling restrictive, and noted an oversized draft collar and a draft tube along the zipper. SectionHiker found the hood large enough to hold a pillow inside, and the perimeter draft tube still pulls it snug around the face. The shell and liner feel soft rather than slick or plasticky, as many synthetic bags do.

**Warmth.** The 35°F rating is a threshold, not a comfort promise. Backpacker's testers were comfortable at 40°F under the stars near Moab. They advised cold sleepers to add thick socks or down booties in the thirties, because the roomy footbox holds a lot of air to heat. A SectionHiker reviewer who sleeps cold was chilly from the low 30s into the low 40s, even with stacked pads, long underwear and a down jacket. Backpackers.com judged the rating fairly accurate for typical use, and Treeline Review's tester found the bag right for summer and early fall in the Cascades and the Badlands, sometimes adding a liner as temperatures dropped. For most sleepers the practical range starts around 40°F. The 31°F lower limit takes a warm pad and extra layers.

**Weight and packability.** SectionHiker's scale read 984 g for the 960 g bag, plus 82 g for the compression sack. That is a fair figure for a synthetic bag at this rating, but it is well above what a 35°F down quilt or bag weighs. Reviewers generally find the stuffed size small for a synthetic. A Great Outdoors tester said she was surprised how small it compressed. Treeline's reviewer measured 6.2 L compressed for a long Forte 35 and said ultralight backpackers may want something more compact. On a sub-10-pound base weight, the Forte is a luxury item.

**Wet weather and durability.** Synthetic fill keeps working when damp, which is the main practical reason to pick this over down in a wet climate. A Great Outdoors tester noted it stays warm when damp. SectionHiker adds the usual caveat: synthetic insulation loses loft with repeated compression and wears out faster than down. Treeline's tester saw no cold spots after a full season, but stored the bag outside its compression sack to keep it that way. That habit is worth copying.

**Zippers and venting.** The zipper is the most contested part of the design. The bag has a full-length side zip with two sliders, so the feet can be vented while the rest stays closed, plus Thermo Gill vents for dumping heat without letting in drafts. Forbes praised the no-catch zippers and the reinforced draft collar. Others disagree. SectionHiker flagged a snagging zipper, and a Great Outdoors tester found the full-length zip catchy at times. REI owners rate the Men's bag 2.6 out of 5 across nine reviews, and one headline complains that the zipper design ruins an otherwise great bag. Anyone who has fought a stuck zipper in a dark tent should weigh that.

## Choosing a Version

The Forte 35 comes in two Regular-length versions on this page: the Men's Regular at 907 g and the Standard Regular at 960 g. The temperature rating, 31°F ISO lower limit and spoon cut are the same for both, and Regular length fits sleepers up to 6 ft. The practical difference is 53 g, or about 1.9 oz. That is less than a pair of camp socks and not a reason to pick one over the other.

Choose by fit. Sleepers under 6 ft who want the Forte's roomy cut should buy whichever version they find in stock. Anyone taller than 6 ft needs NEMO's Long length, which fits up to 6 ft 6 in and adds about 5 oz. Men's and Women's Fortes have zippers on opposite sides, so a Men's and a Women's bag can zip together into a double.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Spoon shape gives real knee and hip room for side sleepers
- Synthetic fill keeps insulating when damp
- Compression sack included, and the bag packs small for a synthetic
- Soft shell and liner, with a well-regarded hood and draft collar
- Costs far less than a comparable down bag; REI listed the Men's at $189.95

**Cons**

- Heavier and bulkier than down bags rated to 35°F
- Real-world warmth runs cooler than the rating for cold sleepers
- Zipper design draws repeated complaints
- Synthetic insulation loses loft with repeated compression
- Roomy footbox is harder to keep warm near the lower limit

## Who Should Buy This

The Forte 35 suits side and back sleepers who dislike the squeeze of a mummy bag and want one bag for summer and shoulder-season trips. It also works for the car camper moving into backpacking who wants a bag that can do both. Wet-climate hikers who want insurance against damp will get value from it. Gram-counting thru-hikers, cold sleepers who expect to use the full 35°F range, and restless sleepers who change position all night should look at a down quilt or a snugger down bag.

## Verdict

The Forte 35 does what it sets out to do: a roomy, comfortable synthetic bag at a modest price and a reasonable 907–960 g. Treat the rating as optimistic, plan on using it above 40°F without extra layers, and expect the zipper to test your patience. For comfort-first backpackers on a budget it is a sound buy. Ultralight purists should skip it.
