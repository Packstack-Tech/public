## Overview

The Berghaus Verden 65+10 is a conventional trekking rucksack built around a one-size-fits-most back system, a big top-loading main bag and a long list of pockets, straps and attachment points. It was designed as a value pack for multi-day trips, and owners rate it on those terms. The headline trade-off is plain: at 2.16 kg, it carries a lot of kit comfortably and cheaply, but it starts the trip well over the weight an ultralight pack should.

## Performance

**Carry comfort.** The BioFit system is the pack's defining feature. It lets the wearer slide the harness to match torso length, so one pack suits a wide range of backs. A reviewer at Go4awalk handed the pack to the tallest person in the office, who is 6'2", and he adjusted it on the fly without taking it off. The same tester found the suspension stable, with load transferring onto the hips and a good air gap between back and pack. UK Amazon owners are similarly warm: the pack averages 4.1 out of 5 across 72 reviews, and 69 percent of those are five stars. Buyers single out the padded hip belt and shoulder straps for handling heavy loads.

Not everyone agrees. One owner who took the Verden on a five-day trip found the lower-back padding on the hip belt too firm after a full day, and reported that the shoulder-strap foam thinned noticeably within a few outings. The same reviewer said the flexing hip-belt frame developed an irritating squeak. A US buyer who otherwise liked the pack suggested that very tall hikers might struggle with the fit, which sits at odds with the office test above; torso-length adjustability helps, but the 79 cm body is not a tall pack by trekking standards.

**Capacity and organization.** The 65-litre main bag is deep enough for traditional multi-day loads, and the extendable lid adds the "+10". One buyer fitted clothing for an 18-day trip in Peru into it, fleece included. Go4awalk's tester carried a sleeping bag, a one-person tent and overnight kit without difficulty. The base compartment with a bivi divider lets a sleeping bag live separately from everything else. Two expansion side pockets hold bottles, and zipped front pockets keep small items from disappearing into the main bag. One owner wanted more external lashing points, though called it a minor gripe.

**Durability.** Owner reports are better than the price suggests. One reviewer used a Verden for a year in Central American jungle and reported only some cosmetic fraying and a couple of broken plastic buckles, both easy to replace. Another reports nearly five years of use in sun, rain, wind and snow with the pack still close to new. Buckles appear to be the weak point, not the fabric.

**Weather protection.** The coated ripstop nylon sheds rain, and the supplied cover is integrated. An Italian owner reported the cover held up well in heavy rain. As with any top-loader of this type, a liner remains the surer way to keep a down bag dry.

**Weight.** This is where the Verden loses ultralight readers. At 2,160 g, it works out to about 33 g for every litre of nominal main capacity. The zipped pockets, bivi divider, lid, rain cover and padded suspension all add up. Berghaus lists the pack as out of stock on its own site, so buyers will mostly find it through retailers and the used market.

## Choosing a Version

The Verden 65+10 comes as a single adjustable-back pack, so there are no torso sizes to choose between: BioFit handles the fit. Hikers on shorter trips will find Berghaus also made a smaller Verden 45+8, with 75 × 32 × 28 cm dimensions, which a reviewer found right for two-night outings but short of what longer treks demand. For week-plus trips with bulky gear, the 65+10 is the one to look at.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- BioFit adjustment fits a wide range of torso lengths without swapping sizes
- Padded hip belt and shoulders handle heavy loads comfortably, according to owners
- Generous organization: bivi compartment, zipped front pockets, expanding side pockets
- Rain cover included and hydration compatible
- Reports of multi-year durability under hard use
- Typically priced as an entry-level pack, around £100 RRP

**Cons**

- 2,160 g is heavy for 65 litres
- Hip-belt padding is firm enough to bother some hikers
- Shoulder-strap foam compresses with use, according to one owner
- Hip-belt flex mechanism can squeak
- Plastic buckles have broken on some packs
- Limited external lashing points
- No longer sold direct by Berghaus

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who carry a traditional 12–16 kg load, want an adjustable back and a pack with every pocket accounted for, and see a pack as a tool rather than an optimization project. It also suits trekkers on a budget, and anyone borrowing or sharing a pack across torso lengths. Thru-hikers and ultralighters with a sub-5 kg base weight should look elsewhere: a frameless or light framed pack in the 1 kg range will cut more than a kilogram from every day of the trip.

## Verdict

The Verden is a solid, sensible mid-range trekking pack that owners hold on to for years, and its adjustable harness is its best trick. It is not an ultralight pack and does not try to be. Buy it for comfort and value on conventional multi-day trips; skip it if grams are the point.
