## Overview

Back Country Cuisine is a New Zealand-made freeze-dried meal brand that's a staple on trails throughout NZ and Australia. The Nasi Goreng is their gluten-free take on the classic Indonesian fried rice dish — brown rice, pumpkin seeds, vegetables, and diced egg in a mild Asian-style sauce. It's aimed at hikers who want a quick, no-fuss dinner and don't mind a milder flavor profile than you'd get from the Southeast Asian dish itself.

## Performance

The mechanics of this meal are straightforward and well thought out. You add boiling water — 188ml for the small pouch — stir, and wait 10 to 15 minutes. One nice touch retailers mention repeatedly: the pouch has a <cite index="1-2">lower tear tab so once the meal has been reconstituted you can tear the lower perforation and use the pouch as a bowl</cite>. That's a small detail, but on trail it means one less thing to pack and one less thing to wash.

Rehydration itself isn't the issue — it's the flavor, and that's where reviews get split. On the positive end, one reviewer called it <cite index="10-3,10-4">"a good version of Nasi Goreng and a nice meal after a long day hiking," with "lots of flavour and pleasant enough to eat for a freeze-dried meal."</cite> An Australian Hiker reviewer landed in the middle, saying they <cite index="6-2,6-3">"didn't dislike this meal nor would I rave about it either," noting there were "too many other excellent options from Back Country" they preferred.</cite>

But several verified buyer reviews on the official Back Country Cuisine site are considerably harsher. One customer described it as having <cite index="4-2,4-3">"no flavour" at all, saying "I actually couldnt be bothered eating this."</cite> Another went further, calling it flavorless and joking that <cite index="4-4,4-5,4-6">"if this was my emergency meal and I was stuck in the bush an extra 3 days I still think I would end up throwing this away uneaten," adding the group "could only laugh at how sad a meal it made," and speculating the pouch might be "defective and missing a spice sachet."</cite> That same reviewer was clear that this was an outlier in the range, noting <cite index="4-7">"all the other Back Country variations were pretty good, but out of the 10 or so we tried this was by far the worst."</cite> That's a meaningful signal — it suggests the Nasi Goreng specifically underperforms relative to the rest of the Back Country Cuisine lineup, rather than the brand as a whole having a flavor problem.

On the nutrition side, the small serve comes in at roughly 366 calories with 11.8g of protein and 8.8g of fat — perfectly serviceable as a dinner, though not calorie-dense enough on its own to be your sole fuel source for a big mileage day. The regular size doubles the water and roughly doubles the nutrition, which is the better bet if you're hiking hard rather than car camping. Shelf life is a genuine strength here: a <cite index="9-6">four year best before date</cite> means you can stock up when on sale without worrying about it going stale in your pantry before your next trip.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Genuinely lightweight — the pouch itself is a fraction of the finished meal's weight, per the brand's own claim that the <cite index="9-6">pouch weighs one third of the meal it makes</cite>
- Convenient tear-tab pouch doubles as a bowl, cutting down on dishes
- Gluten-free and vegetarian, which broadens who in your hiking group can actually eat it
- Four-year shelf life makes it easy to stockpile for emergency kits or infrequent trips
- Quick 10–15 minute prep, no cooking gear beyond boiling water required

**Cons:**

- Flavor is inconsistent across reviews — ranges from "pleasant" to "no flavour at all," which is a real gamble if you're buying blind
- Multiple reviewers rank it as the weakest option in the Back Country Cuisine range
- Only available for purchase within New Zealand from the official site, due to export restrictions, per the retailer note that <cite index="1-1">these foods are for sale within New Zealand ONLY due to issues when exporting foods</cite> — Australian buyers need to go through NZ- or Australia-specific retailers instead
- Not calorie-dense enough in the small size to serve as a standalone big-mileage dinner

## Who Should Buy This

This is a reasonable pick for casual trampers, car campers, or anyone building a gluten-free/vegetarian emergency food stash who values convenience and shelf life over bold flavor. If you're doing serious ultralight thru-hiking and rely on dinner as a psychological high point of the day, the mixed flavor reports are worth taking seriously — you might want to test a single small pouch at home before committing to a resupply box full of them.

## Verdict

The Nasi Goreng nails the practical side of freeze-dried meals — light, fast, gluten-free, and shelf-stable for years — but the flavor reviews are genuinely split, with more than one buyer calling it the weakest meal in Back Country Cuisine's otherwise well-regarded lineup. I'd rate it a 5.5/10: worth trying once before you buy in bulk, and if it doesn't land for you, the rest of the brand's catalog is reportedly a safer bet.
