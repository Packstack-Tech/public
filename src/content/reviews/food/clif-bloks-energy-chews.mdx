## Overview

Clif Bloks are pocket-sized cubes of pectin-set sugar, built for runners and cyclists who need carbohydrate every 30 minutes without unwrapping anything. Backpackers have borrowed them for the same reason: a cube can be eaten one-handed, mid-stride, with trekking pole still in the other. The trade-off is energy density. A packet is a fast, digestible carb hit, not a calorie-dense trail food.

## Performance

**Fueling.** The formula is a blend of tapioca syrup, cane sugar and maltodextrin, thickened with pectin rather than gelatin. That mix of sugars is designed to be absorbed quickly, and it is the reason the chews are marketed for efforts longer than an hour. Six cubes at 33 calories each works out to roughly 200 calories per packet, or about 94 kcal per ounce. For comparison, a handful of nuts or a tortilla with peanut butter carries far more energy per gram. Bloks earn their weight by being fast and easy to digest, not by being efficient.

**Dosing.** Clif's guidance is one packet per hour of activity, with water. That is about 48 g of carbohydrate per hour, a sensible target for a hard day of climbing or a long push on a long-distance route. Hikers moving at a relaxed pace will find a few cubes an hour is plenty, and the individual cube format makes that easy to meter. It is the format's main advantage over gels: calories can be taken in small, regular bites instead of one sticky dose.

**Sodium and caffeine.** Electrolyte content varies by flavor. Standard flavors such as Strawberry and Mountain Berry carry 50 mg of sodium per serving, while Salted Watermelon doubles that to 100 mg and the Margarita flavor goes higher still. Orange and Black Cherry add caffeine (25 mg in the Orange), useful for a pre-dawn start or a mid-afternoon slump. These amounts are modest. On a hot day with heavy sweating, the chews support an electrolyte routine but do not replace one.

**Eating experience.** Reviewers describe the texture as soft and chewable without the sticky cling of a gummy candy, and the sweetness as restrained for a product that is mostly sugar. One long-time user at Cycling Independent says the cubes go down easily when tired and are gentle on an unsettled stomach, with small bites making it easier to feed calories in gradually. The sour flavors are a useful change of pace after hours of sweet. Flavor rotation matters; limited editions come and go, and favorites can be hard to find.

**Carry.** At 59.5 g, a packet is light, flat and tough enough to survive a hip-belt pocket or shoulder-strap pouch. The tear-open pack reseals poorly, so a partly eaten packet is best finished or folded tight. Cubes can firm up in cold weather and soften in a hot pocket, which is a minor annoyance on both ends of the thermometer.

**Ingredients.** The organic, plant-based formulation and the absence of artificial flavors are real selling points for hikers who read labels. Nutritionally, though, this is concentrated sugar with a little salt. There is no fat, protein or fiber, so Bloks cannot carry a meal and will not hold off hunger for long.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Small cubes let hikers meter carbs by the bite
- Easy to open and eat one-handed on the move
- 24 g of carbs per three-cube serving; 59.5 g packet
- Pectin-based and organic, with no high-fructose corn syrup or artificial flavors
- Wide flavor range, including caffeinated and extra-sodium options
- Widely stocked at outdoor, running and cycling shops

**Cons**

- Low energy density at roughly 94 kcal/oz, so a food bag built on Bloks gets heavy fast
- All sugar: no fat, protein or fiber
- Sodium on standard flavors is low for heavy sweaters
- Per-calorie cost is high next to candy or dried fruit
- Texture shifts with temperature

## Who Should Buy This

Bloks suit hikers who treat fueling as a rate, not a meal: fastpackers, trail runners, peak-baggers on long summit days, and anyone who bonks without steady carbs. They also work as a emergency or race-day supplement tucked in a pocket. Thru-hikers building a daily calorie budget should look elsewhere for the bulk of their food. Nuts, nut butters, olive oil and dense bars deliver far more energy per ounce, and Bloks are better used as an occasional top-up on big climbs.

## Verdict

Clif Bloks are a well-made, easy-to-eat carb source that does one job cleanly: delivering quick sugar in small, controllable doses. They are not an efficient way to carry calories, and the sodium on most flavors is light. Pack a few packets for hard efforts and long days, and leave the heavy lifting to denser food.
