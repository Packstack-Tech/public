## Overview

The Osprey Talon is a four-size family of ventilated, multi-sport packs, from a 635 g, 11-liter running-and-biking pack up to a 44-liter framed pack for multi-day trips. It suits hikers who want structure, a padded hipbelt and a pocket for everything without moving up to a full trekking pack. The trade-off is weight: the Talon is light by mainstream standards and heavy by cottage-industry ones.

## Performance

### Fit and suspension

The Talon's selling point is how it carries. The harness adjusts to torso length, which is uncommon in a pack this light, since many competitors rely on fixed sizes. The AirScape panel is injection-molded foam under mesh, and it holds the load close to the spine. Reviewers who compare it with the tensioned trampoline panel on Osprey's Exos say the Talon feels steadier on scrambles and technical ground.

The cost is ventilation. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers rated the 22's breathability as average, and hikers in hot weather feel more contact heat than they would on a suspended mesh back.

The hipbelt is thinly padded. Because it is sewn directly to the frame, it transfers weight well at moderate loads. Osprey rates the 44 for 15 to 35 lbs, but owners and reviewers put the comfortable ceiling nearer 30 lbs. Past that, the belt softens and the pack starts to feel its limits. A long food carry that pushes a 44 toward 35 lbs is where hikers notice it.

### Organization and capacity

Osprey counts only closed pockets in its volume figures. The open side bottle pockets, hipbelt pockets and rear stretch-mesh pocket are left out, though cottage makers usually count them. Counted that way, reviewers put the Talon 44 in the mid-50s.

The 44's feature list is long. It has a top-loading main compartment, a removable floating lid with a zippered slash pocket and a key-clip mesh pocket beneath it, a stretch-mesh front pocket, tuck-away ice axe loops and Stow-on-the-Go pole attachments. It also has dual-zippered hipbelt pockets, zippered sleeping bag access and removable sleeping pad straps. Extra webbing under the lid lets bulky gear ride outside the main bag. Owners of the 33 who upgraded from earlier models single out better side pockets, roomier hipbelt pockets and improved pole and ice axe attachments.

The small end of the range is simpler. The 11 is a single-compartment pack with a zippered mesh pocket inside, hipbelt pockets that take a phone or GPS, and shoulder-strap pockets sized for gels. BikeRadar's tester found the compression strapping ineffective at stopping a loose load from shifting, and the interior lacks dividers.

### Pocket and bladder friction

The most common complaint across the line concerns bottle pockets. On a fully packed 44, the main compartment pushes against the side pockets and the compression straps squeeze them further. A soft SmartWater-style bottle fits at partial loads, but a Nalgene does not fit on a full pack. The 11's side pockets take bottles of about 24 oz or less. The internal hydration sleeve costs space as well, because a full reservoir bulges into the main compartment. On the 22, Outdoor Gear Lab found the bladder-hanging clip doesn't work with every reservoir.

### Durability

The 44's 100D and 420D recycled nylon has held up to bushwhacking, snow and crampon-and-axe carries. Owners report clean stitching and working zippers after long ownership, and one Talon 11 owner reported years of use without problems. Sternum-strap stitching failures appear in reports on older models, and later updates seem to have fixed them. Water resistance is modest. The DWR finish won't replace a pack liner, and the floating lid can let water in through its zipper when the pack isn't full.

## Choosing a Version

**Talon 11 (635 g).** This is a run, ride and fast-day-hike pack. Reviewers praise how little it bounces and how the hipbelt stays over the hips instead of creeping upward. The S/M takes a 2 L reservoir and the M/L takes 3 L, and a full bladder eats a large share of the 11 liters. It is not a gear hauler, and it carries poorly when loaded beyond what fits inside. The hipbelt is not removable.

**Talon 22 (1,080 g).** This is a day pack with a full frame and hipbelt. Better Trail's tester carried about 20 lbs in it comfortably and found it more supportive than most 22 L packs. It is also heavy for the volume. Adventure Alan called the frame overbuilt for a day pack, and Outdoor Gear Lab marked it down on weight-to-volume ratio. Buy it for peakbagging or heavy day kits, not for fast-and-light days.

**Talon 33 (1,262 g).** This suits a summer overnight or a fast weekend with a compact kit. It weighs only 68 g less than the 44, so buying the smaller pack saves little. Choose it for the volume that will actually get filled.

**Talon 44 (1,330 g).** This is the multi-day version and the one with the most reviewer data. Its 15–35 lb rating makes it the only Talon suited to longer carries. The S/M holds 42 L and the M/L holds 44 L. It is 250 g heavier than the 22 and gives the most capacity and pocket count in the line.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Torso-length adjustment is rare at this weight
- AirScape panel is stable and close to the body on technical terrain
- The 44's real capacity exceeds its label because of the exterior pockets
- Sleeping bag access and pad straps are uncommon on packs this light
- Recycled fabrics with PFAS-free DWR
- Durable after long use
- The 11 carries steadily with little bounce for its size

**Cons**

- The hipbelt softens above roughly 30 lbs
- Side pockets are tight on a full pack, and compression straps make this worse
- The bladder sleeve takes up interior space
- No rain cover
- Ventilation is only average
- The 22, 33 and 44 are heavy for their volume next to cottage-industry packs

## Who Should Buy This

The Talon suits weekend backpackers who carry 20–30 lbs, peakbaggers who need axe and pole attachments, and hikers who want a traditional lid-topped pack with torso adjustment. The 11 suits runners, bikers and minimalist day hikers. The 22 suits heavy day loads, the 33 short overnights and the 44 longer trips.

Thru-hikers who have dropped their base weight below 10 lbs should look at frameless or light-framed cottage packs, which carry similar volume for noticeably less weight.

## Verdict

The Talon is a comfortable, durable, well-organized pack that fits well and carries steadily. It earns its place for hikers who want structure and pockets and accept the weight. Buy the 44 for multi-day trips and keep the load under 30 lbs. Weight-focused thru-hikers should look elsewhere.
