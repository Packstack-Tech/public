## Overview

The Carinthia Combat is a full-enclosure bivy sack from the Austrian maker of NATO-issue sleep systems. It is a three-layer GORE-TEX laminate with taped seams, built to be zipped shut in foul weather. It is the lightest bivy in Carinthia's catalog and a serious piece of kit for solo mountaineers, hunters and emergency-shelter minimalists. The trade-off is cost: at 659.90 € on the manufacturer's site, it costs more than many complete shelters.

## Performance

The Combat's distinguishing feature is its fabric. Carinthia uses GORE-TEX Gas Permeable Technology, which differs from the membrane in rain jackets. The laminate is waterproof and windproof, and it also passes oxygen and carbon dioxide. That is the basis for Carinthia's claim that the bag can be closed completely. Most waterproof bivies can't be, because they leave a gap at the face to avoid stale air and condensation. Carinthia says the construction cuts interior condensation and moves what does form to the outside. The company also says the sealed bag adds a measure of warmth in the cold.

Owner reports line up with that pitch. One buyer on Carinthia's own site described sleeping zipped up in summer humidity and still finding the bag very breathable. Another slept in it at -2 °C and saw only morning dew beading on the outside. An Australian owner who used it on military exercises reported no condensation problems. He also used it to enclose his pack during a river crossing, and everything arrived dry. These are owner testimonials rather than lab numbers, but they are consistent with each other.

Durability gets good marks as well. One owner slept on rocks, twigs and thorns without tearing the fabric. A European outdoor retailer's reviewer called the workmanship and materials excellent. The three-layer laminate is not a delicate ultralight fabric, and the 700 g weight reflects that. Compare it with the 1.2 kg that one owner said he had been carrying in other brands' bivies.

Fit is generous. At 2.5 m long and 95 cm across the shoulders, it accommodates tall sleepers wearing insulation, with room for a sleeping bag and a pad. A European reviewer who tested it with an inflatable pad and no separate sleeping bag called the comfort impressive. The bag also works as a summer sleeping bag or an emergency bag on its own, though it has no insulation. In cold conditions it only adds warmth to a sleeping bag.

The details are practical. The side zip and chest press studs make for an easy entry. The built-in mosquito net at the face opening is the feature owners single out most often, and one called it a life saver. The 32 x 8 cm packed size is small enough for a side pocket, and a compression sack comes in the box.

The Combat is a minimalist design with no poles or hoops. That keeps weight and bulk down, but it means the fabric lies on you, and it offers none of the headroom or gear storage of a hooped bivy or a small tent. Anyone planning to wait out a multi-day storm in one should take that into account.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fully waterproof and windproof with taped seams
- Gas-permeable laminate lets the bag be closed completely without the usual stuffiness
- 700 g with a 32 x 8 cm pack size and a compression sack included
- Long and roomy at 250 cm, so tall users and bulky bags fit
- Integrated, zippered insect net
- Tough laminate that owners report holds up on rough ground
- Made in Europe

**Cons**

- At 659.90 €, it is far more expensive than most bivies and many tents
- Far heavier than a minimalist bivy built from lightweight fabrics, which can weigh a fraction as much
- No poles or hoops, so there is no interior space for sitting up or sorting gear
- Adds only modest warmth and has no insulation of its own
- Military styling, with an olive finish and an IR-reducing coating, that thru-hikers have no use for

## Who Should Buy This

The Combat suits solo mountaineers, hunters, military and survival users, and anyone who needs a closed-up shelter that will keep working in wet, windy conditions where a tarp is a poor choice. It is also a strong choice for emergency or alpine use, where durability and a fully sealed bag matter more than saving grams.

Gram-counting thru-hikers should look elsewhere. A tarp, a tarp-and-bug-net combination or a lighter-fabric bivy will be lighter and a fraction of the price, and most long-distance trails don't need a bag that stays sealed in a storm. Those who want room to move around under cover should consider a small tent.

## Verdict

The Combat does what it was built for: it is a rugged, breathable, fully closable GORE-TEX bivy that keeps the weather out at 700 g. Few competing products combine this durability with this packed size. The price and the lack of any interior space put it out of reach for most backpackers, and buyers should regard it as specialist equipment.
