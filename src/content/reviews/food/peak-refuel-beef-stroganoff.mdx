## Overview

Peak Refuel's Beef Stroganoff is a freeze-dried, just-add-water dinner built around ground beef, rotini noodles, mushrooms and a cream cheese sauce. It's aimed at hikers who want a high-protein, high-calorie meal that tastes closer to home cooking than the usual pouch fare. The trade-off is price, plus a texture that depends on getting the water right.

## Performance

**Calories and protein.** The numbers are the headline. At 810 calories and 41 g of protein for the whole pouch, the stroganoff carries a heavy protein load for 5 ounces of food. Per half-pouch, that works out to roughly 400 calories and 20 g of protein, so a hungry hiker eating a solo dinner is looking at the full pouch, not the "two servings" on the label. In a head-to-head comparison, Trail Cooking's reviewer picked Peak Refuel over Mountain House's stroganoff, citing nearly double the calories and protein, considerably lower sodium and a lower water requirement, and judged the texture and taste better. America's Test Kitchen also put it on its list of top freeze-dried backpacking meals after trying more than 20 products.

**Taste.** Owner feedback is strongly positive. Hunters and backpackers describe big pieces of beef and mushroom and a flavorful, tangy sauce. One camper reported the group compared it to the real dish. Reviewers at GOHUNT rate it 4.9 out of 5 across 52 reviews, with several calling it among the best backcountry meals they've eaten. A recurring comment is that it sits easier on the stomach than other brands' dinners. Another reviewer noted that the pouch smells fairly neutral when opened, with the onion, garlic and paprika coming through once water goes in. It's also a plain, beige plate of food, so nobody is buying it for looks.

**Rehydration is the weak spot.** The main complaints concern texture. REI's 147 reviews average 4.3 out of 5, noticeably below the scores on retailer sites that skew toward hunters. The critical reviews there mention crunchy beef cubes even after a 15-minute wait with boiling water in a pouch cozy, one pouch that rehydrated poorly and tasted burnt, and one report of a thumb-sized piece of plastic film. A reviewer at Edweird found the noodles too firm and the beef chewy when following the instructions at sea level, and got better results with just over a cup of water and a 15-minute wait. Buyers on Peak Refuel's own site make a similar point: the stated water amount can leave the meal dry. A reasonable approach is to start with the listed 3/4 cup, add a splash more if it looks stiff, and give it the extra five minutes in a cozy, particularly in cold weather.

**Prep and cleanup.** The process is standard: open the pouch, pull the oxygen absorber, add boiling water, stir, seal and wait 10 minutes. There's no pot to scrub. The pouch is slim, which makes it easy to pack, though the full-size 2-serving format means it won't fit as neatly into a small bear canister as a one-person meal.

**Cost.** This is a premium dinner. Owners on the trail generally describe Peak Refuel as a luxury, and say cost and space tend to limit how many they carry. At $12.99–$13.99, it costs a few dollars more per pouch than the Mountain House equivalent.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 810 calories and 41 g of protein in 141.8 g
- Real ground beef, mushrooms and noodles with a creamy, well-seasoned sauce
- Only 3/4 cup of water and a 10-minute wait
- Owners frequently rank it near the top of the freeze-dried category
- Made in the USA, with a 10-year shelf life

**Cons**

- Beef and noodles can come out chewy or crunchy if the water or wait time is short
- Quality is occasionally inconsistent from pouch to pouch, according to some buyers
- Costs more than Mountain House's stroganoff
- Contains milk and wheat
- Looks bland, and the cream-based sauce is heavy for some palates

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers who want a protein-forward dinner for one hungry person, or a small group's shared pouch, will get the most from it. It suits hunters, section hikers and thru-hikers who want a few treat dinners in the rotation and don't mind paying for them. Anyone resupplying a long trail entirely on freeze-dried meals, or trying to hold food costs down, should look at cheaper options. Hikers with dairy or wheat allergies should skip it.

## Verdict

Peak Refuel's Beef Stroganoff delivers more calories and protein per ounce than most pouch dinners, and most owners say it tastes as good as it reads on the label. Plan on adding a little extra water and waiting a few extra minutes for the best results. It's a strong choice for the nights that matter, and an expensive one for every night.
