## Overview

The Spot 400-R is Black Diamond's rechargeable take on its long-running Spot line — a mid-priced headlamp built for backpackers, thru-hikers, and general outdoor use rather than niche applications like night climbing or ultra racing. It swaps the classic 3xAAA setup for a built-in 1500 mAh Li-ion battery charged via micro-USB, and at 86 g it sits right in the sweet spot between disposable-battery headlamps and true ultralight cord-strap models.

## Performance

The headline number here is battery life, and it's genuinely strong for a rechargeable unit. CleverHiker's six-week field test <cite index="11-10,11-11">found a burn time of 2.5/4 hours on high, 5/8 hours on medium, and 200/225 hours on low, ranking the battery life among the best of all the headlamps they tested, especially considering it's rechargeable</cite>. In practice, most reviewers barely touch max brightness. CleverHiker noted that <cite index="11-12,11-13">they rarely found the need to use the headlamp at full power, since the intensity of 400 lumens is seldom necessary for casual night hiking, camping, or walking</cite>. That tracks with my experience with similar Black Diamond lamps — you live on low and medium 90% of the time, and that's exactly where this headlamp's runtime shines.

The Big Outside's testing backs this up with real numbers: <cite index="14-3,14-4">at low power of six lumens the headlamp runs 225 hours, bright enough to find your way in and out of a tent, while at medium power it projects a beam 60 meters and lasts eight hours on a full charge</cite>. After extended daily use, <cite index="14-7">the reviewer found the headlamp still retained two-thirds of its charge after eight nights of regular use at various brightness levels and modes</cite>. That's a comfortable margin for a weekend or even a week-long trip without a recharge, assuming you're not blasting high beam every night.

Beam quality gets mixed marks. The peripheral flood mode is well-regarded — <cite index="14-5">it projects adequate light uniformly to organize gear or illuminate a campsite without getting blotchy or having an annoying dark spot in the center</cite> — but CleverHiker flagged that <cite index="11-7">the overall light quality can be a bit harsh, especially at the brightest setting</cite>, which matches what I've noticed with similar single-LED spot beams: great for distance, a little glaring up close.

Controls are a strong point. Black Diamond's PowerTap feature lets you <cite index="14-8">simply tap the right side of the casing to cycle between max brightness and whatever dimmed level you previously set</cite>, which is genuinely useful when you need a quick blast of light without cycling through the full menu. There's also a proper lockout mode — <cite index="14-9">holding down both buttons for a few seconds turns the lockout on and off, preventing the lamp from accidentally activating and draining its charge inside your pack</cite> — a feature that's saved me from a dead headlamp more than once with lamps that lack it.

On comfort, the strap draws consistent praise. CleverHiker described it as <cite index="3-1">quite comfortable, thanks to its soft strap that strikes a nice balance—not too thick and not too thin</cite>, and noted <cite index="3-10">unlike the ultralight cord-style straps common on many specialty headlamps, the Spot 400-R's strap feels more substantial and supportive</cite>. That's a trade-off worth understanding: you're getting a traditional elastic headband rather than the featherweight cord straps you'll find on dedicated ultralight lamps, which adds a bit of security and all-night comfort at the cost of a few grams.

The waterproofing seems to hold up in practice — reviewers used it through rain and even <cite index="11-9">an impromptu moonlight paddleboarding session</cite> without issue, consistent with its IPX8 rating.

The one recurring gripe across reviews is the charging port. As The Trek put it, <cite index="1-2,1-3">as more electronics move toward USB-C, hikers dream of one charging cord for everything, but the 400-R still uses a micro-USB port</cite>. It's a minor annoyance rather than a dealbreaker, but it does mean carrying yet another cable type if the rest of your kit has moved to USB-C.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent runtime on low/medium — 225 hours on low means it'll outlast most trips between charges
- PowerTap and lockout features are genuinely useful, not just gimmicks
- IPX8 waterproofing holds up to rain and dunking
- Comfortable, secure strap for all-night wear
- Reasonable price point for a rechargeable headlamp with this feature set

**Cons**

- Still uses micro-USB instead of USB-C
- Beam can feel harsh on the highest setting
- At 86 g with a traditional headband, it's not a contender for the true ultralight category
- No option to swap in disposable batteries if you can't recharge on a long trip (unlike the non-rechargeable Spot 400)

## Who Should Buy This

This is the headlamp for backpackers and thru-hikers who want one reliable light they can recharge from a power bank or wall outlet and mostly forget about. It's not built for competitive night racing or ice climbing, but for camp chores, night hiking, and general trail use it hits a strong balance of weight, brightness, and battery life. If you're chasing every last gram and don't mind fussing with tiny cord-strap lamps, look elsewhere — but for most three-season backpackers, this is close to the sensible default.

## Verdict

The Spot 400-R does what a good headlamp should: it's bright when you need it, sips battery when you don't, and stays out of your way otherwise. It won't win any ultralight weight-weenie awards, but as an all-around rechargeable headlamp for backpacking, it's hard to beat at this price. I'd put it at an 8/10 — docked slightly for the micro-USB port and harsh high-beam, but a clear recommend for most hikers.
