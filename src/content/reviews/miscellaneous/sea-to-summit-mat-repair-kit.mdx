## Overview

The Sea to Summit Mat Repair Kit is a flat packet of peel-and-stick patches and spare valve flaps, built for the company's Air Sprung Cell sleeping mats. It weighs next to nothing and replaces the tube of glue that most pad repair kits still demand. The catch is specificity: it's tuned to Sea to Summit fabrics, and half the contents won't fit the brand's self-inflating pads at all.

## Performance

The pitch is simplicity. Sea to Summit says the patches are formulated to bond to the surface fabric of its Air Sprung Cell mats, and the system skips the glue tube entirely: peel the backing, press the patch over the hole, done. The adhesive is 3M VHB, the same family of tape used in industrial and automotive bonding, and the company notes the patches stick to most surfaces, which makes the kit a reasonable source of spare fabric tape for a torn stuff sack or a rain jacket cuff.

That approach has fans among reviewers. TrailGroove's tester, reviewing a Sea to Summit insulated pad that ships with the same style of kit, called out the self-stick patches as light and easy, and said the tester would rather not fiddle with glue in the middle of the night. It's an apt point. A pinhole leak discovered at 2 a.m. is not the moment for mixing, curing and waiting. A dry patch on a clean, dry pad is about as fast as field repair gets.

Sea to Summit sizes the patches sensibly. The four 3 cm rounds suit pinholes and thorn punctures. The four 4 cm squares cover small slits and abrasion leaks, though anything longer than a couple of centimeters is better handled by a larger patch from a separate kit. Eight patches is plenty for a thru-hike's worth of bad luck.

The valve flaps are the unusual extra. The multi-function valve on Sea to Summit's air mats relies on a small silicone one-way flap, and the kit includes two spares. If the flap tears or warps and the pad starts slowly sagging, a replacement takes the place of a pad that otherwise would be retired. These flaps are specific to Air Sprung Cell mats and do not fit Sea to Summit's self-inflating pads.

Compatibility is the real limitation. On self-inflating pads, the patches are rated for the Ultralight, Camp and Comfort Light models. They will not form an airtight bond on the knit upper surface of the Comfort Plus and Comfort Deluxe self-inflating mats. Owners with those pads need the brand's dedicated self-inflating kit instead. Pads from other makers are outside the intended use; nothing in the product's documentation supports it.

Like most patch kits, it also demands good technique. Leaks have to be found first (a bathtub or a stream and a pinch of soapy water do the job), the surface has to be clean and dry, and the patch needs firm, sustained pressure to cure its bond.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- No glue tube: peel-and-stick patches work in a dark tent or on a wet-weather morning
- Under 10 g, even by the manufacturer's packed weight
- Eight patches in two sizes, plus two spare silicone valve flaps
- Flat, thin packet that disappears into a repair pouch
- Adhesive is useful for gear other than sleeping pads

**Cons**

- Valve flaps don't fit self-inflating mats, and the patches don't bond to knit-top pads like the Comfort Plus and Deluxe
- Small patches limit it to pinholes and short tears
- Specified for Sea to Summit fabrics only
- Priced at $9.95 for a few grams of nylon and adhesive, and stock is spotty at some retailers

## Who Should Buy This

Owners of Sea to Summit air mats such as the UltraLight line who want a replenished repair stash, or who have used up the patches that shipped in the stuff sack. It's also a smart pick for anyone whose pad's valve flap has gone soft and who wants a spare in the first-aid pouch. Owners of self-inflating Comfort Plus or Comfort Deluxe pads, and anyone with a pad from another brand, should look elsewhere: the self-inflating repair kit from Sea to Summit, or a universal kit for their pad's own fabric.

## Verdict

A tidy, featherweight kit that does one job well: patching and re-flapping a Sea to Summit air mat in the field without glue. It earns a place in the repair pouch of anyone carrying one of those pads, and no place in anyone else's.
