## Overview

The Air Tech Evo is Grivel's do-it-all mountaineering axe — a one-piece hot-forged steel head on an aluminum "G-Bone" shaft, built for glacier travel, snow slopes, and moderate alpine ice. It's aimed at climbers who want a single axe that can walk the approach, self-arrest reliably, and still chop a decent step or bash in a picket when things get steeper. This isn't a technical ice tool for waterfall pillars, but within the mountaineering/general alpinism category it's earned a reputation as one of the more trusted options on the market.

## Performance

The head is really the star of the show here. Multiple reviewers who've put real mileage on the Evo single out the adze as best-in-class for moving snow. One tester who put it up against several other axes noted <cite index="3-1,3-2">the huge volume of the adze, which really shifts snow, was by far the best tested, and coupled with a pleasing weight to the head, makes for easy step cutting</cite>. That head weight is a double-edged sword — it's genuinely useful for chopping and for adding momentum to a swing, but it's part of why the Evo isn't a featherweight. As one long-term reviewer put it, <cite index="3-10,3-11">the unusual shaft shape is designed to reduce weight without losing strength, but it's still heavy compared to others in its class — though for someone who isn't particularly strong, that head weight helps add momentum to digging and swinging</cite>.

The pick performance backs up the head's reputation. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found that <cite index="1-6,1-7">the hot-forged chromoly steel pick and adze chop steps as well as any axe tested, with the pick being exceptionally durable and providing incredible ice incursion capabilities</cite>. That translates to confident placements in firm neve and the occasional patch of alpine ice — not something every general mountaineering axe pulls off convincingly. One user summarized it simply: <cite index="9-10">the Air Tech Evolution has the same hot forged head that is super sharp and really bites well into neve and even ice</cite>.

Durability is where the Evo consistently earns its keep. A tester who ran it for a full season noted <cite index="10-1,10-2">it showed very little wear, with the hot-forged chromoly steel head holding up well, and a flat area on the head well-suited for pounding pickets</cite>. That's a small but genuinely useful detail if you're setting up glacier travel anchors or building snow protection.

The shaft geometry gets mixed reactions depending on what you're doing. It's gently curved rather than aggressively bent, which helps in walking mode without turning the axe into a liability on steeper terrain. One reviewer summed up the trade-off: <cite index="4-2,4-3">it's perhaps a touch long for very steep terrain, but a great all-rounder, with a very gentle curve sitting high on the shaft so plunging into soft snow is relatively easy</cite>. The rubber grip section draws consistent praise — <cite index="4-5,4-6">the rubberized grip is excellent, short at about one hand span but with slight finger indentations that feel very secure in the hand</cite> — though it's worth noting that lower-end variants in the Air Tech lineup that skip this rubber section are noticeably harder to hold onto without a leash or pommel.

Where the Evo loses points is simply weight and price relative to newer competitors. Outdoor Gear Lab, which had rated it a longtime favorite, eventually noted that Petzl's Summit Evo edged it out with <cite index="1-5">a similar pick and adze, but a more ergonomic shaft that is more comfortable for steep snow climbing and is slightly lighter weight</cite>. And on the value question, at least one recent reviewer flagged that the price has crept up considerably for what is, at its core, still a fairly traditional design.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Hot-forged one-piece steel head is exceptionally durable and bites confidently into neve and alpine ice
- Best-in-class adze for step-chopping and clearing snow ledges
- Rubber grip section is comfortable and secure, even without gloves
- T-rated (Type 2) strength rating gives real margin for technical use, anchor building, and picket pounding
- Trusted by a large share of professional mountain guides for good reason — it's proven, not trendy

**Cons:**

- Heavier than newer competitors like the Petzl Summit Evo, which matches its climbing performance with less weight
- The head-heavy balance that helps chopping can feel less lively on delicate, technical swings
- Premium price point for what is, geometrically, a fairly conservative design
- Overkill if you're only doing mellow glacier walks or early-season approaches

## Who Should Buy This

The Air Tech Evo makes the most sense for climbers doing a genuine mix of glacier travel, moderate snow climbing, and occasional steeper alpine ice or mixed terrain — the kind of person who wants one axe in the 53–58 cm range that can handle a season of varied objectives without being babied. If you're strictly doing flat glacier approaches or gentle snowfields, this is more axe than you need, and a lighter, cheaper option will serve you better. If you're heading into terrain steep enough to want a true technical tool, pair the Evo with a dedicated ice tool rather than asking it to do double duty.

## Verdict

The Air Tech Evo remains a genuinely excellent, if not cutting-edge, all-around mountaineering axe — the steel head and adze performance are hard to fault, and the durability track record speaks for itself. It's not the lightest or cheapest option in its class anymore, but if you want an axe that will outlast your enthusiasm for the sport, this is a safe, well-earned recommendation. I'd put it at an 8.5/10.
