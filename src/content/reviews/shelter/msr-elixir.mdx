## Overview

The MSR Elixir is the tent for backpackers who sleep better with walls that stand up straight. It is a freestanding, double-wall, three-season design with a rectangular floor, steep sides and a footprint in the box, sold as a solo 1 and a two-door 2. The trade-off is plain: livability and toughness cost weight, and neither size is a contender for a sub-two-pound shelter list.

## Performance

**Stability.** The Elixir's pole layout is its calling card. Instead of a simple pair of crossed poles, it uses a double-hub arrangement with multiple crossing points and plenty of stake and guy-out loops. Owners on Reddit describe the Elixir 2 coming through repeated wind and rain storms without trouble, and the tent's rigidity is the feature reviewers praise most often. It has limits. One owner reported a pole bending in a strong gust and the tent folding in on itself before springing back, a reminder that this is a three-season tent and not a mountaineering shelter. Guy it out when the forecast turns.

**Livability.** The steep walls do more than the floor numbers suggest. The 2 has a 30-square-foot floor and a 40-inch peak, enough for two people to sit up at once. The 1 offers 39 inches of headroom over an 88 x 30 in floor, built to swallow a modern wide pad without the taper that pinches shoulders in many solo tents. Both carry a mix of mesh and solid fabric panels, which trades some airflow for warmth and privacy, and both have gear lofts and pockets for headlamps and phones. Taller sleepers in the 2 report head or feet touching the walls once a pad and bag are loaded.

**Condensation and ventilation.** This is the Elixir's recurring complaint. Reddit threads about the tent mention condensation more than any other problem, and owners say the foot of a sleeping bag can pick up moisture where it touches the wall overnight. Pitching in airflow, cracking the vestibule doors and leaving the kickstand vent open helps. The 1,500 mm fly rating is modest, and some owners report seam leaks, so seam-sealing before a wet trip is sensible.

**Setup.** The unusual pole system produces a learning curve on the first pitch. Users describe the first setup as slightly fiddly and say it becomes quick and intuitive after a try or two. Color-coded poles, clips and webbing speed up the process, and a practiced solo pitch takes about five minutes. The fly-and-footprint pitch gives a lighter, quicker setup when bugs aren't a concern.

**Durability.** Heavier fabrics, a 3,000 mm floor, metal corner hardware and 7000-series aluminum poles make this a tent that tolerates rocky sites, rough packing and years of weekend use. Owners rate durability as its top strength, and it is the main reason to accept the extra grams.

**Weight.** At 2,240 g minimum weight for the 2 and 2,770 g packed, this is a heavy two-person tent by thru-hiker standards. Split between two people, the carry works out to roughly 1,120–1,385 g each, which is reasonable but nowhere near ultralight. The Fast & Light pitch drops the 2 to 1,610 g by leaving the inner at home, at the cost of bug protection and a sealed interior. The 1 is listed at 1,780 g, with a packed weight around 2,330 g.

## Choosing a Version

The two sizes differ in more than headcount. The **Elixir 1 (1,780 g)** is a solo tent with a single door, a single 8-square-foot vestibule, an 18-square-foot floor and a rectangular shape sized for a wide pad. Hikers who want room to sit up, spread out gear and keep a pack under cover will like it. It is not a light solo tent, though, and it weighs only about 460 g less than the 2.

The **Elixir 2 (2,240 g)** adds 12 square feet of floor, a second door and a second vestibule, which gives two people independent entry and 24 square feet of covered storage. That makes it the better choice for couples. It also suits solo hikers who want a sprawling camp, since the extra 460 g buys a lot of space. Solo hikers counting grams should look at lighter shelters, and those who prefer to stay put should pick the 2.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Rigid, stable pole structure with many guy-out points
- Steep walls give 39–40 in of headroom and usable floor space
- Durable fabrics, metal hardware and a 3,000 mm floor
- Footprint included
- The 2 has two doors and two vestibules, so two people don't climb over each other
- Fast & Light pitch on the 2 drops weight to 1,610 g

**Cons**

- Heavy against ultralight two-person and solo options
- Condensation is a repeat complaint, especially at the foot end
- Some owners report seam leaks, and the fly's 1,500 mm rating is modest
- Pole system takes a pitch or two to learn
- Taller sleepers may touch the walls in the 2
- The 1 has only one small vestibule

## Who Should Buy This

The Elixir suits weekend backpackers, newer hikers building a first kit and couples who value room and durability over grams. It is also a good pick for solo campers who want to sit up and spread out in bad weather. Thru-hikers carrying a sub-10-pound base weight, anyone camping in persistently humid conditions and tall sleepers who like a long floor should look elsewhere.

## Verdict

The Elixir is a stable, tough, comfortable tent that makes no pretense of being light. Buy the 2 for couples or generous solo space, and the 1 for a livable solo shelter. Skip both if grams are the priority.
