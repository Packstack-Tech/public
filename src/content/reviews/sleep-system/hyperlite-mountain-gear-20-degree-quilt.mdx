## Overview

Hyperlite Mountain Gear's 20-Degree Quilt is a sewn-footbox, three-season down quilt aimed at thru-hikers who want shoulder-season warmth without carrying a sleeping bag's zipper, hood and spare fabric. It pairs 1000-fill grey goose down with a 7D shell, and the regular size comes in at 570 g (20.1 oz). The trade-off is a $530 price, a closed footbox that limits summer versatility, and a shell that rewards careful handling.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The headline number is warmth per gram. Bike Hike Safari's roundup calls it the warmest stock quilt under 21 oz, and credits the 14 oz of 1000-fill down in the regular size, a heavier charge than many 20°F quilts carry. Switchback Travel's testers, who logged a full season in the regular, found that the 20°F rating, which isn't EN- or ISO-certified, felt accurate. CleverHiker's testers reported reassuring warmth in the upper 30s on damp spring nights on the Appalachian Trail in New York, and named it their Quilt Editor's Pick. One owner reported sleeping at Mount Rainier below 20°F without trouble.

**Drafts and fit.** There is no draft collar, only a neck drawcord and three low-profile push clips, and testers note that a minimalist closure can't fully block cold air when a restless sleeper shifts. The pad system helps: four sewn loops and two straps hold the quilt to the pad. That makes the quilt more of a cold-night tool than an all-purpose blanket. CleverHiker's testers described the upper-body cut as trim, while Adventure Alan notes that it runs a touch wider than some rivals, which accounts for part of its weight penalty of roughly 1.5 oz against the lightest competitors.

**Footbox.** The sewn, closed footbox is the quilt's defining design choice. CleverHiker's testers found it notably roomy given the quilt's narrow profile, and it avoids the cold spot an adjustable footbox can leave behind. The cost is that the quilt can't be opened flat as a summer blanket. Hikers who want a quilt that doubles from 20°F to 55°F should look at a convertible footbox design.

**Moisture and shell.** The down and both shell fabrics carry a C0 DWR treatment. One verified buyer woke twice to a damp quilt exterior after nights of fog and tent condensation, with no noticeable loss of loft. Hyperlite also states that the quilt range has been audited for PFAS compliance. DWR down gives up a sliver of maximum loft, per Adventure Alan, a fair price for better wet-night behavior.

**Packability and durability.** The quilt compresses to roughly 8 liters, and CleverHiker's testers packed it easily in a 50-liter ultralight pack. The 7D shell is soft and light but delicate, a point Switchback Travel and several roundups raise. Snags from brush, sharp tent hardware or a careless gear shuffle are the main risk, and thru-hikers should treat it accordingly.

## Choosing a Version

The quilt comes in three lengths, and the differences go beyond a few inches of fabric.

- **Short** (66 in interior length, 54 in shoulder width, 40 in footbox, 12.9 oz of down): the lightest option at 527 g. It suits hikers around 5'6" and under who want to trim every ounce.
- **Regular** (72 in, 58 in shoulder width, 14 oz of down): the standard pick at 570 g, and the size most reviewers tested. It fits the broadest range of builds.
- **Long** (80 in, 62 in shoulder width, 44 in footbox, 15.2 oz of down): 627 g. Taller sleepers and side sleepers who want more shoulder room should choose this one. A verified buyer reports that the width let them clip it into a bag-style tube without pad straps, though the Long has at times been out of stock.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Exceptional warmth-to-weight: 14 oz of 1000-fill down in a 20.1 oz regular
- Sewn footbox is roomy and draft-free
- DWR-treated down and shell tolerate condensation and damp nights
- Pad straps and loops are included
- Three sizes; testers praised the 20°F rating's accuracy
- Packs to about 8 L

**Cons**

- At $530, among the priciest stock quilts
- No draft collar and no customization options
- Closed footbox limits use in warm weather
- 7D shell is delicate
- A bit heavier than the lightest 20°F quilts from Enlightened Equipment and Zpacks

## Who Should Buy This

Cold-sleeping thru-hikers and shoulder-season backpackers who want a ready-to-buy, high-loft quilt will get the most from it. It rewards pad-quilt systems and careful campers, and it suits someone who will mostly use it near its rated temperature. Hikers who want a customizable fit, a draft collar, or a quilt that opens flat for summer should look at Enlightened Equipment's Revelation or Enigma instead, which cost less and offer more options. Anyone rough on gear, or sleeping in brush-heavy shelters, should plan on a cautious routine for the shell.

## Verdict

Hyperlite's 20-Degree Quilt delivers on its headline claim: it's one of the warmest stock ultralight quilts for its weight, with a sleeping-bag-like footbox and a rating that testers found accurate. It costs more than its closest rivals and gives up flexibility in warm weather. For cold-night efficiency in a no-fuss package, it earns a strong recommendation.
