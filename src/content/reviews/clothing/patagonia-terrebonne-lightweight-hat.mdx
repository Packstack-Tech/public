## Overview

The Terrebonne Lightweight Hat is Patagonia's do-it-all running and hiking cap — a 2.7-oz recycled polyester ripstop crown with a NetPlus recycled-fishing-net brim stiffener, built for high-output days on trail and then straight into the brewery parking lot afterward. It's aimed at hikers and trail runners who want a breathable, packable cap rather than a heavy-duty sun hoodie replacement, and who care about the recycled-materials story as much as the fit.

## Performance

The Terrebonne's biggest selling point is breathability, and that seems to hold up in actual use. Patagonia's own spec sheet lists the front-panel fabric at 40 CFM airflow, and that lines up with what reviewers report — one REI reviewer <cite index="3-6">described the hat as lightweight, fast-drying, adjustable fit, water resistant, and easily cleaned</cite>. On Patagonia's own product page, a reviewer with a 7 1/4 head size said the hat <cite index="1-14">fits well and looks decent, stays on in a breeze, wicks well, breathable for high output efforts, and doesn't get sweat stains</cite> — which is about as strong an endorsement as you'll get from someone who's clearly used it for real training, not just a weekend stroll.

The unstructured mid-crown design is worth calling out. Instead of a stiff, pre-shaped panel, the crown molds to your head over time, so fit improves the more you wear it rather than staying rigid out of the box. That's a nice touch if you've got an odd-shaped head or find structured caps annoying on long days, but it does mean the hat looks a little floppier than a classic trucker cap right out of the package.

The brim is the other interesting piece here — it's stiffened with NetPlus, Patagonia's recycled-fishing-net material, rather than a plastic insert. In practice it holds its shape fine for sun and light rain, though it's not a wide, technical sun-hat brim; don't expect the coverage you'd get from something like an OR Sun Runner Cap or a wide-brim boonie. One user noted the hat's shape survives getting crushed in a pack, saying it <cite index="4-3">keeps its shape even after being shoved into an overstuffed backpack</cite>, which matters if you're stuffing it into a hip belt pocket on the trail.

Where opinions diverge is the closure. Patagonia uses an elastic-anchor adjustable strap instead of the classic hook-and-loop tab, and it's the most consistently repeated complaint across reviews. Multiple users flagged it as the weak point of an otherwise well-liked hat — one wrote that on <cite index="4-6,4-7">the most obvious shortcoming is the adjustable strap... on mountain runs the wind often kicks way up in the high country and having to stop, remove the hat, and fiddle with the strap to tighten it, then stop again to loosen it later, is a big drawback</cite>. Patagonia's team even acknowledged the feedback directly in a reply, saying they'd pass along <cite index="4-11,4-12">detailed feedback regarding the hat's fabric and adjustable strap design... and will share your thoughts with our design team</cite>. If quick, on-the-move adjustments matter to you — say, tightening for a windy ridge and loosening again on the climb back into the trees — that's worth knowing before you buy.

Fading under sustained UV exposure is a smaller but real concern. One long-term alpine user docked half a star specifically for that reason, noting <cite index="1-15,1-16">massive fading in a year, though many days spent in high-UV alpine conditions, and wishing for a published UPF rating</cite>. Patagonia doesn't publish a UPF number for this hat, so if sun protection is your primary concern, look elsewhere or budget for it fading over a season of heavy use.

Water resistance is modest, as expected from a PFAS-free DWR rather than a waterproof shell. It'll shed light drizzle and shrug off sweat, but it's not designed to get you through a sustained downpour.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely breathable — the 40 CFM front panel and mesh-adjacent feel show up in real-world sweat and heat reports
- Unstructured mid-crown molds to your head over time for a personalized fit
- Holds its shape well when packed/crushed
- Recycled materials throughout (polyester crown, NetPlus brim, Fair Trade Certified factory) without sacrificing performance
- Packable and light enough to forget you're wearing it

**Cons**

- Elastic-anchor strap is a repeated complaint — no quick, one-handed adjustment on the move like a hook-and-loop closure offers
- No published UPF rating, and at least one long-term user reported significant fading after a year of high-UV alpine use
- Not genuinely waterproof — fine for drizzle and sweat, not a downpour
- Color/pattern options have been a minor gripe among reviewers wanting more neutral choices

## Who Should Buy This

This is a good match for trail runners and hikers who prioritize breathability and packability over technical sun coverage — think shoulder-season hiking, high-output trail runs, or as a lightweight everyday cap that can also handle trail duty. If you run in genuinely windy alpine terrain and need to adjust your hat on the fly without stopping, the elastic strap may frustrate you enough to look at a hook-and-loop alternative instead. If dedicated sun protection with a verified UPF rating is your top priority, this isn't that hat — pair it with a buff or look at a dedicated sun cap instead.

## Verdict

The Terrebonne Lightweight Hat delivers on its core promise: breathable, packable, comfortable for high-output days, and made from genuinely recycled materials without a performance penalty. The elastic strap is the one recurring annoyance that keeps it from being a slam-dunk recommendation for technical mountain use, but for general trail and running duty it's a solid 7.5/10.
