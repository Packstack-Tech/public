## Overview

The Gregory Focal 48 is a lightweight (not ultralight) backpacking pack built around a perimeter aluminum wire frame and a tensioned mesh back panel. It slots into that "lightweight" category that's become crowded lately — the pack for hikers who want the comfort and organization of a traditional backpack without hauling a full-on expedition frame. It's the direct competitor to the Osprey Exos/Exos Pro, and Gregory sells a women's version of the same pack called the Facet 48.

## Performance

**Comfort and carry.** This is where the Focal earns its reputation. The perimeter wire frame combined with the FreeFloat suspension does a genuinely good job of transferring load to the hips rather than the shoulders. One reviewer who pushed the pack well past its rated capacity — <cite index="8-6">around 16kg on one trip</cite> — still came away impressed, noting the vertical aluminum and horizontal fiberglass stays combine with the padded hipbelt to provide <cite index="8-8">"exceptional weight transfer across the entire circumference of my hips without any hotspots."</cite> That said, Gregory's own suggested ceiling is lower: most testers agree <cite index="13-4,13-5">the Focal is comfortable carrying loads up to 25 pounds and becomes increasingly less comfortable as the weight increases, with heavier loads better suited to a more robust pack</cite>. In practice, treat 25 lb as the pack's comfortable sweet spot and 30 lb as the hard ceiling, not a target.

**Ventilation.** The tensioned mesh back panel is a standout feature, and it shows up in nearly every review as a highlight — testers consistently point to it as <cite index="15-2">ultralight and exceptionally well-ventilated</cite>, calling it a strong option for anyone who runs hot on the trail.

**Weight class.** At 1,220g this isn't an ultralight pack by strict definitions, but it undercuts most traditional backpacking packs by a wide margin. One long-term user summed it up well: it's <cite index="1-3,1-4">not an ultralight backpack, but it fits nicely into the lightweight category, and is slightly lighter than the Quechua and the Exos</cite>. Compared to the Osprey Levity/Lumina line, though, it gives up some weight — <cite index="13-6">the Focal weighs 2.54 pounds, making it one of the heavier packs in this category compared to something like the Osprey Levity, which comes in at 1.85 pounds with similar features but less durability</cite>. That's the trade-off in a nutshell: the Focal spends a bit more weight budget on durability and structure.

**Organization and access.** It's a single top-loading compartment with no bottom access, which a few users flagged as a minor annoyance. One long-term owner noted the dark grey fabric interior makes it <cite index="9-16,9-18">hard to see inside even with abundant light, and a central or bottom opening would help with locating gear without opening the top lid</cite>. On the plus side, the floating lid, adjustable straps, and stretch mesh pockets make it flexible for trips where your load fluctuates — reviewers noted it's <cite index="11-9">particularly suited for any thru-hiking where your pack is going to grow and shrink in size depending on supply availability</cite>. You can also shed a bit more weight by pulling the lid entirely and using the included weather flap instead.

**Fit.** Sizing runs S/M/L with a fixed torso length per size rather than an adjustable harness, so try one on or check Gregory's torso-length chart before buying — there's no fine-tuning once you've picked a size.

**Real-world reports.** Field reports have been largely positive. One reviewer testing it on a multi-day trip in southern Utah said the pack <cite index="12-1,12-2">performed great, carried like a champ, held everything needed, and felt very intuitive to use</cite>. On REI, a buyer said it <cite index="14-1,14-2">helped them get their base weight way down after using it all summer</cite>, and praised the compression system, calling out the <cite index="14-3">"synch and release system for the main compartment"</cite> as class-leading.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Excellent load transfer and hip-hugging fit for a pack this light, even when pushed past its rated capacity
- Best-in-class ventilation thanks to the tensioned mesh back panel — great for sweaty hikers and warm climates
- Removable lid + weather flap lets you shave weight when you don't need full organization
- Flexible volume via compression straps and stretch mesh pockets, good for resupply-dependent thru-hikes
- Comparable comfort to the Osprey Exos, with (per some testers) better belt pockets

**Cons**

- Not true ultralight — heavier than direct competitors like the Osprey Levity by roughly 10 oz
- Comfort noticeably degrades above ~25 lb despite the 30 lb rating
- No bottom access; dark interior fabric makes finding gear by feel/sight harder
- Fixed torso length per size means no in-the-field harness adjustment
- Only one color option

## Who Should Buy This

The Focal 48 makes sense for hikers doing weekend-to-week-long trips who want a genuinely comfortable, well-ventilated pack without going full cottage-industry ultralight. If your base weight (pack included) sits comfortably under 25 lb and you run hot on the trail, this is a strong pick. If you're chasing the lowest possible pack weight or routinely carry closer to 30+ lb, look at either a true UL frameless pack or a beefier framed pack designed for heavier loads.

## Verdict

The Gregory Focal 48 delivers genuinely impressive comfort and ventilation for its weight class, backed by Gregory's typical build quality — but it's a lightweight pack, not an ultralight one, and it's happiest under 25 lb rather than at its stated 30 lb max. For three-season backpacking where comfort and airflow matter more than shaving the last few ounces, it's an easy 7.5/10.
