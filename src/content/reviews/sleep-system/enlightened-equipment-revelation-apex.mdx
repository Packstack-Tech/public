## Overview

The Revelation Apex is the synthetic twin of Enlightened Equipment's best-selling down quilt, filled with Climashield Apex and built around a 1/4-length zipper footbox that lets it function as a closed quilt on cold nights or a flat blanket on warm ones. It was sold for years as the Prodigy. It is aimed at thru-hikers and three-season backpackers who sleep in humid, wet or condensation-prone places and don't want to baby a down bag. The trade-off is familiar: more weight and more pack volume than a down quilt of the same rating, in exchange for lower cost and insulation that tolerates moisture.

## Performance

**Warmth and moisture.** Climashield Apex is the reason to buy this quilt. Backpacker's testers, who put numerous synthetic quilts and bags through trips as long as six-month thru-hikes, named the Revelation Apex 30 their best synthetic quilt, citing how well the insulation holds its loft and warmth after long-term use. Enlightened Equipment says the fill dries quickly and is easy to clean. On a drippy ridge, a tent-wall condensation night or a sweaty summer trip, that means the quilt keeps working when a down equivalent would be clumping. One Backpacker tester said an Appalachian Trail thru-hike "totally changed" after swapping a down bag for it.

**Ventilation and versatility.** The zippered footbox is the quilt's other selling point. Cinched shut, it makes a conventional sleeping pocket for your feet. Unzipped, the quilt lays flat as a blanket, which Backpacker called a godsend on hot, muggy nights. Backpacking Light's reviewer liked being able to open or close the footbox as conditions changed, and found the quilt roomy enough to block drafts with a light puffy worn underneath. It was wide enough to drape over a down quilt for extra warmth and moisture protection. The 10D shell and liner met Backpacker's breathability benchmarks, and the construction has no sewn-through seams, so there are no stitched cold spots.

**Weight and bulk.** This is where synthetic costs you. Outdoor Gear Lab, which bent its down-only rule to test the Apex 30, found it heavier than other quilts at the same rating and less compressible than a down-filled one. Backpacker clocked its regular-size 30° at 25.6 oz and said it packs to nearly twice the volume of a comparable 30° down quilt. Testers used a compression sack to make it manageable. Stripped down, the quilt can get light: Backpacking Light listed an XS, slim, 50°F, 10D build at 11.62 oz (329 g). A bigger, warmer quilt with sturdier fabric will land well above that.

**Fit and fiddly bits.** Outdoor Gear Lab praised the generous width at the shoulders, hips and footbox. Owners have a few minor complaints. One Trailspace reviewer wrote that the loose, baffle-free construction takes some getting used to. Another reported the billowy inner fabric can snag on the zipper and snaps if the user isn't careful. Backpacking Light's reviewer found the flat-strap pad attachment system finicky and slow to stay put, and preferred the thin-cord systems used by Katabatic. Durability, though, gets consistently good marks, and one UK retailer reported the quilt holding up across three long-distance hikes.

**Value.** At $210–240, it costs well below a comparable down quilt. Reviewers repeatedly call it the best-value way into a quilt, and one noted it undercuts other cottage makers' Apex quilts while shipping faster. Temperature rating, size, fill and colors are all selectable at order, which lets heavier sleepers or taller hikers dial in a fit that stock quilts don't offer. EE includes a roll-top silnylon stuff sack with the quilt.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Apex insulation keeps its warmth when damp and recovers quickly
- Zippered footbox converts from closed quilt to flat blanket
- Four temperature ratings and three fabric weights, with made-to-order sizing
- Generous cut at shoulders, hips and feet
- Priced well below comparable down quilts
- Durable in long-term and thru-hike use

**Cons**

- Heavier than a down quilt of equal warmth
- Packs much larger; a compression sack is useful
- Flat-strap pad attachment can be fiddly
- Loose inner fabric can catch on the zipper and snaps

## Who Should Buy This

The Revelation Apex fits hikers on rainy, humid or condensation-heavy routes: the Appalachian Trail, Pacific Northwest, Scotland-style conditions. It also suits budget-minded first-time quilt buyers, side sleepers who want room to move, and anyone who sleeps hot and wants the blanket mode. Gram-counters on dry western trails, and anyone short on pack volume, should look at EE's down Revelation or a competing down quilt instead.

## Verdict

The Revelation Apex is the default synthetic quilt for three-season hiking: durable, versatile, and priced sensibly. It is not the lightest or most compact option, and it doesn't pretend to be. If the trail is wet, it is the safer insulation choice, and a smart buy.
