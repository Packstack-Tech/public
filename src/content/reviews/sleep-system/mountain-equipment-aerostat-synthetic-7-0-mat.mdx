## Overview

The Aerostat Synthetic 7.0 is Mountain Equipment's cold-weather sleeping pad, built under license from Exped's welded-baffle construction and stuffed with 160g of Polarloft synthetic fill instead of down. It's aimed at three-season and winter backpackers and mountaineers who want a genuine R-4 pad without babying it around sharp rocks or worrying about wet-down clumping — the trade-off being a noticeably heavier package than most "ultralight" insulated pads on the market.

## Performance

**Warmth.** An R-value of 4.0 puts this pad solidly in three-season-plus territory, and it's comparable in warmth to pads like the Therm-a-Rest XTherm or Sea to Summit Ether Light XT that get name-checked as its natural competitors. One long-term UK forum user reported <cite index="13-9,13-10,13-11">using the Aerostat Synthetic 7.0 all year round in the Scottish Hills, describing it as probably a little heavier than other mats but never let him down</cite>. That lines up with what you'd expect from 160g of welded synthetic fill in 7cm-deep chambers — it's not chasing a warmth-to-weight record, it's chasing reliable warmth.

**Comfort.** The vertically aligned baffle structure (borrowed straight from Exped's patent) is the same design used in Mountain Equipment's down Aerostat, and reviewers of that sibling pad noted <cite index="2-13">a depth of around 7cm makes this one of the thicker mats I've used, which again just seems to help with the sense of comfort</cite>. On the synthetic version specifically, one owner called it <cite index="5-1,5-2">very comfortable, having slept on fairly uneven ground multiple times and calling it the most comfortable mat they've used</cite>, and added that they experienced <cite index="5-7">no cold spots in the pad</cite>. That's a meaningful data point since insulation migration and cold spots are the classic failure mode for baffled synthetic pads.

**Inflation.** The Windsock system pulls double duty as stuff sack and pump, which several sources flagged as a genuine convenience rather than marketing fluff — <cite index="5-3,5-6">the ease of blowing up the pad is amazingly simple, with the stuff sack essentially turning into an air pump</cite>. It saves you from having to bag-inflate by mouth (which introduces moisture into the pad over time) without adding a separate pump's worth of weight and bulk.

**Durability.** This is where the 30D fabric and TPU lamination earn their keep — and also where the picture gets mixed. On the plus side, a UK retailer selling both Mountain Equipment and lighter alternatives pointed out that <cite index="7-1">you get a 30 denier fabric on these mats, making them more durable and puncture resistant than other mats we sell</cite>, while acknowledging <cite index="7-6">this makes them a bit heavier overall, but it might be a price worth paying if you're off on an extended trek or can't guarantee smooth ground</cite>. That said, durability complaints do show up in the wild. One buyer's <cite index="5-9,5-10">first time backpacking with this mat, it failed him, developing two holes — one on the bottom and one on the top</cite> — despite finding no corresponding damage in the tent floor. A second reviewer using it on field trips reported <cite index="24-9,24-10,24-11,24-12">using it on two field problems, one in the Arctic and one in Hawaii, having to make field repairs despite not sleeping on rock or anything sharp</cite>. These are anecdotal and don't outweigh the generally positive comfort/warmth feedback, but they're worth flagging — a 30D fabric is tougher than the 15D-20D shells common on true ultralight pads, but it's still an air-chamber pad, not a foam block, and punctures happen.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuine R-4.0 warmth rating suitable for winter and shoulder-season use
- Welded Exped-style baffle construction resists the insulation shifting that plagues cheaper synthetic pads
- 30D ripstop with TPU lamination is tougher than typical ultralight pad fabrics
- Windsock inflator/stuff sack is fast, keeps moisture out, and needs no separate pump
- Repair kit included in the box
- No down to manage — dries fast, performs fine if it gets damp

**Cons**

- Heavier than most R-4 ultralight competitors (665-685g depending on size, versus ~430-500g for pads like the NeoAir XTherm)
- Some reported puncture/durability issues despite the tougher fabric
- Limited independent long-term reviews available — data here is thinner than for more mainstream pads
- Premium price bracket similar to Exped, Therm-a-Rest, and Sea to Summit flagship pads

## Who Should Buy This

This pad makes the most sense for cold-weather backpackers and mountaineers who prioritize reliable warmth and durability over shaving every last gram — think winter trips, guided courses, or anyone who's tired of babysitting a down pad around condensation and pointy rocks. If you're counting grams for a fast-and-light summer thru-hike, the weight penalty versus something like an XTherm NXT is hard to justify; but if you want a pad you can throw in a pack, inflate in under a minute, and trust through a genuinely cold night, the Aerostat Synthetic 7.0 fits that brief.

## Verdict

The Aerostat Synthetic 7.0 delivers on its core promise — solid R-4 warmth, comfortable vertical baffles, and a tougher-than-average shell — wrapped in a smart Windsock inflation system. It's not going to win any warmth-to-weight comparisons, and the scattered durability complaints mean it's not bulletproof, but for cold-weather use where reliability matters more than ounces, it's a sound, if unglamorous, choice. I'd put it at a 7.5/10: dependable and well-engineered, just not the pad to reach for if grams are your religion.
