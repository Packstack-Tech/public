## Overview

The FLEXTAIL Tiny Pump is a USB-C rechargeable air pump about the size of a chunky shot glass, built to inflate and deflate sleeping pads, pillows and other inflatables. It comes in three versions: the plain Tiny Pump, the Tiny Pump X with a camping light on the base, and the 2X, a slightly heavier, higher-pressure light-equipped model. At 80 g or more it will never help a base weight, but it swaps the nightly pump-sack routine for a button press. The price is a high-pitched whine that carries across a quiet campsite.

## Performance

### Inflation speed

Speed is the reason to own it. Once a nozzle is seated on the valve, owners report a full sleeping pad in roughly 45 seconds, and retailers quote 50–90 seconds for the 2X on standard-size mats. A pillow takes far less: one reviewer filled a Klymit pillow in about six seconds. After a long day, that replaces several minutes of lightheaded huffing into a dry bag.

Pressure is modest at 3.5 kPa. That is plenty to fill a pad, but it won't firm one up the way a higher-output pump can, so most users finish with a few breaths or a hand press to set the firmness. The 2X's 4 kPa rating narrows that gap only slightly.

### Battery life

The 1,300 mAh cell is the spec owners track most closely. One tester got four inflations of a double pad plus four of single pads before the pump quit on the fifth night, with the light still working at that point. The practical guideline from owners is about ten single-pad inflations per charge, which covers a 3–4 night trip with room to spare.

A fully drained pump takes around four hours to charge. A 20-minute top-up from a power bank is enough for one full double-pad inflation. USB-C means the pump shares a cable with a phone and headlamp, and a sealed lithium cell copes with cold and damp better than the AA or AAA pumps it competes with. Alkaline cells sag in freezing temperatures and corrode when wet.

### Valve compatibility

The five nozzles cover most pads. Owners confirm a good fit on Klymit and Exped valves, and on Therm-a-Rest pads with flip valves. Screw-type valves take more work: the rubber nozzle has to be pushed over the valve while the cover is pulled out of the way and held. Nemo Tensor UL owners report the nozzle leaking or popping off mid-fill, and one fixed it by wrapping a small rubber ring around the nozzle tip. Nozzles can also back off the valve during a fill. Reseating one usually lets the pump finish.

### Noise

Noise is the most consistent complaint. One reviewer docked a star for the loud, high-pitched motor. The whine lasts under a minute, but it is harsh enough to draw looks at a busy shelter or a quiet campground at 10 p.m. Anyone sharing a tent early in the morning will notice it too.

### The light (Tiny Pump X and 2X)

Both light-equipped versions put a lantern on the base. The Tiny Pump X has three brightness levels at 40, 160 and 400 lumens. Owners say it works as a tent light, and the hanging bail lets it light the inside of a shelter. One reviewer describes the line as a lantern that happens to inflate pads, and says it replaced the backup light already in the pack.

The 2X adds a built-in magnet for sticking the light to metal surfaces, an IP44 splash-resistance rating and a longer rated pump run time. Its three lantern modes run from 40 to 400 lumens, and the manufacturer cites up to 24 hours of illumination on the lowest setting.

On every light-equipped version, the light and pump share a single button with different press patterns. In the dark, starting the motor when the light was the goal is easy to do. Running the light also draws on the same battery that inflates the pad.

## Choosing a Version

All three share the same core: 180 L/min airflow, USB-C charging, five nozzles. Inflation and deflation work the same way. What changes is the light, the pressure rating and the weight.

- **Tiny Pump (80 g):** The pick for anyone whose headlamp already doubles as a tent light, or who wants the battery reserved for inflating pads. One job, one battery draw.
- **Tiny Pump X (80 g):** The same weight as the plain pump, with a lantern added. It suits anyone who carries a separate tent light or backup light, since folding that into the pump can retire a second item. Expect a smaller battery reserve if the light is used often.
- **2X (99 g):** About 19 g heavier than the other two, and taller. In return it gets 4 kPa, a magnetic base, IP44 splash resistance and a longer rated run time. It makes sense for damp-climate trips, for hikers who want the extra runtime, and for anyone who likes parking a light on a metal surface.

For most backpackers, the X is the better buy: it adds a useful function at no weight penalty. The 2X is worth the extra 19 g only if the weather resistance and run time matter.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fills a sleeping pad in under a minute and a pillow in seconds
- USB-C charging uses the cable and power bank hikers already carry
- Roughly ten single-pad inflations per charge covers most trips
- Five nozzles fit most common pad valves
- Small enough for a hip belt pocket
- The built-in lamp on the X and 2X can replace a separate tent light
- The 2X adds splash resistance and a magnetic mount

**Cons**

- High-pitched motor noise is loud in a quiet camp
- Screw-type and some Nemo valves need fiddling, and nozzles can pop off
- 3.5 kPa is modest next to newer FLEXTAIL pumps
- Needs a recharge on longer trips
- 80–99 g is more than a pump sack for gram counters
- The shared button on the light versions makes it easy to start the pump by accident

## Who Should Buy This

This pump suits backpackers on 1–5 night trips who sleep on an inflatable pad and are tired of the pump-sack routine. It pays off most for people who carry both an inflation aid and a tent light, which makes the X or 2X the natural fit. Thru-hikers on long stretches without reliable charging should look elsewhere, as should hikers who already manage fine with a pump sack. FLEXTAIL's newer models are lighter and more powerful: the 3X weighs 67 g and moves 300 L/min at 5 kPa. Buyers paying full price should compare those before settling on this one.

## Verdict

The Tiny Pump line does what it promises. It fills a pad quickly, charges over USB-C and, in X or 2X form, doubles as a tent light. It is loud, valve fit sometimes needs coaxing, and newer FLEXTAIL pumps beat it on paper. It remains an easy recommendation for weekend and short-trip backpackers, with the Tiny Pump X the version to buy and the 2X the upgrade for wet-weather use.
