## Overview

The Xenon Hex Tarp is Dutchware's baseline hammock fly: a six-sided polyester tarp with a stitched ridgeline, built for hikers who want a taut, quiet pitch without paying Dyneema prices. It trades a few dozen grams against the company's bonded and 0.9-denier siblings for a conventional, sewn construction with a long record in the hammock community. The compromise is a bare-bones kit: no guylines, no stakes, and nothing in the box that assumes you'll be pitching it in a hurry.

## Performance

**Pitch and stability.** Xenon is a polyester sil fabric with very little stretch, which is the whole point. Silnylon tarps sag as they take on moisture and cool overnight, sending hikers out into the rain to retension guylines. Polyester doesn't do that to the same degree, so a tarp pitched taut at dusk tends to be taut at dawn. Dutchware's own description leans on this trait, and hammock-forum regulars have long favored Xenon for the same reason.

**Weather coverage.** At 9.5 ft wide, the hex covers a standard hammock with room to spare on the sides, and the 11 ft ridgeline gives a few feet of overhang at each end of a typical 10 to 11 ft hammock. That is generous coverage for light rain and wind-driven drizzle. It is not a storm shell: in sideways weather, a hex without doors or panel pullouts leaves the long open sides exposed. Dutchware sells the tarp in a no-pullout configuration, and side-panel pullouts are an option for those who want to lower the profile in wind.

**Durability and construction.** The ridgeline seam is stitched and enclosed in grosgrain, with grosgrain reinforcing the tie-outs as well. A forum thread on the tarp relays Dutch's explanation that the enclosed seam helps keep water out, so the tarp does not need seam sealing. Beastee D-rings on the corners and ridgeline keep hardware weight negligible while taking a lashing from tensioned cord. Hammock Forums posters weighing sewn against bonded ridgelines generally credit bonded seams with stronger waterproofness, but one poster said he would pick sewn with grosgrain for a hard-tensioned split-ridgeline pitch. That is the use case this tarp addresses.

**Weight in context.** Dutchware has reported 370 g for the 11 ft no-pullout Xenon 1.1 hex, against 343 g for the same fabric with a bonded ridgeline and about 295 g for the bonded 0.9 Ultralight version. The company also notes that individual tarps vary. The sewn tarp is therefore the heaviest of the three Xenon hexes, by roughly 27 g over its bonded twin and 75 g over the 0.9 version. Builders who have worked with Xenon call the fabric strong for its weight and compressible to roughly the size of a softball in a stuff sack.

**What's missing.** Guylines and stakes are not included, so a real-world carry weight needs another 40–70 g or so of cordage and pegs. Hikers who already own a favorite set of Zing-It guylines will not mind. First-time buyers might.

## Choosing a Version

Only the ridgeline length changes: 11 ft or 12 ft. Both share the same fabric, hardware and construction.

- **11 ft (≈ 370 g, no pullouts):** The weight-conscious pick. It fits most hammocks up to about 11 ft and suits three-season trips where coverage matters less than carry weight.
- **12 ft (≈ 416–423 g with stuff sack):** About 46–53 g heavier. It buys extra overhang at each end, useful for tall hammocks, bigger gear stashes under the fly, and hikers who expect long stretches of rain.

Most thru-hikers with a standard 10 to 11 ft hammock will be well served by the 11 ft. The 12 ft makes sense for longer hammocks or for anyone prioritizing storm margin over grams.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Low-stretch Xenon holds a tight pitch through wet, cool nights
- 370 g in the 11 ft version, including a stuff sack
- Grosgrain-enclosed ridgeline and reinforced tie-outs
- Beastee D-rings keep hardware light and secure
- Cheaper than Dyneema alternatives
- Two ridgeline lengths

**Cons**

- Heavier than Dutchware's own bonded and 0.9 Xenon hexes
- Stitched ridgeline lacks the leak-proof reputation of a bonded seam
- Guylines and stakes sold separately
- Open sides offer limited protection in wind-driven rain without added pullouts

## Who Should Buy This

Hammock campers who want a dependable three-season fly at a fair price, especially those who dislike retensioning a sagging tarp. It suits long-distance hikers who value a proven design over the last 50 grams. Hikers chasing the lowest possible weight should look at the bonded Ultralight (0.9) version, and those who regularly camp in winter or heavy storms are better off with a tarp with doors or panel pullouts.

## Verdict

The Xenon Hex Tarp is a sound, no-fuss hammock tarp whose low stretch and tidy construction justify its reputation. It's not the lightest Xenon hex Dutchware sells, but at 370 g in the 11 ft size it is light enough for most thru-hike setups, and it holds a pitch better than silnylon alternatives. Buy it if you want dependable coverage without Dyneema money.
