## Overview

Gregory's Raincover is a seam-taped pack cover in 70D recycled polyester ripstop, sold in three sizes that span daypacks to expedition loads. It's built to be fitted quickly, stowed in its own pouch and ignored until the sky turns. The trade-off is straightforward: it's a well-finished, fully taped cover, but at roughly 3.8–4.8 oz in the larger sizes it carries more fabric and a higher price than the thin silnylon covers many thru-hikers favor.

## Performance

The cover does its main job without drama. Seam-taped construction closes off the stitch lines that let water creep through cheaper covers, and 70D ripstop is a sturdier fabric than the ultra-thin nylons used in many lightweight covers. That matters off-trail, where bushwhacking, rock scrapes and pack drops are routine. Owners of Gregory covers describe them as effective in steady rain, with the elasticized drawcord holding a close fit around the pack body. One reviewer on a three-day Ozarks trip said it kept the pack dry through a sprinkle on day one and singled out the drawstrings as handy.

Fit is the cover's real engineering. An elasticized drawcord cinches the hem, hipbelt loops give a second anchor point, and an adjustable hook closure clips above the shoulder harness so the cover doesn't balloon in wind. A bottom drain hole lets pooled water escape rather than sitting in the base. The oversized reflective logo on the front is a small but practical touch for road crossings or camp-to-privy trips after dark.

Packability is decent but not remarkable. The cover stows in an attached pouch with an envelope closure, which means it never gets separated from the pack. Owners of earlier Gregory covers called them small but a bit bulky, with one comparing the stowed size to a soda can. A 70D fabric will always pack larger than a 15D or 20D silnylon cover, and the Gregory is no exception.

Two limits apply to every pack cover, this one included. Gregory's own guidance acknowledges that in prolonged heavy rain, waterproof dry bags or a pack liner are the safer way to protect sensitive gear. Covers also shield only the outside of the pack: water running down the back panel and through the shoulder-strap area can still reach the load, and a cover does nothing for wet-bushed trails or stream crossings that soak the pack from below. Thru-hikers who rely on a trash-compactor-bag liner for their sleep system will find the Gregory a supplement, not a substitute.

Customer ratings on Gregory's site vary by size: the 30L and 50–80L sit at 4.9 and 5 out of 5 respectively, while the 30–50L averages 3.8, which suggests fit on mid-size packs is less forgiving. Gregory itself advises sizing up when a pack carries gear strapped to the outside, since a cover stretched over a bulging load fits poorly and leaves gaps.

## Choosing a Version

Three sizes cover the range, and the choice is simply a matter of matching the cover to the pack's loaded volume, not its rated volume.

- **30L:** Rated for packs under 30 liters and listed at 0.19 lb (about 86 g). It suits daypacks, fastpacking vests with a modest main compartment and small summer overnight packs.
- **30–50L:** The weekend-to-weeklong size, listed at 136 g (4.8 oz). It fits most modern lightweight frameless and framed packs in the 40–50L range. Anyone who straps a foam pad, a wet tent or a bear canister outside should consider the next size up instead.
- **50–80L:** Listed at 108 g (3.8 oz). It is the right match for winter loads, expedition packs and large-volume packs carrying a week or more of food.

Because the covers stretch on elastic, a slightly generous size is more forgiving than one that is too small. Packs near the top of a size band are better served by the larger cover.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fully seam-taped construction closes the stitch lines
- 70D ripstop is more abrasion-resistant than the thin fabrics common in ultralight covers
- Hook closure, hipbelt loops and elasticized hem hold the cover in place in wind
- Drain hole prevents pooling
- Reflective logo and integrated stuff pouch are practical additions
- Fabric includes 40% recycled content
- Covers daypacks through large expedition packs

**Cons**

- Heavier than the thin silnylon covers that gram-counters favor
- Prices run $32.95–$42.95, a lot for a piece of waterproof fabric
- Does not protect the back panel or the pack from below, so it's no replacement for a liner in sustained rain
- Customer ratings for the 30–50L are noticeably lower than for the other sizes
- Stuffed size is larger than ultralight covers

## Who Should Buy This

This is a cover for the backpacker who values durability and a secure fit over shaving the last ounce: Gregory pack owners, weekend hikers in wet climates and anyone who bushwhacks or scrambles with a pack that takes a beating. The harness hook and hipbelt loops earn their place on windy ridgelines. Thru-hikers building a sub-10-lb base weight, and anyone already using a pack liner to guard their insulation and sleeping bag, should look at a thinner cover or skip one altogether.

## Verdict

Gregory's Raincover is a durable, well-fitted cover that handles real weather and keeps itself organized in its own pouch. It's priced and weighted for general backpacking, not for ultralight purists, and it works best as a second layer over a liner. A solid pick for Gregory owners and rough-use hikers, and a pass for gram counters.
