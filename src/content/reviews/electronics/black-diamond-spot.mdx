## Overview

The Black Diamond Spot is a 350-lumen, three-AAA headlamp that has become the default "good enough, cheap enough" light for backpackers, trail runners and climbers. It pairs a spot beam and a wide proximity flood with a red night-vision mode, a lock function and an IPX8 waterproof rating. The trade-off is weight: at 88 g with batteries it is a comfortable step above the minimalist lamps that gram-counting thru-hikers favor.

## Performance

**Beam.** The Spot's multifaceted lens throws a claimed 86 m on high, which reviewers at The Big Outside describe as enough for climbers hunting a descent route in the dark, runners on pre-dawn missions and ordinary night hiking. The more notable trick is close range. In proximity mode the beam spreads evenly at medium brightness, without the hot spot or dark center that plagues cheaper floods. That makes it good for sorting gear, cooking and reading in a tent. Testers at Outoria found it outclassed by Petzl's Actik Core when picking out detail at distance, but were impressed at close range. Outdoor Gear Lab's earlier test of the 300-lumen Spot scored the beam optics highly, giving 8 out of 10 for trail finding and 9 out of 10 for close proximity.

**Interface.** PowerTap lets a tap on the side of the lamp snap between full and dimmed output, and Brightness Memory means the light comes back on at the level it was switched off, instead of blasting a sleeping tentmate at full power. Outoria's testers all singled out the memory function. A second switch handles lens selection, and holding both buttons toggles the lockout, a feature that exists mainly to stop a headlamp from burning down its batteries inside a pack. Backcountry reviewers still report occasional complaints about button placement and the strap, and Reddit users mention the lamp switching itself on in storage, so the lock mode is worth using.

**Battery life.** This is the Spot's weakest line on the spec sheet. High mode lasts a bit under four hours on a fresh set of alkalines, which is fine for a pre-dawn summit push and thin for an all-night effort at full blast. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers flagged the gap between claimed and measured high-mode runtime on the previous Spot, a reminder to treat the 200-hour low-mode figure as a best case. In practice, most backpackers live on the dimmed settings, where the 3-LED meter makes it easy to see when a fresh set of cells is due. Rechargeable AAAs can be swapped in for those who want to avoid disposables, though the lamp doesn't have a built-in USB battery.

**Weather.** The IPX8 rating means the lamp is tested to 1.1 m for 30 minutes, and it shrugs off rain without complaint. Black Diamond's own fine print notes that water can enter the battery compartment on submersion and the lamp will keep working, but it needs to be dried out afterward. Outoria's testers took that to mean the lamp is better at camp than at stream crossings. In practice, the fix is to open the compartment and let it air out at the end of a wet day.

**Trail record.** Earlier Spot generations have been carried on the Appalachian Trail, Pacific Crest Trail and Pacific Northwest Trail, and the review record on those hikes is mostly of a functional, unremarkable light that did its job. Backcountry's customer reviews average roughly 4.5 stars across 54 ratings, with praise for the brightness, settings and comfort.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 350 lumens with a usable spot-plus-flood beam pattern
- Brightness Memory and lock mode stop the lamp from surprising its owner
- IPX8 rating handles rain and brief dunks
- 200 hours on low, and a battery meter that tells the truth
- Runs on AAAs found at any gas station, or rechargeable AAA cells
- Red night-vision mode with direct access
- Good value, with a 3-year warranty

**Cons**

- At 88 g, it's heavier than minimalist or USB-rechargeable options
- Under four hours on high with fresh alkalines
- Not natively USB-rechargeable
- Water can enter the battery compartment, so it needs drying after a soak
- Button placement draws occasional complaints

## Who Should Buy This

Backpackers, trail runners, climbers and backcountry skiers who want one dependable lamp for night hiking and camp chores, and who would rather carry a spare set of AAAs than a charging cable. It also suits anyone who takes a headlamp on trips where resupplying batteries is easier than resupplying watts. Thru-hikers counting every gram, and anyone who wants all-night output at high brightness, should look at a lighter lamp or a bigger rechargeable one.

## Verdict

The Spot earns its place by doing nearly everything competently at a modest price: bright when needed, gentle when not, and happy in the rain. It is not light enough for the ultralight crowd and not powerful enough for sustained high output, but for most three-season trips it is the sensible pick.
