## Overview

Naturehike's Carbon Fiber Trekking Poles are a conventional telescoping pole built from 3K carbon fiber, with a flip-lock adjuster, a cork grip and a long EVA foam extension below it. At about 200 g per pole, they sit in the middle of the ultralight range: far lighter than aluminum, but not the lightest carbon option, even within Naturehike's own catalog. The trade is familiar: a low-cost, tip-to-tip adjustable pole in exchange for a few extra ounces and a less refined feel than premium names.

## Performance

**Weight and swing feel.** A pair comes to roughly 400 g, which is a real step down from typical aluminum poles and enough to notice at the end of a long day of uphill. Naturehike's own folding models go lighter: the Lightwind UL is listed at 172 g and the First Snow EXT1 at 159 g in the medium size. The telescoping design here gives up that weight in exchange for a continuous adjustment range rather than fixed sizes.

**Adjustment range.** The 62–135 cm span is wide. It covers short hikers and tall ones from a single pair, and it lets the poles collapse small enough for a carry-on-style pack strap or luggage. The external flip lock is the standard system for a reason: it can be adjusted with gloves on, and it can be tightened or loosened with the screw to compensate for wear. Naturehike describes the locks as designed to keep the poles from sliding down under load.

**Grip.** The cork handle is the comfort feature on this pole. Cork takes on sweat well and tends to be less clammy than bare foam in warm weather. Below it, an extended EVA foam sleeve gives a second place to hold on steep sidehills, so hikers can choke down the pole on short climbs without stopping to shorten it. One reviewer on Naturehike's product page singled out this foam extension for exactly that reason. The nylon wrist straps are adjustable and conventional.

**Stability and durability.** A buyer on Naturehike's product page reported finishing the Tour du Mont Blanc with the poles, roughly 160 km with about 9,000 m of climbing and the same of descent, and called them stable. A separate review of another Naturehike carbon pole from the brand's lineup noted that the carbon shafts held up under tent-pitching duty without a collapse. Reviewers who compare carbon poles with the established names generally say a budget carbon pole flexes more than a premium one, which is worth knowing for heavy loads or rough off-trail travel. Carbon also lacks aluminum's tolerance for sharp impacts: a pole jammed between rocks can splinter where aluminum would only bend.

**Tips and accessories.** The tungsten steel tips bite into rock and packed dirt and wear slowly. The box includes baskets and a carry bag, so the poles are usable on mud and snow without further purchases.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About 200 g per pole, a clear weight saving over aluminum
- Wide 62–135 cm range suits nearly any height
- Cork grip with extended EVA foam allows quick hand repositioning on climbs
- Tungsten steel tips for grip on rock and hardpack
- Baskets, spare tips and carry bag included
- Value pricing compared with established carbon brands

**Cons**

- Heavier than Naturehike's own folding carbon models (159–172 g)
- Telescoping carbon shafts flex more than premium poles under heavy load
- Carbon is less forgiving of sharp impacts than aluminum
- Flip locks require occasional tension adjustment
- Not a tent-pole replacement: they don't pitch a trekking-pole shelter with a dedicated handle and tip design

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers stepping up from aluminum poles, three-season backpackers on a budget, and travelers who want one pole size that fits everybody in a group are the natural buyers. They suit day hikes, long trail miles and the occasional trekking-pole tent pitch. Thru-hikers carrying a base weight under 10 lb who count every ounce, trail runners and fastpackers should look at folding poles that save another 30–40 g each and pack far smaller. Hikers who regularly carry heavy loads off-trail, or who want a pole with a long service record and a lifetime repair network, are better served by an established name.

## Verdict

The Naturehike Carbon Fiber Trekking Poles deliver the main thing carbon is supposed to: a light swing at a price that makes the upgrade easy. The cork-and-foam grip and the wide adjustment range are the best parts of the package. Buy them as a good-value first carbon pair; skip them if the goal is the lightest possible kit.
