## Overview

Cocoon's Sleeping Bag Blanket is a rectangular microfleece bag with a water-repellent ripstop nylon shell. It zips fully open into a picnic-style blanket, and it couples to other rectangular Cocoon models with zippers to make a two-person bed. It's a comfortable, quick-drying piece for car camps, huts and festivals. At 1,219 g, it is also roughly double the weight of the fleece liners most thru-hikers consider heavy.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Cocoon rates the blanket at 57–59°F on its own, which puts it firmly in summer-bag territory. Retailers describe it as a summer sleeping bag, and the brand's own copy points to car camping, picnics and music festivals. As a stand-alone sleep system it suits warm, humid nights. As a warmth booster inside a bag, it's a different story. Fleece liners in this class are commonly credited with adding roughly 5 to 10 degrees. That figure is a marketing claim, and skeptics note that liners of any fabric tend to deliver less than advertised. Cocoon's own silk and Coolmax liners are marketed with smaller, single-digit gains.

**Weight and bulk.** This is where the blanket loses the argument with ultralight backpackers. Big Agnes's Polartec fleece liner weighs 20 oz (567 g). A budget microfiber fleece liner from Bundle Monster weighs 22.4 oz (635 g). Cocoon's Coolmax mummy liner is 9.1 oz (265 g), and silk liners run 3.6 to 4.9 oz. The Sleeping Bag Blanket's 43 oz is about twice the heavier fleece liners, and it packs to a 14 x 7 inch stuff sack that takes real room in a 40-liter pack. European retailers list a lighter version of this blanket at 910 to 990 g with a 34 x 17 cm pack size, but the US model Packstack covers is the 43 oz version.

**Moisture and weather.** The microfleece lining does what fleece does. It wicks moisture to the surface and dries quickly, which is why Cocoon calls it suited to wet conditions. The DWR-coated ripstop shell handles dew, a damp tent wall or a wet picnic table. It is a coating and not a waterproof membrane, so it won't replace a bivy or a rain fly.

**Versatility.** The design earns its keep here. A two-way YKK zipper lets the bag open from either end, which helps with venting on warm nights. Unzipped, it works as a blanket on a couch, in a car or around a fire. The pillow pocket holds a stuff sack or folded jacket, and the stuff sack reverses into a fleece pillowcase. Cocoon sells the blanket as a single unit. Couples who want the TravelSleepSystem need two zippered Cocoon pieces to join.

**Durability and care.** Reinforced, double-stitched gussets and a YKK zipper are the right places to spend money on a bag that gets stuffed and unstuffed often. European care labels on the same blanket call for a gentle 30°C machine wash, with no bleach and no dry cleaning.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Soft microfleece lining that dries fast
- Water-repellent ripstop shell resists damp and light moisture
- Zips open to a full blanket or couples with other rectangular Cocoon models
- Two-way YKK zipper, pillow pocket and reversible stuff-sack pillowcase
- Machine washable

**Cons**

- At 1,219 g, it weighs about twice as much as heavier fleece liners and far more than silk or Coolmax options
- 14 x 7 inch packed size is large for a pack-carried item
- 57–59°F rating limits stand-alone use to warm nights
- Rectangular cut with no hood or footbox shaping traps less heat than a mummy liner
- Around $91 is a lot for a fleece blanket

## Who Should Buy This

This is for car campers, hut and hostel travelers, festival-goers and couples who want a blanket that doubles as a warm-weather bag and can be paired with a partner's Cocoon. It also suits anyone who wants an emergency blanket living in the trunk. Thru-hikers and gram-counters should look elsewhere. A silk or Coolmax liner at under 300 g adds a few degrees and keeps a bag clean for a fraction of the weight. A sub-600 g fleece liner is the better pick when a cold-weather boost is the goal.

## Verdict

The Cocoon Sleeping Bag Blanket is a well-built, versatile piece of base-camp comfort. It doesn't belong in an ultralight kit. Buy it for the car, the hut or the couch, and leave it home for the trail.
