## Overview

The PreCip Evo Full-Zip Pants take the long-running PreCip formula and put it on a diet: a thin 2.5-layer recycled nylon shell, full-length side zips, and a weight of 297 g (10.5 oz). They suit three-season backpackers who want rain pants that go on over muddy boots without a wrestling match. The trade-off is a delicate fabric that rewards careful handling and offers little help with moisture on climbs.

## Performance

**Weather protection.** The Evo wears a lightweight shell with a DWR finish, and the PreCip name has been a staple of budget-minded rain kits for years. Outdoor Gear Lab's testers found that the standard PreCip Evo pant, the same fabric without the full side zips, beaded water effectively at first. They also noted it fell somewhat short in their water-resistance testing. For a hiker in sustained rain, that means treating the DWR as a maintenance chore: wash and re-treat the fabric, and expect the face fabric to wet out sooner than a heavier shell would. Owners of earlier full-zip PreCips report that the waterproofing holds up over years of use. One owner recounts more than a decade of service through monsoon-style downpours and wet brush.

**The zips.** This is the reason to choose this pair over the pullover Evo. Full-length side zips mean the pants slide over boots, trail runners or crampons without removing them. Owners of earlier full-zip PreCips cite quick on-off in sudden downpours and easy changes inside a tent. They also mention pockets of the pants underneath staying reachable through the open sides. The zips double as ventilation. Unzipped partway, they let air in where the fabric itself won't. Owners who hike in them report that the opened legs mean far fewer stops to strip them off. The storm flaps keep the zips weathertight when closed, and the zippered hand pockets and back pocket add a bit more venting.

**Breathability.** The honest limitation. Outdoor Gear Lab rated the Evo's breathability as comparable to other pants on the market, but noted that the non-zip version has no ventilation features and that the unlined fabric felt clammy against bare skin. The full-zip design addresses part of that complaint, because opening the zips is the ventilation. Even so, owners on the Backpacking Light forums have long cautioned that PreCip-style pants run hot for aerobic effort except in cold, wet or windy conditions. These are pants for sitting out a storm or for walking in a cold downpour, not for steep climbs in warm drizzle.

**Durability.** The thin fabric is the weak point. Outdoor Gear Lab describes the 2.5-layer construction as fragile and found that the Evo performed worse than other pants in its abrasion tests. Owners of the older PreCip tell a similar story at the extreme end: one tore a pair in two places glissading down a snow slope, and concluded that the pants aren't for anyone hard on gear. Sitting on rocks and mud has proven fine for others. Briars, scree slides and sharp rocks are another matter.

**Fit and packing.** The regular fit is relaxed and straight, with room to pull on over hiking pants, tights or a fleece layer. A 32 in inseam suits average-height hikers, though one owner of an earlier PreCip found the legs slightly long. The pants stuff into their own pocket, and Outdoor Gear Lab noted that the pullover Evo fits into a standard 5-liter running vest. The full-zip version, at 297 g, weighs more than the 8.9 oz (about 252 g) pullover, which is the cost of the zips and flaps.

**Small annoyances.** Zippers on earlier PreCip full-zips occasionally stick. Several owners also report that the hook-and-loop waist tabs can work loose when the zips are open or under strain, and one forum user fixed it by adding snaps. The fabric is also noisy, as nearly all stiff nylon shells are.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Full-length side zips allow on-off over boots and double as ventilation
- 297 g and a pocket-sized stuff, light enough to carry on dry days
- Recycled nylon ripstop with a windproof, waterproof 2.5-layer membrane
- Three zippered pockets, including a back pocket, with storm flaps over the zips
- Relaxed regular fit that goes over hiking pants and base layers

**Cons**

- Fragile fabric that abrades and tears more readily than sturdier rain pants
- No liner, which feels clammy against bare skin
- Warm for sustained aerobic effort unless the zips are open
- Waist closure reportedly loosens on earlier PreCip full-zips
- Heavier than the pullover Evo, and heavier than the lightest rain pants on the market
- No RECCO reflector

## Who Should Buy This

Three-season backpackers and thru-hikers who want a dependable pair of storm pants for cold, wet days. They suit people who value changing in and out without removing shoes, and who will treat the fabric with care. They also fit those who want a lightweight second layer of wind protection in camp. Hikers who bushwhack, glissade or scramble should look at a heavier, tougher shell. Hikers who need the lowest possible base weight, and are willing to give up the side zips, should consider the pullover Evo or a dedicated ultralight wind pant.

## Verdict

The PreCip Evo Full-Zip is a practical, sensibly priced storm pant whose side zips solve the two biggest annoyances of rain pants: getting them on over footwear and venting them once you're moving. Its thin fabric needs careful handling and its breathability is merely average. For the typical three-season hiker who keeps rain pants in the pack for the worst days, it earns a spot.
