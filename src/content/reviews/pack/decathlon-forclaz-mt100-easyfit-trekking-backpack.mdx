## Overview

The Forclaz MT100 Easyfit is Decathlon's women's-specific 50-liter trekking pack, built for multi-day trips and priced well below the cottage-industry and big-brand competition. Its party trick is the Easyfit system, which resizes the back length and tightens the shoulder straps at the same time. The trade-off is mass: at 1,600 g, it is a comfortable, cheap, durable pack rather than a light one.

## Performance

**Fit and carry.** Easyfit is the reason this pack exists. Decathlon's patented system lets the wearer change back height and strap tension together by pulling a single strap on each side, which means a mid-trail adjustment is a two-handed tug rather than a stop-and-unbuckle exercise. The women's version moves the system to a lower adjustment range, with a 41–50 cm back length, and pairs it with a more open shoulder-strap shape and a higher-set belt cut for a female chest and hip profile. The belt spans 74 to 131 cm, a range wide enough to cover a lot of waists without swapping parts. Aluminum back stays give the pack a frame to transfer weight to the hips. A tester at Mountain Weekly News who carried the unisex Trek 100 Easyfit sibling in the Wind River Range, including a heavy rack-and-rope load, reported that it sat well on the hips and distributed weight sensibly.

**Capacity and organization.** At 50 liters, the pack handles a three-season kit for a long weekend through a week with some discipline. The layout is conventional: two compartments, bottom access so a sleeping bag can be reached without unpacking everything, and five pockets. Decathlon's own page offers a water bottle holder, a trekking pole holder and hydration compatibility. Buyers praise the layout, though one owner on Decathlon's US site wished for more compartments.

**Durability and weather.** The body is a thin polyester with a reinforced base, and it carries a 10-year warranty, an unusual promise at this price. Decathlon says trekkers tested the Easyfit line for five weeks across the Canary Islands, the Pyrenees and the Mont Blanc range. The included rain cover handles downpours, which helps, because the polyester will not stay dry on its own through a long storm.

**Ratings.** Owner scores are strong. The women's pack holds a 4.8 average from 711 reviews on Decathlon's Australian site, and the men's equivalent sits at 4.77 across more than 1,400 reviews on the UK site. Reviewers repeatedly cite comfort, ease of access and value.

**Weight.** Here the pack falls short for the ultralight crowd. A 1,600 g, 50-liter frame pack is roughly double what many frameless 50-liter packs weigh. Anyone who has trimmed a base weight under 10 pounds will not need the stays and will feel the extra mass. For a hiker carrying 25 pounds or more, the frame earns its keep.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Easyfit adjusts back length and strap tension in one pull per side
- Women's-specific strap shape and belt height, with a 74–131 cm hip belt range
- Aluminum stays and padded carry system suit heavier loads
- Two compartments, bottom access and five pockets
- Rain cover included
- 10-year warranty and a low price
- Consistently high owner ratings

**Cons**

- 1,600 g is heavy for a 50-liter pack by ultralight standards
- Thin polyester is not waterproof, so the rain cover is mandatory in wet weather
- Some owners want more compartments
- Women's back-length range (41–50 cm) will not suit taller torsos

## Who Should Buy This

This is the pack for a woman making the step from day hikes to multi-day trips, for anyone with a short or medium torso who wants a frame without a premium price, and for hikers who value a long warranty over saved ounces. It also suits those who change loads often and want to retune the fit on the move. Thru-hikers chasing a low base weight, and anyone with a torso longer than 50 cm, should look at lighter frameless packs or a longer-torso model.

## Verdict

The MT100 Easyfit is a comfortable, well-organized and cheap trekking pack that backs its durability with a decade of warranty coverage. It is not an ultralight pack, and it does not pretend to be. For first-time backpackers and weekend-to-week hikers, it is an easy recommendation.
