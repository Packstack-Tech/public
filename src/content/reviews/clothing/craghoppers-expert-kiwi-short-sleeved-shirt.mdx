## Overview

The Expert Kiwi is a button-up workwear shirt in a polyester-cotton blend. Craghoppers pitches it at walkers, travelers and outdoor workers, and it is built around two things: a tightly woven fabric meant to stop biting insects, and SolarShield UV protection. The trade-off is weight and dry time. At 255 g in a cotton-bearing fabric, it is a durable, inexpensive shirt, but it is not a gram-counter's shirt.

## Performance

**Durability is the Kiwi's calling card.** Owner reviews on Craghoppers' own site read like a longevity survey. One buyer is on a third Kiwi after the first reached 15 years old. Another replaced shirts worn for a decade that had only begun to fade. Reviewers of the Kiwi line consistently describe a shirt that outlasts most synthetic hiking tops. It also shrugs off washing, and the drying loops at the hem let it hang from a line or a pack strap.

**Bug protection is structural.** NosiDefence relies on fibres constructed too tightly for mosquitoes and midges to bite through, rather than a permethrin-style coating. Reviewers note that the barrier doesn't wash out the way chemical treatments do. That makes it useful for Highland midge season or evenings at a lakeside camp, though a woven shirt does nothing for exposed arms below the sleeve, neck and hands. Short sleeves leave a lot of skin out. The flip-up collar covers the back of the neck from the sun.

**Sun protection is moderate.** SolarShield is rated UPF 30–50+, a range that tells owners less than a single tested figure would. It covers the torso and upper arms; everything else needs sunscreen.

**Comfort and fit lean generous.** The cut is relaxed, and a tester of the long-sleeved Kiwi found the sizing roomy enough that a medium fit comfortably despite falling between sizes on the chart. The 31-inch back length gives coverage under a pack hipbelt, though the extra fabric can bunch there. A Great Outdoors reviewer testing the women's Kiwi III noted that the non-stretch fabric felt restrictive across the shoulders and back when reaching forward, notably when pulling on a rucksack. Expect the same from this men's cut.

**Drying and sweat are the weak points.** The 35% cotton content is the reason. A Trailspace reviewer of the Kiwi long-sleeve reported that the fabric darkens when wet, which makes sweat obvious, and that on a long walk sweat left white salt patches that washed out cleanly. The same reviewer said that if drying clothes looked difficult, an all-synthetic shirt would be the better pick. On a humid multi-day trip, a polycotton shirt will stay damp longer than a nylon or polyester one.

**Weight is the obstacle for ultralight use.** At 255 g, this is a heavier shirt than most sun hoodies and trail tees in its price range. The standard Kiwi short-sleeve, listed by Craghoppers at 195 g, saves about 60 g. The extra mass buys the Expert version its extra pocket and workwear feel, but not any measurable trail benefit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Owners report shirts lasting 10 years or more
- Woven insect barrier that doesn't wash out
- UPF 30–50+ sun protection and a flip-up collar
- Hidden zipped pocket for a key or card
- Recycled polyester and cotton; Craghoppers' retailers cite roughly 16 plastic bottles per shirt
- Frequently sold at £18–30

**Cons**

- 255 g is heavy for an ultralight kit
- Cotton blend dries slowly and darkens visibly with sweat
- Non-stretch fabric can restrict reaching
- Short sleeves limit the bug and UV coverage
- Relaxed cut is baggy under a pack

## Who Should Buy This

This is a shirt for day hikers, section walkers, travelers, and anyone who works outdoors in warm weather and wants a tough shirt that survives years of use for the price of a few trail mixes. It suits bug-heavy, dry-climate trips where laundry gets done in a sink and hangs overnight.

Thru-hikers and base-weight-conscious backpackers should look elsewhere. A synthetic sun shirt or lightweight long-sleeve covers more skin, dries in a fraction of the time, and weighs less. Hikers in humid or rainy regions will also find a cotton-blend shirt a poor match.

## Verdict

The Expert Kiwi is a well-priced, durable workhorse with real insect and sun features, and the long-term owner reports back that up. It is simply built for a different hiker than the ultralight one: heavier and slower to dry than the shirts Packstack readers typically carry. Buy it for car-camping, day hikes and travel; skip it for a long trail.
