## Overview

The Gamma LT is a thin softshell pant that has spent more than a decade as a default choice for climbers, guides and backpackers who want one pair of trousers for scrambling, wind and drizzle. It is cut from Fortius DW 2.0, a stretchy nylon-elastane blend with a DWR finish, and weighs 295–355 g depending on version. The trade-off is simple: more weather protection and abrasion resistance than a typical hiking pant, at the cost of some ventilation on hot climbs.

## Performance

**Mobility.** The Gamma LT's reputation rests on how it moves. Arc'teryx pairs the four-way stretch fabric with an articulated cut and a gusseted crotch, and owners consistently describe high-stepping, scrambling and climbing in them without any binding. One Sierra backpacker, writing on Trailspace after a long solo trip, said the pant fit "like a glove" and called it the best hiking pant he had owned. A reviewer who climbs in the pant says it is light and easy to wear, and sturdier than its weight suggests.

**Weather resistance.** The DWR-treated Fortius shell sheds wind well and handles light rain. Owners report steady showers beading off a new pair. Long-term owners add the caveat that the finish fades: one who has bought five pairs over the years sprays older pairs with DWR before hiking in rain. Reviewers at SierraDescents frame the pant as a stand-in for a hard shell in most conditions. The exceptions are all-day downpours and bitter, windy cold.

**Temperature range.** Because the fabric is thin and tightly woven, the pant covers a wider band than its weight would suggest. Owners describe wearing it in warm weather and in winter. One reports walking to work at -35 degrees with a base layer underneath. Another uses it with long underwear for snowshoeing. The flip side is that it is a softshell, not a mesh-backed trail pant. In sustained heat, it runs warmer than a woven hiking pant or a nylon trail short.

**Durability.** Fortius DW 2.0 is Arc'teryx's abrasion-resistant softshell weave, and owners who bushwhack, scramble over rock and push through deadfall report it holds up. A repeat buyer who wears it daily for biking, hiking and travel has gone through several pairs without complaint about the fabric. The one durability warning from owners is crampon or spike strikes. Winter users suggest gaiters to protect the cuffs and lower legs.

**Features and fit.** The integrated webbing belt is a regular point of praise because it removes the need for a separate belt under a hip belt or harness. The pockets are deep enough to hold a phone, and one owner uses the zip pocket for a passport. The one gap reviewers mention is the lack of back pockets, which matters little on trail and more in town. Owners also say the regular inseam runs an inch or two long, so shorter hikers should look at the short length. Several wear the pant off-trail as well, since its slim, tidy look passes in town.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions differ chiefly in weight: the Men's pant weighs 355 g, about 60 g more than the Standard version at 295 g. That is roughly a fifth heavier, a difference that matters to gram-counters but is hard to feel on the body. Fabric, pocket layout and closure are the same across the line, so the choice comes down to which pattern fits. Hikers should pick the version cut for their build rather than chase the lighter number. Trailspace and retailer reviewers report that sizing is trim, and owners with thicker legs say they sized to their usual number and found the fit comfortable.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Four-way stretch, an articulated cut and a gusseted crotch suit scrambling and climbing
- Wind-blocking, DWR-treated fabric handles light rain
- Integrated webbing belt works under a harness or hip belt
- Abrasion-resistant Fortius DW 2.0 holds up to rock and brush
- Usable from warm days to winter outings with the right layers
- Three zip pockets, including a thigh pocket that holds a phone

**Cons**

- Not waterproof, and DWR wears off with use
- Warmer than a woven trail pant in hot weather
- No rear pockets
- Regular inseam runs long for shorter hikers
- Expensive next to most hiking pants

## Who Should Buy This

The Gamma LT suits backpackers, scramblers and alpine climbers who want a single pant for shoulder-season trips, ridgelines and mixed terrain. It is also a good choice for anyone who wants a pant that looks respectable in a trail town. Desert and summer thru-hikers who spend most days in heat should look at a lighter woven pant or shorts, and anyone expecting multi-day rain needs a proper rain pant in the pack.

## Verdict

The Gamma LT delivers the mobility, wind protection and durability that have kept it in the Arc'teryx line for years. At 295–355 g, it is a good pair of pants for three-season trips, with the usual softshell limits on heat and heavy rain. Buy it if you want one dependable pant for varied terrain, and skip it if your hiking is mostly hot and dry.
