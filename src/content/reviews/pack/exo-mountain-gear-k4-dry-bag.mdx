## Overview

The K4 Dry Bag is a roll-top liner cut to fit inside Exo Mountain Gear's K4 3600 pack. It replaces the pack cover or the contractor bag with a fitted, seam-sealed shell around the gear that has to stay dry. It is built for mountain hunters and wet-climate backpackers, and the trade-off is simple: 184.7 g and roughly $80 buys protection that the pack fabric alone doesn't claim to provide.

## Performance

Exo's K4 bags are already water resistant. The main fabric handles light rain and snow, and the bags have a waterproof-lined bladder compartment with a drain grommet. Exo itself draws the line at extreme conditions, and that is where the liner comes in. The shell is 70D TPU-laminated ripstop with a roll-top closure, which makes it waterproof in the way a pack's coated fabric and zippered pockets can't be. A pack cover keeps rain off the outside. A liner keeps water out of the gear even when the pack gets dunked or the rain comes in sideways.

Owner reports on Exo's site, all 12 of them positive, put the liner in exactly those conditions. One Alaska hunter described rain every day and several water crossings, including packs that went fully into the water, and still had a dry sleeping bag at night. Another hiked out 24 km in pouring rain, hail and snow without a single breach. A backpacker who had carried pack covers for years called the liner the better option and noted that it doubles as a high-quality standalone dry bag.

Fit is the other recurring theme. The 3600 liner is sized to the K4 3600's main bag, and owners say it slides in cleanly, which is the point: no wrestling with a loose trash bag, no dead space eating volume. Because it is sized for the pack rather than a generic liner, it also carries the pack's compression behavior with it, since K4 bags compress considerably.

The liner also serves a second job. Exo says it can ride in the load shelf between the pack and frame, which turns it into extra waterproof carrying capacity for bulky items such as a sleeping bag or camp kit. Carryology's tester ran a dedicated 3600 cu-in dry bag in that position as part of a modular K4 setup.

The weight cost is real. The K4 3600 main bag weighs 2 lb 1 oz, so the liner adds roughly a fifth to the bag's weight. That is a fair price in the context of a pack system that weighs 5 lb 8 oz, and K4 owners are not counting grams the way a thru-hiker does. Carryology specifically lists ultralight backpackers as the people the K4 system isn't for.

Roll-top closures need a little discipline. The seal depends on three or so clean rolls, and anything stuffed into the top of a full liner will fight the closure. That is true of every roll-top bag, and it's a smaller hassle than a pack cover that flaps loose in wind.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fully waterproof, which the K4 pack fabric alone doesn't claim to be
- Sized to the K4 3600, so it fits without dead space or loose folds
- 70D TPU-laminated ripstop with custom roll-top hardware, made in the USA
- Works as a standalone dry bag or as a load-shelf bag between pack and frame
- Owners report it holding up through river crossings and multi-day storms

**Cons**

- 184.7 g is a poor deal for anyone who would otherwise carry a light trash bag
- From $79.99, it costs more than generic dry bags and liners
- Cut for the K4 system, so it is a poor fit in other packs
- Roll-top access means digging from the top of the bag rather than from side pockets

## Who Should Buy This

K4 3600 owners who hunt or hike in wet country, especially anywhere with river crossings, should buy it. It suits Alaska, the Pacific Northwest and any trip where a soaked sleeping bag would end the hunt. It also suits anyone who wants a single fitted piece of kit that serves as a liner, a standalone dry bag and a load-shelf bag.

Skip it if the pack isn't a K4 3600, or if base weight is the priority. Ultralight hikers on dry-climate routes will do just as well with a lighter liner, and anyone carrying a different pack should look at generic roll-top liners.

## Verdict

The K4 Dry Bag does one job: it makes a K4 3600 fully waterproof, and owners say it does that without leaks. The price and 185 g are the cost of that certainty, and for K4 owners in wet country, it's a fair one. Others can skip it.
