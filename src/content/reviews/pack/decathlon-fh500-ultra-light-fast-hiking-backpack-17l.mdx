## Overview

The FH500 is Decathlon's answer to the fast-hiking daypack: a frameless, 400 g, 17-liter sack that hugs the torso like a running vest and carries a 2L bladder, a pair of belt pockets and trekking poles. It is built for people who want to cover ground quickly with a light kit, not haul a full day-hike loadout. The trade-off is the usual one for frameless packs at this price: very little weight, very little structure, and a few ergonomic compromises that owners are quick to point out.

## Performance

**Carry and fit.** The FH500 has no internal frame or stiffener, so it moulds to the back rather than holding a shape of its own. One owner who bought it for 75 km and 55 km hilly ultra-walks described it as light and comfortable, a deliberate step down from a full-size walking pack. Decathlon's "enveloping" design is meant to keep the load close and still, and a Swiss buyer reported it sits snug on the body in high-mountain hiking. Customer scores for carrying comfort mostly sit at the top of the scale, with a handful of 2/5 and 3/5 outliers.

**Organization.** The headline feature is a large front opening that reaches the bottom of the bag, so pulling out a rain shell or lunch does not mean excavating from the top. Beyond the main compartment, there is a stretch net pocket, a dedicated smartphone pocket and two belt pockets. Poles stow in a dedicated holder. A UK owner who runs and walks with it called the capacity right for emergency waterproofs and a few extras. It is a one-compartment bag, so it rewards a tidy packer.

**Hydration.** The pack takes a 2L bladder, passed through a port and held upright by two velcro straps, according to one owner. The flip side is heat. Because the reservoir sits almost directly against the back, one UK owner reported the water warming to lukewarm after a few miles and planned to add thin insulation. Another reviewer noted the side pocket only fits a small bottle, not a large one.

**Ergonomic quirks.** The complaints are specific and consistent. A US reviewer said the chest strap rides up and presses on the throat, and that the belt pockets are just a bit too small for a modern smartphone. Another pointed out that the internal phone pocket means taking the pack off to reach the device, which defeats the point on a fast hike. A French reviewer reported a soaked back even on a one-hour outing in cool weather with breathable clothing, which is the predictable cost of a snug, unventilated frameless design.

**Weather and durability.** The pack is not waterproof, and the spec sheet lists no reflective elements, so a pack liner and a headlamp are on the owner. The polyamide body is described by Decathlon as abrasion-resistant, and the 10-year Quechua backpack warranty covers repair, replacement or refund for defects, zippers to seams. It is also machine washable, a useful trait for a pack that will get sweaty.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 400 g (14.1 oz) for 17 liters, with hip belt and chest strap included
- Large front opening gives access to the bottom of the bag
- Pole holder, belt pockets, net pocket and bladder sleeve in a small package
- 10-year warranty and a price that undercuts most ultralight-brand daypacks
- Frameless design moulds to the back and moves with the body
- Machine washable

**Cons**

- Chest strap rides up toward the throat for some wearers
- Belt pockets are tight for current large smartphones; the internal phone pocket is awkward to reach on the move
- Bladder rests against the back, so water warms and the back runs wet
- Side pocket suits only small bottles
- Not waterproof and no reflective details
- Frameless build limits comfortable load weight

## Who Should Buy This

Fast-packers, trail runners who walk, and day hikers who carry a shell, a layer, food and water, and not much else. It also works as a summit or side-trip pack for thru-hikers who want a second, cheap, light bag. Anyone who regularly carries more than a light daypack load, who wants a ventilated back panel or who needs a bottle pocket that takes a liter bottle should look at a framed pack with a mesh back.

## Verdict

The FH500 delivers a real 400 g, 17L daypack with belt pockets, a bladder sleeve and a long warranty for well under what ultralight cottage brands charge. It has a few annoyances, among them a chest strap that wanders, small belt pockets and a sweaty, warm-water back, but none is a deal-breaker for light, fast days. It earns a recommendation for the light-load hiker on a budget.
