## Overview

The Camp Self Inflating Mat sits near the entry point of Sea to Summit's sleeping pad lineup — a self-inflating foam-and-air hybrid built for campers and backpackers who want set-it-and-forget-it convenience rather than the fuss of a blow-up-only ultralight pad. It's the kind of pad that's happy on a car camping trip, a scout outing, or a backpacking trip where you're willing to trade a few extra ounces for a pad that basically inflates itself and won't leave you second-guessing your patch kit every night.

## Performance

**Warmth.** A 4.2 R-value puts this squarely in 3-season territory — comfortable from spring through fall, and you can push it into shoulder-season winter camping if you're not dealing with truly frozen ground. That's in line with what Sea to Summit's own product literature claims — <cite index="14-5">the Sea to Summit Camp Self-Inflating Foam Sleeping Mat is described as a great all-around, 4-season sleeping pad for backpacking, camping, and scouting trips</cite>, though I'd call that generous marketing framing. Practically speaking, treat the 4.2 rating as a solid "not-quite-winter" number rather than a true four-season pad — you'll want a closed-cell foam pad underneath once temps drop much below freezing.

**Comfort.** At 4 cm thick, this isn't a plush pad by inflatable-mat standards, but the foam core does a good job of preventing that "feeling every pebble" sensation you get with thin air-only pads. One retailer selling the Regular size summed up the appeal well, noting <cite index="15-6">Sea to Summit's use of a 75D Polyester makes for a tougher, more abrasion resistant base, while at 3.8cm thick, the Camp Mat is thick enough to keep even the boniest of hips from pushing through to the ground</cite>. Side sleepers and anyone with hip or shoulder sensitivity will still probably want something thicker — this pad is more "adequate" than "cushy."

**Inflation & valve.** The self-inflating design means you open the valve, let the foam do its thing for a few minutes, then add a handful of top-off breaths to firm it up — no dedicated pump or lung-searing full inflation required. The XPEL valve is a twist-open/twist-closed design that also allows for quick one-way deflation, which matters more than it sounds like when you're trying to roll up a pad before your foam has fully "settled" — trying to roll a self-inflating pad that's still fighting you is a good way to strain a shoulder.

**Durability.** The 75D recycled polyester face fabric with TPU lamination is on the tougher end for this pad category, and Sea to Summit's broader self-inflating line has a reputation for holding up over the long haul. One long-time owner of the brand's self-inflating pad line noted <cite index="2-1,2-2">they'd had the original S2S ultralight pad for probably longer than 10 years now, calling it "a beast"</cite> — anecdotal, and for a different model in the lineup, but it speaks to the general build quality Sea to Summit tends to deliver across this product family. I couldn't find much dedicated, model-specific feedback for the base Camp Self Inflating Mat on Reddit or BPL — most enthusiast discussion clusters around the pricier UltraLight and Camp Plus versions — so treat durability claims here as reasonably confident but not exhaustively field-tested by the ultralight crowd, since this pad isn't really aimed at them.

**Weight & packability.** At 944.9 g, this pad is not going to make anyone's ultralight gear list — it's a "camp" pad, and the name is honest about that. If you're backpacking and grams matter, this is a pad you carry because you value the low-fuss inflation and foam comfort over shaving weight, not because it competes with 400-500 gram air pads.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely easy inflation — no pump needed, minimal lung effort
- Durable 75D face fabric that should shrug off typical ground abrasion
- Solid 3-season warmth for the price point
- Foam core adds a margin of safety against punctures compared to air-only pads
- XPEL valve makes packing down quick and controlled

**Cons**

- Heavy relative to true backpacking/ultralight pads
- 4 cm thickness is fine, not plush — side sleepers may want more cushion
- R-4.2 is solid but not a genuine winter pad despite some four-season framing
- Limited independent long-term reviews specific to this exact model (most chatter is about siblings in the lineup)

## Who Should Buy This

This pad makes the most sense for car campers, scouts, and beginner-to-intermediate backpackers who want a no-drama pad that inflates itself and won't need constant top-offs through the night. If you're chasing an ultralight base weight or need genuine winter performance, look elsewhere in Sea to Summit's lineup — the Camp Plus or an air-pad with a higher R-value will serve you better. But for weekend trips, family camping, or a first backpacking pad that won't punish your wallet or your patience, this one earns its keep.

## Verdict

The Camp Self Inflating Mat does exactly what its name promises: it self-inflates, it's reasonably warm for three-season use, and it's built from tough enough materials to survive years of casual abuse. It's not a pad for gram-counters or winter camping, but as a dependable, low-maintenance sleeping pad for camping and general backpacking, it's a solid 7/10.
