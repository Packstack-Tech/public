## Overview

The GP-VPT3 is Sony's latest take on the shooting grip/tripod/remote combo that's become a staple accessory for Alpha shooters and vloggers. It's a 3-in-1 device: a comfortable handgrip for handheld shooting, a fold-out tabletop tripod, and — new for this generation — a fully detachable Bluetooth remote (the RMT-VP2) that pops off the top of the grip. If you shoot solo content, run-and-gun vlogs, or just want a lighter alternative to a gimbal for talking-head videos, this is built for you.

## Performance

The headline feature here is the detachable remote, and it's the thing every reviewer seems to zero in on. <cite index="1-3">The most significant change is that the wireless remote controls are now incorporated into a separate Bluetooth remote control module (RMT-VP2) which is detachable from the shooting grip.</cite> On the previous GP-VPT2BT, the buttons were fixed to the grip — fine if you're always holding the camera, less fine if you want to set the camera on the tripod legs and control it from across the room or from in front of the lens. One Best Buy reviewer who'd used two GP-VPT2BT units for years called the swap to a removable remote <cite index="1-6">"a game-changing feature when shooting talking-head videos."</cite>

Dustin Abbott's review echoes the same theme from a competitive-pressure angle: <cite index="10-2,10-3">the old grip's biggest liability was that while it used Bluetooth rather than a wired connection, the controls were fixed, and third-party alternatives soon improved on that by having a removable remote control.</cite> Sony basically caught up to the aftermarket here rather than innovating first, but the execution seems solid — Alpha Universe's roundup of early reviewer reactions included comments like <cite index="9-5">"This thing is very handy for when you want to control your Sony camera wirelessly,"</cite> and more than one reviewer said they'd be buying the remote for themselves.

As a physical grip, it's smaller than its predecessor — <cite index="14-1">the grip is 6 7/8 inches long compared to the previous model's 8.5 inches</cite> — which is a mixed bag. Smaller means less reach for those creative low/high-angle shots the old grip was known for, but it also means less bulk in a bag. One reviewer specifically liked the extra reach of the older, longer grip <cite index="1-8">for shooting from high angles and low angles, without being a contortionist</cite>, so if that's your primary use case, know that the GP-VPT3 trims some of that length.

As a tripod, it does what a tabletop tripod does — nothing more, nothing less. One user was upfront about that trade-off: <cite index="11-1">"It may not be a stabilizing gimbal, but it is compact and lightweight, very utilitarian, easy to use and carry and store in even a small camera bag."</cite> That's the right way to think about this thing. It's not going to give you gimbal-smooth walking shots; it's a stable base for interviews, desk setups, or a quick handheld-to-tripod transition mid-shoot.

For vlogging specifically, the balance point seems to be a genuine strength. One ZV-E1 owner running a 16-35mm G Master lens said the combo let them skip a gimbal entirely, calling it <cite index="2-9">"the perfect solution right weight and well balance."</cite> They also liked that <cite index="2-10">controlling the camera from the handle let them focus on shooting rather than the technical aspects</cite> — which is really the whole point of a device like this: fewer trips to the camera body to hit record.

On the spec side, the tilt/rotate range is respectable for a grip this size — <cite index="2-6">the integrated head can tilt upward 80° (40° when set up as a tripod), downward 90°, and rotate 360° for easier positioning.</cite> And Sony didn't skimp on durability: <cite index="2-7">the grip is dust and moisture resistant to suit its use with select weather-sealed cameras.</cite>

Compatibility is broad across recent Alpha bodies. It's confirmed to work with the a7 III, a7 IV, the newer a7 V, the ZV-E10, and the ZV-E1, among others — basically anything in Sony's current Bluetooth-enabled mirrorless lineup. Double-check your specific body against Sony's compatibility list before buying, since older or budget models may lack the Bluetooth remote pairing needed to use the full feature set.

One thing I can't speak to with confidence: long-term battery life on the CR2032 or how the Bluetooth pairing holds up after months of daily attach/detach cycles on the remote's connector. The product is recent enough that there isn't a large body of long-term ownership data yet, so treat durability claims beyond "dust/moisture resistant" as unproven until more people put a year or two on theirs.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Detachable remote finally closes the gap with third-party grips that beat Sony to this feature
- Genuinely useful as a gimbal alternative for solo, relatively static vlogging setups
- Dust and moisture resistant build
- 360° rotation and generous tilt range for a device this small
- Compact and light at 174–179 g, easy to leave in a bag permanently

**Cons**

- Shorter grip than the previous GP-VPT2BT means less reach for extreme high/low angles
- Not a stabilizer — don't expect gimbal-smooth walking footage
- Bluetooth-only connectivity means it's limited to select Sony bodies with that pairing support
- Premium price relative to third-party grip/tripod combos with similar functionality
- Limited long-term reliability data so far, given how recently it launched

## Who Should Buy This

This is aimed squarely at solo Sony shooters — vloggers, YouTubers, and content creators who need to start/stop recording, zoom, or trigger the shutter without walking back to the camera. If you shoot mostly stationary desk or interview setups and want a stable, portable base plus wireless control, this fits well. If you need active stabilization while walking or running, this isn't a gimbal replacement — pair it with one instead of expecting it to do that job.

## Verdict

The GP-VPT3 is a meaningful upgrade over the GP-VPT2BT, mainly because the detachable remote fixes the previous model's biggest annoyance. It won't replace a gimbal, and the shorter grip length is a real trade-off for anyone who loved the old model's extended reach, but as a lightweight, weather-resistant grip/tripod/remote combo for solo Sony shooters, it earns its place in the bag. I'd put it at a 7.8/10 — solid, purpose-built, and clearly informed by what third-party competitors were already doing better.
