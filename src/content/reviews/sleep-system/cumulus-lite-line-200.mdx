## Overview

The Cumulus Lite Line 200 is a Polish-made, 850-fill-power down sleeping bag built for warm-weather backpacking — think late spring through early fall, or as a lightweight summer bag for people who run cold. Cumulus has been making down gear in Poland since 1989, and the Lite Line is their minimalist, weight-obsessed take on a three-season-adjacent bag rather than a true shoulder-season workhorse. If you're chasing a sub-500g sleep system and don't need frost-level margin, this is squarely in the conversation with bags like the Western Mountaineering SummerLite or the Enlightened Equipment Enigma.

## Performance

**Warmth for the weight.** At 495g with only 200g of fill, this bag lives or dies on how well it uses that down, and the 850 fill power is doing real work here — Cumulus's own numbers put the <cite index="6-1">comfort temperature at 9 degrees, which makes it best suited for use in late spring, summer and early autumn</cite>. That tracks with how most ultralight sleeping bags in this weight class perform in practice: fine down to around 45°F, and you'll want a puffy jacket, a bag liner, or a warmer layer on your legs if temps dip toward freezing. Cumulus itself pitches this as a warm-weather piece, and one retailer even suggests it can flex into three-season use if you're willing to sleep in a <cite index="5-14">down vest, thermoactive underwear and wool socks</cite> — a fair characterization of how thin-fill bags like this one work: they're a system component, not a standalone cold-weather solution.

**Fabric and fill quality.** The Pertex Quantum shell at 29 g/m² is about as light as down-proof fabric gets while still holding up to normal backpacking abuse — it's not going to survive a bushwhack through blackberry thickets, but for trail use it's proven itself in plenty of ultralight bags and quilts over the years. Cumulus sources and inspects its own goose down rather than outsourcing quality control, and <cite index="6-4,6-5">all of the kit Cumulus makes is manufactured in Poland, with the company inspecting all the down they use to guarantee quality</cite>. That in-house QC is a big part of why 850 fill power bags from smaller European makers like Cumulus punch above their price point compared to mass-market 800-fill options.

**Design details that matter on trail.** The two-way zipper is a nice touch for a summer bag — you can crack the footbox open on a warm night without unzipping the whole thing, since <cite index="5-9,5-10">Cumulus changed their zipper on all Lite Line series, adding a small flap at the top to keep the cold zipper off your face and a second zipper at the bottom to vent the feet area</cite>. Cumulus also addressed a common down-bag annoyance — Pertex catching in the zipper track — by adding a small plastic zipper guard, per the same product listing. The hood is adjustable via drawcords, and there's a stretch mesh interior pocket sized to hold a phone and small power bank, letting you use your phone from inside the bag without fishing it out into the cold. None of this is groundbreaking, but it's the kind of detail work you notice on a multi-week trip.

**Packed size and field reports.** At 19 x 15 cm packed, it compresses to roughly the size of a 1L water bottle, which is genuinely tiny for a full sleeping bag and makes it an easy fit in a 40-50L ultralight pack with room to spare. Independent field data on the Lite Line 200 specifically is thin online — most of the detailed first-hand accounts I found were for Cumulus's related X-Lite and Panyam lines rather than this exact model — so treat the packed-size and compressibility claims as manufacturer-plus-retailer sourced rather than exhaustively field-verified by third parties. That said, Cumulus's broader reputation among European ultralight hikers is solid, and the brand shows up regularly in gear lists from thru-hikers doing GR20, the Alps, and Scandinavian trails where warm-but-not-hot nights are the norm.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely light — 495g for a full-length mummy bag with a hood is competitive with cottage-industry quilts
- 850 fill power and in-house down sourcing suggest good loft-per-gram value
- Thoughtful zipper design (two-way vent, cold flap, anti-snag guard) that shows real user-experience thinking
- Packs down small enough to disappear into any ultralight pack
- Can be paired/zipped with other Cumulus bags for double-bag setups

**Cons**

- Comfort rating of 9°C / 48°F means this is a true 2-season bag, not a shoulder-season one — plan your layering accordingly if nights get cold
- Pertex Quantum shell, while light, isn't especially abrasion-resistant; care needed around rough tent floors or gear with velcro
- Limited third-party long-term reviews specifically on this model make it harder to verify durability claims independently
- Cumulus ships from Poland, so US buyers may face longer lead times and higher shipping/import costs than domestic brands

## Who Should Buy This

This bag is best for hikers who want the lightest possible bag for summer trips, warm-climate thru-hikes, or as the "warm half" of a layered two-bag system for shoulder seasons. If your trips regularly see nights below 40°F, look at Cumulus's heavier Lite Line or Panyam models instead — this one is not the bag for that. But if you're counting grams for a summer PCT section, a Scandinavian or Alpine trek, or bikepacking trips where pack volume is at a premium, the Lite Line 200 hits a weight-to-warmth ratio that's hard to beat without stepping into custom quilt territory.

## Verdict

The Lite Line 200 delivers on its narrow promise: a genuinely ultralight, well-detailed summer sleeping bag from a manufacturer with a long track record in down insulation. It's not a do-everything bag, and buyers should go in expecting exactly the 9°C comfort rating Cumulus advertises rather than hoping for extra margin. For the right season and the right hiker, I'd rate it an 8/10 — dock a point or two if you need broader temperature range, add one back if summer-weight is exactly what you're shopping for.
