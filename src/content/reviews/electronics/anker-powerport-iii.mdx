## Overview

The Anker PowerPort III is a one-port, 20W USB-C wall charger with PowerIQ 3.0, built here with a UK Type G plug. It suits hikers who resupply in British towns, hostels and bothies-with-sockets and need to bring a phone back from 10% overnight. The trade-off: it charges phones briskly, but the fixed three-pin body makes it a heavier, bulkier carry than the sugar-cube chargers it resembles.

## Performance

**Phone charging.** Twenty watts is the sweet spot for the iPhone, and it shows. Android Authority's testing of the Nano-format charger measured 18.7W reaching an iPhone 13, a little shy of the rating but, in practice, a negligible gap. GIGAZINE's testers, using a USB power meter, logged roughly 17.8W into an iPhone 11 Pro Max and about 19.4W into a Pixel 3a XL. Both phones went from single digits to about 90% in an hour. Anker's own claim is 53% on an iPhone 11 in 30 minutes. For a trail-town stop, that means a phone is mostly full by the time dinner is finished.

**Compatibility.** PowerIQ 3.0 is Anker's branding for a port that follows the USB Power Delivery 3.0 standard. It also covers Quick Charge, Apple Fast Charging and other older protocols. Android Authority found it did a decent job with older handsets such as the LG G8X and Nokia 7.1, and that it handles a Nintendo Switch and other low-power USB-C accessories. Pixel-class phones that top out at 18W charge at their full rate. Faster phones fall back to 18W Power Delivery, so nothing fails to charge. They just charge more slowly.

**Where it falls short.** The charger lacks PPS (Programmable Power Supply). Android Authority recorded only 14.3W reaching a Samsung phone that can pull 25W on a PPS-capable charger. Tablets and laptops will draw power but not quickly; the reviewer's verdict was to forget about charging larger USB-C devices with it. Anyone carrying a modern phone that can take far more than 20W will find the ceiling low. TechRadar made the same point, calling it a competent plug rather than a standout.

**Portability.** At 54 g, this is the real cost. The US-plug version of the Nano is quoted at about 30 g, so the UK variant carries nearly twice the mass, and the three-pin Type G plug cannot fold away. TechRadar marked down the PowerPort III 20W for its non-foldable prongs, noting that Anker's pricier 30W Nano charger has them. Owners of the related Nano models make the same complaint: the fixed prongs scrape against the inside of a pouch and are awkward to pack flat.

**Build and durability.** Reviewers describe the casing as neat and sturdily built. Owner reports on Walmart and eBay are overwhelmingly positive, including one buyer who handed several to hard-on-gear children and reported them still working six months later. Anker backs the line with an 18-month warranty, according to buyers.

**Value.** TechRadar's list of pros starts with price: the charger is very cheap. No cable comes in the box, so budget for a USB-C cable. One eBay buyer was caught out buying a Lightning-to-USB-C cable for an iPhone, since the old USB-A cable will not fit the port.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Fast, reliable 20W charging for iPhones and most Android phones
- Broad protocol support, including Power Delivery 3.0, Quick Charge and Apple Fast Charging
- 100–240V input works abroad with a plug adapter
- Inexpensive, with solid build quality reported by owners

**Cons**

- 54 g is heavy for a single-port 20W charger; the US-plug Nano is about 30 g
- Fixed Type G prongs do not fold
- No PPS support, so some Samsung phones charge slower than they can
- One port only, with no second device charging
- Cable not included

## Who Should Buy This

Buy this if you are a UK-based hiker or a visitor on a British long-distance path who charges a single phone from a wall socket every few days. It also suits anyone who wants a spare, low-cost charger and is not trying to shave every gram. Look elsewhere if you carry a phone, watch and headphones together (a two-port charger weighs less than two separate adapters), if you own a Samsung that benefits from PPS, or if you want a folding-prong charger that packs flat. Weight-obsessed hikers should consider a smaller GaN charger with foldable prongs, or one of Anker's 30W Nano models.

## Verdict

The PowerPort III does its one job well: it fills a phone quickly, runs on any mains supply in the world with a plug adapter, and costs little. It loses points for its 54 g mass, fixed prongs and single port, which make it a competent choice for a UK town stop, but not a standout pick for an ultralight kit.
