## Overview

The SnapBox Original is a palm-sized polypropylene box with a snap-lock lid, designed by Joachim Nordwall and made in Sweden by Light My Fire. It suits backpackers who want a rigid, reusable container for snacks, spices, tinder or small kit. The trade-off is simple: it protects its contents far better than a bag does, and it weighs far more than a bag does.

## Performance

**Closure and weatherproofing.** The lid is the point of the whole design. It snaps down tight, and a pull tab opens it without a fingernail fight. Light My Fire calls the box waterproof and airtight, and the lid is marketed as protecting both the contents and the rest of the pack. That makes the SnapBox useful for the jobs where a zip bag fails: crushable crackers, tortilla chips, a tablespoon of olive oil or hot sauce, damp tinder, or a handful of salt and sugar that should not end up in a sleeping bag.

**Size and capacity.** At 170 ml, the box is small by design. It holds a day's trail mix for one person, a few spice portions, or a bundle of electrolyte tablets and a few meds. It is not a lunch container. Anyone planning to carry a full sandwich or a hearty portion of couscous will need the larger Light My Fire boxes. The internal measuring lines at 50, 100 and 150 ml are crude, but they work for rehydrating a small portion of couscous or measuring out a drink mix, with no extra cup.

**Packing and nesting.** The square 101 x 101 mm footprint stacks cleanly, and the 40 mm height fits flat into a lid pocket or a side pocket. Light My Fire designs the box to nest inside its MealKit and LunchKit, and a folded Pack-up Cup or Spill-free Cup also fits inside. For anyone already using those kits, the SnapBox takes up almost no extra space. For everyone else, it is a rigid 101 mm square that does not compress as a bag does when the food runs out.

**Durability and cleanup.** Polypropylene is tough, and the box has no sharp corners to trap food. Light My Fire describes the plastic as stain and odor resistant, which matters if the same box carries curry powder one week and granola the next. It is dishwasher and microwave safe, and it floats, so a box dropped in a stream at least stays visible. Owner comments on retailer pages are brief and positive: small boxes that fit easily in a bag, and a favorite snack container.

**Weight.** This is where ultralight hikers will balk. At 38 g, one box weighs more than a handful of zip bags or a small roll-top bag, and the 2-pack is listed at 89 g, slightly more than two singles. Gram-counters will call it a luxury. Hikers who carry a bear-canister-sized pile of loose items, though, will find it easier to organize a pack with a few of these than to dig through a dozen bags.

## Choosing a Version

The two versions contain the same box. Nothing changes in capacity, dimensions or materials.

- **Standard (38 g):** One box. The right pick for someone who wants a single container for spices, a snack, or a fire kit. It is also the lightest way to try the concept.
- **2-Pack (89 g):** Two boxes, supplied in contrasting lid colors. The colors are useful in practice, since one box can hold sweet snacks and the other savory, or one can hold food and the other first-aid items, with no need to open either to check. The listed 89 g works out to about 44 g per box, a little more than the single. The 2-pack is the better buy for couples, groups, or anyone who plans to separate food from kit.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Tight snap-lock lid with an easy pull tab
- Crush-proof and reusable, with no seams to fail
- Measuring lines at 50, 100 and 150 ml
- Stackable, and nests inside the MealKit, LunchKit and Pack-up Cup
- Floats, and is dishwasher and microwave safe
- Resists stains and odors
- Made in Sweden from BPA-free, food-approved plastic

**Cons**

- 38 g per box is hefty for a 170 ml container
- Rigid shape does not shrink as food is eaten
- Too small for a full meal
- Measuring lines are approximate

## Who Should Buy This

The SnapBox suits section hikers, weekenders and campers who want a tidy, crush-proof way to carry snacks, spices or small kit, along with anyone already using a Light My Fire mess kit. It also works well as a lunchbox item or for travel. Base-weight purists and thru-hikers who count every gram should look elsewhere. A zip bag or a repurposed peanut butter jar fills most of the same roles for less weight and no cost, and the SnapBox only earns its place when crushability or leaks are the problem.

## Verdict

The SnapBox Original is a well-made, sensibly priced box that does one job cleanly. It is better suited to organized car campers and weekend hikers than to anyone shaving ounces, and it earns a recommendation on that basis.
