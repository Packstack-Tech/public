## Overview

The Spark Pro is Sea to Summit's flagship ultralight mummy: 950+ fill-power goose down in a 10D nylon shell, rated to -9°C / 15°F, with a three-zipper layout that lets it open out like a quilt. It is built for three-season and shoulder-season backpackers who want real cold-weather margin without a pound-plus of insulation. The trade-off is the usual one at this level: a $649 list price and a shell that needs gentle handling.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Reviewers who have taken the Spark Pro below freezing describe it as honest about its numbers. A tester at The Big Outside slept through a sub-freezing night zipped up in a light wool T-shirt, underwear and socks, adding a long-sleeve top and a wool hat only in the pre-dawn chill. A Trekitt reviewer on a March trip in the Peak District, with temperatures around -2°C, stayed warm in merino base layers, wool socks and a thin balaclava. At Backwoods Pursuit, the bag kept its tester comfortable on nights that dipped into the teens Fahrenheit, below the 27°F comfort figure. Adventure Alan's testers called the rating conservative and said the bag could have been labeled a 10°F model.

**Down and construction.** The 950+ fill is the headline. Most premium bags use 850 or 900 fill, and the Spark Pro's down lofts to roughly four inches when pulled from its stuff sack, according to The Big Outside. Baffling is mixed: vertical baffles over the chest, horizontal baffles on the lower top side and underside, and box-wall construction throughout. The idea is to keep the down from migrating and to avoid cold spots, and testers at Backpacker and The Big Outside both noted that it worked. The Ultra-Dry treatment is non-PFAS. It helps the down shed damp and dry faster, but it doesn't make the bag waterproof.

**Ventilation and versatility.** This is where the Spark Pro departs from a conventional mummy. The full-length side zip, footbox zip and shoulder zip let the bag open flat like a quilt, vent at the feet, or free the arms while the hood stays on. Owners on REI and r/Ultralight like the flexibility: one runs it fully open on 40–50°F nights and zipped up when it's colder, and a hot sleeper at 6 ft likes having so many places to dump heat. The skeptics have a point. Adventure Alan's reviewers argued that the quilt-conversion zippers add weight and cost and leave the bag short of a true ultralight rating. Section Hiker's testers, reviewing the 30°F version, singled out the short side zipper for coming off its track and being hard to re-seat from inside the bag.

**Fit and comfort.** The cut is roomy for a bag this light, with 61 inches of shoulder girth reported by Backpacker's testers. One r/Ultralight owner said he can fold his legs inside and turn around easily, and he called it one of very few sub-900 g wide bags with a below-zero comfort rating. The 10D liner is soft and breathable. Side sleepers have mixed reports: Section Hiker's testers found the hood comfortable even for them, while one r/Ultralight user gave up the bag because she kept sliding off her pad, something a quilt strapped to the pad avoids. A taller sleeper at 6 ft with a regular-length bag said he had room to spare but would have wanted more if he'd bought the long.

**Durability.** The 10D shell and liner are the weight-saving trade. The Equipment Guide noted the fabric calls for careful handling. Anti-snag zipper tracks and a zipper plow help, but this is not a bag to drag across granite.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 950+ fill-power down at 861 g, with a 15°F lower limit that testers report is accurate or conservative
- Packs to 15.5 x 22.5 x 41 cm
- Free-Flow zips allow quilt-style venting, arms-out sitting and an open footbox
- Mixed vertical and horizontal baffles keep the down in place
- Roomy for a mummy, with a soft 10D liner
- Fits sleepers up to 198 cm

**Cons**

- $649 list price
- The extra zippers add weight and complexity; some reviewers wanted a simpler bag
- Reports of the short side zipper derailing on the 30°F version
- 10D fabric needs careful handling
- A hooded mummy can't be strapped to a pad as a quilt can, which some side sleepers find a problem

## Who Should Buy This

The Spark Pro suits backpackers who sleep cold, who camp from spring through late fall, and who want one bag that covers freezing nights and also vents on mild ones. It is a good fit for those who like a mummy's hood and draft collar but still want to throw it open like a blanket. Warm sleepers who rarely see temperatures below 40°F will carry unneeded insulation and should look at the 30°F model or a quilt. Gram-counters who never use a footbox zip or open the bag flat can find a lighter, simpler bag for less money. Side sleepers who roll off pads are better served by a pad-strapped quilt.

## Verdict

The Spark Pro delivers class-leading down and a warmth rating that holds up in the field, in a bag that packs to roughly the size of a cantaloupe. It carries more zippers than a purist wants and costs more than most rivals, but for cold sleepers who value versatility, it is among the best three-season-plus mummies available.
