## Overview

The Selva 450 is Cumulus's full-length, three-season down underquilt for hammocks. It is filled with 850 fill power Polish goose down, wrapped in Pertex Quantum, and built with the kind of baffle architecture usually reserved for pricier cottage-industry gear. The headline trade-off is straightforward: it gives up the extra warmth of the brand's heavier Selva 600 and the savings of the sewn-through Selva 120 to sit in the middle, where most hammock campers actually spend their nights.

## Performance

**Warmth.** The numbers need reading carefully. Cumulus's -6 °C comfort and -13 °C limit ratings are given for the Selva 450 used together with its Taiga 360 topquilt, not for the underquilt alone. That is how a hammock system works, since the underquilt handles the compression-prone underside and the topquilt handles everything above, but it means the Selva is not a standalone -6 °C solution. Cumulus markets the Selva, Taiga and Mugga hammock as a combination for nights hovering below freezing. One hammock-forum owner who bought the Large noted that the quilt's roughly -6 °C rating, in his words, "should perform fine" as his only underquilt for now, and a separate forum poster who sleeps under Cumulus gear says the brand's temperature ratings have matched his own experience. That fits the brand's reputation for fairly honest numbers.

**Construction.** The design choices explain most of the Selva's warmth for its weight. Ten longitudinal H-chambers hold the down in place under gravity, which matters in an underquilt because loose fill migrates toward the sides. The differential cut, with inner and outer fabrics sized differently, lets the down loft fully instead of being pinched by the tension of the outer shell. Retailers describe the H-baffle design as markedly more insulating than sewn-through alternatives such as Cumulus's own Selva 120. The trade-off is that the down still benefits from a regular fluff to keep loft even, and the quilt has a head end and a foot end: one end is slightly wider and goes at the shoulders.

**Weight.** At 725 g for the Large, with 480 g of that being down, the shell, baffles and suspension account for about 245 g. That is a respectable figure for a quilt with full horizontal baffling and a roomy 205 cm height rating. The Quantum DWR fabric is a windproof, lightly water-resistant 27 g/m² material, which suits an underquilt that spends the night in the wind, but it is not a substitute for an underquilt protector in sustained rain.

**Fit and setup.** The oval, tapered shape (110 cm at the top, 95 cm at the foot on the Large) is cut to follow a gathered-end hammock, and the quilt ships with elastic-cord suspension that can be tuned to close drafts. A Hammock Forums owner who fitted the Large to a Hennessy Ultralight Explorer Zip described it as a good fit, and said that building the primary suspension from the supplied shock cord produced no noticeable sagging or air channels in day testing. Cumulus also says the Selva can be used as a lightweight sleeping bag or topquilt in a pinch, a useful bit of versatility for shoulder-season trips.

**Packability.** The down compresses well. The shorter Medium stows in a 6-liter stuff sack, a relevant benchmark for planning pack space.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 850 FP goose down in H-baffles with a differential cut, which is real construction rather than a sewn-through shortcut
- 725 g for the Large, with 480 g of down
- Temperature ratings that owners say track real-world experience
- Shaped to fit a hung hammock, with adjustable elastic suspension included
- Large fits users up to 205 cm
- Made in Poland, with a lifetime warranty from Cumulus

**Cons**

- The -6 °C comfort rating assumes a topquilt on top, so it is not a standalone figure
- Needs periodic fluffing to keep the down evenly distributed
- Not a four-season quilt, and the Selva 600 is the better pick for deep winter
- Direction matters: the wider end has to go at the shoulders
- Down needs protection in persistent wet weather

## Who Should Buy This

Hammock campers who want one quilt that covers spring through autumn and a good part of winter will find it a strong fit, especially those already sleeping under a Taiga 360 topquilt or something similar. Taller hammock sleepers, who struggle to find underquilts that reach their feet and shoulders, are well served by the Large's 205 cm rating. Anyone who regularly camps below -10 °C, or who wants a single quilt to carry the full load of a cold night, should step up to the Selva 600. Summer-only hikers will find the Selva 120 lighter and cheaper.

## Verdict

The Selva 450 is a well-built, honestly rated underquilt that puts premium construction at a mid-weight price. It does what an underquilt should, which is to keep the down lofted under the body and the cold air off the back, and it does so at 725 g in the Large. For three-season hammock use paired with a matching topquilt, it is an easy recommendation.
