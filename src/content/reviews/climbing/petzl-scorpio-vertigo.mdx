## Overview

The Petzl SCORPIO VERTIGO is a Y-shaped via ferrata lanyard built around a progressive-tearing energy absorber, two elastic arms, and a pair of purpose-built VERTIGO WIRE-LOCK carabiners. <cite index="1-1">At 365 g, it's the lightest lanyard in Petzl's via ferrata range</cite>, making it a natural first look for anyone who wants capable, certified protection without a lot of hardware clunking around on the approach. <cite index="6-2">It's easy to use, durably built, and suited to everyone from complete beginners to experienced ferrata regulars.</cite>

## Performance

### Energy Absorber

The absorber is the most important component in any via ferrata lanyard, and Petzl's execution here is solid. <cite index="2-15,2-16,2-17">It's a folded length of load-bearing tape, stitched into a compact block. Under a real load, the stitching breaks progressively — literally letting you down gently.</cite> This "screamer" approach is well-proven, and because <cite index="2-18">it's stored inside a zipped sleeve, you can inspect it to confirm it's in working order</cite> before every outing. That inspectability is a genuine safety feature, not just a nice-to-have — any deployed absorber needs to be retired, and the zip makes that obvious at a glance.

### Elastic Arms

<cite index="13-7">
  Each arm consists of a tubular sling outer sleeve — the load-bearing element —
  wrapped around an elasticated inner core.
</cite>
In practice, this means the arms stay tidy against your body as you move instead
of drooping and catching on rock.
<cite index="6-5">
  The retractable lanyards are very elastic and snap back reliably to their
  original size.
</cite>
The 68 cm retracted / 106 cm extended range is typical for the category, though
a few taller users have noted the arms feel a touch short when reaching between
widely spaced clips — something to be aware of if you're over 185 cm.

### VERTIGO WIRE-LOCK Carabiners

This is where the SCORPIO VERTIGO earns its price. <cite index="6-7,6-8,6-9">The WIRE-LOCK carabiners are well-designed ergonomically — they feel good in hand even with gloves or smaller hands, the gate opening is wide to facilitate clipping onto cables, and the automatic locking system works smoothly.</cite> The double-action locking mechanism means you squeeze and push rather than twist, which is faster and more intuitive on a pumpy sequence. <cite index="3-1">A keylock nose helps prevent the carabiner from snagging during use</cite> — a small detail that matters on old iron rungs and fixed U-bolts. The wire gate also keeps weight down relative to solid-gate alternatives.

### Short Rest Arm

<cite index="1-6">
  The lanyard includes a short arm that allows you to attach a carabiner (sold
  separately) for resting on a rung mid-route.
</cite>
At 22 cm, it keeps you close to the wall without loading the main absorber arms.
It's a practical addition — just budget for the extra carabiner, because the arm
is genuinely useless without one.
<cite index="11-7">
  Adding a matching VERTIGO WIRE-LOCK carabiner to the short arm is worth the
  extra cost for simplifying mid-route rests.
</cite>

### Harness Attachment

<cite index="2-19">
  The absorber sleeve connects to the harness via a twisted loop of tape,
  attached with a girth hitch to the belay loop.
</cite>
The tape loop is functional but on the shorter side.
<cite index="11-11,11-12">
  The tape to larks-foot the lanyard to the harness is quite short, so threading
  it through the belay loop correctly requires opening the absorber zip, which
  makes removal at the end of a long day unnecessarily fiddly.
</cite>
This is a real ergonomic annoyance on multi-day trips and worth knowing before
you buy.

### Arm Twisting

The most consistently reported real-world limitation: <cite index="11-13">without a pivot or swivel between the two arms, they can twist around each other in a spiral as you progress up a route, requiring occasional manual resetting.</cite> It doesn't compromise safety, but it's a minor irritant that Petzl's own SCORPIO VERTIGO SW variant solves with an integrated swivel — that model adds 20 g and is currently aimed at rental/operator fleets rather than retail consumers. <cite index="12-3">With practice you can learn to alternate which side you clip from to largely avoid the tangling issue.</cite>

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- <cite index="1-1">
    Lightest in Petzl's via ferrata lanyard lineup at 365 g
  </cite>
- Inspectable energy absorber with clear visual-check access via zip
- <cite index="6-7">
    Ergonomic carabiners that work well even with gloves or smaller hands
  </cite>
- <cite index="7-2">
    Wide weight-range compatibility (40–120 kg) makes it practical for shared
    use
  </cite>
- Identification panel for equipment lifecycle tracking — handy for clubs or guides
- Comes with a recycled-polyester storage pouch
- CE EN 958 and UIAA certified

**Cons**

- No integrated swivel — arms can twist with repeated use
- <cite index="11-11">
    Girth-hitch attachment tape is quite short, making harness installation and
    removal fiddly
  </cite>
- <cite index="9-6">Arm reach can feel a little short for taller climbers</cite>
- Rest-arm carabiner is not included — adds cost and an extra item to source
- Absorber is single-use; a real fall means retiring the whole lanyard

## Who Should Buy This

The SCORPIO VERTIGO is a strong choice for recreational via ferrata enthusiasts — from day-trippers on accessible Dolomite classics to more committed ferrata regulars logging multi-day objectives. <cite index="11-5,11-6">Multiple users have reported positive experiences across week-long trips to the Dolomites and would recommend it</cite> without hesitation for moderate to challenging routes. It's also sensible for guides or clubs who need an easy-to-inspect, clearly identifiable kit they can audit at the start of each season. If arm twisting is a known pet peeve, look at the operator-facing SCORPIO VERTIGO SW, or consider Petzl's SCORPIO EASHOOK which brings an anti-twist swivel to a retail-oriented format.

## Verdict

The SCORPIO VERTIGO does what matters most — providing a reliable, inspectable energy-absorbing system in the lightest package Petzl currently offers — and pairs it with genuinely good carabiners that hold up across multi-day use. The girth-hitch tape fussiness and lack of a swivel are real niggles rather than dealbreakers, and both have practical workarounds. If you're getting into via ferrata and want a certified, no-drama system from a trusted manufacturer without carrying unnecessary weight, this is a confident choice. I'd rate it **7.5/10**.
