## Overview

The Regatta Foam Sit Mat is a small rectangle of lightweight foam meant to go between your backside and cold, damp, or rocky ground. At 28 g and roughly the price of a sandwich, it's the sort of item that gets tossed into a daypack for a picnic or a festival field. The trade-off is simple: it is cheap and nearly weightless, but it is a general-outdoor product rather than a piece of purpose-built backpacking kit.

## Performance

Regatta pitches the mat at picnics, camping, festivals, and garden afternoons with the kids. That framing is honest about what it does. The foam construction is meant to cushion against hard ground and take the edge off the chill that creeps up through a damp log or a stone wall, and the 35 x 30 cm open size is enough to cover a seat, which is all a sit mat has to do.

That footprint is also the limit. It covers roughly the area of a large placemat, so it works for a perched sit on a rock or a trailside lunch stop but not for stretching out, and it won't double as a pad for feet, a stove, or a pack. Hikers who want a sit pad to pull double duty as a pack-frame stiffener or a kneeling pad for water-filtering will find the size marginal.

On weight, 28 g is competitive with the ultralight accordion-style seat pads sold for backpackers, many of which land in the 30–55 g range. The difference is in construction. Those folding pads typically add a reflective or textured face, a defined fold pattern, and a stuff-friendly shape. Regatta's mat is a plain, flat piece of foam. Retailers describe it as easy to fold for carrying, but a flat slab of foam doesn't compress, so it rides best strapped to the outside of a pack or slid against the back panel rather than packed into a pocket.

Durability is the open question with any foam this thin and this cheap. Closed-cell foam of this type typically scuffs, compresses, and picks up grit over time, and nothing about a roughly £5 mat suggests it was designed for years of abrasive trail use. Regatta's own product page carries a 4.0 out of 5 customer rating, which fits the profile: people are generally satisfied for what they paid, without raving about it.

Wet-ground performance is where a sit mat earns its keep. Foam doesn't absorb much moisture, so the mat keeps a damp seat from soaking through shorts at a lunch break, and it wipes dry on a sleeve. It will not substitute for a sleeping pad, and Regatta doesn't claim it does; the thermal insulation it offers is the modest, sit-for-twenty-minutes kind.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 28 g, light enough to forget about in a pack
- Inexpensive enough to treat as disposable or buy in multiples for a group
- 35 x 30 cm covers a seat on rocks, logs, and wet grass
- Foam construction cushions and takes the chill off cold ground
- Widely stocked by UK and European outdoor retailers

**Cons**

- Small footprint; not useful as a pad for anything beyond sitting
- Flat slab doesn't pack down, so it has to ride outside the pack or against the back panel
- No reflective face, strap, or loop for attaching it to a pack
- Not designed for hard backpacking use, so long-term durability is doubtful
- Material and spec listings vary between Regatta's regional pages

## Who Should Buy This

This is for casual hikers, festival-goers, parents, and day-trippers who want something between them and the ground without spending money or carrying weight. It also suits ultralight hikers on a tight budget who want to try a sit pad before committing to a more refined one.

Thru-hikers who sit on the ground several times a day should look instead at an accordion-fold pad that packs smaller, stays attached to a pack, and is built for repeated abrasion. Anyone who wants a seat pad that also stiffens a frameless pack or stretches into a ground sheet needs something larger than 35 x 30 cm.

## Verdict

The Regatta Foam Sit Mat does exactly one job, keeping cold and wet ground off your seat, for about £5 and 28 g. It's a sensible buy for day hikes, picnics, and short trips, and a low-risk way to find out whether a sit pad belongs in your kit. Dedicated backpackers will outgrow it quickly.
