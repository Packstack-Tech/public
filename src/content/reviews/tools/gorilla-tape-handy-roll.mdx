## Overview

The Gorilla Tape Handy Roll is the pocket-sized sibling of the hardware-store staple: 10 yards of 1-inch black cloth duct tape with Gorilla's double-thick adhesive. It costs about four dollars, patches nearly anything, and tears by hand. The catch for ultralight hikers is that the full roll is more tape than any trip needs, and its plastic-and-cardboard core adds weight a thru-hiker would rather not carry.

## Performance

**Stick.** The adhesive is the reason this tape is in so many repair kits. Gorilla's technical sheet lists adhesion at 40 ounces per inch on a stainless steel test panel. Owners say it grips smooth, rough and uneven surfaces that cheaper duct tape slides off, and that it stays put through winter rain and snow. Halfway Anywhere, which carries Gorilla Tape in its 2025 and 2026 ultralight gear lists, describes it as sticky, waterproof and thick. The site's author patched a hole in a pair of shorts with it, and the patch survived multiple washes.

**Strength and handling.** The tight cloth weave gives the tape its heft, and it still tears cleanly by hand. That matters when a sole is peeling off a trail runner and no scissors are within a day's walk. At 15 mils, it is noticeably thicker than the thin, stretchy tape in many repair kits. That thickness helps on a torn tent fly or a split pack seam, and it makes the tape stiff to wrap tightly around a small-diameter object. Tensile strength is rated at 50 pounds per inch.

**Size.** Buyers compare the roll to a hockey puck or a roll of electrical tape. It fits in a pocket, a first-aid kit or a toolbox, and a one-inch width suits the small jobs that make up most trail repairs: pole-tip splits, pad pinholes, frayed straps, hot spots on a heel. Owners who keep several around the house or in the car also praise it for not wasting tape on small jobs, which is the argument for the narrow roll over the 1.88-inch standard.

**Weight.** This is where the Handy Roll is weakest for the ultralight crowd. Retailer specs for a full roll cluster around 4 ounces. A few listings show 0.95 oz (27 g), and Gorilla's own technical data sheet lists 4 oz. Even the lower figure is more than most hikers need for a five-day trip. Experienced hikers handle this by not carrying the roll. Halfway Anywhere's advice is blunt: bring tape, but not the whole roll. The usual approach is to wind a few feet around a trekking pole, lighter or water bottle, which cuts the carried weight to a few grams.

**Heat and limits.** The rated service temperature is 150°F, with brief exposure to 200°F. The application range is 40–120°F, so tape applied in a freezing camp needs a warm hand or a warm pocket first. Owners report that Gorilla tape turns gummy when left in a black SUV in summer. A roll in a hot car trunk at a trailhead is the likely place that happens. The tape is also not a substitute for purpose-made fabric or inflatable-pad patches. It holds, but on silnylon and Dyneema it can leave residue and tends to lift at the edges when flexed repeatedly.

**Value.** The roll sells for roughly $3.88 at big-box retailers, and it holds a 4.6-star average across hundreds of reviews there. One typical owner says it sticks better than Duck brand for a bit more money.

## Choosing a Version

The two listed versions are the same tape. One inch is 25 mm, and 10 yards is about 9.1 m. The labels differ because of the market: the imperial listing is sold in North America, and the metric listing uses millimeters and meters. The difference in weight between the two entries comes from how each listing reports it, not from a different product. Retailer specs for the full factory roll fall between 0.95 oz and roughly 4.8 oz depending on the listing. Choose by where you shop, not by weight. Wherever you buy it, the carry-weight answer is the same: wind off a few feet and leave the core behind.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Strong adhesive that grips rough, smooth and uneven surfaces
- Tears by hand, with no scissors needed
- Weather-resistant backing that holds up in rain and snow
- Narrow 1-inch width suits small repairs and small kits
- Cheap, widely available and made in the USA
- Has been carried on the ultralight gear lists of experienced hikers

**Cons**

- A full roll weighs about 4 oz by most retailer specs, which is too much for ultralight loadouts
- The 15-mil thickness makes it stiff to wrap around small objects
- Application is rated for 40–120°F only
- Turns gummy if left in a hot car
- Can leave residue on technical fabrics

## Who Should Buy This

Hikers and bikepackers who want a dependable, inexpensive tape for their repair kit, and who are willing to wind off a few feet instead of carrying the roll. It also suits car-camping kits, boat bags and glove boxes, where weight doesn't matter. Gram-counters who want a ready-made kit should look at purpose-built flat tape wraps or small fabric patches, which add almost no weight and work better on pad and tent fabrics.

## Verdict

The Handy Roll is a cheap, tough tape that earns its place in a repair kit, though the full roll is too heavy for ultralight use. Buy it, peel off a few feet, and keep the rest in the garage. For the price, it is hard to beat for general trail repairs.
