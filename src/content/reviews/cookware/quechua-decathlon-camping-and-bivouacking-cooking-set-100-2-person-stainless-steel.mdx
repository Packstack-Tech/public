## Overview

Quechua's Cooking Set 100 is a ten-piece stainless steel kit for two: one saucepan, a lid that doubles as a strainer, two soup bowls and two sets of cutlery, all nested inside the pot and locked shut by a folding handle. It costs about £15 in the UK, which is less than many people spend on a single titanium mug. The trade-off is weight and cooking finesse: at 560 g, this is a car-camping and casual-overnighter kit, not a gram-counter's.

## Performance

**Packing.** The design works well. The pot swallows the bowls and cutlery, the lid clips on top, and the patented folding handle swings over to hold the lot together without rattling. Buyers on Decathlon's own review pages praise exactly this: everything a pair needs, compacted to roughly the footprint of the saucepan. The finished bundle is about the size of a large mug with a lid, and it fits upright in a pack's side pocket or a bear-canister gap.

**Cooking.** Quechua pitches the 100 range as deliberately simple. The pot is single-layer stainless with no non-stick coating, and the company says this keeps it compatible with any heat source and any washing method. The cost shows at the stove. One Decathlon reviewer reported that everything they cooked burned and stuck badly, which is the usual stainless-without-a-heat-spreader story. Pasta, rice cooked with plenty of water and boil-in-bag dinners suit it fine. Anything that needs a low, even simmer asks for constant stirring and a flame turned well down. The pricier MH500 sets add an aluminium-core base specifically to fix this; the 100 does without.

**Capacity.** The 1.6 L working volume is right for two freeze-dried meals or a pot of pasta for a couple, and the 1.95 L brim figure gives some headroom before a boil-over. The lid's strainer holes make draining noodles a one-handed job, though the lid is plastic. A buyer of the four-person sibling reported a lid left on a pan with pasta water slightly melting, so the lid and flame should be kept apart.

**Weight.** The saucepan accounts for 290 g, leaving about 270 g for the lid, bowls and cutlery. Solo hikers who already carry a spork and a mug will find those extras are dead weight, and a titanium pot of similar volume weighs far less. Swiss listings quote a slightly lighter 530 g for the set; the UK page states 560 g.

**Accessories.** Owners are lukewarm on the cutlery. Reviewers describe the knives as barely cutting anything, the forks as wide, and one reports the blue cutlery fading in the wash. The bowls get better marks, with a gently curved rim that suits stews and soups. Some buyers wish cups were included; this set deliberately omits them.

**Durability.** Stainless is the reason to buy this over aluminium at the price. It shrugs off scouring, dents and open-fire blackening, and the plastic parts are the only things likely to fail. Decathlon's listings carry ratings of about 4.7 out of 5, with 718 reviews on the US site.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Complete kit for two at roughly £15
- Compact nesting; the folding handle locks the lid in place for transport
- 304 stainless is tough, rust-resistant and works on gas, wood fires and induction
- 1.6 L is a practical size for two dehydrated meals or a pasta dinner
- Lid works as a strainer

**Cons**

- 560 g is heavy for ultralight use
- No heat-spreading base or non-stick, so food can scorch and stick
- Plastic lid can warp or melt near flame
- Cutlery is mediocre, and the knives are close to decorative
- No cups

## Who Should Buy This

Couples, families with older kids and new campers who want one box ticked for under £20 will get the most from it. It also suits scout leaders and hostel-style bivouac trips, where a pot gets abused and replacing it is no tragedy. Thru-hikers, solo walkers and anyone who counts grams should look at a titanium or hard-anodised aluminium pot and a long-handled spoon instead. Serious stove cooks who simmer rather than boil should step up to Quechua's own MH500 set.

## Verdict

The Cooking Set 100 is a sensible, well-packed budget kit that does what it says, as long as the menu stays at boil-and-pour. Its weight and scorch-prone single-layer pot keep it out of the ultralight conversation, but for a two-person camping meal at this price it is hard to fault.
