## Overview

The Durston X-Mid is a double-wall, trekking-pole-supported tent built on a diagonal-ridgeline geometry that buys headroom and storm resistance without a pile of poles. It comes in a solo X-Mid 1 and a two-person X-Mid 2, and it is aimed at three-season thru-hikers who want a real double-wall shelter at a weight that used to belong to single-wall tarps. The trade-off is the usual one for pole tents: it is not freestanding, and it wants a generous, stakeable patch of ground.

## Performance

### Geometry and Livability

The X-Mid's party trick is its offset layout. A diagonal ridgeline puts the two trekking poles farther apart than a conventional mid would, and keeps them away from the edges where they would spoil wind performance. The payoff is headroom along the whole length of the tent: sitting up, changing layers and rolling over do not involve a negotiation with a center pole.

The inner sits diagonally inside the fly. That creates large side vestibules and leaves a parallelogram floor with spare room above the head and foot of the pad, which is where camp shoes and a headlamp end up. The 1P is long enough for hikers up to about 6'4" and wide enough for wide pads, though anyone over 6'3" will find the mesh close at the ends. On the 2P, Durston's own numbers are a 52-inch width and 92-inch length, enough for two wide pads side by side. Switchback Travel's tester found the 2P's 33.2 sq ft well ahead of two popular freestanding 2P tents, the MSR Hubba Hubba LT and Big Agnes Tiger Wall UL 2, at 32 and 28 sq ft.

### Gen 3 Changes

Gen 3 is the biggest revision the 1P has had. A new high-tenacity 15D silpoly gives roughly the strength of the previous 20D at about 20 percent less weight, and narrower, more precise seams with slimmer tape trim more. Total weight dropped by just under 100 g to 720 g. The interior grew at the same time: one more inch of width, three more inches of length, more vertical mesh walls so shoulders no longer brush the sides, and much larger pockets. The overall footprint shrank.

### Storm Performance

This is where the X-Mid built its reputation. The offset mid shape is strong in wind and rain, and snow tends to tumble down the steep fly panels rather than pile up on top. Owners report riding out 18 hours of steady rain and high wind without worrying about the tent, and one Reddit commenter said they barely noticed gusts that flattened a friend's pop-up tents.

Polyester does much of the work. It does not sag the way silnylon and most nylon flies do, so there is no 2 a.m. trip outside to retension guy lines after the fabric relaxes. It also takes on less water weight and holds its strength when wet.

### Setup

The fly can be pitched alone, which keeps the inner dry in a downpour. It can also go up with the inner attached. Stake the four corners taut, drop the poles through the peak vents, seat the carbide tips in the peak grommets and extend until the walls firm up.

There is a learning curve. Corner stake placement matters before the poles go up, and it takes a couple of pitches to feel natural. The tent needs reasonably flat, stakeable ground. Owners on sloped or tight sites report that if the footprint is not laid out as a clean rhomboid, the panels never tension properly and flap or sag.

### Ventilation and Condensation

Peak vents at opposite corners keep air moving even in rain, and they close with Velcro when not needed. The fly stands well clear of the inner, so even a lazy pitch avoids mesh contact and the damp that goes with it. Most owners describe condensation as manageable.

### Build Quality

The seams get double stitching, double-folded edges and generous bartacking, which Durston says goes further than most pole tents. The company backs its products with a two-year warranty on the 1P, and Dan Durston is known for answering questions and fixing problems personally.

## Choosing a Version

**X-Mid 1 (720 g).** The solo tent for hikers who want a double wall at the lowest weight in the range. It has a 93 × 33 inch floor, 47 inches of headroom and two doors. It is the right size for one person plus a pack under the vestibule.

**X-Mid 2 (1,090 g).** A genuine two-person tent, not a roomy solo one. The 52-inch floor takes two wide pads, the two vestibules total about 23 sq ft, and the peak is around 48 inches. The sleeping area is asymmetric, but the tent is large enough that two people can sleep the same direction comfortably. Split between two hikers, it works out to about 545 g each, well under the 720 g a pair of 1Ps would cost. The footprint is large and can be hard to fit into tight forest sites. Solo hikers who want a lot of room can carry it for roughly 370 g more than the 1P, but most should take the 1P.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Gen 3 shed close to 100 g versus Gen 2 without losing strength or interior space
- Two doors and two large vestibules, uncommon in a solo double-wall
- Fly-first pitch keeps the inner dry in heavy rain
- Steep panels shed wind and snow
- Polyester fly stays taut overnight
- Fits hikers up to about 6'4" in both versions
- Good value at $239 for the 1P and about $319 for the 2P
- Responsive designer and two-year warranty

**Cons**

- Not freestanding, so wooden platforms, rock slabs and sand are difficult
- Needs a fairly large clear site, which can be hard to find in dense forest
- Pitching has a learning curve, especially on uneven ground
- At about 819 g with stakes, the 1P is heavier than the Dyneema-based X-Mid Pro tents
- The 1P's interior is adequate for shoulders but not generous
- Mesh inner offers no cold-weather insulation; the Solid variant suits late-fall trips better

## Who Should Buy This

The X-Mid 1 suits three-season thru-hikers and long-distance backpackers who already carry trekking poles, camp mostly on dirt sites, and want double-wall comfort and dual vestibules without paying Dyneema prices. Trails like the Appalachian Trail, Colorado Trail and Bibbulmun Track are its natural habitat. The X-Mid 2 fits couples or hiking partners who want a livable shared shelter at a weight close to many solo tents. Anyone who regularly camps on rock or platforms, or who wants the lightest possible shelter, should look at a freestanding design or the Pro versions.

## Verdict

The X-Mid combines a non-sagging silpoly fly, two doors, a fly-first pitch and real interior room at 720 g for the 1P and 1,090 g for the 2P. Outdoor Gear Lab concluded that Gen 3 fixed the earlier shortcomings, and few trekking-pole tents match its value. It loses a point only because it cannot go everywhere a freestanding tent can.
