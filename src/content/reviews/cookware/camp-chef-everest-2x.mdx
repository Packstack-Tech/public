## Overview

The Camp Chef Everest 2X is a two-burner propane stove built for car camping, tailgating, and basecamp cooking rather than anything you'd carry in a pack. It's the redesigned, "burlier" successor to Camp Chef's long-running Everest line, and it's aimed at people who cook real meals at camp — pancakes, stir-fries, simmered sauces — rather than just boiling water for freeze-dried bags. At 12 lb, it's not a stove you'll see on a thru-hike, but for a family or small group cooking out of a trunk, it's one of the more capable options out there.

## Performance

The headline feature here is raw output. Each burner puts out 20,000 BTU, and that shows up directly in boil times. <cite index="9-11,9-12">When it comes to boil time, the Camp Chef Everest 2X handily outperformed all the other stoves tested, boiling a liter of water in just under 4 minutes.</cite> OutdoorGearLab's numbers back that up almost exactly: <cite index="7-6">in the wind test, the Everest 2X took 3 minutes and 45 seconds, and in the wind-less test, it took 3 minutes and 25 seconds to boil one liter of water.</cite> That's fast enough that morning coffee doesn't turn into a 15-minute wait.

Wind resistance is where this stove really separates itself from cheaper double-burners. <cite index="7-4,7-11">With a three-sided, nearly seamless windscreen, and burners slightly recessed in the stove body, it performs impressively well in windy conditions.</cite> CleverHiker tested it side-by-side with a compact canister stove in gusty conditions and noted <cite index="9-16">the burners on the ONTOMYO BRS-32 stove that were testing alongside the Everest blew out within 30 seconds in similar weather</cite> — the Everest just kept running. If you camp somewhere exposed (desert, alpine, coastal), that windscreen design alone is worth the weight penalty for a lot of people.

It's not just a blowtorch, either. <cite index="11-1">The Camp Chef Everest 2X excels at nuanced heat control, offering four full knob rotations from off to full power</cite>, which is unusually fine-grained for a stove this powerful. <cite index="1-18">Though the sheer power is impressive, the burners also have exceptional simmer control and maintain an even heat.</cite> That combination — hard boil in under 4 minutes, but also capable of a genuine simmer — is what most reviewers flag as the standout trait. A lot of high-BTU stoves are all-or-nothing; this one isn't.

Build quality gets consistently good marks. Switchback Travel called it <cite index="2-2,2-3">one of the most solidly built stoves they'd tested, with a mostly metal construction that felt burly and confidence-inspiring even after weeks of nightly use on a 6,000-mile road trip.</cite> That said, it's not flawless. The same review noted <cite index="2-6">one of the plastic burner adjustment knobs popping off after months of use — it slides back on but has continued to pop off, which is admittedly annoying</cite>. Home Depot's aggregated customer feedback echoes a similar pattern: praise for power and construction, but <cite index="8-4">some customers have experienced issues with rust over time, difficulty in maintaining a low simmer, and concerns about the quality of certain components.</cite> One reviewer specifically called out the fold-down lid/windscreen as unreliable, saying it <cite index="8-7,8-8">does not lock in place, just rests on top of the stove and falls over at the slightest breeze.</cite>

There's also a documented history worth knowing about if you're shopping secondhand: <cite index="7-1,7-2">Camp Chef had a regulator recall on earlier units, and the newer MSHPX version remedied this issue and is not part of the recall.</cite> If you buy used, it's worth checking whether the fuel regulator has been replaced.

Fuel efficiency is the trade-off for all that power. <cite index="9-18">This stove falls somewhere in the middle when it comes to fuel efficiency</cite>, and multiple reviewers note you'll burn through propane faster than you would on a lower-output stove — worth factoring in if you're running it off small canisters rather than a full 20 lb tank. Cold-weather performance is solid but not magic: like any propane stove, output drops as temperatures approach freezing, so a canister stand or insulating sleeve helps if you're winter camping.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros:**

- Fastest boil times in most head-to-head tests (under 4 minutes per liter)
- Genuinely useful simmer control, not just high/off
- Strong, nearly seamless windscreen that holds a flame in real wind
- Solid, mostly-metal build that holds up to repeated car-camping abuse
- Large enough cooking area (215 sq in) for two pans side by side

**Cons:**

- Heavy and bulky — 12 lb, 25 x 13 in folded, a non-starter for backpacking
- Burns through propane faster than lower-output stoves
- Some reported quality-control gripes: popping knobs, rust over time, a windscreen lid that some users say doesn't lock securely
- Pricier than basic double-burner stoves
- Regulator hose can rattle in transit

## Who Should Buy This

This is a stove for car campers, tailgaters, and basecamp cooks who actually want to cook — not just boil water for dehydrated meals. If you're feeding a group, need a stove that won't gutter out in wind, and don't mind the weight because it's living in a truck bed or trunk rather than a pack, the Everest 2X is one of the stronger options in its class. It's a poor fit for anyone counting ounces; backpackers and ultralight thru-hikers should look elsewhere entirely.

## Verdict

The Everest 2X delivers on its core promise: it's fast, wind-resistant, and controllable enough to actually cook a real meal rather than just heat water. The scattered complaints about knobs, rust, and the windscreen lid keep it from a top-tier score, but for car camping and basecamp use it's a dependable, high-output workhorse. I'd put it at a 7.8/10 — excellent for its intended use case, irrelevant for anyone thinking about pack weight.
