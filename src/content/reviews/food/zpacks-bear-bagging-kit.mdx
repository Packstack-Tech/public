## Overview

The Zpacks Bear Bagging Kit is a complete hang system built around a roll-top Dyneema Composite Fabric food bag. It's aimed at thru-hikers and ultralight backpackers who work in bear country where hanging food is legal and sensible. The trade-off is the one every hang kit makes: it saves roughly a pound over a hard canister, but it protects food only if the hang is good.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 3.2 oz for the whole system, the kit adds little to a pack. Ultimate Gear Lists, which names it the best ultralight bear hang kit in its roundup, calls a hang kit at this weight "as good as it gets." The bag is a Dyneema sack, so it collapses to almost nothing when empty and does double duty as an ordinary food bag on trips where hanging isn't required.

**Fabric and weather.** Zpacks makes its food bags from 1.6 oz/sqyd DCF, heavier than the fabric used in much of its other gear. The thicker material is meant to resist abrasion from pointy food items and the scraping a bag takes when it's hoisted over bark. Fully taped seams keep the contents dry, and a long-term owner on Vetted Outdoors reports they have kept food dry through rainy trips over several years. Buyers who have used the Large bag report it holds five days of food with room left over, and they like the roll-top snap closure.

**Setting a hang.** The kit is built to make a notoriously fiddly job easier. The rock sack gives the line enough weight to throw over a branch. The slick-sheathed cord is made to slide over bark without grabbing, and the carabiner clips the bag to the line. None of that removes the difficulty of finding a branch that's the right thickness, extends into open air and sits high enough. Reviewers note that a hang is prone to user error and is sometimes impossible in treeless or open terrain.

**Durability.** Owners find the bag and carabiner hold up well. The rock sack is the wear item. One long-term user reported scuffing from repeated contact with the ground. That same owner swapped the stock cord for a reflective-tracer bear line, because a bag hanging in a dark tree is hard to find at 5 a.m.

**Bear and critter resistance.** The fabric is strong enough to discourage small rodents, but Zpacks itself says the bag should still hang out of reach overnight. Testers are blunter about larger animals: Dyneema is durable but no match for a determined bear, and a persistent rodent given enough time can chew through it. The bag is not IGBC certified. The kit works only as well as the hang does.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- About 92 g for bag, rock sack, cord and carabiner
- Waterproof, fully taped DCF bag in a thicker 1.6 oz/sqyd fabric
- Large size carries 5–6 days of food
- Rock sack and slick cord make a hang easier than improvised gear
- Bag works on its own where storage lockers or poles are provided

**Cons**

- Not bear-resistant and not IGBC certified
- Useless as a primary system where canisters are mandatory
- Hang quality depends on terrain, technique and available branches
- Rock sack shows wear faster than the rest of the kit
- Costs far more than a stuff sack and a length of cord

## Who Should Buy This

This kit suits thru-hikers and experienced ultralighters in regions where hanging is accepted practice and who already know how to set a counterbalance or PCT-style hang. It also works for anyone who wants a durable waterproof food bag with the cord and rock sack already sorted. Anyone heading into areas that require hard-sided canisters or IGBC-certified bags (including many national parks and parts of the High Sierra and the Adirondacks) should look elsewhere. So should anyone who camps often above treeline, or who doesn't want to practice hanging. An Ursack or a canister is the better choice for them.

## Verdict

The Zpacks Bear Bagging Kit is the lightest credible way to hang food, and its Dyneema bag survives rain and abrasion. It's an excellent tool for hikers who can hang well and a poor substitute for a canister where one is required. Buy it with that limit understood.
