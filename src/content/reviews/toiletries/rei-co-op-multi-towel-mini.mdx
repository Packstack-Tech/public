## Overview

The REI Co-op Multi Towel Mini is a 15.5-inch square of microfiber that weighs 17 g (0.6 oz) and costs $9.95. It is built for the hiker who wants something better than a bandana for wiping hands, dishes, and tent walls, without carrying anything that earns the word "towel" in the full sense. The trade-off is in the name: it is a mini, and it behaves like one.

## Performance

**Size and weight.** At roughly the footprint of a large handkerchief, the Mini is sized for faces, hands, and bowls rather than torsos. Owners on REI's product page say it is light enough that they never notice it in the pack, and the small pouch with its carabiner lets it hang off a shoulder strap or sit in a hip-belt pocket where it can dry as the miles pass. One buyer reported clipping the pouch to a motorcycle to finish drying in the wind.

**Drying speed.** This is the towel's strongest suit. The weave is thin, so it wrings out well and dries fast. Reviewers say it is typically ready by the next meal, unlike thicker microfiber towels that can take a day or two to dry in humid weather. A hanging loop on the towel makes it easy to string up at camp or on a pack.

**Absorbency.** REI rates it at up to three times its weight in water. For a 17 g cloth, that is about 50 g of water, which is enough for a wet tent wall or a rinsed pot but not a soaked body. A long-distance hiker who carried the earlier version of this towel on the Appalachian Trail used it to wipe condensation off a tent after rain, as a sweatband, and for sponge baths. He said it holds a lot of water and wrings out nearly dry. Reviewers who tried it as a shower towel found the limit quickly: more wringing, and still a damp finish. As one gear writer put it, a towel this size handles a partial towel-off. It does not replace a bath towel.

**Feel.** The texture is functional rather than soft. At least one buyer found it scratchy on the face and recommends against using it on glasses, binocular lenses, or camera glass. Microfiber towels in general also don't feel like terry cloth, and the Mini is no exception.

**Use cases.** Most owners point to camp chores: a mess-kit napkin, a dish towel, a tent-wiping rag, a face wipe on a ski day. Travel writers like it as a paper-towel replacement in airport restrooms. Those are the jobs where 15.5 inches is plenty.

**Materials.** The fabric is mostly recycled polyester, a plus for buyers who care about that, though as with any synthetic microfiber, it will shed fibers in the wash. Washing it in a laundry bag or filter reduces that.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 17 g, light enough to ignore
- Dries quickly, often by the next meal
- Pouch, carabiner, and hanging loop make it easy to carry and dry on the move
- 70% recycled polyester content
- $9.95, with a 4.5-star average across more than 220 REI reviews

**Cons**

- Too small to dry a whole body; more wringing and a damp finish
- Can feel scratchy on the face and isn't suited to lenses
- Absorbs up to 3x its weight, which is modest next to larger microfiber towels
- Synthetic fabric sheds microfibers in the wash

## Who Should Buy This

Ultralight and weight-conscious backpackers who want a dedicated cloth for dishes, hands, condensation, and sweat will get the most from it. It also suits travelers and day hikers who want a small towel on hand. Anyone who showers at campgrounds, swims, or wants to dry off after a river wash should look at a larger towel such as REI's own Multi Towel Lite or Deluxe, or a pocket towel with more surface area. Skip it as a face cloth if sensitive skin is a concern.

## Verdict

The Multi Towel Mini does a small job well: it adds almost no weight, dries fast, and costs less than a freeze-dried dinner. Buy it as a camp rag and a sweat wipe. Don't buy it expecting to dry off completely.
