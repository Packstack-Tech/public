## Overview

The SnowDrifter is Patagonia's 20-liter pack for quick-strike days on skis or a splitboard: dawn patrols, resort-access sidecountry, and short tours where the load is a shovel, probe, a layer and snacks. It borrows from ski-mountaineering packs, putting storage on the shoulder straps, hipbelt and lumbar so nothing requires dropping the pack. The trade-off is plain: a tough shell and a smart layout in a small package that costs $249.

## Performance

**Carry and comfort.** The harness is a stretchy double-weave rather than a stiff, padded affair, and the idea is that it moves with the torso. Backcountry.com's staff describe the low-volume build as the sort that makes skiers forget they're wearing a pack on the descent. BetterTrail's tester scored comfort and support 4 out of 5, crediting the minimalist design and low weight on both the climb and the downhill. At 886 g for 20 liters, it works out to roughly 44 g per liter. That isn't skimo-race light, and it isn't trying to be.

**Access and organization.** This is where the SnowDrifter earns its price. Large shoulder-strap pockets take water flasks up to 500 ml, and they can be cinched down on storm days. The hipbelt pockets handle small items. A side-accessible lumbar pocket holds skins, an extra pair of gloves or a bottle, and can be reached without taking the pack off. A lined top pocket fits goggles and gloves. BetterTrail rated organization 4 out of 5 and called the external storage exceptional for a pack this size. The one nit: the same tester found the lumbar pocket difficult to close completely.

**Snow safety.** Dedicated sleeves for the probe, shovel handle and shovel blade keep the avalanche kit separate from lunch and layers, so it can be pulled quickly. A U-shaped lid opens the main compartment wide, which spares users from digging through a tube of gear.

**Durability and weather.** The body is a 7.9-oz recycled ripstop with a TPU coating, a step up in coating from earlier Snow Drifter generations, which used 420-denier nylon. BetterTrail's tester found the shell burly, with good durability and water resistance, scoring durability 4 out of 5. Retailers describe the DWR finish as built to fend off light precipitation, not sustained rain. That's appropriate for snow, and a reminder that this is not a pack for a wet-season trail hike.

**Ski and board carry.** A side loop and a shoulder-mounted catch allow diagonal carry of skis or a splitboard without removing the pack, a handy trick on a short bootpack or when transitioning between skin tracks.

**Capacity.** Twenty liters holds the essentials and not much beyond them. BetterTrail's tester judged capacity too limited for most full backcountry days and gave versatility the lowest score of the pack's ratings, 3 out of 5. It overall scored 4.1 out of 5. On Patagonia's own site, the pack carries a 4.7 out of 5 average from 10 reviews.

## Choosing a Version

The SnowDrifter comes in three sizes. Width and depth don't change at 10.5" by 9.25"; only the pack's height does, at 19" for the small, 21" for the medium and 23" for the large. Volume stays 20 liters across the range, so the choice is about how the pack sits against the back and where the hipbelt lands, not how much it holds.

- **Small (19")**: for shorter torsos, where a taller pack would ride into the helmet or bottom out on the hips.
- **Medium (21")**: the default for average builds.
- **Large (23")**: for longer torsos, keeping the hipbelt and lumbar pocket where they belong.

Size by torso, not by height, and favor the shorter option if between sizes: a pack that rides low and snug suits the fast-and-light intent.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Storage reachable without removing the pack: shoulder pockets, hipbelt pockets and a side-access lumbar pocket
- Dedicated sleeves for probe, shovel handle and blade
- Tough, coated recycled-nylon shell that tolerates ski edges and rock
- Diagonal ski or splitboard carry without taking the pack off
- 886 g, with a low-profile, close-fitting build
- Fair Trade Certified construction and recycled fabrics

**Cons**

- $249 is steep for 20 liters
- Too small for long tours or anyone carrying more than the essentials
- Lumbar pocket is awkward to close fully
- Water resistance is a DWR finish, not a waterproof build

## Who Should Buy This

The SnowDrifter suits skiers and splitboarders who do short tours, dawn laps or lift-served backcountry, and who value reaching a flask, skins or gloves on the move. It is a ski pack, and ultralight backpackers should treat it as a winter day-trip tool, not a multi-day or three-season hiking pack: there is no room for a shelter and quilt system, and the harness is designed around ski carry. Anyone doing full-day tours with extra layers, a stove and a big first-aid kit should step up to a larger pack.

## Verdict

The SnowDrifter 20L is a well-organized, durable pack that does exactly what it says for short, fast days in avalanche terrain. It's expensive for its volume and too small to be someone's only touring pack, but for the user it targets, the access and build quality justify a recommendation.
