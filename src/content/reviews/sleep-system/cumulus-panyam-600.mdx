## Overview

The Panyam 600 is a Polish-made down mummy that sits at the cold end of Cumulus's three-season range. It carries 600 g of 850-fill goose down in a 29 g/m² Pertex Quantum shell, and it weighs 970 g. The trade-off is the usual one for a direct-from-Poland brand: excellent warmth for the weight and price, with zipper and sizing quirks that pricier bags tend to have sanded off.

## Performance

**Warmth.** Reviewers who have taken the Panyam 600 below freezing generally find the -6°C comfort rating honest and sometimes conservative. One European tester who ran it through a series of trips judged the rating "a bit on the safe side" and found it warmer than the number suggests. The trapezoid baffles are part of the reason. They hold the down in place across the chamber and leave fewer cold spots than simple box or H-style construction. A down-filled collar and a hood that cinches around the face handle the drafts that tend to ruin cold nights.

There is a caveat for cold sleepers. A long-term user who logged about 500 nights in the bag found it too cold below freezing, especially at the foot end, and routinely stuffed a down jacket around her feet. On r/Ultralight and r/backpacking, the standard advice to people who sleep cold is to treat the rating as optimistic. Pair the bag with a warm pad, and carry a hooded down jacket for the coldest nights.

**Weight and packability.** At 970 g for a -6°C comfort rating, the warmth-to-weight ratio is the main selling point. Owners describe the bag as small when stuffed and quick to loft once it is out of the sack. One independent weigh-in on a bag with its stuff sack came to 1,034 g, so expect real-world numbers a little over the claimed figure if you carry it in a heavier sack.

**Fabric and durability.** Pertex Quantum at 29 g/m² is a thin shell, yet the 500-night user reported almost no visible wear. Reviewers also describe the materials and YKK hardware as high quality. The standard DWR finish only goes so far, and the same long-term user noted that the bag takes on moisture in damp conditions. Cumulus offers hydrophobic down and a sturdier shell as paid upgrades. One owner who ordered both, bringing the price to roughly €600, reported that the bag stayed warm after getting wet on a hike. That is a lot of money for a bag whose appeal is value, but it suits wet-climate hikers.

**Zipper and fit.** The full-length zipper runs nearly to the foot, so the bag unzips flat and works as a quilt-style blanket in milder weather. Side sleepers in particular like that option. The self-locking sliders keep it from creeping open overnight, but a long-term user said the zipper often snags on the fabric. Pertex shells and coil zippers are a known snag pairing, so work the slider slowly in the dark.

Sizing deserves attention. At 208 cm long, the bag is rated for users up to 190 cm, but a 190 cm tester found his feet hit the end and the shoulder drawcord would not close fully. A shorter hiker, 172 cm, found the length roomy enough to pull the hood fully over her head. Taller hikers should use the configurator to order extra length.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 970 g for a -6°C comfort rating is hard to beat at this price
- Trapezoid baffles and a down collar minimize cold spots and drafts
- Full-length zipper lets the bag double as a quilt-style blanket
- Fill, length and shell can be customized, and Cumulus offers down replacement
- Long-term owners report little shell wear after hundreds of nights

**Cons**

- Cold sleepers report cold feet and sub-freezing nights that need extra layers
- Zipper can snag on the thin shell
- Standard down takes on moisture in damp conditions, and the hydrophobic upgrade adds significant cost
- Length runs short for users near the 190 cm limit

## Who Should Buy This

The Panyam 600 suits three-season and shoulder-season backpackers who expect nights near or just below freezing and want the lightest bag they can buy without paying for an American cottage-brand label. It is a strong choice for European hikers who can get one quickly and for anyone who likes sleeping in a bag but wants the option of venting it like a quilt. Cold sleepers planning regular nights well below freezing should look at a warmer model or plan on a hooded down jacket and a high-R-value pad. Hikers over about 188 cm should order a longer version rather than the standard cut.

## Verdict

The Panyam 600 delivers the warmth-to-weight and the build quality that justify Cumulus's reputation, at a price that still undercuts most competitors. Its faults are a snag-prone zipper and a standard length that runs small for tall hikers. For a sub-kilo bag rated to -6°C, it is one of the better buys in the category.
