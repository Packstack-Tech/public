## Overview

The Snow Peak Trek Titanium Plate is a 7.5-inch round of Japanese-made titanium that weighs 2 oz (56.7 g). It is built for backpackers who want a real surface to eat from and are willing to pay for metal that won't crack, stain, or hold onto last night's curry. The trade-off is size: this is a plate for a tortilla, a sandwich, or a pile of snacks, not a full dinner.

## Performance

**Weight and packability.** At 2 oz, the plate costs about as much pack weight as a tent stake set of four. The shallow, 1-inch profile means it lies flat against the back panel or slides into a pot's lid space without eating volume. Snow Peak describes it as stackable, and owners who buy several report that plates nest cleanly with each other, with the matching Trek bowls forming their own separate stack.

**Food handling.** The edge tilts upward just enough to corral food, a detail owners point out specifically. One reviewer on REI used the plate for salad, fruit, pasta, and for hydrating morning oatmeal with very hot water, and had no complaints. Another found it just big enough for a single flat tortilla. That is the practical ceiling. A pile of rice and beans will crowd the rim, and anyone cooking for appetite rather than efficiency will find the surface small.

**Heat.** Titanium conducts heat poorly compared with aluminum, but it is still bare metal, and a thin sheet of it. Owners note that the plate gets hot quickly when loaded with something just off the stove, with no insulation to protect fingers or a lap. A hot meal means holding it by the rim edge or setting it down. Used as a cold-food surface or a lid-sized staging plate, the issue disappears. Reviewers who stay off open flame also report no warping or discoloration.

**Cleaning.** Titanium's non-porous surface doesn't absorb odors or flavors, and one owner called the plate a breeze to clean next to the plastic and coated plates they had used before. Another found it no easier to wash than any other camp plate, which is fair: a wipe with a bit of water and a finger handles most trail meals, and the flat geometry has no corners to chase.

**Durability.** The material is the selling point. Titanium shrugs off the dents, scratches and stains that plague plastic and enamel plates, and it won't rust. One skeptic on REI worried that a plate this thin would bend in a crowded pack. Most owners describe it as durable in practice, and a stiff-rimmed 7.5-inch disc is hard to deform when it rides flat. Stuffing it loose between a bear canister and a stove is another matter.

**Versatility.** Owners have found uses beyond dinner: a makeshift lid for a pot, a fan for coaxing a campfire, a frisbee at the trailhead, and, in one case, a dog's feeding dish, where the low rim suited a pet that struggles with deep bowls.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- 56.7 g (2 oz), light enough to justify carrying a real plate
- Titanium resists stains, odors, rust and dents
- Flat 1-inch profile packs easily; plates stack with plates
- Upturned edge keeps food on the surface
- Made in Japan with a retail price around $19
- Works as a lid, fan or feeding dish in a pinch

**Cons**

- At 7.5 inches, it holds roughly a tortilla's worth of food
- No heat insulation; hot meals make the rim uncomfortable to hold
- Thin metal can flex if packed loose or under load
- Not a replacement for a bowl for soups, stews, or freeze-dried meals
- A plain titanium disc costs more than a cheap plastic or silicone alternative, with minimal weight savings over a good plastic plate

## Who Should Buy This

This plate suits a hiker who eats cold lunches, tortilla wraps, snack spreads, and sandwiches, or one who builds a two-person or car-camping kit around Snow Peak's titanium line and wants a matching stackable set. It is also a good fit for anyone who finds eating off a pot lid or a bag tedious and wants a clean surface for prep. Freeze-dried-bag eaters and anyone who cooks in a pot and eats from it will gain little. Hungry hikers with a big dinner should take the Trek bowl instead, since the plate's capacity is limited.

## Verdict

The Trek Titanium Plate is a well-made, genuinely light piece of tableware that does what a plate should and nothing more. It earns its place in kits that are built around wraps and snacks rather than soupy dinners. Everyone else will be better served by a bowl or a mug.
