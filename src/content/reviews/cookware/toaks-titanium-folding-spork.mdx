## Overview

The TOAKS Titanium Folding Spork (SLV-06) is a bare-bones eating utensil aimed at ultralight backpackers who want to shave weight off their cook kit without spending much money. It's a titanium spoon/fork hybrid with a hinge in the handle that locks open or closed via a sliding collar, and it's the kind of thing most people buy once, forget about, and use for a decade.

## Performance

**Eating experience:** The bowl end is a genuine spoon/fork hybrid — deep enough to scoop oatmeal or rehydrated rice, with short tines at the tip for stabbing chunks of freeze-dried chicken or tortellini. TOAKS <cite index="15-6,15-7">describes it as a lightweight titanium spork with matte finish, with a folding design that saves space when weight and space is limited</cite>. The head is polished while the handle is matte, which is a nice touch — the polished bowl feels smoother against your lips and is a bit easier to lick clean than a raw matte finish would be.

**Titanium taste:** This is the recurring gripe with almost every bare titanium utensil, TOAKS included. One reviewer testing a similar TOAKS titanium spork noted <cite index="1-1,1-2">you can taste a very slight hint of "metal" when using it, and a polished finish may generate less of the metal taste, but also, may add weight to the item</cite>. Since this spork's bowl is polished, it should mute that effect somewhat, but don't expect it to disappear — thin bare titanium almost always has a faint tang, especially with hot, acidic foods.

**The folding mechanism:** This is the spork's main differentiator from cheaper one-piece titanium sporks — and also its main point of contention. The hinge is spring-loaded and a sliding collar locks it in the open position so it doesn't flop shut while you're mid-bite. It works well in the box, but it's a wear part, and it's the first thing to loosen up over years of use. One buyer on a TOAKS retail listing summed up the common complaint: the slider "doesn't stay in place very good," though they still liked the spork overall. That kind of intermittent, low-grade complaint pops up more than a design flaw and less than a dealbreaker — most people who mention it are still using and recommending the spork.

**Durability:** Between the titanium body and the stainless hardware in the hinge, this thing is more or less indestructible under normal use. It won't snap like a plastic spork, and titanium doesn't corrode, so you can leave it damp in a food bag for days without worrying about rust. The folding hinge is really the only mechanical failure point, and even then, "failure" usually just means the lock gets a little loose, not that the utensil breaks.

**Size and packability:** At 165 mm open and 95 mm folded, it's noticeably more compact stashed away than a rigid long-handled spork, and it'll drop into a hip belt pocket or a small pot without poking through mesh. The trade-off, as one reviewer of a similar TOAKS spork put it, is that <cite index="1-6">it would be nice if this folded up and compressed even smaller like some other sporks on the market, but that would likely add to the weight</cite> — so TOAKS made the sensible call to keep the design simple and light rather than chase maximum compactness.

**Weight:** At 18 g, it's not the lightest spork on the market (some rigid, hinge-free titanium sporks dip into the 8-13 g range), but the fold-down length is the trade you're making for that extra few grams. If you're chasing every last gram and don't care about the folding feature, a simple flat titanium spoon or spork will beat it on the scale.

## Pros & Cons

**Pros**

- Genuinely lightweight at 18 g while still folding down to under 4 inches
- Titanium body resists corrosion and won't hold onto food odors
- Polished bowl is more comfortable to eat from than a raw matte finish
- Comes with a drawstring pouch, keeping it out of the rest of your kit
- Inexpensive — usually $11–14
- Sturdy enough to double as a light cooking utensil for stirring in a pot

**Cons**

- Folding lock mechanism can loosen over time, per user reports
- Bare titanium carries a faint metallic taste, though the polish helps
- Heavier than non-folding ultralight titanium sporks
- Tines are shallow — not great for anything beyond soft or bite-sized food
- No spoon-only or fork-only option if you'd rather specialize

## Who Should Buy This

This is a solid pick for thru-hikers and backpackers who want a compact, packable utensil without paying $20+ for a premium folding spork, and who don't mind a small amount of long-term wear in the hinge. If you're a gram-counter who never needs to fold your utensil down (because it lives in your pot the whole trip anyway), a rigid titanium spork will save you a few grams. But for most people looking for a "buy it and forget it" camp utensil, this hits a good balance of weight, price, and durability.

## Verdict

The TOAKS Titanium Folding Spork does exactly what it promises: a light, durable, inexpensive utensil that folds down small and holds up over years of trail use. The locking hinge is a genuine convenience but also the one part worth keeping an eye on, since it's the component most likely to loosen with age. At around $12, it's an easy recommendation for anyone assembling a cook kit on a budget. I'd rate it a 7.5/10 — reliable and well-priced, just not quite as refined as pricier folding sporks with sturdier lock mechanisms.
